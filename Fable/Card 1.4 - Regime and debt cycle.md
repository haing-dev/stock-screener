## 1. Verdict

1. The playbook, as far as the pack shows it: follow how debt, deficits and policy feed spending, profits and the demand for bonds; work out what prices already assume; spread risk so that no single outcome sinks the book. The framework the brief names is not laid out in the quotes: none of the 183 states growth and inflation regimes or risk parity; the nearest to a debt-cycle template is RD naming a recurring dynamic, "the amounts of debt creation and debt monetization that whole dynamic and last time that happened was in 1930 to 45 period" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C05-3|MT058-C05-3]]), with no stages or thresholds; NY's growth-and-inflation remark is second-hand ([[MT053 - Episode 4- Noah Yechiely - Former Head of Equities at Bridgewater Associates#^MT053-C03-3|MT053-C03-3]]).
2. Little of it can be coded: 2 rules, 8 versions. Debt, deficits, profits, savings, tariffs and positions have no series on the data sheet. The codable form of the growth and inflation idea is the Economic trend pod we already hold.
3. Strongest link: KT's profit accounting and equity arithmetic (SHOWN in words, one worked case in the quote).
4. Weakest link: the step from debt arithmetic to a dated market move. Nobody gives a threshold or a horizon. Ledger (machine-coded): 5 of 13 resolved rows right; this card disputes two of them (MT068-V002-1 resolved early, MT138-V005-1 not scoreable; pack problems 2 and 3), leaving 5 of 11; the two headline calls, US stocks down in 2022 and yields up in 2023, missed at 12 months.
5. Dating: both rules are hindsight-flagged. Two pre-2011 uses are recalled: BP's levered bond-futures mandate (no year in the quote) and RD's use of 1930s history, which the quote ties to the 2008 crisis ("that understanding of History led me to be able to anticipate the 2008 financial crisis", [[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C05-2|MT058-C05-2]]), so it is dated before 2011; neither quote describes a trade, and neither carries a rule.
6. Counts: 2 rules, 10 analyst questions, 7 open forecasts (F1-F7; F8's window has closed and F9 has no horizon or live series, both kept as context), 2 diagnostics.
7. Against card 1: agrees that easing into a boom is inflationary and that the years of zero rates and bond buying starve such a playbook; conflicts on Treasuries and on sizing (spread here, concentrate there).

## 2. Causal model

**Chain 1. Debt squeeze (RD, 2023).** Debt grows, so "Debt Service becomes a higher and higher percentage of your expenses" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C06-2|MT058-C06-2]]). Holders need a real return: "if you don't have a return in relation to inflation that is High Enough by holding that debt you won't hold that debt" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C06-3|MT058-C06-3]]). The central bank then "is forced to choose between is interest rates Rising a lot" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C07-2|MT058-C07-2]]) or printing (claim text). Other side: investors who "all lost a lot of money" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C14-X1|MT058-C14-X1]]; that the losses are on bonds is claim text), reserve holders who fear they "can get sanctioned" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C14-3|MT058-C14-3]]), a central bank now "essentially a seller of those bonds" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C14-2|MT058-C14-2]]). ASSERTED. Limits: no threshold or horizon; if the bank prints, the loss comes through money, not bond prices; ledger: his yields-up view wrong at 12 months.

**Chain 2. Fiscal limit (GJ, 2025).** Past a limit, "spending money becomes counterproductive rather than productive" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C09-1|MT068-C09-1]]), and "the correlation between bonds and equities and currency will shift when you hit limits" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C10-3|MT068-C10-3]]): "we're all Brazil now" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C08-3|MT068-C08-3]]). Other side: investors who hold bonds as a hedge. ASSERTED. Limits: each asset's direction and the place of the limit are claim text; no trigger.

**Chain 3. Competition for capital (GJ, 2025).** Deficits plus AI spending give "this big competition for capital between fiscal and AI" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C11-1|MT068-C11-1]]), so real rates rise (claim text); if politics forces rates down, "lowering real rates into a boom like this is particularly inflationary" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C08-2|MT068-C08-2]]). Other side: bond holders. ASSERTED. No series for either demand.

**Chain 4. Profits need spending that is not wages (KT, 2022).** A business grows its margin if it can "raise revenue or get people to pay for its products without paying labor" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C01-1|MT147-C01-1]]); that aggregate profits need non-wage spending is claim text. The money comes from household "leverage borrowing spending down your savings" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C01-2|MT147-C01-2]]), company borrowing ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C01-3|MT147-C01-3]]) or the deficit ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C01-4|MT147-C01-4]]). When these fade, with spending "totally funded by households basically dis saving" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C03-2|MT147-C03-2]]), mortgage rates up ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C03-3|MT147-C03-3]]) and "the tightest labor market in decades" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C03-4|MT147-C03-4]]), profits fall. Other side: shareholders, since "the market is not expecting a very significant slowdown" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C05-2|MT147-C05-2]]). SHOWN (words): an accounting identity, which she herself limits: "that's a mechanical answer right these are all people governments individuals businesses they have to choose to borrow for a reason" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C01-X2|MT147-C01-X2]]); the identity alone does not say that borrowing will fade. Limits: no timing. Ledger: growth slowed, yet the S&P rose 16% in 12 months. In 2025 she expects the deficit back: "26 is where you can actually get the fiscal stimulation" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C03-4|MT138-C03-4]]).

**Chain 5. Reading what is priced (KT, 2022).** A stock is discounted cash flows ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C04-X1|MT147-C04-X1]]). Worked case: "if interest rates rose one and a half percent on a 10-year bond and maybe you have a 10-year stream of earnings that's a 15 equity decline" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C04-4|MT147-C04-4]]). So "equities have basically fallen in line with the fact that rates have risen that's it" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C05-1|MT147-C05-1]]): no profit fall is priced. SHOWN (one worked case; arithmetic, not evidence of returns). Also, ASSERTED: "back then about 30 of the stock market was commodity producers today it's more like 3" ([[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C04-2|MT147-C04-2]]), so the index gains less from inflation than history suggests.

**Chain 6. Policy asymmetry (KT, 2025).** Tariffs raise US prices, perhaps as "a one-off" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C04-X1|MT138-C04-X1]]); abroad "it's much more deflationary" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C04-1|MT138-C04-1]]). The Fed is stuck, others can ease: "I like fixed income a lot more in other countries" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C03-5|MT138-C03-5]]). On the dollar, "the big currency drivers" will "take a while to work through governance" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C04-4|MT138-C04-4]]). Other side: investors "extremely vulnerable" if the US stops winning ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C03-1|MT138-C03-1]]). ASSERTED, a dated view. Ledger: euro and inflation rows right, slowdown and yen rows wrong (the yen is in no quote).

**Chain 7. Bonds as the hedge, and its floor (BP, KT; firm-level).** Institutions hold bonds because "if there's a recession at least I'll get some easing and that'll be kind of balancing out of all my risky assets" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C06-X1|MT138-C06-X1]]). BP recalls the fear that in "a deflationary depression it everything's going to go down together" ([[MT049 - Episode 12- Bob Prince - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Associates#^MT049-C07-1|MT049-C07-1]]) and the answer built: "25 year duration implemented with futures two two times two and a half times leverage" ([[MT049 - Episode 12- Bob Prince - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Associates#^MT049-C07-2|MT049-C07-2]]; an action, no year). That the hedge had no room at zero rates is claim text ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C06-X1|MT138-C06-X1]]); the quotes say only that "rates were already zero it was sort of a breaking point of the paradigm" (KT 2022, [[MT147 - Sohn 2022 - A Macro Approach with Karen Karniol-Tambour#^MT147-C02-X2|MT147-C02-X2]]) and that duration and currency alpha "seemed uninteresting for a while where rates were zero and are kind of coming back in a big way" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C06-2|MT138-C06-2]]), which is about alpha, not the hedge. This is the nearest the pack comes to the balance idea. SHOWN for the mandate's terms; ASSERTED for the hedge: no result is given.

**Chain 8. Regime change (BP, GJ, KT, 2025).** The old world was "globalization and free markets" ([[MT049 - Episode 12- Bob Prince - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Associates#^MT049-C11-2|MT049-C11-2]]); the new one is "modern mercantalism" ([[MT138 - Rob Citrone and Karen Karniol-Tambour at Sohn 2025#^MT138-C01-2|MT138-C01-2]]). GJ calls it inflationary: "You get tariffs" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C07-2|MT068-C07-2]]; the inflation link for tariffs is claim text), and "there's nothing more inflationary really than military spending" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C07-3|MT068-C07-3]]). Capital "is more concentrated in the US than ever" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C01-2|MT068-C01-2]]); its holders are the other side. ASSERTED. GJ concedes: "I thought it would have bigger impact than it has" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C18-2|MT068-C18-2]]; that "it" is mercantilism is claim text).

NY, second-hand: macro investing reads "how much the economy is growing inflation", forces "shifting the entire asset class together" ([[MT053 - Episode 4- Noah Yechiely - Former Head of Equities at Bridgewater Associates#^MT053-C03-3|MT053-C03-3]]). Nothing follows from it that Economic trend does not hold.

## 5. Analyst layer

4. Priced in (chain 5): given the S&P 500's 12-month change and 10 times the 12-month change of GS10, SPY falls at least 4%.
5. US against the rest (chain 8): SPY returns less than the equal-weight non-US stock indexes of the panel, in dollars.
6. Policy asymmetry (chain 6): the US 10-year bond returns less than the equal-weight non-US 10-year bonds of the panel.
7. Dollar (chain 6): FXE rises; FXY rises.
8. Inflation (GJ): the 12-month change of CPILFESL in the last release before the horizon is 3.0 or more; T5YIFR is higher than today.
9. Fed (GJ): at least two dissents at the next scheduled meeting; resolves from the Fed's statement.

## 6. Process rules

- Spread risk. RD: "diversification properly done could reduce my Risk by up to 80% without reducing my return" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C04-2|MT058-C04-2]]). ASSERTED. Reader's arithmetic: 80% needs 25 equal uncorrelated bets; an average correlation of 0.1 caps the cut near 68%. Already the shop's rule (equal risk). NY, second-hand: "all assets tends to go up and down together they are correlated" ([[MT053 - Episode 4- Noah Yechiely - Former Head of Equities at Bridgewater Associates#^MT053-C01-X1|MT053-C01-X1]]).
- Survive first. GJ: "how do we survive in the wide range of possible worlds rather than try to pick the best thing" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C09-2|MT068-C09-2]]). ASSERTED. Registered: the pod sits under the book's caps; no conviction sizing.
- Overrides. GJ on COVID: "we totally screwed it up because we were trying to systemize" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C15-2|MT068-C15-2]]); asked, on the claim text's reading, whether overruling the system helps: "Barely but yes." ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C15-3|MT068-C15-3]]). GJ's quote makes systemizing a new shock the error, and he puts survival on "risk control" ([[MT068 - Greg Jensen - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Podcast In Good Company Norges Bank Investment Managem#^MT068-C15-1|MT068-C15-1]]); BP takes the other side: "laying it out the way you think it works to the point that you've literally coded into the computer and now you're going to live with it" ([[MT049 - Episode 12- Bob Prince - Co-CIO of Bridgewater Associates#^MT049-C05-2|MT049-C05-2]]). Registered: no override of the rule layer (BP's side and the shop's rule); GJ's side is carried by the analyst layer, whose would-be overrides are logged and scored (question 10).
- Test on old history. RD: rules checked on "the past such as the 1930s" ([[MT058 - Five Forces Shaping the Global Economy - With Ray Dalio, Founder of Bridgewater Associates#^MT058-C12-2|MT058-C12-2]]).
