# Alan Dunne



Alan Dunne is a Dublin-based allocator and macro writer (Archive Capital, launching a Regime Adaptive Fund) who appears as a co-host and guest host of the Systematic Investor / Top Traders Unplugged series. He sees markets as regime-dependent systems in which the 1980-2020 disinflation regime has ended: deficits have decoupled from the cycle, bond-market discipline has weakened, inflation is stickier, stocks and bonds are more correlated, and the likely end-state is fiscal dominance and financial repression. He does not claim to predict this; he makes money (for himself and for the allocators he advises) by building portfolios robust to many scenarios: a risk-based mix of growth assets (about 40% of risk), diversifiers (bonds and gold, 20%) and a large trend and macro adaptive sleeve (40%), leveraged through futures and return stacking, with trend treated as a zero-correlation diversifier and 'midfielder' that is not timed on narratives, and with managers judged on statistics and process rather than stories. His own edge is as a synthesiser: he reads and criticises papers (AQR, Man, Quantica, CFM, BIS, GIC/JPMorgan), tracks Fed, Treasury and fiscal events, and tests ideas with arithmetic and history rather than his own backtests, flagging sponsor incentives and hidden assumptions.

## Evidence style
Mostly second-hand and narrative-analytical: he summarises and critiques other people's papers, surveys and books (AQR, GIC/J.P. Morgan, Man, CFM, Quantica, BIS, SF Fed, Goldman, Marks, Furman), cites data releases, index returns and betting-market odds, and uses historical analogy (1990s Greenspan, 1993 Carville, 1997/1998/2007, Nixon-Burns, 1940s repression, Plaza 1985) plus first-principles arithmetic (R>G, Sharpe times vol plus cash, portfolio Sharpe formula, balance-of-payments reasoning). He runs only a few own tests (quarterly decile test of a 30% trend overlay since 2000; a hypothetical backtest since 1990 of his 40/40/20 portfolio; correlation arithmetic) and often says he is relying on memory or has seen no study. He repeatedly flags qualitative vs quantitative parts of papers, assumption-dependent optimizer results, endpoint sensitivity, sample-size limits and sponsor incentives, and separates decision quality from outcomes ('resulting'). Allocator conversations and conference observations supply the behavioural claims.

## How views changed
Several views moved with new evidence, usually with a stated reason: (1) tariffs: from a one-off price-level bargaining chip the Fed can look through (Nov 2024) to a stagflationary shock and then a growth drag with delayed inflation (Mar-Dec 2025), citing FOMC projections, weak 2025 payrolls and the SF Fed cross-country study; (2) fiscal: from 'nothing priced until a public event focuses the market' and expected Fed easing dominating (Jul 2024) to 'all roads lead to higher yields' (May 2025) to 'regime theme moved from inflation to debt sustainability' (Aug 2026), prompted by deficit data, Treasury and yen interventions; (3) bonds: from stability and convexity in a sell-off (Nov 2024) to a weaker diversifier as stock-bond correlation turned positive (2025-26); (4) Germany: from structural gloom (2024) to radical positive fiscal reform (Mar 2025) after the debt-brake unwinding; (5) AI: from hype relative to proven benefit (Jul 2024) to a supply-disinflation plus capex-demand dilemma with Fed policy-error risk (Dec 2025-2026), prompted by capex announcements being punished and the Greenspan 1996-2000 analogy; (6) trend Sharpe assumption drifting from 0.4-0.5 to 0.1-0.3, never explicitly reconciled; (7) number of managers from 15-20 to fewer, explicitly 'my own change of mind' for stickiness. His core framing of trend (zero correlation, do not time it, judge by statistics) and the fiscal-direction view did not change.

## Blind spots (reader)
- No own out-of-sample backtests of any macro call (higher yields, fiscal dominance, regime shift, Fed policy error); his 40/40/20 portfolio is a hypothetical backtest since 1990 and the Regime Adaptive Fund has no live record in the pack.
- Never states what would falsify 'all roads lead to higher yields' or fiscal dominance beyond saying a recession pulls yields down temporarily; timing is explicitly left open (Dornbusch), so the view is hard to score.
- Commercial position (Archive Capital, a fund allocating to trend followers, the Regime Adaptive Fund, a Substack) is never discussed even though he warns repeatedly that research supports the sponsor's business model.
- Trend Sharpe assumptions (0.1-0.5) and the 40/40/20 weights are asserted without a derivation of why 40% trend and not 20% or 60%; no cost, capacity or tax analysis of his own portfolio.
- Little on credit, emerging markets (beyond one comment), China, single-stock selection, or implementation details (rebalancing rules, leverage limits, margin management in his own fund).
- Reliance on relayed third-party arguments (Asness, Rogoff, Posen, Eichengreen, Marks, Goldman) that are often adopted or qualified without independent data; the herding/efficiency claim is acknowledged as a hypothesis.
- Positive stock-bond correlation is treated as a decade-long regime shift on a short sample (one decade at +0.3) after earlier admitting it was persistently positive before the 1990s and negative only in an 'outlier' era.
- Behavioural explanations (committees, career risk, extrapolation) are asserted from allocator conversations with no data on how large they are.

## Reader inferences
- (reader) Dunne's operating pattern is scenario-robust allocation plus a regime-agnostic trend sleeve, with macro analysis used mainly to explain why the sleeve is needed (fiscal dominance, positive stock-bond correlation, supply shocks) rather than to time it; the macro narrative and the 'do not time trend' rule coexist.
- (reader) His recurring fragility triggers are the same few: 10-year Treasury near 5%, debt/GDP step-ups in recessions, an equity bear market in a financialised household sector, a politicised Fed after Powell (2026), and a yen/JGB or margin-driven deleveraging; these are candidates for monitoring signals.
- (reader) He consistently prefers low-correlation, convex, liquid diversifiers and distrusts labelled 'alternatives' (credit, private markets, market-neutral, many multi-strats): a testable screen is correlation in equity down-months, not strategy name.
- (reader) Several statements about trend are reported from papers and may not be his own estimates; his own estimates appear in the arithmetic (Sharpe 0.1-0.3, cash 3.5-4%, 12% vol) and in the three-way 40/40/20 risk split.
- (reader) His timeline shows a consistent sequence: policy-expectation shock (2024) to growth/tariff drag (2025) to supply-shock regime with AI and energy (2026): a testable pattern is that Fed-pricing complacency precedes his stagflation-type warnings.
- (reader) The 'death by a thousand cuts' admission (all components losing together by chance, March 2026) and 'stay invested is a weak message' imply he accepts periodic simultaneous losses as the price of avoiding a 50% equity drawdown; a risk-limit rule for how much of that discomfort is tolerable is never stated.
