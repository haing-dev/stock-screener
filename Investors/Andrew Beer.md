# Andrew Beer



Andrew Beer (co-founder of DBi / manager of the DBMF managed-futures replication ETF; earlier a commodity hedge-fund and multi-manager founder, 'not a quant') sees markets as having two layers: prices that move because real information changes, which informed participants and a few price-only detectors (trend followers) catch early, and noisy sentiment that whipsaws anyone who trades headlines. He believes trend followers are paid mainly by slow-by-design capital (60/40 holders, pensions, model portfolios) and by humans who hold losers, not by beating each other; the edge sits in medium- to long-term trend in a handful of deep liquid markets (Treasuries, equities, gold, oil, yen, euro), and complexity (short-term models, vol controls, stop-losses, hundreds of esoteric markets, non-trend add-ons) has not raised Sharpe while implicit trading costs (3-5x explicit, ~200bp all-in) eat the extra return. He makes money as a product builder, not a forecaster: a weekly, unchanged-since-July-2016 top-down regression of CTA index returns on ~10 liquid futures, held at ~10-20bp cost and no incentive fee, which he says tracks the industry at 0.8-0.9+ correlation and beats it by ~300-400bp a year through a 53-54% weekly hit rate that compounds. Beneath this is a macro-allocation story: 60/40 was a historical accident of a 20-year bond bull market, bonds stopped diversifying after 2022, so allocators should replace part of the 40 with a 3-5% completion allocation to managed futures, which is held back mainly by allocator career risk, lack of an 'information advantage' narrative and sales-driven product design (liquid alts, bank QIS indices that lose ~75-90% of backtest Sharpe, stacked wrappers). He distrusts macro prediction and backtests, favours slow de-risking and simplicity, relies on his own live record and recalled industry statistics, openly admits his key claims are unproven and that he is an interested party.

## Evidence style
Mixed and mostly self-referential. (1) Own live track record of a replication product since July 2016, quoted as rolling-window statistics (53-54% weekly, ~80% annual, ~90% three-year; 300-400bp a year) with openly acknowledged selection bias. (2) Third-party data quoted from memory (SG CTA index and sub-indices, Morningstar/Wilshire category returns, Pivotal Path holdings data, Bloomberg QIS trackers, Barclay AUM, SG trend-indicator factor analysis). (3) Stylised models and arithmetic (10-11-12 vs 10-11-9 price paths, +50%/-200% short payoff, fee-versus-quality examples, hypothetical market 193). (4) Anecdote and analogy from his Pinnacle commodity-firm and allocator career (Kuhn, Federer, poker, LeBron, Louis Vuitton vs Tesla, 9/11). (5) Industry-history narrative (2015-16 survey of bank models, late-2010s 'long winter', 2022). Frequently flags uncertainty ('I could be totally wrong', 'run it before me', 'no concrete evidence') and that he is an interested party selling a replication product (speaker notes in MT136, MT143, MT188 say so); the central causal claim (major-market signal, complexity detracts) is described by him as unproven. Does not present controlled out-of-sample tests on air.

## How views changed
Core framework did not change from Jun 2024 to Oct 2026: trend works because of information change and slow counterparties; cost and simplicity beat complexity; replication is the efficient route; 60/40 broke because bonds stopped diversifying. What did move: (1) Strength of the anti-complexity claim: Mar 2025 concedes short-term models win at inflections; May 2025 'no concrete evidence'; Jul 2025 'complexity crisis'; Dec 2025 admits unproven; Aug 2026 'always concludes short-term adds no Sharpe' - reason given is the accumulating 2024-26 relative record of his own product, not new tests. (2) Macro tone: optimistic on the new administration and animal spirits (Dec 2024 - Jan 2025), then policy noise and head fakes (Mar-May 2025), then a geopolitical/Iran shock (Apr 2026), then AI-as-venture-capital valuation worries and a possible bond tantrum (Aug-Oct 2026); events given as the reason, no revised framework. (3) Allocation size from 'at least 5%' (Aug 2024) to 3% to 3-5% to 10% tactical complement (Oct 2026); not explained. (4) Stacking: opposed (Oct 2024, Mar 2025) to accepting capital efficiency at higher rates (Jan 2026) - explained only by higher interest rates. (5) Crisis alpha: from warning that it invites timing (Jun 2024) to criticising the label (Aug 2026) to using it as the lead argument (Oct 2026) - audience-driven, not explained. (6) Replication outperformance quoted as 300bp -> 350bp -> 400bp as the window changed.

## Blind spots (reader)
- Never gives a tested mechanism for why stock-bond correlation turned positive or for when it will revert; the 60/40 break is described, not forecast, and his bond-volatility figure is flagged by him as unverified.
- Evidence for the central claim that simplicity beats complexity is mostly his own product's relative record since July 2016 with an acknowledged selection bias and short post-2022 sample; no out-of-sample or pre-registered test, and no treatment of whether a replicator's capacity or the industry copying it changes the edge.
- Replication depends on managers' reported returns (lagged, composite conventions, European closes) but the dependence risks (index reconstitution, reporting gaps, tracking error in stress) are mentioned only in passing.
- Statistics are quoted from memory (Morningstar, Wilshire, Pivotal Path, Barclay AUM, QIS decay, pod payouts); several are marked low confidence by the ledger or the speaker.
- The 'informed participants' explanation of trend (and the 'against slow institutions' counterparty claim) is asserted by reasoning and anecdote; no counterparty or flow data shown.
- Carry, value, and other non-trend premia in the CTA space are dismissed as satellites or add-ons without his own test; no discussion of cross-sectional or relative-value trend.
- Little on position sizing or volatility targeting inside his own portfolio beyond 'about 10 markets, weekly'; no stated leverage, risk target or drawdown limit.
- No analysis of taxes outside the US, FX hedging of wrappers, or counterparty/swap risk of the swap-based route.
- Macro views (administration effects, Iran shock, AI as VC, bond tantrum) are narrative with no stated indicators or horizon.

## Reader inferences
- (reader) His business logic is a structural arbitrage, not forecasting: alpha is the fee/cost gap between a cheap 10-contract weekly replica and expensive complex CTAs plus the slow-capital transfer that all trend followers earn; evaluating his claims therefore reduces to testing cost-adjusted performance by market count and speed.
- (reader) The recurring trend design he endorses is: medium/long lookbacks, ~10 deep liquid markets, low turnover, no vol-control or stop overlay, weekly rebalancing, 3-5% portfolio weight paid from bonds. This is a candidate strategy family to compare against the faster and wider books he criticises.
- (reader) He treats Sharpe of single strategies as low (0.3-0.4; MT215) and reaches higher Sharpe by blending, which aligns with a stacking/portfolio-construction view even though he criticised stacked wrappers; the difference may be wrapper cost, not concept.
- (reader) Crowding-reversal (Trump trade 2025, March 2026 smart-money) is the only drawdown mechanism he links to positioning data; it could be tested against 13F/hedge-fund crowding measures as a filter for exposure to the dominant theme.
- (reader) His allocator-behaviour theory (career risk, benchmark regret, narrative) is the explanation for slow adoption and is testable through flow data; it is separate from the market-return theory and should be scored separately by a judging model.
- (reader) Statements by the host and counterparts in the same episodes often supply the missing counter-evidence (ISAM, hundreds-of-markets managers, indices omitting strong smaller managers); a judging model should weight Beer's performance claims as advocacy from an interested party.
