# Cem Karsan (captions: Jim Kasang, Jim Carson, Jim, Cham, Jem; several attributions inferred)

Cem Karsan is an options and volatility strategist (captions: Jim Kasang/Carson, Cham, Jem; some attributions inferred) who believes prices over days to about two years are set by who is positioned and what flows must happen (dealer gamma/vanna/charm, option expiries, structured-product vol selling, year-end rebalancing, hedge-fund degrossing), not by news or fundamentals; valuation is only a distance-from-the-ground risk gauge. He tries to forecast the distribution and the path (compression then expansion, pinned then unpinned) rather than direction, ties forecasts to a calendar of events and expiries, and expresses them mostly with options: long-dated right-tail calls instead of stock, long-dated gold calls funded by oil puts, long FX/bond vol, calendar spreads, curve steepeners, and hedges in narrow windows. Underneath sits a structural macro thesis, repeated in all 16 appearances: an inequality-driven populist, fiscal, protectionist regime like 1964-82 (the analogy), in which rates and inflation rise, equity multiples and margins compress (flat nominal, down in real terms), 60/40 stops diversifying, gold/FX/bond volatility is the best vol, the Fed is political and in a box, debt ends in monetisation, and election years are pumped then retrace. Over 2024-2026 his calls swing between short-term bullish flow windows (year-end into about 13-15 January, summer compression) and bearish structural calls (stair-step decline in early 2025, equities down 30-40% in 12-18 months from March 2025, a 10-15% grind to September opex 2025), then a bullish 1998-99-style liquidity bubble (Oct 2025), then by July 2026 a state-directed-capitalism thesis (Treasury backstop plus a money-printed sovereign fund buying trillions of equities after the November 2026 midterms) in which one should not bet against the market or the administration short to medium term. He supports views with first-principles market structure, historical analogy (1998-2000, 2007, 2017-18, 1968-82, Nixon), on-air arithmetic and his own earlier calls; no backtests are shown and several statistics are quoted from memory.

## Evidence style
Mechanism-first and first-principles: reasons from option-market and fund-flow structure (gamma, vanna, charm, skew, vol supply, expiry cycles), incentives of officials, and reflexivity (Soros). Heavy use of historical analogy (1998-2000, 2007, 2017-18, 1968-82, Nixon-Burns, Japan, Norway, 250-year/fourth-turning cycles), on-air orders-of-magnitude arithmetic (collateral and flow sizes that vary episode to episode), and reference to his own earlier public calls (gold call at 2500 / 3000 calls, 10-year to 5% about ten days late, Nvidia top, Feb-April 2025 dip buy, the August 2024 unpin, Feb-March opex pattern). Statistics are recited from memory (election-year averages of 21.5%, 1968-82 real return, Sharpe 0.35, 'data screams'); samples are tiny (4-6 elections) and no backtests, hit rates, sample sizes or tests are shown. Several claims come second-hand (coin-flip Taiwan odds from government contacts; asset-flow figures with no source). Theory comes first and data second (MT031-C16-4). In 2026 he explicitly mixes speculation ('where there is smoke') with inference and offers no quantification of the state buying plan.

## How views changed
Unchanged core: positioning and flow structure drives the path; a populist, inflationary, rising-rate regime like 1964/68-82; equities not an inflation hedge; gold/FX/bond vol and steepeners; non-correlated assets and early-inning flows; election-year pump then retrace; Fed boxed in and political; debt resolved by monetisation. Changed: the market call and its justification. Apr-Aug 2024: pullbacks bought, Fed/Treasury put floors markets, 2024 positive, broad rally to year end with blow-off; 'pin' breaks mid-August earlier than expected, bigger fireworks early 2025. Late Sep-Dec 2024: bullish flows to 13-15 January 2025, S&P 6,000-6,200, hedge mid-January; Trump win seen as inflationary (steepener, gold, vol). Jan 2025: stair-step decline in Q1 and yields higher (he notes the late-December decline and the 10-year hitting 5% about ten days after his call). Mar 2025: Fed forced to QE and equities down 30-40% in 12-18 months. May 2025: ceiling near 5700-6000, slow stagflation, bonds fail. Jul 2025: base case 10-15% grind to September opex or a 1998-style blow-off alternative. Oct 2025: bullish into mid-January, 1998-99 liquidity bubble. Dec 2025: fragile, late bubble, 2026 midterm window risk. Jul 2026: state-managed market, do not bet against it. Reasons given: (a) Oct 2025 - structured-product vol compression, institutions underweight (0th percentile in June, 25th later), the Fed takeover expected, market structure reflexively supports prices (MT104-C04-X1); (b) 2026 - incentives of an administration needing the market and the debt exit; (c) he says paths are conditional and revised when news changes (MT150-C16-X2) and that timing is imprecise (MT132-C09-4). Why the March 2025 30-40% fall did not become the Oct 2025 bullish view is not explicitly explained in the pack ('not explained').

## Blind spots (reader)
- (reader) No track record or hit-rate statistics are shown for the many dated calls; no sample sizes or significance tests for seasonal, election-year or opex claims; data are recited from memory.
- (reader) No explicit position sizes, stop-losses or risk budgets; sizing appears only as '30-50% non-correlated' and '10x as much as you can' for the hedge; the cost of option carry and its drag are not quantified.
- (reader) The framework is hard to falsify: paths are conditional, windows wide (e.g. 'no later than shortly after June OpEx'), and misses are explained by early/late timing or by the publicised pattern being front-run.
- (reader) The 1964-82 analogy is a single regime, justified by an inequality/generational mechanism but never tested out of sample; he does not address how fiscal dominance and the 2020s differ in structure (e.g. central bank independence tools, passive flows) beyond brief concession.
- (reader) Motive attribution about the administration, the Fed and China (controlled demolition, protection racket, shadow Fed chair) is speculative and admitted as such in places; the state-equity-fund thesis has no quantification of size, timing or inflation cost.
