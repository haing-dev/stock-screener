# David Harding



Founder of AHL and Winton who treats finance as a social science with no immutable laws: markets are made of people who over-react, under-react and panic, so they are not efficient, prices of whole asset classes trend, and a diversified, rule-based, empirically tested approach earns money. He judges any model like a natural-science model, by falsifiable predictions tested against the market (1994), and distrusts Sharpe ratios because returns are non-stationary, non-parametric and gameable by smoothed or short-option payoffs (QP03). In 2013 he describes simple breakout/multi-horizon trend rules across about 100 futures as high-capacity, to be run without over-leverage and with discipline; edge comes from many weak signals across many markets, continually renewed as signals decay (2016). His core worry throughout is that success attracts capital and erodes edge, which by 2019 he says has made futures trend following crowded 'beta' after a Sharpe near 1 for about 30 years: Winton lowers its forecast, cuts leverage (about 2x gross vs rumoured 10x elsewhere), and shifts a third to a half of weight to a 7,000-stock equity book (event, organizational-metric, customised technical) built on cloud computing and long research cycles. He also holds that government macro data are too heavily revised to trade, that trend following is not a dependable crash hedge, and that volatility is a commercial leverage choice. Evidence is first principles, recalled track record and episodes (1987, LTCM, 2008, Feb 2018), mostly without statistics.

## Mechanisms
- **Trend persistence to trend profits (2013)**: markets are social systems, trends change slowly -> asset-class prices trend over months (2008 bear market, 2000s commodities) → simple breakout / multi-horizon rules buy new highs and sell new lows, matching chart experts' signals -> make money → many markets -> something always moving -> smoother, larger, higher-capacity returns; no over-leverage and discipline -> eventual success and inflows. Fails when: Reversals (2009 losses), over-leverage, lack of discipline, too much money in the strategy ([[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C36-2|QP11-C36-2]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C35-2|QP11-C35-2]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C14-1|QP11-C14-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C15-1|QP11-C15-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C28-1|QP11-C28-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C27-2|QP11-C27-2]])
- **Edge decay through crowding**: Sharpe near 1 for about 30 years -> capital and competitors pile in; easy to replicate and seen as beta → fast futures trend systems crowded first, then slower ones (2/20-day crossover paid in the 1980s, not now) -> lower returns and return-to-risk → forecast lowered year by year -> diversify into equity and other strategies without abandoning trend. Fails when: Not stated; trend following can still work but cannot be relied on ([[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C06-1|MT070-C06-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C02-1|MT070-C02-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C02-3|MT070-C02-3]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C05-3|MT070-C05-3]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C44-2|QP11-C44-2]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-9|QP04-C03-9]])
- **Many weak signals to reliable edge**: behavioural biases (over-reaction, slumbering inaction) -> patterns in prices not fully reflecting information → competition -> only weak relationships remain profitable -> combine many weak signals across a broad universe -> aggregate edge; signal decay -> large ongoing research. Fails when: Signals decay; no out-of-sample discipline is described ([[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-8|QP04-C03-8]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-4|QP04-C03-4]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-3|QP04-C03-3]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-9|QP04-C03-9]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-10|QP04-C03-10]])
- **Sharpe maximisation leads to hidden tail risk**: investors rank managers by Sharpe -> managers maximise it -> negative-skew, smoothed or option-like streams (illiquid marking, take-profit/run-loss) → non-stationary returns -> misleading Sharpe, hidden tail risk; symmetric standard deviation penalises upside-volatile strategies. Fails when: Returns close to i.i.d. normal; Sortino has the same stationarity requirement ([[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C01-2|QP03-C01-2]], [[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C02-7|QP03-C02-7]], [[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C01-9|QP03-C01-9]], [[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C02-1|QP03-C02-1]], [[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C01-4|QP03-C01-4]], [[QP03 - A Critique of the Sharpe Ratio (David Harding, Winton)#^QP03-C02-3|QP03-C02-3]])
- **Why efficient market theory has low utility and models need market tests**: model makes falsifiable predictions -> test against the market's common view; trading profit is the incorruptible test → EMH predicts only a null and parametric returns -> low scientific utility; bank profits from EMH models come from relative-value arbitrage, and portfolio insurance failed in 1987. Fails when: Profit test ignores luck and multiple testing (no standard given) ([[QP14 - Making Money from Mathematical Models (David Harding, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. A 1994)#^QP14-C05-2|QP14-C05-2]], [[QP14 - Making Money from Mathematical Models (David Harding, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. - **Crowding, fat tails and leverage**: crowded strategies with many holders -> risk of overnight systemic unwinding (LTCM, 2008) → catastrophe risk -> leverage cut (about 2x gross in long/short portfolios vs about 10x rumoured elsewhere); lower leverage plus near-zero cash -> lower headline returns that look similar to the past once adjusted. Fails when: Not stated ([[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C02-5|MT070-C02-5]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C03-1|MT070-C03-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C02-4|MT070-C02-4]])
- **Trend is not a crash hedge**: trend systems are long indices after rallies -> sudden fall hits CTAs long (Feb 2018) → slow declines (2001-02, 2008) -> trends establish before the bulk of the loss -> gains; vol spikes after calm cannot be anticipated. Fails when: A 1987 or Feb 2018-style fast crash; 'one swallow does not make a summer' ([[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C09-2|MT070-C09-2]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C08-4|MT070-C08-4]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C09-1|MT070-C09-1]])

Fails when: Interviewer doubts a trend firm can carry across to equities; no performance data given ([[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C01-3|MT070-C01-3]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C11-1|MT070-C11-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C04-4|MT070-C04-4]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C05-1|MT070-C05-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C03-3|MT070-C03-3]])

- [signal] Futures: Donchian-style N-day channel breakout and multi-horizon trend across about 100 markets (N not given in the pack; 'frame days' in the transcript), with a checklist of pattern, seasonal, fundamental and volume. Equities: event, organizational/governance-metric and customised technical strategies; weather, inventory and seasonality in commodities.. Why: Simple rules reproduce chart experts' signals; stock markets need equity-specific signals ([[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C14-1|QP11-C14-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C15-1|QP11-C15-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C12-2|QP11-C12-2]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-6|QP04-C03-6]], [[QP04 - The Numbers Man David Harding profile (Financial Times, Clive Cookson)#^QP04-C03-7|QP04-C03-7]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C04-4|MT070-C04-4]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C05-1|MT070-C05-1]])
- [universe] About 100 futures, extended to about 1,000 single stocks (2013) and about 7,000 stocks (2019); very long-term strategies only in capacity-limited markets such as agricultural futures.. Why: More uncorrelated markets improve results and capacity; cloud compute makes big universes tractable ([[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C38-3|QP11-C38-3]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C39-1b|QP11-C39-1b]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C39-1|QP11-C39-1]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C01-3|MT070-C01-3]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C03-3|MT070-C03-3]])
- [research] Empirical, assumption-free pattern search and conditional-distribution research on trend, seasonality, spreads and cross-asset relations; judge models on data unseen at creation and by trading against the market; stop a project when evidence cannot be established (sunk cost not a reason); cumulative secret team research, cloud compute, in-house data cleaning (Hivemind).. A 1994)#^QP14-C05-2|QP14-C05-2]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C04-3|MT070-C04-3]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C08-2|MT070-C08-2]], [[MT070 - Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns#^MT070-C10-2|MT070-C10-2]])
- [sizing] Volatility set by one leverage 'volume knob' (10, 20 or 30 vol) as a commercial choice, independent of strategy; forecast risk, costs and returns and optimise (risk and costs easy, returns hard).. ## Evidence style
Philosophy of science (1994) and conceptual critique without data (QP03, QP14); recalled personal track record and episodes in an oral history (2013) and a magazine profile (2016, a single unaudited 12.8% CAGR net); in 2019 a long record (nearly 40 years, Sharpe near 1 for 30 years), historical episodes (LTCM, 2008, 1987, Feb 2018), data-revision anecdotes and analogies (jungle vs atoms, Renaissance). He cites few quantitative tests, withheld the AHL research that showed EMH wrong, and says his PMI study was inconclusive rather than negative.

## How views changed
Unchanged core: markets are inefficient because of human behaviour, ideas need empirical tests, and edge decays as money arrives. What changed (1994 to 2019): (1) trend following goes from a durable, high-capacity engine with 'success eventually' for the disciplined (2013) to crowded accessible beta with a lowered forecast (2019), reason given: success attracts capital (foreseen in QP11-C44-2); (2) from avoiding over-leverage in general (2013) to a stated 2x gross limit and a leverage-as-commercial-knob view (2019); (3) breadth grows from about 100 futures to about 1,000 then 7,000 stocks, enabled by cloud computing; (4) from 2008 as a crisis-alpha episode (2013) to 'not a dependable crash hedge' (2019); (5) from forecasting returns, covariances and costs, to concluding government macro data are unusable after an inconclusive PMI study; (6) fees from 1/20 to 0.9%/16%, because returns did not justify them. The QP03 Sharpe critique is undated in the pack, so its place in the sequence cannot be stated.

## Blind spots (reader)

- No performance data for the equity book or the new strategies in 2019; the single 12.8% CAGR figure in 2016 is unaudited
- Crowding mechanism (more money, less return) asserted but not measured, though central to his story
- No out-of-sample or multiple-testing standard for profit as the test of a model
- QP14 and QP03 are conceptual and QP04 is a journalistic profile; most statements about 1994-2016 views cannot be dated to a recording or tied to later statements
