# Howard Marks



Credit and distressed-debt investor who says returns depend on the price paid relative to value, not on asset quality: no asset is so good it cannot be overpriced, and few are so bad they cannot be cheap enough (the Nifty Fifty fell about 95% over five years from 1969). Markets are made of people, so randomness is large and outcomes are poor evidence of skill; he therefore controls risk first, judges decisions by process, and targets neglected, less efficient niches (convertibles, high yield from 1978, distressed debt from 1988) where he can quantify default risk against the spread, diversify and demand adequate pay. He does not forecast macro or time markets; cycles are driven by excessive optimism and pessimism, reversion of extremes is more dependable than continuation, but only at rare extremes and never with timing. Because consensus is already in the price, money is made only where consensus is wrong; his action is to ask what consensus thinks, what is wrong with it and what would expose the error, then buy what everyone hates when it is cheap (after Lehman, about $650m a week for 15 weeks). The edge is interpretation and judging where we stand in the cycle, not data everyone shares. In mid-2024 he saw US stocks about 20-25% overvalued (PE 21 vs post-war 16) but with only slightly better than 50/50 odds of a decline within a year, and he declined to call AI or China for lack of knowledge. Evidence is experience, memos, analogies and quotes, with almost no data.

## Core beliefs
- Return depends on price paid relative to value, not asset quality; buy things cheap enough regardless of quality and avoid very high valuations. Investors who ignore price (Nifty Fifty 'no price too high') lose. _(stated 2×; stable)_ ([[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-2|MT051-C03-2]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-3|MT051-C03-3]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-R1|MT051-C03-R1]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C05-R1|MT051-C05-R1]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-4|MT078-C07-4]])
- Risk is not good or bad but how risky and what return compensates: take risk that is aware, analyzable, diversifiable and highly paid (high yield priced like life insurance; quantify default against spread). Excessive certainty is the enemy. _(stated 2×; stable)_ ([[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-4|MT051-C03-4]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-5|MT051-C03-5]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C04-6|MT078-C04-6]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C09-2|MT078-C09-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C09-3|MT078-C09-3]])
- Neglected or unpopular niches produce bargains and reward skill; efficient markets (large-cap equities) do not, since one cannot know more than others there. _(stated 2×; stable)_ ([[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C02-1|MT051-C02-1]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-1|MT051-C03-1]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C02-2|MT051-C02-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C01-2|MT078-C01-2]])
- Consensus is already in the price, so money is made only where it is wrong and the payoff to the unlikely outcome is high; a superior investor sees the distribution of outcomes better and plays only when upside outweighs downside. Contrarianism is not mechanical. _(stated 2×; stable)_ ([[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-1|MT078-C07-1]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-2|MT078-C07-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-3|MT078-C07-3]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C05-1|MT051-C05-1]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C05-R2|MT051-C05-R2]])
- Do not forecast macro or time markets: macro forecasters are not right consistently enough to follow and show no record; forecasts are useful only at rare extremes (five contrarian calls in 50 years) and then name a price or a date, not both. The most profitable asset class a decade ahead cannot be forecast. _(stated 2×; stable, but see tension: he does state views on Fed cuts and valuation)_ ([[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C01-3|MT078-C01-3]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C01-4|MT078-C01-4]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-3|MT078-C03-3]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-4|MT078-C03-4]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C09-X1|MT051-C09-X1]])

## Mechanisms
- **Why high quality can lose and low quality can win**: consensus 'great companies, no price too high' -> prices far above value -> about 95% loss over five years from 1969 → unloved low-quality credit priced low enough -> spread exceeds expected default loss -> compensated risk. Fails when: Expected losses not quantified or spreads do not cover them ([[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-1|MT051-C03-1]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-2|MT051-C03-2]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-3|MT051-C03-3]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C03-4|MT051-C03-4]])
- **Cycles and reversion of extremes**: good growth -> firms all build capacity -> utilisation falls -> retrenchment (greed to fear) → people assume a process continues -> bubbles and crashes -> regression toward the mean, dependable in direction → overpriced markets can become more overpriced first, so timing is not dependable. Fails when: Timing, cause and speed unknown; prices stay extreme for long ([[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-1|MT078-C03-1]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-2|MT078-C03-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-3|MT078-C03-3]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C03-4|MT078-C03-4]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C04-3|MT078-C04-3]])
- **From consensus to contrarian bet**: consensus belief embodied in price -> little profit from agreeing → consensus too strong / optimism too high -> payoff to the improbable outcome compelling (underdog odds) → test: what do they think, what is wrong, what would expose the error -> position different from others even if wrong for a while -> buy what everyone hates because it is cheap. Fails when: Froth is hard to measure; contrarianism is easier in frothy than fearful markets; one can look wrong a long time ([[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-1|MT078-C07-1]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-2|MT078-C07-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-3|MT078-C07-3]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C07-4|MT078-C07-4]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C08-2|MT078-C08-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C08-4|MT078-C08-4]])
- **Distressed debt payoff**: bond bought at par defaults -> falls to about 10 -> buy after the collapse, join the restructuring -> recovery to about 30. Fails when: Not stated; credit crises are when the best opportunities come ([[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C02-2|MT051-C02-2]], [[MT051 - Episode 17- Howard Marks - Co-Chairman of Oaktree Capital Management#^MT051-C04-X1|MT051-C04-X1]])
- **Mid-2024 read on rates and equities**: consensus priced six Fed cuts against a dot plot of three (Dec 2023) -> Goldilocks thinking corrected to two or three → optimism falls at the margin -> market stalls but does not fall (optimists still govern) → PE 21 vs 16 -> about 20-25% overvaluation but only slightly better than 50/50 odds of decline. Fails when: A possible AI bubble that he says he cannot judge ([[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C05-2|MT078-C05-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C05-3|MT078-C05-3]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C06-2|MT078-C06-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C04-2|MT078-C04-2]], [[MT078 - Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment#^MT078-C04-3|MT078-C04-3]])

- [signal] Judge price against value; buy cheap enough regardless of quality; use valuation (S&P PE 21 vs 16) as context, not a timing trigger.. ## Evidence style
Experience, memos and aphorism: decades of observation, historical episodes (Nifty Fifty, 2000 internet bubble, GFC, post-Lehman buying), analogies (life insurers, balls in a bag, betting on the Lakers, dentist) and quotes (Dimson, Twain, Boorstin, Taleb, Feynman). Few statistics (PE 21 vs 16), and he says his contrarian calls were not data driven; no track-record tables.

## How views changed
Not explained and not visible: the two interviews (2024-06 and 2025-05) state the same method, and MT051 gives no market view for 2025. The career account shows lessons from the 1969 Nifty Fifty loss (price over quality), the high-yield and distressed niches, and learning from failure, but no later change of mind is stated.

## Blind spots (reader)
- No numbers on realised performance, hit rate, or the size of positions relative to the portfolio
- How 'cheap enough' or value is estimated for non-credit assets is not stated; the unit of 'value' is never defined
- Does not say what action a 20-25% overvaluation implies for portfolio exposure
- No explicit rule for when contrarian buying stops or for recognising being wrong early
- 2025 market view not given in MT051

## Reader inferences
- (reader) His testable core is a price-versus-value gap in neglected credit niches, with a spread-minus-expected-loss calculation that could be backtested; the macro and cycle talk is context and explicitly not a timing signal.
- (reader) The 'moderate temperature' and 'optimists still govern' judgements are second-derivative sentiment readings; they could be coded as changes in survey optimism.
