# Katie Kaminski



Katie (Katy) Kaminski, a trend-following researcher (AlphaSimplex) and Systematic Investor co-host, sees markets as a sequence of macro themes (inflation, policy divergence, geopolitics, AI growth, deglobalisation) that trend followers capture by following price rather than forecasting, so she makes money by staying unbiased, liquid and adaptable, diversified across speeds, parameters, markets and managers. Her central research claims are that higher inflation turns the stock-bond correlation positive and breaks the bond leg of 60/40, so long/short trend in bonds, dollar, gold and commodities is the repair; that geopolitical (supply-side) risk leads inflation and pays trend through commodities and bonds; that trend is a second responder that loses early in short, reversing shocks and drawdowns under about 15% and pays in deeper, longer dislocations; and that return dispersion among trend managers is a fingerprint of design choices (speed, market set, vol sizing, carry share) that rotate year to year, so no one choice can be picked in advance. She advises holding trend as a reactive core (about 15-20% in a 60/40), rebalancing into drawdowns rather than timing, giving each holding a role, judging strategies out of sample with robust parameters, and treating trend plus equities as a jointly risk-managed pair because alpha and beta are not fully separable. Her evidence is her own and peers' papers, factor regressions and long histories, with open admission of short samples; her recurring caution is that bonds, fixed-income trend and fast-reversing shocks are where trend is weakest.

## Evidence style
Academic and empirical. Cites her own papers and studies (crisis alpha, Bonds Behaving Badly, Trend Following in a Defensive Rotation, CTA style factors and CTA style evolution, drawdown paper, turbulence metrics, Trade War: Crisis or Correction, replication papers, geopolitical-risk and managed-futures paper) plus peers (Quantica, AQR, Man AHL, Harvey et al., Caldara-Iacoviello GPR). Prefers a data study over a qualitative statement, writes a paper after emotional events to counter emotion, uses charts of drawdown depth/duration, quintile plots, factor regressions, hypothetical-manager simulations and long histories (to 1970s, '800 years' of bond data). Argues from first principles (portfolio theory, estimation error, 1/N) and practitioner experience with clients. Open about sample limits (post-1990, monthly frequency, one-off events, about 10 years of alternative-market data) and about what she has not tested (China CTAs, correlation-conditional trend, replication tracking-error numbers). Rarely gives numbers on her own trading or P&L.

## How views changed
The framework (trend follows themes, dispersion is design-driven, inflation breaks bonds, second-responder payoff) is nearly constant across Aug 2024 - Oct 2026; what changes is the year's evidence and emphasis. (1) Speed: Aug 2024 she leans toward shorter signals in a divergent world; 2024 results favour slow/big-market; 2025 punishes the mid-range and favours very slow or very fast; she concludes no speed always wins - reason given: path of the year. (2) Vocabulary: 'crisis alpha' gradually yields to 'second responder' and role-based language (2025-26) because insurance narratives did not help investors hold trend (host's point, which she adopts). (3) Geopolitics moves from a side remark (Jan 2026 preview) to a paper (Jun 2026) to the central thesis for bonds and 60/40 (Oct 2026), citing the GPR-leads-inflation literature. (4) Price vs economic trend: macro ahead in headline-driven 2025, price ahead in early 2026 as fundamentals clarified; she starts macro-vs-technical research in Sep 2025. (5) Bonds: Oct 2024 she notes the classic stock-bond pattern returned at 2-2.5% inflation, but from Apr 2025 'bonds still behaving badly' and by Oct 2026 an inflation premium is being repriced; reasons are the tariff/inflation threat and supply-side geopolitics. (6) Carry: 2024 carry is the threat to crisis alpha; March 2026 FX and energy carry worked (no reconciliation offered). (7) Crowding: 2024 crowded positions rewarded; 2026 not a concern; 2025 she flags capacity-limited markets as untested.

## Blind spots (reader)
- No explicit stand-alone trading P&L, live track record or risk limits of her own book are given; claims about what 'trend' does rest on indices (SG Trend), papers and client work.
- Never gives a threshold for 'high' inflation, for when bonds are cheap enough, or for what crisis depth makes a drawdown 'deep' beyond about 15%.
- Rarely addresses transaction costs and capacity quantitatively (except that alternative markets cost more); no numbers on slippage of her own systems.
- Valuation, earnings or credit-cycle views are absent; markets are viewed through trend, macro themes and inflation/policy rather than fundamentals; equity-valuation risk (AI) only appears as a theme direction.
- Assumes investors can be educated into holding trend; little evidence beyond anecdotes and Quantica 'Paid for Presence'.
- Treats the SG Trend Index and style factors as a stable object even though she says indices are not investable and managers drift.
- Short samples are often admitted (monthly GPR since 2000, post-1990 Quantica data, one three-day shock) yet implications for the next three to five years are drawn ('history rhymes').
- Does not specify how to set the share of risk to equity-long positions in trend when it is stacked with equities beyond 'size dynamically or cap beta'.
- Foresight statements (2026 boom-bust, a fall pivot) are given without a stated test.

## Reader inferences
- (reader) Her operative rule for the 60/40 problem is regime-based: replace some static bond/gold ballast with long/short trend when inflation is above about 2.5%, and add on drawdowns; testable with a CPI regime gate.
- (reader) A consistent signal ranking is: Sharpe -> 12-month; convexity/crisis -> add 1-3 month; avoid concentrating in 4-9 month, which she names worst in 2025 and the most common CTA setting.
- (reader) She treats the equity-drawdown depth of about 15% as a state variable for whether trend is a first or second responder; this is a natural gating variable for a macro-book overlay.
- (reader) Geopolitical-risk index spikes are an observable lead for inflation and commodity/bond-short trends over 2-3 years, giving a macro input independent of price signals.
- (reader) Most of her predictions are conditional descriptions of where trend does well, not directional market forecasts, so she is a source of regime-conditional payoff tables rather than views.
- (reader) Her emphasis on role, rebalancing and not timing means any strategy built from her ideas should be evaluated as part of a total portfolio and with dollar-weighted (investor) return in mind.
- (reader) Her economic-trend vs price-trend point maps to a macro-book design question: macro signals win in headline-noisy but fundamentally clear years; price signals win in stress.
