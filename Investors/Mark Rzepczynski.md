# Mark Rzepczynski



Mark Rzepczynski (several appearances are captioned only 'Mark'; see reader_inferences) is a systematic trend follower who calls himself a 'trend follower plus fundamentalist'. His starting point is that prices come first ('prices are primal': they show the current supply-demand balance, though not value) because the link from news or fundamentals to price reaction is loose and markets are a 'wicked' learning environment, so sizing and diversification matter more than forecasting. His organising idea is the split between risk (countable: volatility, VIX) and uncertainty (not countable: policy, regime and ambiguity), with volatility understating true risk when newspaper-based uncertainty indices are high. Uncertainty and ambiguity make investors scale in and out slowly (dollar-cost-average), which is what creates trends; trend following therefore makes money from regime changes, supply shocks, dislocations and relative safe-asset shifts, and is a divergent, antifragile strategy compared with convergent multistrat or mean-reversion books. He reads macro through plumbing and institutions (Treasury dealer capacity and levered basis trades, Fed operating target and balance sheet, inventories and buffer stocks, who the marginal buyer is, bills versus coupons, fiscal pressure and term premium), treats 'safe' as relative (Treasuries less so, gold and trend as substitutes), and worries that calm markets and rising prices build leverage. He looks for money in trends, long-trend bias and wide market breadth, adding fundamental and regime context only as confirmation, with intermediate speed as his Bayesian prior; he is sceptical of machine learning and complexity, insists on realistic backtests including footprint and costs, and argues allocators should hold managed futures rather than a bundle of alternatives when they expect high uncertainty, because (in his own hedge fund regressions) managed futures return is insensitive to high-VIX regimes while most other strategies lose alpha. His 2024-26 calls: post-election bond sell-off supported by data (Nov 2024), tariff and policy uncertainty (Jan-May 2025), gold as structural safe asset (2025), possible metals and softs bubble peak and US-asset rotation (Feb 2026), new Fed chair and Middle East supply shock favouring trends (Apr-Jun 2026), Treasury-plumbing fragility (Aug 2026) and long yields of 5-5.5% as a return to normal (Oct 2026).

## Core beliefs
- Prices come first. A price shows the current supply-demand balance and is the anchor of a systematic model, but price is not value. ## Evidence style
Mostly conceptual, literature-based and analogical rather than data-led. He relays and interprets recent papers and books (Hudson Bay Capital on Treasury issuance, GIC/JPMorgan framework, Elm Capitalist experiment, Hogarth 'wicked' environments, Quantica, State Street narrative research, Lo's adaptive markets, Cochrane, Campbell Harvey on gold ETFs, Mehrling, Kindleberger, Fed studies on Treasury plumbing, Niederhoffer), recites history (Bretton Woods, Carter-era rate spike, Plaza, 1987, ERM, 1914, 1970s, WWII Treasury-Fed Accord, UK 1925 to 1975), uses personal anecdote (the Merc in the 1980s, February 1994 mortgage losses, John Henry, a Manhattan allocator story) and rules of thumb with back-of-envelope arithmetic (bond fair value). His own empirical work is described but not shown: regressions of hedge fund strategies on VIX terciles and VIX changes (MT110, MT182), cocoa COT analysis and central-bank gold buying data (MT211), replication research (MT182), uncertainty-index work (MT007, MT119) and network research with Vienna collaborators (MT197); no samples, coefficients or significance levels are given on air. He flags his own limits openly ('I haven't solved it', 'one anecdote cannot be extrapolated', 'the claim is an opinion that needs a test', too few high-uncertainty regimes to test) and several times labels himself as 'talking my long-term book'.

## How views changed
The core frame (price first, risk versus uncertainty, trends from slow adjustment, relative safety, diversification) is the same from Aug 2024 to Oct 2026. What moved: (1) Trend in uncertainty: Jan 2025 (MT007) he expected trend to do better in the Biden-to-Trump regime; trend lost money in 2025, and by May 2025 (MT112) he explained this as man-made, quickly reversed uncertainty with no slow behavioural response, then tied trend gains to long crises, supply shocks and negative resolution (MT182). (2) Fundamentals: from 'price is the valuation' toward 'trend follower plus fundamentalist', with weights shifted to price when data quality is poor (MT110) and cross-market network signals described as a possible edge (MT095). (3) Gold and metals: Dec 2025 (MT211) gold is regime change rather than bubble; Feb 2026 (MT008) metals and softs may be past a bubble peak; Oct 2026 (MT219) gold fell from above $5,000 to about $4,100, challenging the safe-haven label. (4) Bond yields: Nov 2024 rising term premium; Dec 2025 fair 10-year high 4s; Oct 2026 5-5.5% fair and 'return to normal'. (5) Weight shifts from uncertainty indices (2025) to plumbing, inventories and institutions (late 2025 to 2026). (6) Market outlook: optimistic baseline for 2025 (MT155, Dec 2024) turns to a 2026 pessimist (MT221, Dec 2025). He gives reasons for most of these shifts only implicitly through events; he does not explicitly review his own forecast record, except the Sept 2024 Fed-cut mistake (MT002).

## Blind spots (reader)
- (reader) Almost no quantified results: his VIX-regime regressions, replication research, cocoa COT work and uncertainty-index work are described but never shown with samples, coefficients, significance or out-of-sample tests.
- (reader) No explicit record of past forecasts and hit rate; only one admitted mistake (Sept 2024 Fed cut, MT002). The calls on gold, bubbles, buyback, 1914-style non-discounting and equities are unscored.
- (reader) Transaction costs, capacity and the actual size or turnover of his own book are rarely stated although he stresses footprint; his own fund's performance is never discussed except as 'my long-term book'.
- (reader) No rules for when to override or exit a regime view, and no thresholds for combining uncertainty indices, VIX, dispersion and network measures into a decision; the dashboard is described, not specified.
- (reader) The size of the trend-following benefit from fragmentation, stock-outs and intermediation shortage is asserted qualitatively; no correlation, inventory or capital data are shown.
- (reader) Equity and credit alpha beyond trend are barely addressed; valuation (ERP, bubbles) is used for warnings only, with no sizing rule; the causal-network edge is explicitly 'unsolved'.
- (reader) Mechanisms are often reasoned from a single episode (2025 tariffs, Sept 2024 cut, 2026 oil shock); samples of comparable episodes are small, which he sometimes concedes.
