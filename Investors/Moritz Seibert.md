# Moritz Seibert

Moritz Seibert is a practitioner of long-term, commodity-heavy trend following (he runs his own CTA, captioned Takahe/Takahi) who also hosts Open Interest and sometimes Systematic Investor. He believes the money comes from applying a weak, simple entry signal (about 70% of trades lose) to as many truly independent markets as possible, keeping every loss small, and letting rare outliers pay; entry details matter little, initial size and exits matter most. He rejects dynamic resizing (calling it hidden mean reversion that trims winners and cuts the right tail), uses next-settlement execution with no resting stops, avoids overfitting and never tweaks the system after a drawdown. He treats costs and execution as first-order (a high-turnover futures strategy faces roughly a 10% a year hurdle), thinks calendar and flow effects tied to human schedules persist while published or crowded effects decay, and sees trend as a non-equity-beta stream that deserves a performance fee while industry volatility has halved and fees have not. Much of his material is questions to guests, so many of his views are stated briefly and without data.

## Core beliefs
- Trend following needs no forecast. The entry signal is weak (about 70% of trades lose, 30% win); profits come from a few outliers and from keeping losses small. Entry details matter little for a long-term system, and the trend follower's edge is enduring bad periods ('pain arbitrage'). _(stated 3×; stable - same risk-first logic in 2024 (MT020, accepting Eckhardt's frame) and 2025 (MT207 own statistics).)_ ([[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C09-2|MT207-C09-2]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C09-3|MT207-C09-3]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-1|MT207-C10-1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-3|MT207-C10-3]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-X1|MT207-C10-X1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-4|MT207-C10-4]], [[MT020 - Bill's Turtle Trading Journey, Modern Systematic Trading, and more! - Open Interest 10#^MT020-C14-2|MT020-C14-2]], [[MT020 - Bill's Turtle Trading Journey, Modern Systematic Trading, and more! - Open Interest 10#^MT020-C07-1|MT020-C07-1]])

## Mechanisms
- **Independent markets to higher risk-adjusted return and a capacity cap**: small trend edge per market -> apply it to many independent markets -> add small, uncorrelated markets (Chinese futures, canola, lumber, livestock, regional carbon) -> fund capped near USD 500m. Fails when: A large correlated shock (Liberation Day, tariffs) puts positions on in many markets at once, so more markets with positions means a bigger single-day loss; he does not force positions in every market. ([[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C05-1|MT207-C05-1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C05-2|MT207-C05-2]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C05-3|MT207-C05-3]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C06-1|MT207-C06-1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C14-1|MT207-C14-1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C03-2|MT207-C03-2]])
- **Weak entry, small losses, outlier-driven profit**: entry signal poor (about 70% losers) -> entry details matter little for a long-term system -> profit depends on initial size, stop room and give-back exit -> losses stay small and rare outliers pay -> less volatility drag and easier recovery from drawdowns. Fails when: A manager may not stay with the system through wild single-month swings (behavioural risk, not a model failure). ([[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C09-2|MT207-C09-2]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C09-3|MT207-C09-3]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-1|MT207-C10-1]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-3|MT207-C10-3]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-4|MT207-C10-4]], [[MT207 - What Makes an Alternative Investment Truly Valuable- - Systematic Investor - Ep.364#^MT207-C10-X1|MT207-C10-X1]])
- **Why resizing is rejected**: price moves in the trader's favour or volatility rises -> vol- or risk-based sizing trims the winner -> trend system behaves like mean reversion with extra commissions and bid-offer -> lower asymmetry and fewer right-tail years, smoother returns and smaller drawdowns. Fails when: Event risk: a no-resizing position can be stopped out by a one- or two-day move and give-back potential is larger (Niels's counter); he calls the choice a matter of taste. - **Delayed next-settlement execution instead of resting stops**: signal from last settlement -> trade at next day's settlement, no stop orders -> not picked off and not stopped by flash spikes (South African rand) -> slightly better overall returns than stops in own tests.
