# Nick Baltas



Baltas is a quant/QIS strategist who thinks of trend following as a rules-based, permanent, never-timed way to be positioned for sustained moves and prolonged drawdowns (trend earns when information is digested gradually and fundamentals are uncertain; it does not earn in short V-shaped spike-and-revert events, which hit the medium speeds twice and are the main cause of manager dispersion). He makes his case from first principles and from papers he reads closely (Man Group, Quantica, ATP, Graham, AQR, Harvey et al.) plus his own year-by-year speed/universe grids and attribution, and he flags hunches, hindsight bias and data mining himself. His working rules: scale every exposure by volatility (and correlation), let risk flow to where trends exist, keep a broad 50-100 market universe without screening out past losers, add beta-neutral carry and reversion to diversify the periods when trend fails, match universe and speed to the objective (core liquid markets for crisis alpha, broad for long-run Sharpe), size the allocation by tracking error against the policy benchmark rather than by optimiser, never change a model because of one episode or one year's ranking, and treat crowding as a question of whether the return is a genuine risk premium (survives, only varies) or a flow imbalance (erodes). Money is made by harvesting a small number of durable premia (trend, carry, reversion/skew, vol selling) in diversified, volatility-controlled form and by staying invested through known losing regimes; he does not forecast markets and says even reading the current state is hard.

## Evidence style
Mix of (a) first-principles and algebraic reasoning (PCA, implied returns, leverage arithmetic, loss-aversion, equilibrium/constraint arguments, risk premium vs flow-imbalance), (b) close reading of practitioner and academic papers (Man Group, Quantica, ATP, Graham, AQR, Harvey et al., Penman, Longtail Alpha) reported as reviewer, (c) his own desk data: 25-year speed-by-universe ranking grids, scaled correlations, attribution and trend barometer, and short-window episodes (one week to one month), (d) client/allocator conversations as anecdote. He repeatedly states limits ('I could be wrong', 'I have not tested it', 'my gut feeling', 'I cannot prove'), flags hindsight bias and data mining in his own suggestions, asks for false-positive counts and postmortem data, and rarely shows formal tests on air; several citations are from memory or second-hand.

## How views changed
Core framework is stable across 2024-09/2026: V-shapes hurt trend (especially the middle speeds), do not change models after pain, trend is strategic and untimed, diversify with carry/reversion, vol-scale. Changes are in emphasis, not reversals, and are mostly tied to market events rather than to explained revisions. (1) Sizing: May-Aug 2024 he personally prefers dynamic vol scaling and dynamic asset-class allocation (MT117-C09-1, MT099-C08-R3); Oct 2024 he says static and dynamic converge long-run and matter at turning points (MT200-C10-2); June 2025 he floats a lower/time-varying vol target after repeated V-shapes but flags hindsight (MT087-C08-2, MT087-C08-X1); Jan 2026 he is 'reactive and diversified by design' and does not claim it was wrong though it cost performance (MT116-C08-1). (2) Speed/universe: Aug 2024 'neither speed better' (MT099-C10-3); Dec 2025/Jan 2026 slow and small-universe won 2023-25 but due to V-shapes (MT134-C03-4, MT116-C08-2); Mar 2026 faster speeds won (MT167-C06-1); Jul 2026 prefer slower for defence, objective decides universe (MT174-C09-X1, MT174-C06-2).
