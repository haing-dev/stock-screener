# Pierre Andurand



Discretionary energy and metals trader who believes commodity prices are set by physical supply-demand balances modelled year by year: the question is what price clears the market, not where price sits in its historical range. Where demand is inelastic (small share of end cost) and supply responds slowly (shale about 12 months, other oil about 7 years, mines 10-15 years, cocoa trees 3-5 years), a shortage can only be cleared by a large, non-linear price move once inventories run out, and he wants those multi-month to multi-year moves, long or short. His edge is tracking high-frequency data (exports, port and exchange stocks, consultant surveys, backwardation, stocks-to-grindings) faster than conservative analysts and pod funds that cannot hold multi-year views. He expresses views with futures and long options (never sells options), sizes so he survives a 50% adverse move in illiquid markets, and cuts risk when markets move against him for reasons he does not understand (Sep 2022). His big calls were Brent $200 (Mar 2022, not reached by Sep when the Russian loss had been offset by strategic-reserve releases), Europe getting through the winter without Russian gas (Sep 2022), and cocoa above $20,000 and copper multi-year deficit (May 2024), where he said oil disruption risk had been overestimated in 2022-23. Evidence is first-principles arithmetic and historical analogues (2008, 2011-14, 1977 cocoa), not backtests.

## Evidence style
First-principles supply-demand arithmetic with back-of-envelope numbers (grams of cocoa per person per day, bcm, mb/d), high-frequency data and historical analogues (1977, 2008, 2011-14, April 2020), plus personal track record; figures quoted from memory without charts. Probabilistic about timing; concedes the market may disagree (Sep 2022).

## How views changed
The method (balance sheet, inelastic demand, slow supply, high-frequency data) is constant. Oil moved from $200 Brent and long-lasting Russian outage (Mar 2022) to the observation by Sep 2022 that the Russian loss was only about 0.5 mb/d and was offset by SPR releases, so oil fell and he cut positions; by May 2024 he says disruption risk was overestimated in 2022-23. Europe: from possible gas rationing (Mar 2022) to getting through the winter (Sep 2022) once governments plan for zero Russian gas. Demand: from 'no demand destruction' at $110-114 to recession as the main downside. He gives observed outcomes as the reason; he does not label the $200 call an error. Focus shifts from oil to softs and metals in 2024.

## Blind spots (reader)
- No track record of calls shown; $200 Brent not reached and not reviewed in later interviews
- No account of how position size and stop levels were set beyond 'survive a 50% adverse move' and 'cut and survive'
- Sell-side and policy forecasts treated as slow without evidence; own estimates (17.7% cocoa loss) not cross-checked in the pack
- Back-of-envelope demand elasticities are asserted, not measured; price level that destroys demand is judged
- Little on shorts or on when a deficit thesis stops working beyond a good crop

## Reader inferences
- (reader) His hit rate rests on timing of price-clearing events, which the interviews do not test; a rule-based version would use stocks ratios, backwardation and lead-time classes.
- (reader) The same physical-balance logic is applied across oil, gas, cocoa and copper; cross-commodity tests of one inventory-to-use signal are possible.
- (reader) The Sep 2022 position cut despite an unchanged thesis shows risk takes precedence over conviction when liquidity thins, a testable liquidity-conditioned sizing rule.
