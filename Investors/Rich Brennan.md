# Rich Brennan

Brennan is an Australia-based systematic trend follower who calls himself an 'outlier hunter'. He treats markets as complex adaptive, reflexive, path-dependent and non-ergodic feedback systems: price moves because orders have impact (not because news reveals value), trends are the reinforcing structure left when divergent (trend, momentum, stop-loss, passive buy-the-biggest) participants outweigh convergent (value, mean-reversion, market-maker) ones, and a few rare outlier trends (cocoa, gold, wheat, coffee) make nearly all the profit while 60-70% of trades lose. He therefore does not predict: he keeps the widest feasible, mostly absolute-momentum, medium-to-long-horizon universe (just under 100 markets) as a watch list, enters small ATR-normalised equal-risk bets, runs an ensemble of structurally different trend systems with trailing stops that switch on one after another as a trend persists, sizes new risk from closed (realised) balance rather than open equity, and never cuts a winner because volatility rose. He rejects Gaussian tools (Sharpe, VaR, Kelly, expectancy, vol targeting) because calm stores hidden fragility and survival, not average return, is the test; he wants convexity (small linear losses, open right tail), walk-forward testing on untouched data, loosely parameterised plateau rules, and a long live record. His market views follow one regime story: coordinated central banks and QE (roughly 2010-2018) compressed trends, whereas a fragmented, decoupled, multipolar world after 2020 (stated most strongly 2026-01) is the best environment for trend following in years. His evidence is mostly first principles, analogy (weather, avalanche, race car) and his own unpublished simulations/tests (68-market Hurst/kurtosis fingerprint, agent-based model with a 20-30% divergent threshold, 68-market subset test) described verbally without figures.

## Evidence style
Mostly first principles and analogy from complex-systems science (Holland, West, Wolfram, Mandelbrot, Bouchaud, Peters, Gabaix; weather ensembles, avalanche, sandpile, race car, F1, starlings) plus Dredge/Taleb convexity ideas, backed by his own research that is reported verbally and not shown: walk-forward vs backtest comparison, 10 vs 20 model test, 68-market Hurst/kurtosis fingerprint, agent-based model, 40-year reshuffles, 125-year Dow/S&P fractal analysis, 68-market random-subset test. Anecdotes (cocoa, wheat 2022, oil 2026, Brisbane floods, LTCM, Winton). Several numbers are cited without source (40-year S&P months, 60% passive, WHO fertility, 5,791x). Very little displayed data; several claims explicitly unpublished. The framework is constant from 2024-06 to 2026-07 (complex adaptive systems, outliers, ensemble, convexity, distrust of Gaussian tools). What changes: (1) Rigour of the claims: 2024 reasoning is analogy and Faber/Holland-style theory; 2026 episodes cite his own quantified research (Hurst 0.866, tail exponent 3.33, 68 markets since 1984, agent-model threshold, 40-year reshuffles). He gives no explicit reason for this shift (the research phases are mentioned as his own factors-of-finance work). (2) Sizing: 2024-06 fixed-dollar risk with 25-day ATR stop; 2024-12 vol targeting called shortsighted; 2025-02 risk a small fraction of closed core capital; 2025-08 static ATR-normalised bets; 2025-10 closed-balance sizing; 2026-03 fixed vol targeting understates tails; 2026-05 says ATR/variance is the control; 2026-07 absolute leverage cap and equal risk. Reasons given: convexity, path dependence and Kelly noise, not new data. (3) Number of systems: about 20 (2024-10) to depends-on-regime (2024-12) to about 10 saturation (2025-08), reason not explained. (4) Regime outlook: Oct 2024 correlated reversals and chop, mid-2025 deep drawdown, then 'trend heaven' (2025-10) and best in years (2026-01), reason given is central-bank decoupling and fragmentation. (5) Typology: two tribes (2025-06) becomes four species (2025-08). (6) Passive: 2026-05 adds the passive/inelastic-markets argument not present before.

## Reader inferences
- (reader) Operationally this is a high-skew, low-hit-rate (30-40% winners) absolute-momentum strategy on a very wide universe with static equal-risk entries, ensemble pyramiding and trailing stops; it should be evaluated on skew, top-trade share and MAR over long samples, not on Sharpe or short-term rankings, and compared with SG Trend and vol-targeted peers.
- (reader) His two main differentiators for testing are (a) breadth beyond about 70 markets with no cross-sectional ranking, and (b) no volatility resizing after entry plus closed-balance sizing; both are testable with public futures data and a trade log.
- (reader) The regime claim reduces to testable conditioning variables: central-bank coordination/policy dispersion, QE periods, compression (low realised vol) followed by shocks, and passive share; the hypotheses should be registered before looking at holdout data.
- (reader) His distrust of Gaussian tools and his reliance on ruin/survival suggest preferring drawdown, ruin probability and geometric growth with reshuffled paths over Sharpe when comparing his rules with others (e.g., Carver, Kaastrup-Larsen).
- (reader) Overlap with Dave Dredge's convexity framework (brakes and acceleration, barbell, options versus stops) is large; claims quoted from Dredge in MT107, MT186, MT222 and MT224 may be adoption of Dredge's ideas rather than independent evidence.
- (reader) The role label for the same person varies across the pack (East Coast Capital Management in MT107, Stockcross in MT181, 'at Dunn' in MT222 as the ledger gives it, East Coast/Australia elsewhere); treat firm affiliation as uncertain, and note that several early appearances (MT019, MT063, MT105) lean on speaker attribution by content.
- (reader) His rule of thumb that structure (the engine) is permanent but opportunity varies suggests a two-layer model for a strategy: a permanent trend allocation (harvest) and a conditional exposure sized by regime indicators, which he declines to do himself (he stays always on).
- (reader) Statements about AI and LLMs (pattern matching, overfit, will not end trends) are consistent across MT019, MT057, MT105, MT181 but are asserted; they matter mainly as a reason he keeps to medium-long horizons.
