# Rob Carver



Carver is an independent systematic futures trader, author and ex-AHL head of fixed income who trades his own money in a slow, diversified, volatility-targeted book of trend, carry, skew, slow mean-reversion and relative rules across about 250-300 futures. He sees these returns as risk premia, not an edge over other participants, so the only durable edge is discipline: accurate and low costs, slow trading, breadth, moderate leverage (below half Kelly, rarely above 20-30% volatility), continuous volatility-scaled sizing with no stops, and an almost religious distrust of backtests, narratives and timing. He treats short samples as noise (bad years are normal, decay of a low-Sharpe strategy cannot be detected, a 'the world has changed' story is untestable), so the default is full-history evidence and buy-and-hold of strategies. He keeps opinions on macro (tariff inflation, Treasuries as a failing haven, Fed independence) strictly separate from the systematic book and admits when he was wrong. Money is made by leaving the system alone, keeping costs and leverage down and staying diversified; allocators should add diversifiers by correlation and size them with leverage-aware portfolio logic, not by recent performance.

## Evidence style
Mixes first-principles statistics (Kelly, Sharpe vs skew, multiple testing, statistical power, drawdown mathematics), his own long backtests (data since 1970) and live P&L/cost reviews, small self-run simulations, and reading of papers (Quantica, Man AHL, Safari-Schmidhuber, Asness, Ilmanen, Winton, Alpha Architect, Morningstar). He often flags numbers as from memory or hand-waving, welcomes hard evidence over his own guesses, treats null results as informative, and admits his macro/discretionary errors. Macro arguments rely on FT commentary and reasoning with no data of his own.

## How views changed
Core method has not changed by his own account (advice and principles unchanged for 10-20 years: MT137-C10-R1, MT137-C11-1). Changes are in application and in discretionary opinions, and he usually gives the evidence: (1) macro: bearish on US assets Feb-May 2025 (inflation, higher risk premium, deep recession, sold all US stocks and went to cash) then in Feb 2026 admits the bearish expectation and his April 2025 equity sale cost relative performance (MT128-C08-X2, MT128-C08-1); (2) trend allocation timing: twice he added capital after strong runs and regretted it (MT160-C02-1, MT196-C09-X1), reinforcing leave-timing-to-the-model; (3) AI: from 'no serious use' (2024) to limited idea generation under a hindsight-aware process (2026); (4) crisis alpha: from 'no guaranteed protection' to a decomposition showing it sits in beta timing (MT038), after Treasuries and the dollar failed as hedges in April 2025; (5) trend speed: as of April 2026 slow signals did best over the last year, but he refuses to infer a regime change from one year or from the VIX; (6) risk level: half of target (2024) falling to one third / 9% annualised (2026) as signals weakened, with +23.7% in the 12 months to April 2026 despite that. Reasons for changes are given as evidence from his own results or papers, not as revised theory.

## Blind spots (reader)
- (reader) Almost no valuation or fundamental equity analysis; macro opinions rely on FT commentary and are explicitly kept out of the systematic book, so there is no tested macro signal.
- (reader) His own track record is quoted only as fragments (YTD, 12-month, drawdown) with no full audited series, and the Kelly/Sharpe inputs (0.5-1) come from his own in-sample backtests.
- (reader) Many quantitative claims are from memory or 'hand-waving' (stop-ATR holding periods, R-squared figures, LTCM Sharpe), and several key results (pattern-fitting experiment, terminal-wealth simulation) are not shown.
- (reader) Capacity and market impact for larger assets are mostly addressed by reference to AHL; his advice is calibrated to a small retail-sized account, so costs and execution results may not scale.
- (reader) Survivorship, data-quality and backfill issues in the 50-year (since 1970) backtests are not discussed; he asks others for full-history evidence but the construction of that history is not described.
- (reader) Regime detection is rejected rather than studied (no conditioning variable works), so how to react when a real structural break occurs is not specified beyond 'very strong evidence'.
- (reader) Dealer positioning, options flows, and liquidity-flow drivers of price are barely addressed; crowding and volatility-scaler feedback are argued by reasoning without data.
- (reader) Tail behaviour of the whole book in a trend-and-everything-else-fails crisis is acknowledged (MT160) but not quantified; the skew bucket cap of 30% is only illustrative.

## Reader inferences
- (reader) Carver is a process and cost-control investor: the stated edge is not a signal but breadth, slow trading, accurate costs, moderate leverage and refusal to override; any strategy built from his ideas should be judged first on those operational choices.
- (reader) His heavy reliance on full-history evidence and equal weights implies a strong prior for simple, pooled, slow rules; when a candidate strategy needs recent-window fitting, per-instrument weights or regime switches, his framework predicts it will not survive out of sample.
- (reader) His macro views (tariffs, Fed independence, Treasuries, oil) are best treated as a record of opinions to be tested against his own systematic positions, not as inputs; in several cases the system and the opinion disagreed (long bonds Sept 2025; long equities June 2026).
- (reader) Recurring signals most reusable as testable rules: asset-class index trend plus residual momentum, convergent sleeve of about 40%, skew bounce, vol-scaled sizing with 4-5x diversification multiplier, 1-month vol / 6-month correlation estimation, buffers and cost-aware optimisation, and the Sharpe-unit drawdown rule.
- (reader) His risk was reduced to one third of target for many months across 2025-26 by signal weakness, yet the 12 months to April 2026 returned 23.7%, suggesting forecast-scaled risk is not itself a timing tool; the self-described neutral trend barometer is a useful state variable to test.
- (reader) The MT038 attribution (Fidelity portfolio manager, co-author) conflicts with other 2026 sources; treat the beta-timing and carry-over-vol results as weaker evidence of Carver's own view until confirmed.
- (reader) Disagreements with Niels Kaastrup-Larsen are largely about framing (crisis alpha, number of markets, replication) rather than method, and most would be resolved by data on independent bets, conditional correlation and out-of-sample replication tracking.
