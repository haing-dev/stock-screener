# Steve Eisman



Steve Eisman appears only as host of The Real Eisman Playbook (April to October 2026), so his own views are thin and mostly come in questions, summaries and closing remarks; he defers to guests on Fed mechanics, seasonality and ETF flows. Across the four episodes he reads the market top-down through a few drivers: oil and the 10-year Treasury yield move daily equities; the economy eventually slows at some (unknown) long rate and the market is 'getting closer to the Rubicon'; QE inflated asset prices without helping the real economy; Treasury's 6 billion dollar buyback was a BB gun and Bessent lost credibility. He worries about concentration (US equities are effectively one AI-hardware theme, tech near 40% of the S&P, with nowhere to hide if it reverses), about AI-ecosystem fragility (about 70% of hyperscaler revenue from OpenAI and Anthropic, both with negative cash flow; Meta's costs outrunning revenue), and about a private-credit-led credit-cycle tightening. He is sceptical of Bitcoin and DeFi, accepts calendar patterns because humans program algorithms, and in the closing takeaway formats asks what threshold would change the bullish stance. He gives no explicit portfolio rules; his first-hand evidence is fund-manager experience and his reading of company numbers.

## Mechanisms
- **Possible bazooka for Treasury demand via bank liquidity rules**: loosen the LCR so big banks need to hold less liquidity -> banks buy more Treasuries -> demand for Treasuries (a bazooka Treasury lacks on its own). Fails when: Guha notes banks may prefer bills to long bonds. - (reader) Because he is a host, treat first-hand statements (QE opinion, buyback as BB gun, Bitcoin theft, Meta numbers) as his views and closing summaries as relayed views.
