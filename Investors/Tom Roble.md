# Tom Roble

Tom Roble is an index-provider and allocator-side researcher (SocGen Prime Services; runs the SG CTA, Trend and Short-Term Traders indices) who thinks of managed futures as a diversifying, risk-mitigating stream whose return comes from breadth across many markets, from disciplined risk allocation and execution, and not from clever trend models, which he calls off-the-shelf. He sees trend as the dominant common factor with dispersion coming from the periphery (speed, universe, sizing, stops), notes a long-run drift to slower models (a 'speed factor') while keeping short-term models valuable at inflection points, and argues that replicators and CTA indices capture the beta but not the drawdown profile or the idiosyncratic bets behind a Sharpe near 0.9. He favours portable alpha and managed accounts that overlay futures margin on full equity exposure, warns that crisis-alpha marketing set unrealistic expectations, and cautions that the CTA index is a set of the largest active managers with survivorship and cash-composite biases. He reads each year through the SG trend indicator (about 200-500 breakout parameters): 2025 was decided by risk management in a few trending markets (metals), and 2026 shows broad, diversified gains. His evidence is index data, manager meetings and client conversations, not backtests of his own.

## Core beliefs
- Diversification across many markets is the only free lunch. A single market's trend Sharpe is only 0.2-0.5; around 0.9 needs many uncorrelated markets and idiosyncratic bets; excluding a market is itself a bet that the chosen ones do better net of cost, and a market should be added when marginal diversification beats its cost (15/50 to 150 markets is material, 150 to 151 is not). Adding markets efficiently is the trend philosophy, not over-engineering. _(stated 4×; stable on principle; he concedes that in 2025 concentrated programs created return (MT013-C10-4) and that alternative-market CTAs such as Man Evolution and Systematica are now working poorly (MT146-C17-3).)_ ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C11-2|MT028-C11-2]], [[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C14-2|MT028-C14-2]], [[MT146 - Slower Models, Bigger Risks - Systematic Investor - Ep.358#^MT146-C13-3|MT146-C13-3]], [[MT146 - Slower Models, Bigger Risks - Systematic Investor - Ep.358#^MT146-C14-1|MT146-C14-1]], [[MT146 - Slower Models, Bigger Risks - Systematic Investor - Ep.358#^MT146-C17-2|MT146-C17-2]], [[MT146 - Slower Models, Bigger Risks - Systematic Investor - Ep.358#^MT146-C13-X2|MT146-C13-X2]], [[MT189 - Trend Following Is Becoming Something Bigger Than Crisis Alpha - Systematic Investor - Ep#^MT189-C03-1|MT189-C03-1]], [[MT189 - Trend Following Is Becoming Something Bigger Than Crisis Alpha - Systematic Investor - Ep#^MT189-C11-X3|MT189-C11-X3]])

## Mechanisms
- **Why a replicator cannot reach full-universe Sharpe**: single-market trend Sharpe 0.2-0.3 -> adding many markets raises portfolio Sharpe (execution efficiency opened access) -> a replicator holds about 10 instruments and misses the idiosyncratic bets (top commodity contributors) -> long-run return and correlation match but drawdowns differ.
