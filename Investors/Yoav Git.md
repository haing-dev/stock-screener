# Yoav Git

Git is a systematic trend-following practitioner and researcher (ex-Winton, AHL and Brevan Howard; runs fixed-income trend at Gresham/Safi and, per MT190, a commodity fund trading the full futures curve) who sees trend as the harvest of positive return autocorrelation that comes from sustained imbalances, which differ by asset class and over time (rate gradients and coupons in bonds, supply shocks and slow supply response in commodities, value traders in equities, no anchor in crypto). He makes the case for trend in portfolio terms rather than standalone-Sharpe terms: it is zero correlated to what it trades by construction, similar in Sharpe to 60/40 (about 0.5 versus 0.55-0.76), and so deserves close to 30% of portfolio risk; in an inflationary regime bonds fail and commodity trend is their mirror image, so 50% bonds plus 50% commodity trend gives roughly CPI+4% in every regime without forecasting. He thinks the signal is a commodity worth only about 0.2 Sharpe per market, so the edge lies in market selection by structure, risk scaling, diversification via low beta to the first factor, execution and cost awareness (patient execution, cost-aware skipping of reverting signals), and he is sceptical of backtests (a 50% haircut, negative-lag and random-noise tests). He explains trend drawdowns as nothing happening (volatility compression, fewer than about 40% of markets winning), not model failure, advises staying true to the sold style, and watches market-structure changes (0DTE dealer gamma eroding short-term equity trend, financialisation of commodity fronts, CTA crowding in crises) while admitting that several of his own theories are unproven.

## Mechanisms
- **Sustained imbalance creates trend (asset class by asset class)**: two central banks hold different rates -> sustained carry flows tolerated by policymakers -> positive autocorrelation; EM FX with high carry trends strongly, EURUSD without a gradient does not → no valuation anchor (crypto) or slow supply response (power plants, cows, mines) -> permanent or prolonged imbalance -> large, long trends → equity value traders -> negative autocorrelation -> weaker equity trend. Fails when: No sustained imbalance (no rate gradient); Rob Carver finds no statistically significant asset-class difference; structure and regime change, and Niels notes bond autocorrelation may reflect 40 years of falling rates. ([[MT029 - CTAs- The Good, The Bad, & The Misunderstood - Top Traders Unplugged 148#^MT029-C08-1|MT029-C08-1]], [[MT029 - CTAs- The Good, The Bad, & The Misunderstood - Top Traders Unplugged 148#^MT029-C08-2|MT029-C08-2]], [[MT029 - CTAs- The Good, The Bad, & The Misunderstood - Top Traders Unplugged 148#^MT029-C05-2|MT029-C05-2]], [[MT029 - CTAs- The Good, The Bad, & The Misunderstood - Top Traders Unplugged 148#^MT029-C09-2|MT029-C09-2]], [[MT029 - CTAs- The Good, The Bad, & The Misunderstood - Top Traders Unplugged 148#^MT029-C09-3|MT029-C09-3]], [[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C14-5|MT024-C14-5]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C03-1|MT223-C03-1]])
- **Elasticity and adjustment speed set trend size and length**: demand or supply shock -> price must move to clear the market → inelastic demand or slow supply response (electricity, cows take a year) -> larger move and longer adjustment -> prolonged trend, harvested at slow speed → fast-adjusting markets (equity ETFs: negative autocorrelation within a week) -> intraday trend at best; each market's lookback should avoid its mean-reversion zone. Fails when: A lookback falls inside the market's mean-reversion zone, or supply is elastic (bonds). ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C03-1|MT223-C03-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-1|MT223-C08-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-2|MT223-C08-2]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-X2|MT223-C08-X2]])
- **Inflation regimes (ERAS): bonds fail, commodity trend is the mirror**: inflation rises toward and beyond about 4.5% -> bonds earn CPI minus and correlate with equities → supply shocks and constraints (20 years to build a mine, low storage) -> commodity prices trend -> commodity trend profits in inflation, with smaller drag than long-only in low inflation → 50% bonds plus 50% commodity trend -> roughly CPI+4% in every regime with little equity correlation. Fails when: Falling inflation is bad for commodity trend (Covid to Ukraine); regime boundaries (2% and 4.5%) were chosen for equal time shares; rates and inflation rising together could hit bonds harder than commodity trend gains; the 1920s-onwards data are less reliable. ([[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C06-1|MT024-C06-1]], [[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C06-3|MT024-C06-3]], [[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C06-4|MT024-C06-4]], [[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C07-1|MT024-C07-1]], [[MT024 - Building an Inflation-Proof Portfolio - Systematic Investor - Ep.384#^MT024-C07-3|MT024-C07-3]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C11-1|MT223-C11-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C11-2|MT223-C11-2]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C13-1|MT223-C13-1]])
- **Why a low-Sharpe uncorrelated diversifier raises portfolio Sharpe**: add a strategy negatively or zero correlated to the portfolio, even flat -> portfolio volatility falls -> higher risk-adjusted return → high-volatility diversifier -> capital efficiency, small allocations hedge meaningfully → 60/40 Sharpe about 0.55-0.76 versus CTAs about 0.5 (2000-2025), correlation near zero -> forward Sharpe of 0.7 and 0.3 still puts close to 30% of risk in trend. Fails when: Correlation to 60/40 turns positive or forward trend Sharpe falls far below 0.3; allocators cannot justify a low-Sharpe diversifier to committee; bond diversification is unstable in inflation. ([[MT192 - Trend Following Still Works — So Why Do Allocators Ignore It- - Systematic Investor - Ep.3#^MT192-C04-3|MT192-C04-3]], [[MT192 - Trend Following Still Works — So Why Do Allocators Ignore It- - Systematic Investor - Ep.3#^MT192-C05-1|MT192-C05-1]], [[MT192 - Trend Following Still Works — So Why Do Allocators Ignore It- - Systematic Investor - Ep.3#^MT192-C09-4|MT192-C09-4]], [[MT192 - Trend Following Still Works — So Why Do Allocators Ignore It- - Systematic Investor - Ep.3#^MT192-C09-5|MT192-C09-5]], [[MT192 - Trend Following Still Works — So Why Do Allocators Ignore It- - Systematic Investor - Ep.3#^MT192-C09-6|MT192-C09-6]], [[MT221 - Why Trend Following Still Works in Tough Markets - Systematic Investor - Ep.381#^MT221-C02-1|MT221-C02-1]])
- **Diversification through low average beta to the first factor**: each market has a beta to the first principal factor -> portfolio risk is dominated by average beta (idiosyncratic risk falls with square root of N, index risk does not) → allocate to low-beta markets (metals, some energy) -> higher diversification factor (factor 3 turns Sharpe 0.25 into 0.75) -> market count matters little → CTAs load about 70% on the first factor with about 50% pairwise correlation, stable since 2011. Fails when: Correlation structure shifts quickly in a crisis; capacity pushes risk into the biggest markets and shrinks effective breadth. ## Evidence style
Mechanism-first reasoning with back-of-envelope arithmetic (Sharpe per signal speed, diversification factor, hit-ratio break-even), close reading of academic and industry papers (Galanter, Quantica, SG, Seep, Gabaix-Koijen, Man AHL inflation paper, CFM blogs, Satka et al.) with critique of their identification, own research (the ERAS inflation-regime study, the autocorrelation paper with Adam Pedel, a COT regression), data relayed from Rob Carver's blog, and practitioner experience at AHL, Winton and Safi. Figures are often quoted from memory (70-75% of AHL P&L from fixed income, 5bp and 30-40bp execution cost, Safi about 12% over three years after fees), regime boundaries were chosen for round numbers, and he flags that several theories are unproven, that his first premise in a blog was wrong (MT073) and that he is a pessimist about performance (MT223).

## How views changed
The core framework is constant across April 2025 to July 2026: trend as a zero-correlated diversifier, structure-driven autocorrelation, bonds failing in inflation and commodity trend as the hedge, drawdowns as fewer winners. Changes: (1) The allocator-side case moves from MT029 (April 2025: a 0.7-Sharpe generalist needs 1.2-1.3 Sharpe or a tailored role; CTAs are a crude convexity hedge) to MT192 (June 2025: close to 30% of risk is justified; trend is zero correlated by construction, not a hedge) and MT221 (a third of risk at Sharpe 0.5 versus 1.5, but flows follow performance). The reason is a change of vantage point (allocator bucket arithmetic versus portfolio arithmetic), not an explicit retraction. (2) In MT073 (August 2025) he explicitly reverses the first premise of his own blog: long-horizon CMA allocators are mean-reverting and sit on the other side of trend, not adding to equities as he first assumed; reason given is the Vanguard revision and an allocator conversation. (3) Fixed income moves from 'the most consistent trend asset since 2010' (MT029, 70-75% of AHL P&L over 25 years) to 'very difficult', 'three poor years' and 'choppy since 2023' (MT073, MT223, MT024, MT168); the reason given is choppy, mean-reverting yields rather than model failure, and specialised trend held up better. (4) Speed: MT192 says speed matters less than risk scaling and 12-month TSMOM is near optimal, MT223 says each market has its own adjustment speed, MT190 says short-term equity trend is eroded by 0DTE gamma but slower trend is barely affected. (5) Crowding: MT073 finds no sign of crowding or capacity, while MT205 and MT221 stress synchronised CTA exits and financialisation of iron ore; the topics differ (positioning data versus crisis flows). (6) Rates: MT024 declines to forecast inflation, MT168 says higher yields are near-inevitable but untimeable, MT221 says 10-year around 6% most likely. (7) Equities: MT223 says there is a bit of a bubble but the flow multiplier is overstated; MT190 adds that AI capex has near-zero recovery value if AI fails; he gives no change of mind in response to events beyond these.

- (reader) Almost none of his numbers are shown with data in the conversations (single-market Sharpe 0.25 or 0.2, 70-75% of AHL P&L from fixed income, 5bp and 30-40bp execution costs, Safi about 12% over three years); the pack gives no live track record beyond that single figure.
- (reader) He explains drawdowns as fewer trends and low hit ratios but gives no tests that distinguish this from alpha decay or crowding beyond his COT regression.
- (reader) No explicit stop-loss, drawdown or leverage limits are stated; risk is described only as volatility scaling, per-market caps and factor hedging.
