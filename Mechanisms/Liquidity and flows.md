- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C02-2|MT225-C02-2]] — Index pinning by dealer hedging forces single-stock volatility up and correlation down, driving massive rotation, because idiosyncratic news must be offset inside a pinned index.
- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C02-X1|MT225-C02-X1]] — Forecast made about four months earlier: through roughly April-May 2026 the S&P would show index-level volatility compression together with massive single-stock rotation, which the speaker says is what happened; used as evidence for the options-pinning framework.
- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C03-2|MT225-C03-2]] — Options and structured-product flows grow exponentially like a technology tipping point (network effects, demand for non-correlation and capital efficiency), so each expiry matters more, especially quarterly opex (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec).
- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C04-1|MT225-C04-1]] — When dealers are long gamma/short stock hedges and the left tail is not activated, hedges must be bought back as time and vol decay (vanna and charm flows), giving sell-off-then-buy-back behaviour and support into March opex; after opex these flows drop off, so late March-April is more cautious with a stair-step down expected.
- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C15-1|MT225-C15-1]] — Start with the market as the voting machine: model what flows do first, then see how that affects long-term macro outcomes; do not draw a linear line from news to markets, since markets are the largest driver of liquidity.
- [[MT225 - Why the Market Isn’t Moving, And Why That’s Dangerous - Systematic Investor - Ep.389#^MT225-C16-1|MT225-C16-1]] — Rising asset prices create collateral that can be re-leveraged (a 20% rise on $500tn of assets yields $100tn) which dwarfs central bank liquidity; if markets stop rising, this reflexive counterbalance disappears, and issuance limits, debt refinancing after the 2021 rate bottom and soft QE slowing draw liquidity, leading to decollateralisation risk.
