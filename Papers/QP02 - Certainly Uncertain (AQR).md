# Certainly Uncertain (AQR)

**Authors:** Jordan Brooks (AQR Capital Management)

**Summary:** Question: will the elevated post-2020 macro uncertainty persist and what should investors do. Method: JLN macro uncertainty index persistence regression, Fed-tradeoff and market-disagreement evidence, decade Sharpe ratios and SG Trend in drawdowns. Conclusion: uncertainty likely stays high, so diversify beyond equities toward bonds, commodities, trend following and global macro.

## Argument map

**Question:** Is the high macroeconomic uncertainty of 2020-23 likely to persist rather than revert to the calm 2010s, and what should investors hold if it does? It matters because high uncertainty coincides with high equity volatility and poor equity returns.

**Thesis:** Macro uncertainty is highly persistent statistically and is reinforced by central-bank trade-offs and market/forecaster disagreement, so it will likely stay high; investors should diversify away from equity risk into bonds, commodities, risk parity, trend following and macro strategies. ### Foundations (premises)
- Uncertainty can be measured as the volatility of forecast errors of macro data (JLN). _[empirical, stated; support: JLN 2015 methodology; cross-check with a simple forecast-error index (corr 0.7)]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C01-1|C01-1]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-1|C02-1]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-2|C02-2]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-7|C02-7]])
- Volatility-type quantities are persistent and therefore forecastable, unlike returns. _[empirical, stated; support: analogy to stock volatility; AR(1) on JLN]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-1|C03-1]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-2|C03-2]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-4|C02-4]])
- Monetary policy acts with long and variable lags via rate-sensitive demand and bank credit. _[theoretical, stated; support: Friedman citation; standard transmission argument]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-1|C04-1]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-9|C03-9]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-9|C03-9]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-2|C04-2]])
- Central banks effectively have a dual mandate, so inflation-employment trade-offs create policy uncertainty. _[institutional, stated; support: assertion; ECB example]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-8|C04-8]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-4|C04-4]])
- Disagreement between prices and forecasts must resolve, and resolution produces volatility. _[assumed, stated; support: logic only; no historical test of the disagreement measure]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C05-5|C05-5]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C06-2|C06-2]])
- Markets incorporate new information gradually, which is why trend following works. _[behavioural, stated; support: assertion plus SG Trend drawdown evidence and AQR citations]_ ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C08-1|C08-1]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C08-2|C08-2]])

### Design rationale
- **Use JLN (conditional vol of forecast errors, 132 series) as the uncertainty measure**: more theoretically appealing (expected not realized errors) and broader data. Alternatives: simple average absolute forecast error of GDP, CPI, IP. Sensitivity: corr 0.7 with the simple measure; results with it not shown ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-8|C02-8]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-6|C02-6]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-7|C02-7]])
- **AR(1) without constant to forecast JLN**: persistence is the key feature; simple model. Alternatives: none shown. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-2|C03-2]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-6|C03-6]])
- **Trade-off months: core PCE > 4% and unemployment > CBO NAIRU**: 4% = twice the Fed target. Alternatives: none shown. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-5|C04-5]])
- **Equal-risk portfolio with inverse realized-vol weights**: simple risk parity to show consistency across regimes. Alternatives: single asset classes. Sensitivity: not shown (vol lookback, rebalancing unstated) ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C07-5|C07-5]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C07-5|C07-5]])
- **Splice Ibbotson intermediate govt bonds before 1973**: not given (index availability implied). Alternatives: . Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C07-6|C07-6]])

### Evidence map

Does not rule out: (reader) regime shifts; JLN is itself a smoothed conditional-volatility estimate, which mechanically raises persistence. Strength: moderate - strong persistence but model is trivially simple and in-sample ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-2|C03-2]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-4|C03-4]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C03-5|C03-5]])
- JLN averages 0.6 in trade-off months vs -0.1 otherwise, t > 7. Tests: step 2 (trade-offs raise uncertainty). Rules out: no association. Does not rule out: (reader) trade-off months are few and clustered (1970s-early 80s, 2022), so effective sample is tiny and the t-stat ignores autocorrelation; causality may run from shocks to both. Strength: weak - few independent episodes ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-5|C04-5]], [[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-6|C04-6]])
- JLN > 1 months: equities -16% vs +6%; vol 21% vs 12%. Tests: step 4. Rules out: no relation between uncertainty and equity outcomes. Does not rule out: (reader) contemporaneous only; equity crashes may raise JLN rather than JLN predicting returns. Strength: moderate - contemporaneous ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-5|C02-5]])
- JLN corr 0.6 with VIX, 0.5 with realized vol. Tests: step 4. Rules out: . Does not rule out: (reader) common shock driving both. Strength: moderate ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C06-3|C06-3]])
- SG Trend positive in 3 largest equity drawdowns since 2000, avg +21.1% vs -32.1%. Tests: trend benefits from large shocks. Rules out: . ### Regimes
- JLN > 1 → equities weak and volatile ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-5|C02-5]])
- Fed trade-off (core PCE > 4% and unemployment > NAIRU) → macro uncertainty above average ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C04-5|C04-5]])
- Low-uncertainty decade (2010s) → trend following below average ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C02-9|C02-9]])
- Short sharp drawdowns (Q1 2020, Q4 2018) → trend following gave little or negative protection ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C08-2|C08-2]])
- High pre-existing front-end uncertainty → small shocks (SVB) cause large repricings ([[QP02 - Certainly Uncertain (AQR)#^QP02-C06-6|C06-6]])

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) The JLN level (and its AR(1) forecast) is a months-to-years lead for equity volatility and a conditioning variable for trend/macro allocations; a Fed trade-off dummy (core PCE > 4% and unemployment > NAIRU) and the gap between SEP dots and futures-implied rates are candidate leading indicators of rate volatility over months. They would stop working if central banks regained a no-trade-off regime, if JLN mean-reverted faster than its AR(1), or if the disagreement resolved quietly.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the authors)
- (reader) The half-life arithmetic does not match the rounded 0.98 coefficient; the true estimate is likely ~0.985.
- (reader) JLN is a smoothed conditional-volatility estimate, so its persistence is partly mechanical.
- (reader) The JLN>1 equity result is contemporaneous and cannot by itself be used as a timing signal.
- (reader) Trade-off months cluster in few episodes, so the t > 7 overstates the evidence.
- (reader) A testable rule for a macro book: scale up trend/macro allocations when JLN is high; the paper does not test this and argues against timing diversification.
