# Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)

**Authors:** Choong Tze Chua (Singapore Management University); Winston T. H. Koh (Singapore Management University); Krishna Ramaswamy (Wharton, University of Pennsylvania)

**Summary:** Do yield curve trades that bet on mean reversion of level, slope and curvature to the historical-average ('unconditional') curve earn excess payoffs? Method: cash-neutral, duration-weighted one-month trades on the Fama-Bliss zero curve 1973-2000, volatility-matched against a Lehman intermediate Treasury index and a cash-neutral S&P benchmark. Spread (2-B) and curvature (3-B) trades on adjacent maturities beat both benchmarks gross and, for 2-B, after costs, add alpha to an S&P holding, but the edge faded after the mid-1980s.

## Argument map

**Question:** Can a systematic trader earn reliable, risk-adjusted profits by betting that the US Treasury curve's level, slope and curvature return to their long-run averages, against the expectations-hypothesis view that such curve trades cannot pay consistently, and do these profits survive costs and add value to an equity portfolio?

**Thesis:** Local mean-reversion trades on adjacent-maturity spreads (2-B) and curvature (3-B) of the one-month forward Treasury curve earned 3-6x the risk-adjusted payoff of bond and equity benchmarks in 1973-2000, with 2-B still significant after costs; level trades did not work, and the edge shrank markedly after the 1980s. ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C03-3|C03-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C18-1|C18-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C20-2|C20-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-5|C21-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-5|C25-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C26-3|C26-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C26-4|C26-4]])

### Foundations (premises)
- The Treasury curve's level, slope and curvature are bounded and revert to a historical norm. _[empirical, stated; support: observation that rates, spreads and curvature stay in tight bounds here and abroad; mean-reverting term-structure models]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C07-1|C07-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C07-3|C07-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C04-1|C04-1]])
- The expectations hypothesis (which would rule out consistent curve-trade profits) does not hold empirically. _[empirical, stated; support: citations: Culbertson 1957, Hamburger-Platt 1975, Shiller-Campbell-Schoenholtz 1983, Mankiw-Miron 1986, CIR 1985]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C07-5|C07-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C08-1|C08-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C08-3|C08-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C08-4|C08-4]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C09-1|C09-1]])
- Maturity segments are partly segmented by preferred habitats/ALM constraints, so cross-sector arbitrage is incomplete. _[institutional, stated; support: intuition plus the location of the most profitable trade segments (offered after the results)]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C24-5|C24-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-1|C25-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-2|C25-2]])
- The expanding full-history average since 1964 is the right anchor ('unconditional curve') for each maturity. _[assumed, implicit; support: none beyond the mean-reversion premise; no alternative anchors tested]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C10-6|C10-6]])
- Comparing strategies at equal payoff volatility is a fair way to rank fixed income and equity strategies. _[assumed, stated; support: argued as addressing comparability of risk, holding period and credit]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C17-1b|C17-1b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C17-3|C17-3]])

### Mechanism chain
- 1. Local spread/curvature of the forward curve deviates from its long-run norm → Deviation is not arbitraged away quickly because investors stay in preferred maturity habitats (especially around the 12-month short/long-end boundary): ALM constraints restrict lenders/borrowers to sectors so they do not shift to exploit forward vs expected rate gaps _[assertion]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-1|C25-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-2|C25-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C24-5|C24-5]])
- 2. Deviation persists only temporarily → Over the next month the forward-curve feature moves back toward the norm: some mean-reversion mechanism keeps the curve within bounds _[test]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C07-1|C07-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C24-4|C24-4]])
- 3. Duration-weighted, cash-neutral position on the deviation → Profit roughly proportional to the size of the deviation, insulated from parallel (and for curvature, slope) moves: equal dollar-duration legs isolate the targeted feature _[test]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C14-1|C14-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C15-6|C15-6]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C24-3|C24-3]])
- 4. Better information, liquidity, computing and telecoms → Faster arbitrage, shrinking excess returns since the mid/late 1980s: traders can rapidly seek out these opportunities _[assertion]_ ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C26-1|C26-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C25-5|C25-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C41-1|C41-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C42-1|C42-1]])

### Design rationale
- **Trade only strategies directly implied by mean reversion (6 strategies) rather than search**: avoid data snooping. Alternatives: none tested. Sensitivity: Bonferroni over 6 tests reported ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C04-3|C04-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C20-3|C20-3]])
- **Signal on the one-month forward curve vs expanding-mean unconditional curve**: holding period is one month, so the forward curve is the right comparison. Alternatives: not compared with spot-curve signals or rolling windows. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C10-4|C10-4]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C10-6|C10-6]])
- **Binary sign of deviation sets direction; size fixed (k), not scaled by deviation**: not given. Alternatives: threshold trading mentioned as a cost-saving idea. Sensitivity: not shown, though Figures 7-8 imply deviation-proportional sizing would matter ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C04-4|C04-4]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C22-1|C22-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C24-3|C24-3]])
- **Duration weighting k/(X-1) per leg and cash at 1-month tenor**: equal payoff contribution per unit move; isolate level/slope/curvature; 1-month cash adds no duration. Alternatives: none. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C11-1|C11-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C12-2|C12-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C13-3|C13-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C15-2|C15-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C15-6|C15-6]])
- **Adjacent-segment portfolios (2-B, 3-B) vs single whole-curve trades (2-A, 3-A)**: consider whole curve and individual portions. Alternatives: 2-A/3-A whole-curve versions. Sensitivity: local versions far better: 2-B 5.7x vs 2-A 0.37x bond benchmark ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C05-4b|C05-4b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C29-1|C29-1]])
- **Primary-only vs including interpolated maturities**: compare structures; curvature on interpolated points is zero. Alternatives: P and H. Sensitivity: 2-B nearly identical; level strategies worse with H ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C10-1|C10-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C16-1|C16-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C29-2b|C29-2b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C30-2|C30-2]])
- **102-month training period, evaluation Jan 1973-Dec 2000**: Lehman intermediate index starts Jan 1973. Alternatives: none. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C11-3|C11-3]])
- **Equal-volatility scaling using full-sample sd**: fair comparison; decisions remain out of sample. Alternatives: none. Sensitivity: not shown ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C17-3|C17-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C17-4|C17-4]])
- **Cost model: half of 1bp yield spread times maturity, benchmarks cost-free**: Bloomberg spreads ~1bp or less; half-spread per trade. Alternatives: quarterly/threshold trading and derivatives mentioned but not tested. Sensitivity: 2-B survives; 3-B loses DM significance ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-1|C21-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-2|C21-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-4b|C21-4b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C22-1|C22-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C22-2b|C22-2b]])

### Evidence map
- 2-B and 3-B beat both benchmarks at equal volatility (2-B ~5x, 3-B ~3.3-3.8x) before costs. Tests: mean reversion of local spreads/curvature is exploitable (step 2). Rules out: pure expectations hypothesis zero-mean excess returns for these trades in 1973-2000. Does not rule out: (reader) compensation for a risk not captured by S&P/Lehman benchmarks (e.g. liquidity or crash risk in short-end segments); artefacts of Fama-Bliss bill interpolation at 1-12 months; full-sample vol scaling. Strength: strong - t up to 11.8, DM p 0.001 for 2-B ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C18-3|C18-3]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C18-4|C18-4]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C19-1|C19-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C30-1|C30-1]])
- Level reversion (1-A, 1-B) and whole-curve slope (2-A) fail; whole-curve curvature (3-A) not significant. Tests: which aspect reverts at a 1-month horizon. Rules out: simple level mean-reversion as a source of profit. Does not rule out: (reader) level reversion at longer horizons. Strength: moderate - point estimates only for level strategies ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C18-1|C18-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C29-1|C29-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C29-2b|C29-2b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C20-2|C20-2]])
- 2-B remains significant net of costs (DM p 0.0102/0.0135); 3-B only on t-tests. Tests: economic exploitability. Rules out: that 2-B gains are purely a cost illusion at 0.5bp half-spread. Does not rule out: (reader) higher realised costs for 1-2 month bills or market impact; historical spreads in the 1970s-80s far wider than the 2003 Bloomberg numbers used. Strength: moderate - cost assumption from 2003 applied to 1973-2000 ([[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-2|C21-2]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C21-5|C21-5]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C31-1|C31-1]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C05-6b|C05-6b]], [[QP06 - Profiting from Mean-Reverting Yield Curve Trading Strategies (Chua, Koh, Ramaswamy)#^QP06-C06-1|C06-1]])

Does not rule out: (reader) mechanical relation from interpolation/measurement noise in the data (noise in one month's curve reverses next month); the chosen triple differs between text and figure. ### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Adjacent-maturity forward spreads and three-point curvatures on the 1-month forward Treasury curve, measured against their long-run (expanding) means, predict the next month's change in those spreads/curvatures (step 2 of the chain); the larger the gap the larger the expected move. The signal works through slow cross-sector arbitrage between short-end (bill) and longer note habitats. It stops working when arbitrage capital, liquidity and technology close gaps quickly (as seen after the late 1980s), when trading costs exceed ~0.01% of value traded, or when curve 'deviations' are mostly data noise. Level deviations do not predict at one month.
