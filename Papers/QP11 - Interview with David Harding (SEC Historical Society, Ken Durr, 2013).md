# Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)

**Authors:** David Harding (founder AHL, Winton; interviewee); Ken Durr (SEC Historical Society; interviewer)

**Summary:** Question: how did David Harding build AHL and Winton and what does he believe about markets? Method: oral-history interview (18 June 2013) covering career, trend-following origins, research method, CTA industry, 2008, equities expansion. Conclusion: markets trend and efficient-market theory is wrong; a diversified rule-based trend system plus empirical research earned returns, but no system works forever and crowding/capacity erodes it, so he diversifies into more markets.

## Argument map

**Question:** Why does David Harding believe markets are not efficient and that systematic trend following works, how does he think research and risk should be run, and what limits does he see? For an investor: is trend following a durable, scalable source of return and how should a systematic manager be organised?

**Thesis:** Prices of whole asset classes trend because markets are a social system without fixed laws; simple, diversified, rule-based trend following captures this at very high capacity if you avoid over-leverage and stick with it, but no system works forever, so edge must be renewed by a continuous empirical research programme and further diversification as money crowds in. - 2. Persistent trends → simple breakout / multi-horizon trend rules make money: rules buy new highs and sell new lows, matching chart experts' signals _[example]_ ([[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C14-1|C14-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C15-1|C15-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C17-1|C17-1]], [[QP11 - Interview with David Harding (SEC Historical Society, Ken Durr, 2013)#^QP11-C27-2|C27-2]])

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Past price trends of whole asset classes (N-day breakouts, multi-horizon trend) lead returns over weeks to months via slow adjustment (step 1-2); breadth across ~100 futures and more markets raises reliability (step 3). Secondary inputs: seasonality, relative-value spreads (soy-corn, hog-corn, oil-gas), bond-equity relation, and widening credit spreads as a lead on equity tops by months. These should weaken as industry AUM and crowding grow (step 5), in sharp reversals (2009), and when over-levered.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the authors)
- (reader) 'frame days' is most likely a transcription of a specific lookback (e.g. 'twenty days'); the rule is a Donchian channel breakout.
- (reader) 'high short ratio' means high Sharpe ratio: Harding concedes trend following has modest risk-adjusted returns and sells it on capacity and diversification.
- (reader) The credit-leads-equity sequence in 2007 suggests credit spreads as a conditioning input for equity trend exposure, which Harding does not state as a rule.
- (reader) His crowding argument implies monitoring CTA industry AUM and inter-CTA correlation as a capacity signal.
- (reader) The interview is an oral history (2013), not a research paper; all numbers are recalled and unaudited.
