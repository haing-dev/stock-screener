# 5% Rates, Bessent's Failed Gamble & What Comes Next w- Krishna Guha - The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 78

**Speakers:** Steve Eisman (host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Krishna Guha (Evercore ISI, Fed watcher)

**Summary:** Eisman interviews Evercore's Krishna Guha on Fed chair Walsh's rate hike, QE and balance sheet views, forward guidance, and Bessent's Treasury buybacks. Guha attributes the 10-year at ~5.25% to AI/government issuance, higher neutral rate and oil, not Walsh. Main risk: stocks and consumer wealth effect at 5%+ yields.

**Approach:** fed watching, rates, central bank policy, bond market, macro discretionary

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Krishna Guha argues that the 10-year Treasury yield has jumped to about 5.25% because of four fundamentals (AI hyperscaler debt issuance on top of a 6% of GDP deficit, a higher neutral rate from the AI investment boom, an oil and diesel shock after the US-Iran ceasefire broke down, and uncertainty about how far the Fed will hike), amplified by the new Fed chair Kevin Walsh's removal of forward-guidance guard rails and by a failed Treasury buyback attempt to cap yields. At 5%+ the non-AI economy is already crowded out (housing first) while the AI half is less rate sensitive, so the key risk is whether equities and the wealth-funded consumer can withstand yields near 5.25%. Eisman, the host, adds that daily markets simply follow oil and the 10-year and thinks QE only inflated asset prices.
