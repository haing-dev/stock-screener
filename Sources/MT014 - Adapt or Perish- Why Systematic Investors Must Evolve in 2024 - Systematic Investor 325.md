# Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325

**Speakers:** Graham Robertson (co-host, Man AHL/Man Institute, London); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged; captioned 'Neil costr Larson')

**Summary:** Top Traders Unplugged Systematic Investor 325 (Dec 2024): Robertson and Kaastrup-Larsen review 2024 trend following, covering US-election positioning, why medium-term models lagged, Yenmageddon and short vol spikes, quality long-short as a complement, rate-cut oscillation ('harmonic frequency of death'), and how many markets/alternative markets help. Conclusions: trend is neutral to very sharp vol spikes, protection must be paid for, and non-predictive adaptive strategies suit an unpredictable US administration.

**Approach:** trend following, crisis alpha, volatility, speed of models, diversification, alternative markets, central bank rates

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.95 - very thorough first pass (67 claims); only missed the buy-protection-after-spikes behaviour, concentration as the source of big trend gains and the Treasury-pick effect; some speaker slips and two duplicates.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Graham Robertson reviews 2024 for trend following: US assets drove returns, trend was already positioned for the bond selloff before the election, and medium-speed models and bond/FX trends suffered because rate-cut expectations oscillated on roughly the same 3-4 month cycle as trend holding periods ('harmonic frequency of death'), a sensitivity he expects to be transient. Trend is neutral to very sharp volatility spikes (Yenmageddon) and protection from them has to be paid for, with a layered defence of long volatility, quality long-short equity and trend. With the largest economy more unpredictable, both speakers favour non-predictive adaptive strategies and argue investors hold too little trend; alternative markets diversify because they have different price drivers, not because they trend better.

**Chain: Why 2024 bond and FX trends whipsawed (the 'harmonic frequency of death')**
1. Central-bank rate-cut pricing oscillates (six cuts, then two, then ten by end of September, then reversal) → Bond and FX trends reverse every 3-4 months: narrative flips between will they and won't they ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C10-1|C10-1]])
2. Medium-term trend holding periods of 2-6 months (average 3-4 months) → Positions built over about 3 months are caught by the next reversal: the oscillation period matches the holding period ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C10-2|C10-2]], [[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C06-1|C06-1]])
3. Start of a new easing cycle, fixed income closely tied to FX and the dollar → Unusually high sensitivity of trend to central-bank expectations: everyone is watching the next cycle; correlations between fixed income markets are high ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C10-3|C10-3]])
4. Regional dispersion (Europe versus US) grows → The single central-bank factor weakens and the headwind fades: different economic cycles break the common driver ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C10-4|C10-4]])
- Evidence: Man Institute paper on central-bank sensitivity; 2024 desk observation; headlines hid the range-bound bond and FX markets that hurt trend.
- Fails when: Multiple models and speeds run in parallel so the oscillation hits on average only; the end-of-headwind view is hopeful and not tested.

**Chain: A three-layer defence for sudden shocks**
1. A very short, sharp volatility spike (Yenmageddon, three days) → Trend neutral and volatility filters barely change positions in the first days: trend needs a sustained move; scaling responds only after volatility has risen ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C06-5|C06-5]], [[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C08-1|C08-1]], [[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C09-2|C09-2]])
2. Flight to quality in a sudden selloff → Quality long-short equity gains (SVB 2023, August 2024): by definition quality outperforms in flight to quality ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C07-1|C07-1]], [[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C07-2|C07-2]])
3. Layering long volatility first (costly), quality second (short bursts), trend third (sustained crises) → Protection across time scales, at the price of a carry hit for the long-volatility layer: each layer covers a different horizon ([[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C08-3|C08-3]], [[MT014 - Adapt or Perish- Why Systematic Investors Must Evolve in 2024 - Systematic Investor 325#^MT014-C08-X1|C08-X1]])
- Evidence: Man Institute analysis; 17 quality metrics; two out-of-sample episodes.
- Fails when: Crisis from an equity all-time high: trend and a stocks-plus-trend mix are both long equities and take losses; long volatility bleeds if bought after spikes.

**Decision process**
- [portfolio] Run multiple models and multiple frequencies; keep an adaptive, non-predictive strategy in the portfolio.. - [portfolio] Combine trend with quality long-short equity for short sharp spikes; use long volatility only if prepared to pay the carry.. ### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Robertson cites central-bank pricing (rate-cut expectations), US-Europe dispersion, the sign and level of cross-asset correlations (fixed income to FX to dollar), pairwise correlation surges in crises, and positioning (trend long equities from highs) as drivers of what trend earns next; horizon is 3-4 months for rate-expectation cycles. Niels adds single-stock correlation (low as a warning), US equity concentration and election-year or populist-period equity behaviour. Both argue the largest economy's unpredictability favours adaptive, non-predictive strategies.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Robertson's 'harmonic frequency' idea links a macro driver (central-bank narrative cycles) to a model parameter (holding period). It predicts that trend underperforms whenever the dominant macro narrative flips at roughly the same frequency as positions, independent of the direction of rates.
- (reader) Both speakers treat sudden volatility spikes as outside the opportunity set for trend and rely on separate layers (quality, long volatility) for protection; this implies a trend allocation does not replace short-horizon tail hedges.
- (reader) Their agreement that more trend should be held rests on tracking-error and complexity arguments, not new return evidence; it is a conclusion about client behaviour and sizing as much as about trend itself.
