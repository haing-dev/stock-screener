# Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309

**Speakers:** Katy Kaminski (chief research strategist, AlphaSimplex, guest co-host); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Katy Kaminski and Niels Kaastrup-Larsen review the August 2024 yen carry unwind and trend-following performance. Main points: bonds misbehave (positive stock-bond correlation, higher vol) when inflation is higher, long-short trend diversifies a 60/40, and crisis alpha and carry are tested only by sustained shocks.

**Approach:** trend following, managed futures, crisis alpha, carry, stock-bond correlation, volatility/VIX

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.90 - the first reader caught nearly every mechanism and number; misses were secondary points (inflation-volatility agreement incl. deflation, Japan crowding, investor inexperience, stress periods as tests, geopolitics).

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Katy Kaminski argues that bonds 'behave badly' when inflation is higher (stock-bond correlation turns positive and bond volatility rises from about 4% to about 6%), so a static 60/40 loses its safety leg, whereas long-short trend in bonds, dollar, gold and commodities diversifies equity-down periods. With the host she reads the August 2024 yen carry unwind as a test of convergent strategies (carry) in a more divergent, less policy-synchronised world, and stresses that crisis alpha needs sustained moves, is not guaranteed, and is diluted by carry sleeves and by too-slow signals in very quick shocks. Both advise sticking to process rather than reacting to headline numbers such as a VIX of 65.

**Worldview premises**
- When inflation is higher the stock-bond correlation turns positive and bond volatility rises (about 4% on average to about 6% in high-inflation regimes), so bonds stop being the safety trade and a 60/40 portfolio is less stable. _[empirical, stated; support: Estimated over decades of data in her bonds-and-inflation paper; 2022 as the recent example.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-2|C06-2]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C08-1|C08-1]])
- In equity-down periods bond returns are right-shifted with a thin left tail when inflation is low, but negative on average, wider and fat-tailed to the left when inflation is high. _[empirical, stated; support: Conditional return distribution plot in the paper (described verbally).]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C08-2|C08-2]])
- Inflation tends to come in waves rather than disappear, so a smooth fall in CPI is not proof that bonds are safe again. _[empirical, stated; support: General knowledge of inflation episodes ('anybody who studies inflation knows').]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-4|C06-4]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C07-X3|C07-X3]])
- Most investors have never invested through inflation (the 1970s), so they are surprised and frustrated when bonds do not hedge equities. _[behavioural, stated; support: Observation about investor experience.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-X2|C06-X2]])
- When central banks stop moving in sync, there are multiple macro narratives and so more tactical, dynamic opportunity, which favours active management after 2020. _[theoretical, stated; support: Argument from divergence across the Fed, ECB, Bank of Canada and others; no data cited.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C02-1|C02-1]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C05-3|C05-3]])
- Convergence strategies (carry is the classic one) assume things return to normal and are vulnerable to unexpected shocks; divergent thinkers ask where the market could go next. _[theoretical, stated; support: Covid and the August 2024 yen carry unwind as examples.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C02-3|C02-3]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C03-1|C03-1]])
- Crisis alpha means gain during sustained market losses lasting weeks to months; a correction that reverses quickly is not a crisis and trend followers are too slow to benefit from it. _[theoretical, stated; support: Her own definition (she coined the term); trading frequency of trend programs.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C10-3|C10-3]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C10-5|C10-5]])
- Crisis alpha is something trend searches for, not a guaranteed hedge or put option; it appears only when the world moves in a drastically different direction. _[theoretical, stated; support: Title and thesis of 'In Search of Crisis Alpha'.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C12-1|C12-1]])
- A static 60/40 cannot adapt when moves are not all long, and long-run averages hide decade-long gaps in what works (gold, for example). _[theoretical, stated; support: Argument from the structure of long-only portfolios; gold timing never in sync with recessions or inflation.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C10-1|C10-1]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C10-2|C10-2]])
- Big, sudden moves are hard to predict or trade; not overreacting is usually best, and extreme one-day moves can only be caught by a strategy that bears the cost on all other days. _[experience, stated; support: Those who sold at Monday's low missed the rebound; the 'falling knife' point.]_ ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C15-4|C15-4]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C15-5|C15-5]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C11-X4|C11-X4]])

**Chain: Inflation turns bonds from hedge to risk asset, damaging 60/40**
1. Higher inflation regime → Stock-bond correlation turns positive: Bonds fall when equities fall, so they are no longer the safety trade. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C08-2|C08-2]])
2. Higher inflation regime → Bond volatility rises (about 4% to about 6%): Bonds move around more, so the 60/40 'stool' is both unreliable and unstable. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-2|C06-2]])
3. Bonds fail as hedge and are marked to market → Locked-in yield does not protect (2022): Valuation changes matter especially in a flight-to-safety scenario; entry timing and rolling add volatility. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C08-1|C08-1]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C07-4|C07-4]])
4. Inflation arrives in waves and may be volatile → Fixed income volatility stays high even while CPI falls: Another supply or geopolitical shock can push inflation up again, or it can slide into deflation. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C06-4|C06-4]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C07-X3|C07-X3]])
- Evidence: Paper estimated over decades of data plus the 2022 episode; the effect of inflation volatility was not tested.
- Fails when: Inflation is low (bonds then give the classic right-shifted flight-to-safety distribution).

**Chain: Signal speed, trend adaptation and why crisis alpha is scarce in quick shocks**
1. Recent price moves leak information about a turning theme → Faster trend models adjust before the shock: Long-term models stay anchored on the early-year moves. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C04-1|C04-1]])
2. Long-term (12-month) momentum has the higher Sharpe ratio → It dominates in practice: Smoother, higher Sharpe in a world that was converging. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C04-2|C04-2]])
3. Less converging, more divergent world with larger and faster moves → Shorter signals may work better again: SG Trend Index deconstruction shows CTAs used faster models in 2000; Covid and the reflation-then-collapse in commodities favour shorter horizons. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C04-3|C04-3]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C04-4|C04-4]])
4. Shock reverses within days → No crisis alpha for trend: Trend trades medium- to long-term themes; first responders (short-term trading, options) profit when VIX spikes but are hard to capture, second responders change positions once a shift is visible. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C10-5|C10-5]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C11-2|C11-2]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C11-3|C11-3]])
- Evidence: Index deconstruction she co-authored and the 2024 episode; no signal-speed backtest cited.
- Fails when: Shorter signals are whipsawed in a quick rebound; she says the value of trend lies in prolonged crises.

**Chain: Long-short trend as a 60/40 diversifier**
1. Long-only add-ons (bonds, gold, commodities) have poor correlation in equity-down periods → They add correlation and volatility to the 60/40: Diversification depends on correlation and on added volatility. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C09-2|C09-2]])
2. Make positions long or short by trend and manage volatility → Negative correlation with equities in down periods (bonds, dollar, gold) and flat for commodities: The portfolio can be on the right side in either direction. - Evidence: Paper results on trend correlation in equity-down periods (no numbers given).
- Fails when: Not stated.

**Chain: Carry inside CTA portfolios waters down crisis alpha**
1. Pure trend struggled for years → Managers added carry and other convergent strategies to smooth returns: Investors want consistency; her CTA Evolution study finds carry increasingly present in CTA returns. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C12-3|C12-3]])
2. Violent, short event (Covid, August 2024) → Carry sleeves lose, pure and faster trend copes better: Carry is convergent; it is most dangerous run relative-value with leverage, less so directional with controlled leverage. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C12-5|C12-5]], [[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C13-1|C13-1]])
3. Large carry allocation → Less ability to capture crisis alpha: Carry is great until it isn't. ([[MT021 - Bonds Behaving Badly - Systematic Investor 309#^MT021-C13-2|C13-2]])
- Evidence: Her CTA Evolution paper and a Covid-crisis paper; the Nordic CTA loss is the host's speculation.
- Fails when: Carry sleeve is small or unlevered directional (less damage).

- [signal] Core trend signal is classic 12-month momentum, long or short; she leans toward considering shorter signals in a more divergent regime.. ### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Inputs they treat as leading prices: the inflation regime (level, and possibly its volatility) for the sign of the stock-bond correlation and bond volatility; divergence of central bank policy (BoJ hikes versus Fed cuts) for yen carry and equity shock risk; trend signal mix (equity versus bond and commodity direction) as a read on recession-type positioning; the speed of the market move (days versus weeks to months) for whether trend can earn crisis alpha; crowding in carry and Japan-related positions. Horizons are weeks to months for crisis alpha and trend, years for the inflation-bond relationship.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Kaminski's framework gives one switch for bond hedging, the inflation regime, but no threshold or lead time; usable only with an external inflation-regime definition.
- (reader) Both speakers treat convergent (carry, short-volatility-like) exposure as the main tail risk in the August 2024 event, but neither shows that carry exposure predicted the size of the unwind.
- (reader) The crisis alpha argument implies a testable horizon condition: trend gains should show up only when equity drawdowns last weeks to months, and be absent for sub-week reversals.
- (reader) Niels's remark that vol-targeting changes positions even when prices revert implies trend books de-risk after volatility spikes, which can lag a rebound.
