# CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329

**Speakers:** Andrew Beer (DBi, replication strategies); Tom Roble (Societe Generale Prime Services, CTA indices); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, hedge fund manager)

**Summary:** Top Traders Unplugged Systematic Investor 329 (Jan 2025): SG CTA indices, replication-based managed futures versus real CTAs, AUM trends, BlackRock's ETF filing, bonds' failure as diversifier. Conclusions: replication gives cheap, noisy exposure with a compounding cost edge; trend is commoditized; bonds failing and information framing drive diversifier demand.

**Approach:** trend following, CTA replication, managed futures indices, portable alpha

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.87 - speaker attribution in the unlabelled captions is right except one claim (C21-2); first reader caught all main arguments but missed a few caveats (fee data, index evolution counterpoint, replicability limits, stated vs revealed demand).

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Systematic Investor 329 (early Jan 2025) debates how to access and judge managed futures: Andrew Beer (DBi) argues a mechanical weekly factor replication of the SG CTA Index delivers the index's diversification at lower cost and suits wealth managers, who need an index-like, investable product; Tom Roble (SocGen) explains the SG indices and notes that CTA edge lies in many idiosyncratic markets, risk management and implementation that a 10-instrument replicator cannot fully capture. Beer's second argument is that trend following should be sold as a way of extracting changing information (a 3% completion allocation, not a hedge-fund-bucket holding), and that BlackRock's managed-futures ETF could validate and scale the category.

**Chain: How weekly factor replication tracks a CTA index**
1. Recent reported index returns → regression on about 10 liquid markets (S&P 500, 10-year Treasury, etc.) each Monday: Estimates broad factor exposures of the index over a one-week interval. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C06-2|C06-2]])
2. Estimated exposures → positions for the next week targeting pre-trading-cost index returns: Fees vary across investors; the target avoids headline-fee noise. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C07-1|C07-1]])
3. Low trading cost (10-20bp) and lower fees → small weekly edge that compounds to outperformance in most years: 53-54% weekly hit rate becomes ~80% annual and ~90% three-year. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C07-2|C07-2]], [[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C09-3|C09-3]])
4. Large moves in oil or gold → other markets pulled along (planets and moons): Holds some exposure to markets not traded. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C10-4|C10-4]])
- Evidence: Nine years of live record; correlation 0.9 with full factor set, 0.8 limited set; crisis alpha and beta similar to the index.
- Fails when: Many untraded markets (Mexican peso, Canadian rates, softs, commodities) trend at once; sharp inflections like SVB where it lags by several hundred bp before catching up within months.

**Chain: Why replication lags at inflections but catches up**
1. Sharp inflection (e.g. SVB) → replication underperforms by several hundred bp: A weekly model reacts slowly. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C11-4|C11-4]])
2. Half of inflections are head fakes → short-term models get wrong-footed and replication catches up in months: Faster models pay the whipsaw cost; by end-September replication had caught up. ([[MT028 - CTAs- Innovation Meets Legacy & the BlackRock Effect - Systematic Investor 329#^MT028-C11-4|C11-4]])
- Evidence: SVB 2023 episode.
- Fails when: When an inflection continues (credit squeeze following) the lag is larger.
