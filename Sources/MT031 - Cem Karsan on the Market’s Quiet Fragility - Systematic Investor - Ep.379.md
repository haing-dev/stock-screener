# Cem Karsan on the Market’s Quiet Fragility - Systematic Investor - Ep.379

**Speakers:** Cem Karsan (guest, options/vol strategist and wealth manager; called Jim in captions); Niels Kaastrup Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Karsan, an options/vol strategist, argues cash and margin positioning, record valuations and vol dynamics make markets fragile, and that a structural-inflation, populist regime (1960s-70s analog) favours long vol in metals, FX and bonds. He criticises 60/40 and VIX as a fear gauge, and cites election and midterm patterns in populist periods.

**Approach:** options/vol positioning, structural inflation regime, risk management, election-year seasonality, trend following update

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Cem Karsan argues that record-low cash, record margin debt and record valuations make equities fragile, that the market is in a late-bubble phase where long right-tail volatility pays, and that a structural-inflation, populist regime like 1962-82 should favour long volatility in precious metals, FX and bonds and short or lower volatility in long-dated equities. He claims 60/40 only worked because rates fell from 1982, urges risk-adjusted diversification with leverage, and applies the populist-period pattern to presidential-election and midterm years (2026 midterm window carries drawdown risk). Host Niels Kaastrup-Larsen adds survey crowding data, a drawdown-based view of tail risk, the Future Fund resilience definition, and the point that passive flows and volatility suppression could overwhelm the political pressure.
