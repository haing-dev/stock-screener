# Crafting the Perfect Investment Portfolio in 2025 - Systematic Investor 331

**Speakers:** Alan Dunne (co-host, macro); Niels Kaastro Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Niels Kaastro Larsen and Alan Dunne cover Germany's slump, why US long yields rose while the Fed cut (real yields, term premium, fiscal worry), and January 2025 trend-following results. They then review AQR papers (Asness 2035 retrospective, Broad Strategic Asset Allocation) and Howard Marks' bubble note. Conclusions: portfolios rely on correlations from the last 20-30 years, constraints (leverage, career risk, complexity) explain underallocation to alternatives, and trend following is the 'brakes' that lets investors hold equity risk.

**Approach:** trend following, macro commentary, asset allocation, bond yield decomposition, risk mitigation

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.91 - first reader covered nearly every macro and allocation idea; missed a few conditions (late-90s easy Fed, rising-rate caveat, leverage result), the trend barometer reading and Niels' debt scepticism; two attribution/number errors.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Alan Dunne reads Germany's slump as the combined result of external shocks (cheap Russian gas lost, China moving from customer to competitor) and domestic missteps, and reads the rise in US long yields while the Fed cut as mostly higher real yields (recession priced out) plus a rising term premium on deficit worries, a fiscal challenge rather than a credit-bubble crisis. With Niels Kaastro Larsen he then reviews AQR's 'Broad Strategic Asset Allocation', Asness's 2035 retrospective and Howard Marks's bubble note, concluding that portfolios lean on correlations from the last 20-30 years, that constraints (leverage, career and benchmark risk, complexity) explain underallocation to alternatives, and that trend following works as the 'brakes' that let investors hold equity risk and guard against unknown unknowns; Dunne adds that a robust view favours more risk mitigation than AQR's mean-variance blend.
