# Debt, Inflation & Fed Credibility- The Three Market Fractures - Systematic Investor - Ep.415

**Speakers:** Alan Dunne (co-host, macro); Niels Kaastro Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Niels Kaastro Larsen and Alan Dunne (August 2026) cover the US yen intervention, Fed credibility under Warsh, rising US yields, and August trend-following results. Dunne's 'three fractures' (sticky inflation, debt sustainability and fiscal dominance, institutional erosion) imply bonds are a weaker equity diversifier, and trend has earned most in stress periods this decade. They also compare AQR and GMO capital market assumptions and argue trend remains competitive when cash rates are 3.5-4%.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Alan Dunne argues that the macro regime has fractured in three ways since 2020: sticky inflation (stocks and bonds now positively correlated), debt sustainability combined with high equity valuations (a path to fiscal dominance and financial repression), and erosion of institutional norms (pressure on the Fed, tinkering in bond issuance). Signs that Treasury and the Fed are sensitive to rising yields (the US yen intervention, Bessent's bond actions, Warsh's reduced guidance) mean bonds are a weaker equity diversifier, and trend following has earned its decade of returns mainly in the stress periods when these fractures bit. Niels Kaastrup-Larsen adds that trend avoids the timing problem of macro narratives, while both argue that with cash at 3.5 to 4% even a low-Sharpe trend allocation is competitive with the AQR and GMO equity and bond forecasts.
