**Speakers:** Brad Gerstner (Altimeter Capital); Bill Gurley (Benchmark, venture investor)

**Summary:** BG2 Ep14, Aug 2024: Gerstner explains cutting equity risk from 9 to 3 units as skew deteriorated; discusses recession signals, Fed cuts, AI capex vs revenue, yen carry unwind, Google ruling, gen-AI engagement data and AI take-under deals.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Brad Gerstner sizes net public-equity exposure by the skew of expected outcomes (like bet sizing in poker): 9 units of risk in January 2023 (80% chance of +50% vs 20% of -10%) became 3 units by July 2024 (80% chance of 10-20% downside) as prices hit highs, earnings estimates stopped rising and five pressure forces (2025 personal tax cliff, earnings misses/slowing, AI air pocket, yen carry unwind, Middle East) piled up. He reads the August 2024 selloff as healthy consolidation, not a crash, argues the Fed is late and rate cuts alone do not rescue equities, and that near-term AI capex is supported by hyperscaler statements. Bill Gurley adds a supply-versus-demand warning from boom-bust hardware cycles, a conjecture that incumbents spend to deny capital to independent labs, a skeptical view of the Google ruling, and a reflexivity reading of the yen carry unwind.
