## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Andrew Beer (Dynamic Beta Investments, manager of the DBMF replication ETF) argues that CTA alpha comes from a few big cross-asset trends caught early, so a complex model is needed to detect the signal but a second, simpler implementation step (fewer, more liquid contracts) captures it with lower costs; his evidence is the replication fund's outperformance of the 20 original SG CTA index constituents and the finding that simple CTA ETFs matched or beat more complex hedge funds, mutual funds and UCITS. He contends that implementation costs rise geometrically with the number of positions, that bank QIS trend products lose about 90% of their backtest Sharpe live, and that swap-based wrappers and tax efficiency can save hundreds of basis points. On markets (April 2026), he sees the Iran-conflict shock as an unhedgeable, ideology-driven geopolitical regime in which CTAs held up (SG CTA +7.5% in Q1) while March reversals hit the whole smart-money space, not CTAs alone. The host Niels adds a neutral trend barometer (48), the survivorship and 'quant on top of quant' objections, and cost-disclosure comparisons.
