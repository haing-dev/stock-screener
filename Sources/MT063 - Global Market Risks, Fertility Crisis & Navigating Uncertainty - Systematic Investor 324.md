# Global Market Risks, Fertility Crisis & Navigating Uncertainty - Systematic Investor 324

**Speakers:** Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged); Rich Brennan (co-host, trend follower)

**Summary:** Weekly Systematic Investor podcast. Rich Brennan applies weather ensemble forecasting to trend following (a diverse ensemble of models helps in outlier regimes, hurts in calm), argues risk is vulnerability not variance and that convexity and a barbell protect against hidden risk, and discusses falling fertility as a self-correcting cycle.

**Approach:** trend following, systematic macro, risk management, convexity, ensemble models, tail risk

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.85 - first reader captured the ensemble-model, hidden-risk, convexity and fertility arguments thoroughly; missed mostly Ukraine and ruble side-views, the election-reaction datum, gold's 21%, short-term CTA index data, diminishing returns of model count, the Man diversification point and the VaR-versus-stops framing.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Rich Brennan argues that markets, like weather, are mostly regime-based and predictable but periodically chaotic, so trend followers should handle uncertainty with an ensemble of models (which helps when outliers appear and hurts when they do not), and should define risk as vulnerability rather than historical variance: calm periods accumulate hidden fragility that VaR, Sharpe and volatility targeting miss, and the antidote is positive convexity through a barbell. Niels Kaastrup-Larsen adds that trend following is a core allocation whose demand fades after strong equity years, that trend followers know 'when' not 'why', that trend methodologies differ mainly in exits and risk management, and that large managers favour VaR-type small adjustments partly because of market footprint. The episode ends with an off-topic reading of falling fertility as a self-correcting cycle that calls for adaptive rather than static planning.

**Chain: Why an ensemble of trend models should help in chaotic regimes and hurt in calm ones**
1. A single trend model's entry and stop parameters → A narrow price path inside which the trend stays valid: Constraints define when a trend ends for that model; chaotic paths often leave the corridor. ([[MT063 - Global Market Risks, Fertility Crisis & Navigating Uncertainty - Systematic Investor 324#^MT063-C07-3|C07-3]])
2. Ensemble of short, medium and long models → More freedom of movement; some models fail, the balance succeeds: No ex-ante knowledge of the trajectory; less prescriptive, more probabilistic. ([[MT063 - Global Market Risks, Fertility Crisis & Navigating Uncertainty - Systematic Investor 324#^MT063-C08-1|C08-1]])
3. Outlier (regime transition) persists → More models pile into the trend as it matures, nonlinear payoff: Number of models in the position rises with trend age, concentrating risk on the winner by rule, not hindsight. ### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Inputs the speakers treat as leading prices are thin: they say trend followers have no view on the future, and read the price signal itself ('when' not 'why'). The stated forward-looking cues are: presence or absence of outliers/regime transitions (to decide how many trend models to run), signs of accumulating hidden fragility (rising leverage, concentration, cheap options, compressed volatility) as warnings rather than timing signals, and divergence among model forecasts (ensemble spread) as a measure of uncertainty. Horizons are multi-month trend lengths for models and multi-year for fragility; geopolitical views (sanctions, Ukraine settlement before the US administration changes) are narrative, not rule-based.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Rich's 10-versus-20 result implies a testable conditional rule: payoff to adding models should rise with the frequency of extreme moves across markets; this could be checked on a trend-model panel.
- (reader) Niels's observation that trend methods differ mostly in exits and risk management implies that cross-manager return dispersion should correlate more with stop/risk rules than with lookback choice; 2024's wide dispersion with common outliers is consistent but not shown.
- (reader) Rich's hidden-risk argument makes the concrete prediction that option implied volatility and leverage indicators lead tail events, but he offers no rule or lead time, so it functions as a risk-budgeting stance, not a signal.
