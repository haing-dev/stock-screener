# Harding Rewrites Winton's Quant Playbook in Search for Returns

**Speakers:** David Harding (founder, Winton); interviewer (Bloomberg Television)

**Summary:** Bloomberg TV (Mar 2019) interview with Winton founder David Harding on why systematic strategies struggle. Trend following in futures became crowded, near-zero rates cut hedge fund returns, so Winton shifted to equity event, organizational-metric and technical strategies across 7000 stocks. Warns government data are unreliable and trend following is not a crash hedge.

**Approach:** trend following, systematic quant, equity event strategies, crowding, research method

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.94 - first reader captured essentially all mechanisms, research findings and risk methods accurately; missed only the no-discretionary-intervention governance rule and the reason redemption terms were lengthened (asset liquidity)

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** David Harding (Winton) argues that every successful strategy attracts capital until its edge decays, so trend following in futures, once a Sharpe-near-1 machine for 30 years, has become crowded beta and a firm must keep innovating: Winton is moving weight into a 7,000-stock global equity book (event, organizational-metric and customised technical strategies) built on cloud computing and long research cycles. He holds that markets are an evolving ecosystem rather than efficient atoms in a box, that government macro data are too heavily revised to trade on, that trend following is not a dependable crash hedge, and that leverage was cut because crowding plus fat tails creates catastrophe risk.

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Harding names no short-horizon predictive indicator. The inputs he treats as leading future returns are: how crowded a strategy is (money in it, whether it is viewed as beta), interest rate level (cash component of returns), leverage and fat-tail risk from crowding, and whether time series are clean enough to prove an effect (government data often are not). Horizons are years to decades; he stresses that no month or year judges a manager.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Harding's crowding thesis predicts that returns of an openly published, easily replicated rule (simple trend) trend toward cash-like or beta-like outcomes over time, which is testable by rolling Sharpe of managed-futures indices against assets under management.
- (reader) His claim that trend following is not a crash hedge implies it should be treated as a convex-in-slow-trends, not in fast-gap, instrument; the testable form is conditional returns on speed of equity drawdowns.
- (reader) The case for equity event and organizational-metric strategies versus futures trend is a diversification and capacity argument, not backed by reported returns in this interview.
- (reader) His distrust of revised macro data matches the point-in-time / first-release data problem in macro research.
