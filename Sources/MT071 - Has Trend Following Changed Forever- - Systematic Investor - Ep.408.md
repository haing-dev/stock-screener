# Has Trend Following Changed Forever- - Systematic Investor - Ep.408

**Speakers:** Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged, CTA background); Alan Dunne (co-host, allocator/researcher)

**Summary:** Systematic Investor ep. 408: Niels and Alan review H1 2026 CTA performance, the new Fed chair's end of forward guidance, two pro-trend papers (Aspect, Systematica), and the CFM paper attributing the decline of short-term trend following to volatility-adjusted tick size and microstructure. ## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Alan Dunne presents the CFM-linked paper arguing that the decline of fast trend following is a microstructure story: fast trend profited partly from the price momentum created by its own large orders being passed along dealer chains, and since about 2010 (high-frequency traders, banks no longer warehousing risk, small volatility-adjusted tick size) that mechanism survives only in large-tick markets such as bonds, rates and some commodities. Niels broadly agrees with the evidence (his own short-term models have been unprofitable for 15 years) but offers further causes (post-2009 regime, bank trading rules, multi-manager pod shops, execution algorithms), and both note the paper does not explain why fast trend improved after 2020. The episode also covers H1 2026 CTA performance, the new Fed chair's retreat from forward guidance (more volatility and longer rate trends), and two pro-trend allocation papers (Aspect, Systematica) arguing that bonds no longer diversify equities.
