# Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350

**Speakers:** Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged, CTA background); Alan Dunne (co-host, allocator/researcher, Substack Beyond the Cycle)

**Summary:** Systematic Investor ep. 350 (30 May 2025): Alan lays out why all roads lead to higher bond yields (US deficits and debt, R-minus-G, fiscal dominance, weaker bond diversification), then Niels and Alan review a weak May for trend followers, the SG Prime allocator survey, and asset allocation (scenario analysis, forward-looking return estimates, US re-rating). Conclusion: robust portfolios need non-predictive diversifiers such as trend following, and trend allocations should not be timed on macro views.

**Approach:** trend following, fiscal debt and bond yields, term premium, asset allocation, expected returns, allocator sentiment, CTA performance

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Alan Dunne argues that debt, deficits and policy trends over three decades point to structurally higher US bond yields ('all roads lead to higher yields'): bond-market discipline of fiscal policy has weakened, rates above growth make debt dynamics self-reinforcing, and most scenarios end in financial repression or fiscal dominance. For asset allocation he recommends scenario-based construction instead of anchoring on 1980-2020, and notes that forward-looking return estimates point to below-average equity returns. Niels Kaastrup-Larsen reviews a weak May 2025 for trend followers (medium-term models hit hardest) and argues that, because scenario selection can go wrong and unforeseen risks cannot be listed, a regime-agnostic, non-predictive trend allocation is useful and should not be timed on macro or political views; Alan agrees.

**Worldview premises**
- Bond-market discipline of US fiscal policy has weakened: in 1993 a deficit of about 4% of GDP and debt of 45% of GDP intimidated politicians, whereas today debt is about 100% of GDP (net of agency holdings) and the 2025 budget bill adds nearly 4 trillion over ten years. _[empirical, stated; support: 1993 Carville quote and numbers from memory; current debt/deficit figures.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C04-1|C04-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-1|C05-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-2|C05-2]])
- In the 1990s, deficit reduction (Clinton tax hikes) let the Fed ease and lowered yields, fuelling investment; the opposite fiscal stance today does not help. _[empirical, stated; support: Greenspan-Clinton conversation as recounted; a 1990s productivity boom helped as well.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-3|C05-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-X1|C05-X1]])
- From 2017 the US ran large deficits at full employment (a procyclical turning point), followed by MMT ideas and huge Covid spending; the end of the neoliberal era removed ideological appetite for cutting deficits. _[empirical, stated; support: Chronology of US fiscal policy 2017-2025; guest Gary Gerstle cited.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-2|C06-2]])
- High public debt levels are here to stay (Eichengreen); the 2021-22 inflation eroded real debt, but disinflation reverses this, so the debt burden rises again. _[theoretical, stated; support: Jackson Hole paper by Barry Eichengreen and a conversation with him.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C04-X1|C04-X1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-3|C06-3]])
- When interest rates exceed growth (R greater than G) debt service takes a growing share of resources and debt becomes unsustainable regardless of the debt/GDP level. _[theoretical, stated; support: Standard debt-dynamics arithmetic.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-4|C06-4]])
- Higher yields make debt less sustainable, which raises yields further: a self-reinforcing loop. _[theoretical, stated; support: Argument from fiscal arithmetic.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-2|C07-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-1|C07-1]])
- Fiscal crises develop slowly then move fast (Dornbusch), so the timing of the bond break is hard to call while the direction is clear. _[experience, stated; support: Rudi Dornbusch quote.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-7|C07-7]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-8|C07-8]])
- Most scenarios end in financial repression or fiscal dominance (forced bond buying or easier-than-warranted policy); politicians act on deficits only after a crisis forces them to. _[theoretical, stated; support: Political-economy reasoning, no precedent beyond the 1990s contrast.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-6|C07-6]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-1|C09-1]])
- Growth is unlikely to fix the debt soon: labour force limits (immigration, ageing) and a 10-15 year lag from new technology (AI) to productivity gains. _[empirical, stated; support: Internet and computers lag as precedent; contrast with post-WWII growth.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C08-2|C08-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C08-3|C08-3]])
- A negative stock-bond correlation let investors accept a low term premium; as that hedge value fades they require a higher yield, so real yields can decouple from inflation. _[theoretical, stated; support: Portfolio-role argument; real yields about 2% now, over 3% in 1993.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-4|C09-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-5|C09-5]])
- Reindustrialising a full-employment US economy creates bottlenecks, skill shortages and wage pressure that may spill over into broad inflation, though the tariff effect cannot be modelled with conviction. _[theoretical, stated; support: Reasoning from labour market slack.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C10-1|C10-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C10-R1|C10-R1]])
- Private equity and private credit are less diversifying than assumed: low distributions (DPI) constrain new flows, the last buyer may be retail, and private credit holds the same sectors and weaker ratings. _[empirical, stated; support: Conversations with institutional investors and listened-to discussions.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C02-3|C02-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C02-4|C02-4]])
- The BOJ's bond purchases moved losses from the private sector to the central bank, so the JGB yield rise did not cause private-sector stress or a yen carry unwind. _[empirical, stated; support: Observation that dollar-yen stayed in range; insurers possibly shifting from 40-year to 10-year bonds.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C03-3|C03-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C03-4|C03-4]])
- Asset allocation inputs should not be anchored on one period such as 1980-2020, which was favoured by disinflation and falling yields; use scenario-based construction and robustness to volatility and correlation assumptions. _[theoretical, stated; support: Investor questions and the shift from the 1980-2020 regime.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-3|C13-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-4|C13-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C14-1|C14-1]])
- Forward-looking return estimates (dividend yield plus earnings growth) correlate positively with realised 10-year returns, whereas trailing past returns correlate negatively; realised returns overshoot high estimates and undershoot low ones. _[empirical, stated; support: AQR papers and examples (2010 estimate 8-10% vs 15% realised; 2000 estimate 3-4% vs flat).]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-3|C15-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C16-1|C16-1]])
- US outperformance came largely from re-rating (CAPE from about half of non-US in 1990 to double today), so mean reversion argues for less US bullishness; if the US is safer its required return is lower (Dimson). _[empirical, stated; support: AQR paper and a conversation with Elroy Dimson.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-1|C15-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-2|C15-2]])
- Large equity drawdowns follow periods of optimism and rich valuations, as in 2000, not pessimism. _[empirical, stated; support: Dimson's point; 2000 as example.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C16-3|C16-3]])
- Trend allocations should not be linked to a macro view or political backdrop; waiting for conditions to look favourable misses years. _[experience, stated; support: 2016-2022 example: a 'central banks control markets' view held for a year or two, 2019-2021 were good for trend, consensus only in 2022.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C17-3|C17-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C17-6|C17-6]])
- True diversification means balancing across risk factors; equities, private equity and credit share the same tail risk. _[theoretical, stated; support: Argument that these assets share a big negative tail.]_ ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C17-2|C17-2]])

**Chain: Why all roads lead to higher US yields**
1. Large deficits at full employment, no ideological appetite for restraint, 2025 budget bill adding nearly 4 trillion → Rising debt supply and debt/GDP: Procyclical fiscal stance since 2017, end of the neoliberal era, disinflation reversing the 2021-22 erosion of real debt. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-2|C06-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-3|C06-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-1|C05-1]])
2. Rising debt with weak bond-market discipline and Treasury leaning on bills → Doubts about demand at the long end and a higher term premium: Less hedge value from bonds and more supply require a higher yield. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C05-X1|C05-X1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-5|C09-5]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-4|C09-4]])
3. Yields of 5% or more versus nominal GDP growth of 4-4.5% → R above G, worse fiscal arithmetic, and a vicious cycle of higher yields: Debt service takes a growing share of resources. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C06-4|C06-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-1|C07-1]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-2|C07-2]])
4. Pressure on yields → Financial repression or fiscal dominance as the likely end state: Forced buying by the Fed or private sector, or easier-than-warranted policy, e.g. a Fed chair under administration influence after Powell's term ends in 2026 (Nixon-Burns). ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-6|C07-6]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C07-5|C07-5]])
5. A fast spike from 5% to 6% → Stops only with a credible deficit policy: Politically unlikely; 'no votes in doing the right thing'. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C08-4|C08-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C09-1|C09-1]])
- Evidence: 1993 versus 2025 comparison, R-G arithmetic, Eichengreen and Dornbusch, term premium logic; the recent pattern that weak data brings yields back down is acknowledged.
- Fails when: A recession pulls yields down short-term as the Fed eases, but automatic stabilisers widen the deficit, so it delays rather than solves; strong growth or AI productivity could help but lags 10-15 years.

**Chain: Why US exceptionalism implies lower, not higher, future US returns**
1. US outperformance over 15 and 125 years → Largely a valuation re-rating (CAPE half of non-US in 1990, double today): AQR decomposition. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-1|C15-1]])
2. US considered safer, entrepreneurial, rule-of-law → Its required return should be lower: Dimson: safer means lower required return, so bullish expected return and required return views are inconsistent. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-2|C15-2]])
3. Rich valuations and forward-looking estimates of about 5-6% → Below-average equity returns and downside risk, with mean reversion: Forward-looking estimates correlate positively with realised returns; drawdowns follow optimism. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C15-3|C15-3]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C16-2|C16-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C16-3|C16-3]])
- Evidence: AQR papers, Dimson conversation, Global Investment Returns Yearbook (which in 2000 was right on lower returns but wrong on less US outperformance).
- Fails when: Valuation-based forecasts have limits (the 2000 yearbook missed US outperformance); returns can overshoot estimates in both directions.

**Chain: From shifting regime to scenario-based allocation**
1. 1980-2020 disinflation and falling yields favoured financial assets and private markets → Historical correlations and returns are a poor guide for the new regime: The regime is shifting. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-3|C13-3]])
2. Map a portfolio for each macro scenario (stagflation, inflation) and weight by probability → Tilt away from financial assets toward real assets, commodities and pure diversifiers: More robust than a point estimate; avoids over-weighting the last regime (commodities in 2021). ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C14-2|C14-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-4|C13-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C14-R1|C14-R1]])
- Evidence: Investor discussions at London events; no test.

**Decision process**
- [portfolio] Construct allocations by mapping a portfolio for each macro scenario and weighting by probability, rather than plugging in historical averages or anchoring on 1980-2020.. Why: The regime is shifting; scenario weights tilt toward real assets, commodities and pure diversifiers. ([[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-4|C13-4]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C14-2|C14-2]], [[MT072 - Higher Yields Are Here to Stay - Systematic Investor - Ep.350#^MT072-C13-3|C13-3]])

- [portfolio] Hold a higher allocation to pure diversifiers (e.g. trend following), diversified across risk factors rather than across labels such as equities, private equity and credit.. ### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Observable inputs that Alan says lead yields: the 10-year yield relative to nominal GDP growth (4-4.5%; a move to 5% or above breaks R-G), the deficit and debt path (2025 budget bill, Treasury reliance on bills), term premium and the stock-bond correlation (hedge value), Fed leadership after Powell's term ends in 2026, and near-term inflation forcing a Fed response. Niels offers no predictive input: trend signals, the Trend Barometer and model speed describe the state of trend performance, and he says trend allocations should not be tied to macro views.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Alan's chain implies a testable relationship: episodes when the 10-year yield exceeded nominal GDP growth with rising debt/GDP should show higher subsequent term premia; he gives no sample.
- (reader) The disagreement over scenarios versus trend suggests a combined approach: scenario-based core with trend as the untimed residual for unplanned risks.
- (reader) Both speakers treat the weak-data environment as bad for macro-data-driven strategies, which would predict relatively better results for price-based trend, but May 2025 results were poor for trend as well.
- (reader) The pattern Alan describes (deficit worries fade on weak data and return) implies yields at a given growth state should be higher than in earlier cycles, an effect observable in the level of yields at similar unemployment rates.
