# Howard Marks - Co-founder of Oaktree - Podcast - In Good Company - Norges Bank Investment Management

**Speakers:** Howard Marks (co-founder Oaktree Capital); Interviewer (NBIM host, unnamed)

**Summary:** Marks outlines Oaktree's philosophy: risk control first, no macro forecasting or market timing, target less efficient markets, understand cycles and act only at sentiment extremes. Market valuation was somewhat above fair value in mid-2024 but not extreme. Contrarian betting needs payoff-over-probability thinking, temperature-taking and tolerance of looking wrong.

**Approach:** cycles, contrarian value, risk management, credit

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.96 - first reader captured essentially every idea; only two minor points (most years are good years; patience over quick action) were missing.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Howard Marks argues that because markets are made of people, randomness and the limits of knowledge dominate, so investors should control risk first, target less efficient markets, avoid macro forecasting and timing, and judge decisions by process rather than outcome. Cycles are driven by excessive optimism and pessimism, so reversion of extremes is more dependable than continuation, but forecasts are useful only at rare extremes and never for timing; real returns come from careful contrarian bets against a consensus that is already in the price. In mid-2024 he sees US stocks as moderately overvalued, not at a dependable extreme.

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Marks gives few forward inputs. He watches: valuation versus norm (S&P PE 21 vs 16), the uniformity and self-satisfaction of opinion (market temperature), whether the market is carefree and not risk-conscious, and consensus versus central-bank guidance (rate-cut expectations versus the dot plot). Horizon: the next year for odds of a decline (a little better than 50/50), no dependable timing for extremes.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) His framework treats expectations versus guidance gaps (six cuts versus three) as sentiment signals rather than price forecasts, suggesting the useful variable is the margin of change in optimism.
- (reader) His claim that experience makes contrarian action 'muscle memory' suggests the method is partly unfalsifiable and learned by pattern recognition rather than rules.
