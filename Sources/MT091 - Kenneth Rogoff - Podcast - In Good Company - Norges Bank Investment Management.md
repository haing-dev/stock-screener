## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Rogoff argues that the 2025 US policy regime (random tariffs, fracturing globalization, pressure on institutions) resembles the Nixon-era 1970s, and that the real damage is institutional: the loss of predictability, rule of law and, eventually, Fed independence. His central forecast is a political-economy call, not a data call: central banks will not stand firm in the next big shock, US debt (7% deficit) will be resolved partly through inflation and higher rates, financial repression and price controls will return, and the dollar's global footprint, which peaked around 2015, will slowly shrink. He also sees China in a Japan-style deflationary crisis with manufactured growth figures and AI as both a possible disinflationary force and a source of fast, chaotic change.
