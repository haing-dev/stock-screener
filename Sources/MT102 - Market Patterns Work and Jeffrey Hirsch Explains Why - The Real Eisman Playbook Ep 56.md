## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Jeffrey Hirsch argues that market calendar patterns (four-year/midterm cycle, best six months Nov-Apr, January barometer, monthly and intraday flows, sector seasonals) persist because people and institutions behave habitually and rebalance on calendar dates, and that algorithms inherit these habits because people program them. He treats seasonality as an average tendency that must be combined with technicals, fundamentals, monetary policy and sentiment and re-checked as patterns decay, and he adds a long-run super-boom thesis (war plus inflation plus a paradigm-shifting technology, now AI). Host Steve Eisman supplies the window-dressing mechanism, is sceptical of Bitcoin and sees a credit-cycle tightening from private credit.
