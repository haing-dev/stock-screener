# Markets Aren’t Random- The Drivers Behind Trend Formation - Systematic Investor - Ep.371

**Speakers:** Rich (Richard Brennan, Australian trend follower, 'outlier hunter'); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Systematic Investor 371 (Nov 2025): Rich argues markets are reflexive, non-ergodic, fractal systems where trends arise from reinforcing feedback across time scales; Gaussian statistics fail. Approach: ensembles of behaviorally orthogonal trend systems, survival-first closed-balance position sizing; Niels defends dynamic (volatility-based) sizing. Conclusions: process over prediction, avoid ruin, low volatility is latent risk; AI will shorten prediction horizons, favouring trend.

**Approach:** trend following, fractal markets, reflexivity, non-ergodicity, position sizing, closed balance equity, risk management

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.75 - first reader caught Rich's core framework (impact, reflexivity, non-ergodicity, fractals, closed-balance sizing, Niels' rebuttals); missed secondary mechanisms (adaptive liquidity, reversal mechanics, multi-scale cascade example, directional vs directionless volatility), several caveats/disagreement details, market-view items (energies, oil, dip-buying) and had one speaker error.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Brennan argues that price moves because trade impact (not information) sets off reflexive feedback, so markets are path-dependent, non-ergodic, fractal systems in which trends are reinforcing structure that builds across time scales and low volatility is latent compressed risk; Gaussian tools (Sharpe, VaR, Kelly, expectancy) are therefore the wrong toolkit. His response is engineering for survival: behaviourally orthogonal ensembles of trend systems and sizing from closed balance. The host agrees with most of the picture but defends dynamic (volatility-based) sizing as an equally valid, proven design choice, so the episode ends as a polite disagreement on position sizing rather than on trend following itself.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) The core logic is a feedback-structure argument: trade impact creates reflexive feedback, reinforcement survives at longer scales while short-term moves cancel, so trend profits come from rare structural expansions after compression; the sizing dispute follows from non-ergodicity, not from signal design.
- (reader) The two sizing approaches differ mainly in what they react to: closed balance responds to realized drawdown (slow, path-based), dynamic sizing responds to measured volatility (fast); the host's point that both reduce volatility suggests the empirical gap should be small, consistent with his reading of long track records.
- (reader) Brennan's 'volatility is not risk' and 'low volatility is latent risk' imply that volatility-targeted books mechanically enlarge positions into compression, the same point he and Dredge make in the later episode MT224, but here it is not tested.
- (reader) The behaviourally orthogonal ensemble (breakout, volatility expansion, smooth persistence, mean-reversion-into-trend) is a form of model diversification meant to hedge uncertainty about which trend structure will occur.
