**Speakers:** Mark Rzepczynski (systematic/quasi-fundamental researcher); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host)

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Mark Rzepczynski argues that trend followers profit from regime changes and uncertainty that slow price adjustment, and that the key regime change for H2 2026 is structural: a new Fed chair (Warsh) who is expected to shrink the Fed's balance sheet, cut forward guidance and tolerate worse data, all of which he sees as favouring price-based trend following over speech-driven or data-driven approaches. His own hedge fund study finds that most strategies lose excess return and alpha in high-VIX regimes and shocks, while managed futures is insensitive with a negative beta, so an allocator expecting high uncertainty should choose managed futures rather than a bundle of alternatives. Niels reviews H1 2026 trend performance, stresses breadth of markets and data-reliability risk for economic trend models, and supports the VIX-regime finding with similar analysis at his own firm.
