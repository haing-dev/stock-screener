# Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots

**Speakers:** Pierre Andurand (founder, Andurand Capital Management); Tracy Alloway (Bloomberg, host); Joe Weisenthal (Bloomberg, host)

**Summary:** Odd Lots interview (10 March 2022) with commodity hedge fund manager Pierre Andurand on the Russia-Ukraine oil shock. Approach: fundamental supply, demand and inventory balances, backwardation and storage as tightness gauges, long options for big moves. Conclusions: oil up to about $200 needed to destroy demand, supply response is slow (shale 12 months, metals 7-15 years), sanctions will persist, commodities cap growth for a decade.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Pierre Andurand argues that oil is priced off physical balances (inventories, spare capacity, storage limits) and that supply cannot respond for years (shale about 12 months, other oil about 7 years, metals 7-15 years), so with Russian barrels leaving the market and demand almost inelastic, price must rise until a large recession or a roughly 2% demand cut balances the market, which he puts near $200 Brent this year. Beyond oil he argues that a decade of underinvestment will make commodity supply, not demand, the cap on economic growth.

### Gaps and conflicts
- No quantified model for the $200 target: it rests on a 2% residual gap and an assumed price needed to cut demand; the price elasticity is never given. ([[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C07-2|C07-2]], [[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C08-6|C08-6]])
- Tension: demand destruction is said to need a large recession, yet the $200 case assumes demand falls through price alone with no recession; the way out is mandates or a recession triggered by the price. ([[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C08-1|C08-1]], [[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C08-6|C08-6]], [[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C07-X2|C07-X2]])
- Regime-change premise for sanctions is a political judgment with no evidence offered; the outage duration, the key swing factor, is therefore unsupported. ([[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C06-X1|C06-X1]])
- The $100 vs 'market is pricing a short outage' argument (Brent $114) is stated as a mispricing but also acknowledged as uncertain (the move could be the end). ([[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C08-5|C08-5]], [[MT124 - Pierre Andurand on How We Might Get $200 a Barrel Oil - Odd Lots#^MT124-C02-3|C02-3]])

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) His edge is a physical-balance model plus option convexity; a long-option, capped-loss structure fits a view with fat tails around supply cliffs.
- (reader) The $200 call is the intersection of two conditions he never ties together numerically: an unstorable-shortfall price spike and a large-recession demand reset.
- (reader) Holding bullish views longer than bearish ones is consistent with asymmetric supply lags (long up-cycles, fast demand crashes).
