## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Bessent argues that global macro returns come from a few large, asymmetric bets built on imagining a state of the world that has not happened (UK housing and the pound in 1992, subprime, Abenomics and the yen) and from detecting when authorities will have to reverse an unsustainable policy, with micro information from companies as the best macro input. He sizes by the year's P&L cushion (a chip stack that resets on 1 January) and stays liquid, never concentrated, illiquid and leveraged at once. His late-October 2024 views are election-dependent: a Republican sweep means a growth shock; long gold (largest position), constructive on Japan, sceptical of China and Europe.
