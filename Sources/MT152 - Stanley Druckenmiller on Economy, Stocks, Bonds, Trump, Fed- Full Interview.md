# Stanley Druckenmiller on Economy, Stocks, Bonds, Trump, Fed- Full Interview

**Speakers:** Stanley Druckenmiller (macro investor, ex-Duquesne); Eric (Bloomberg Television interviewer)

**Summary:** Bloomberg interview, December 2018: Druckenmiller argues the Fed should pause hikes and QT because stock-market internals (cyclicals vs defensives), the yield curve, credit spreads and falling M2 growth are amber warning signals. Discusses QE-driven bubbles, Fed forward guidance, his long Treasury, short financials/credit and long cloud-growth positioning, the degraded price signals from algos, and shrinking hedge-fund alpha.

**Approach:** discretionary macro, stock-market internals as leading indicator, central bank liquidity, credit spreads, yield curve, Treasuries, risk management, market microstructure

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.89 - the first reader (57 claims) captured the Fed-pause case, stock-market internals, credit, QT/M2, positioning and algo/price-signal themes well; misses are the EPS-versus-buyback gap, no-real-deleveraging, hidden malinvestment, Fed political-pressure logic, QE3 'noose', the do-not-hold-defensive-longs rule and the shift to more fundamentals.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Druckenmiller argues that after years of QE and free money the Fed has built another debt-fuelled bubble, and that his own leading indicators (cyclical-versus-defensive stock internals, a flattening yield curve, widening credit spreads, and falling M2 growth) are amber, so the Fed should pause hikes and slow its balance-sheet runoff; the cost of an unneeded pause is small while the cost of an unneeded hike is five to ten times larger. Positioning follows from that: long Treasuries, short financials and credit, long secular-growth cloud names, with a modest 0-5% a year US equity return expected over five years. He also says that price signals have been degraded by central banks and algos, and that hedge-fund alpha has shrunk because the competition is smarter.

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Observable inputs he says lead the economy and prices: the relative performance of cyclicals (autos, builders, banks, small caps, retail) against defensives (utilities, staples, pharma); the yield curve (2s5s, 2s10s); credit spreads, CDS and high-yield and leveraged-loan indexes; M2 growth; commodity and oil prices for inflation; weakness in autos and housing at low rates; global QT and ECB policy. The horizon for the economic signals is 18 to 24 months; for the equity outlook three to five years (0-5% a year) with a possible favourable period over the next 16 months if policy turns. Policy (Fed pause, Chinese stimulus, the US government) is the swing factor. He gives no thresholds for turning amber to red.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) His edge claim is a mix of a leading-indicator set (internals, curve, credit, M2) and asymmetric positioning; the pairing of long Treasuries with credit shorts is one scenario (growth shock) expressed in two assets.
- (reader) Because he treats price action as signal and also says algos weakened it, his framework is conditional on horizon: weeks are noise, months informative. A systematic rule would need an explicit horizon filter.
- (reader) The rate-of-change argument (the level of rates matters less than how fast conditions tighten) suggests a signal built on changes in financial conditions, M2 and balance sheet rather than on the policy rate.
- (reader) Being right on the thesis but early (short in July, three months early) is his own cautionary example of the cost of anticipation.
