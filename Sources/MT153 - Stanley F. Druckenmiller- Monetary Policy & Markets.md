# Stanley F. Druckenmiller- Monetary Policy & Markets

**Speakers:** Stanley Druckenmiller (macro investor, ex-Duquesne/Soros); Karl Sokoloff (Real Vision interviewer; caption spells Carol Sokolov); Raoul Pal (Real Vision CEO, intro; caption spells Ralph Powell)

**Summary:** Real Vision interview (Oct 2018): Druckenmiller explains his shift from bonds/currencies to equities under free money, his view that global liquidity (QE shrinkage, ECB stopping, Fed QT) is the driver, how algos degrade the price-vs-news signals he relied on, and his style (concentration, scale-in on confirmation, hot/cold sizing). Also views on EM stress, zombie credit, populism, wealth disparity and the US trade/sanctions policy.

**Approach:** discretionary macro, global liquidity, QE/QT, algorithmic trading impact, position sizing, peg breaks, credit cycle, populism, behavioral

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Druckenmiller argues that liquidity, meaning the rate of change of central-bank balance sheets (about $1 trillion a year of purchases going to zero within 12 months), is what moves markets, and that years of zero rates removed the investment hurdle rate, creating debt, malinvestment and zombie companies that tightening will expose, starting in emerging markets. He says algorithms have broken the price-versus-news and group-relative-strength signals he used for 35 years, so he is cautious, still positions around liquidity triggers, and sizes by whether he is hot or cold, starting with a third and adding on confirmation. Sokoloff supplies the debt, entitlement, populism and trade context and Druckenmiller links it to wealth disparity, sanctions and trade-system risk.
