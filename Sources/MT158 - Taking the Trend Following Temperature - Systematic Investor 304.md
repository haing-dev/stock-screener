# Taking the Trend Following Temperature - Systematic Investor 304

**Speakers:** Graham Robertson (partner, head of client portfolio management, Man AHL); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Graham Robertson (Man AHL) reviews H1 2024 trend following and argues trend delivers equity-like returns with zero correlation, strongest in sustained sell-offs and as a partial bond substitute. AHL's approach is maximum diversification across many price drivers (especially alternative markets), a constant volatility target and uncapped equity exposure; AI has not revolutionised the strategy.

**Approach:** trend following, diversification, crisis alpha, alternative markets, volatility targeting, AI

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.72 - first reader (43 claims) captured the main arguments and data points; 18 additions are supporting numbers (2022 returns, barometer), market-access, regional demand and behavioural flow observations and caveats; no misstatements found.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Graham Robertson (Man AHL) argues that trend following is a long-run equity-like, zero-correlation, crisis-friendly return source whose mid-2024 temperature (strong Q1, consolidation in Q2, July pick-up) cannot be read from equity headlines but only from the full positioning across bonds, FX, commodities and stocks. The structural case is diversification: AHL's last 13 years of performance were determined by trading many distinct price drivers, especially alternative (OTC, non-macro) markets that give steady Sharpe but no crisis alpha, while liquid futures give crisis alpha at a lower Sharpe. He also argues that bonds' failure as an equity hedge in 2022 and the record stock-bond correlation make trend a candidate bond substitute, that capping equity longs inside trend costs long-run return, and that AI has not yet revolutionised trend following. The host Niels adds trend-barometer data, allocator behaviour (line-item and bucket problems, stacking), and the view that investors should have no opinion on their trend allocation.

### Forward-looking inputs (reader synthesis)
(reader) Observable inputs the speakers treat as leading or conditioning trend returns: (1) magnitude and persistence of price moves, especially in bonds and FX, since trend earns on movement; inflation moving up and down is cited as a driver. (2) Direction of bonds: a rally would turn the short-bond trend position into a loser, a sustained bond sell-off (higher yields) would keep it working. (3) 1-year stock-bond correlation (27-year high) as a marker for when bonds fail as hedges and trend may substitute. (4) Positioning at the time of a sudden equity sell-off, which determines whether crisis alpha shows up. (5) Reversal weeks in strongly trending periods: 12-month windows around worst weeks tend to be good. Horizon: months to a year, and a multi-year diversification argument. Neither speaker forecasts price levels.

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) Graham's position implies trend's value to an allocator is conditional on regime: it is an inflation/shock regime strategy now being sold as a bond substitute because bond correlation turned positive.
- (reader) The alternative-markets argument is partly a capacity and crowding story: because they are OTC and harder to access, fewer trend followers compete, so they offer steadier returns but no insurance.
- (reader) The host and guest agree that the allocator's behavioural problems (line-item risk, timing, narrative) matter as much as the strategy's returns, which is why they favour stacking and treating trend as insurance.
- (reader) The statement that crisis alpha does not hold for sudden drops implies the ideal hedge for sudden equity drops is long bonds, which trend does not reliably have; this explains the interest in pairing with quality or capping equities.
