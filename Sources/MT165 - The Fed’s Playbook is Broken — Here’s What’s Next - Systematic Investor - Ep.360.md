## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Dunne argues that the first six months of Trump 2.0 produced a slower, supply-constrained US economy (tariffs, immigration limits, no stimulus yet), putting a Fed under attack from both hard- and easy-money critics and facing a leadership change in which Kevin Warsh would be the radical and market-unfriendly choice. For trend following, both speakers read the 2025 slump (a few good markets, choppy rates, narrative-driven noise) as cyclical: the WSJ-style negative press may mark a bottom, the case for a zero-correlation diversifier is unchanged, and the real cost is behavioural patience. AQR's 'Diversifiers Forever' paper is presented as evidence that even permanent investors who spend 5% a year gain from smoother returns.
