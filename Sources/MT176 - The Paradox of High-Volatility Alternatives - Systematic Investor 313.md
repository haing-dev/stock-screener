# The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313

**Speakers:** Alan Dunne (co-host, macro and CTA analyst); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Weekly Systematic Investor episode: Dunne and Kaastrup-Larsen discuss Fed easing and neutral rate, Rogoff on inflation volatility, the yen carry unwind (BIS), trend-following August update and Asness's paper on high-volatility alternatives. Theme: high-volatility uncorrelated components are capital-efficient with cheap futures leverage, and trend followers have zero (not negative) equity correlation. Main conclusions: data-dependent Fed implies higher rate vol; carry regime fragile; institutions over-prefer low-vol products.

**Approach:** trend following, CTA, carry unwind, central bank policy, portfolio construction, high volatility alternatives

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.65 - first reader got the main macro, carry, trend-update and Asness arguments; it missed several supporting data points, positioning/flow mechanisms, and some views and caveats.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Dunne and Kaastrup-Larsen (Systematic Investor 313, recorded mid-September 2024) argue that the post-2022 regime is one of higher macro and rates volatility: a data-dependent Fed starting an easing cycle toward an uncertain neutral rate, a possible further inflation surge (Rogoff), a fragile decades-long carry regime that depends on trust in central banks, and leverage structures that turn volatility spikes into deleveraging (the August 2024 yen carry unwind). On the investing side they argue, via Asness's paper, that uncorrelated high-volatility alternatives such as trend following are capital-efficient (cheap futures leverage, portfolio-level Sharpe, zero not negative equity correlation) and that institutions over-prefer low-volatility products for governance and behavioural reasons.

**Chain: Why a high-volatility uncorrelated alternative improves the portfolio (Asness)**
1. Component has high volatility but zero correlation to equities → Small weight delivers the same risk contribution; losses are rebalanced against the rest: Portfolio volatility drag applies to the total, not to each component. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C14-2|C14-2]], [[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C14-3|C14-3]], [[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C14-R1|C14-R1]])
2. Optimizer given 10% vol target and zero-correlated alts → Allocates to alts instead of bonds (stock-bond-only solution is near 60/40): Result depends on the assumed correlations (alts 0, bonds 0.3). ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C14-4|C14-4]], [[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C14-X2|C14-X2]])
3. Alternative at 25% instead of 10% vol → Smaller allocation, room for bonds, better Sharpe and return per unit of risk: Capital efficiency of the high-vol vehicle. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C15-1|C15-1]])
4. Futures fund trades larger positions → Investor obtains leverage priced near overnight rate, rather than bank spread of 2-4%: Futures embed cheap financing; banks may refuse to lend against a 60/40. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C15-2|C15-2]])
5. Individuals and family offices cannot easily run managed accounts (tax reporting of components) → Pooled high-vol funds are the practical route: Operational complexity. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C15-X1|C15-X1]])
- Evidence: Asness's simulations under stated expected-return and correlation assumptions; Dunne notes assumptions could be debated.
- Fails when: If the alternative's equity correlation is not near zero, or the investor abandons it during early drawdowns (transition risk in the first 3-6 months).

**Chain: Why institutions choose low-volatility products and how that is changing**
1. Boards and investment committees must explain large drawdowns → Institutions request 8-10% vol products: Explaining -15% is harder than -5%, even though they know the maths. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C15-3|C15-3]])
2. Zero interest rates → Institutions wanted low-vol CTAs with 5-6% returns: No interest income meant returns had to come from the strategy alone. ([[MT176 - The Paradox of High-Volatility Alternatives - Systematic Investor 313#^MT176-C17-1|C17-1]])

**Decision process**
- [portfolio] When building an alternatives sleeve, prefer a 20%-vol uncorrelated strategy at a smaller weight, and add bonds with the capital saved; do not just ask for 'half the amount' of a diversifier.. Why: Better Sharpe and capital efficiency; 'give me half' arguments do not persuade investors.
