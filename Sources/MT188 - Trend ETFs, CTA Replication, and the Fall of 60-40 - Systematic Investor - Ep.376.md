# Trend ETFs, CTA Replication, and the Fall of 60-40 - Systematic Investor - Ep.376

**Summary:** Dec 2025 Systematic Investor: Beer and Kaastrup-Larsen discuss the shift away from 60/40 as bonds stopped diversifying, why a 10-market replication ETF (DBMF) beat the CTA index in 2025, a Simplify index-based CTA ETF and the true-cost debate for swaps vs fees, and how to explain trend following ('cloudy crystal ball', someone knows something). ## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Andrew Beer argues that 60/40 broke because bonds stopped diversifying, that a rules-based 10-major-market replication of CTA beta captures the same trend signal (beta, crisis alpha, zero stock-bond correlation) at far lower implementation cost than 70-190 market CTAs, and that allocators should compare all-in costs, not headline fees. Trend following works because prices reveal informed participants and because slow institutional models (60/40 rebalancers) give away alpha; it is best explained to non-specialists as a 'cloudy crystal ball'. Kaastrup-Larsen pushes back on calling replication a cheap CTA (swap costs are hidden, replication has tracking error) while agreeing on transparency of costs and on the logic that trend following captures correct views without forecasting.
