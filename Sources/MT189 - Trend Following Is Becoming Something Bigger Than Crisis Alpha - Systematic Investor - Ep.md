# Trend Following Is Becoming Something Bigger Than Crisis Alpha - Systematic Investor - Ep.413

**Speakers:** Andrew Beer (co-host, DBMF replication ETF); Tom Roble (co-host, SG CTA index / CTA research, London); Niels Kaastrup-Larsen (host, Top Traders Unplugged)

**Summary:** Aug 2026 Systematic Investor: CTAs had an exceptional 12 months (SG CTA +23%) from diverse trends (equities, metals, energy, short gold); the group debates crisis alpha vs all-weather, trend vs non-trend CTAs, managed accounts and portable alpha, leverage lessons from the Situational Awareness fund, and survivorship/selection bias in CTA indices. Beer argues short-term models are a zero-Sharpe exercise and that trend alpha comes from humans failing to act on changes.

**Approach:** trend following, CTA, crisis alpha, managed accounts, portable alpha, index selection bias, non-trend CTA, risk management

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.80 - the first reader caught the main CTA-index, crisis-alpha, leverage and survivorship arguments; it missed several supporting mechanisms and data points (July 2026 index divergence, August index numbers, Beer's handcuffs rationale, Roble's disagreement on adding markets, mutual-fund flow mistiming, index construction method) and mis-attributed one speaker.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Beer argues trend alpha is a persistent, all-weather source of return because most investors' portfolios barely change and humans cannot cut losers, but that the 'crisis alpha' label hides lumpy, cash-plus-250bp returns that need a higher Sharpe to be all-weather; engineering more complexity, short-term models and non-trend add-ons has not raised Sharpe. Roble defends managed accounts, portable alpha and adding markets as efficient delivery of the same idea. All three agree that CTA indices carry survivorship and selection bias that make manager selection look easier than it is, and that leverage in liquid futures is not the same as borrowed leverage (Situational Awareness).
