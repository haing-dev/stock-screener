## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Katie Kaminski argues, from her paper 'Trade War: Crisis or Correction?', that trend following usually loses in the first phase of an equity drawdown below about 15% because shocks run against prevailing trends, and earns crisis alpha (second-responder payoff) only in deeper or longer drawdowns, so April 2025 is either a short dislocation like 2018/2020 or the start of something like the tech bubble. Wide dispersion across trend managers in a shock is normal (position placement, parameters and speed, like coin tosses), robust managers diversify parameters, and adding trend to equities turns the question into a joint portfolio-construction and risk problem (correlation and volatility handling). Niels Kaastrup-Larsen adds the April 2025 performance numbers, the long-gold crowding survey and the point that drawdown periods are historically when to allocate.

**Chain: Why trend loses early in an equity drawdown and wins only if the drawdown deepens or lengthens**
1. A sudden shock hits against existing trend positions (long equities) → Trend losses in the first part of the drawdown (typically drawdowns under about 15%): positions are on the wrong side and must adjust to the new theme ([[MT194 - Trend Following during Trump Tariff Turmoil! - Systematic Investor 344#^MT194-C06-3|C06-3]], [[MT194 - Trend Following during Trump Tariff Turmoil! - Systematic Investor 344#^MT194-C06-2|C06-2]])
2. The drawdown becomes deeper and longer (tech bubble, GFC, 2022) → Trend turns positive: crisis alpha as a second responder: the system has repositioned and rides the unravelling in the slipstream of the selloff ([[MT194 - Trend Following during Trump Tariff Turmoil! - Systematic Investor 344#^MT194-C06-4|C06-4]], [[MT194 - Trend Following during Trump Tariff Turmoil! - Systematic Investor 344#^MT194-C06-5|C06-5]])
