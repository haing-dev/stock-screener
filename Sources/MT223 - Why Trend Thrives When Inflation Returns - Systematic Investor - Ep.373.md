# Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373

**Speakers:** Alan Dunne (host, guest-hosting); Yoav Git (CTA manager)

**Summary:** Top Traders Unplugged episode with three papers: the inelastic markets hypothesis ($1 inflow raises valuation $5, critiqued as trend-following reverse causality), Man AHL on the best strategies for inflationary times (trend and commodities do well), and backtest overfitting in alternative beta (average 60% Sharpe haircut). Conclusion: elasticity governs trend speed, inflation suits trend, and portfolio construction matters more than signal.

**Approach:** trend following, supply and demand elasticity, inflation, QIS, backtest overfitting

**Cross-check completeness estimate of first read:** 0.80 - the first reader captured the paper summaries, critiques, Man inflation findings and overfitting haircut well; it missed several secondary mechanisms (rebalancing spillover, price-impact versus volatility, trend-speed implication for commodities versus equities, option-like payoff of trend, wealth effect, money-market cash argument) and mis-framed the bond 'elasticity' number.

## Argument map

**Episode thesis:** Yoav Git reads three papers through a trend follower's lens. The inelastic markets hypothesis ($1 of inflow lifts US equity valuation about $5) is useful because elasticity and adjustment speed set how big and how long trends are, but its 5x multiplier is probably nearer 1 because trend-following flows are a reverse-causality channel. The Man AHL inflation paper shows trend (commodity and fixed income) works in inflationary periods and suits inflation-linked mandates better than TIPS or real estate, and the backtest-overfitting paper shows alternative-beta (QIS) products lose about 60% of their backtest Sharpe live, so portfolio construction, risk management and execution matter more than the signal.

**Worldview premises**
- Trend size depends on the elasticity of supply and demand: inelastic markets (electricity, commodities) need a large price move to clear a shock, and delayed adjustment (cows take a year to grow) makes the trend persist. _[theoretical, stated; support: Supply-demand explanation with butter, cows and electricity examples]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C03-1|C03-1]])
- Elasticities are hard to estimate because shocks hit both curves; an instrument that moves only one side is needed, and the Gabaix-Koijen granular instrumental variable uses shifts in each holder's share of demand as idiosyncratic shocks. _[theoretical, stated; support: Explains the method and has corresponded with the authors]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C03-2|C03-2]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C04-1|C04-1]])
- In the inelastic markets hypothesis $1 of inflow into US equities raises aggregate valuation by about $5, so flows drive valuations (a possible bubble) and the efficient market view fails. _[empirical, stated; support: Reports the paper's result (Gabaix and Koijen), not his own estimate]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C02-2|C02-2]])
- Fixed income reacts far less to flows than equities: $1 trillion of Fed buying in the GFC did not lift bond prices by 50%, because Treasury supply expands to meet demand while equity supply (about $500bn of issuance a year) is tied to the real economy. _[empirical, stated; support: QE natural experiments and US Treasury growth from $10tn to $25tn (ledger-corrected: price-impact multiplier of 0.25-0.75 versus 5)]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C05-1|C05-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C07-2|C07-2]])
- The paper's contemporaneous regression confuses causality: rising equity prices attract trend-following flows (CTAs, closet trend followers, funds, retail), so the true flow-impact multiple is probably just above 1. _[theoretical, stated; support: Back-of-envelope: about $300bn of CTAs gives quarterly trend flows of roughly 0.2% or 2% of market value (garbled) against the 6% flow figure; no estimate of his own]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C06-1|C06-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C06-2|C06-2]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C10-1|C10-1]])
- The paper's data are slow and short (quarterly flow of funds, Morningstar fund flows and 13F, roughly 2000-2021), so only one quarter's horizon is shown and the multiplier cannot be tracked over time. _[empirical, stated; support: Reading the paper's data description]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C07-1|C07-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C09-X1|C09-X1]])
- Markets differ in how fast they adjust: equities find a new equilibrium quickly (ETF shares outstanding show strong weekly negative autocorrelation), whereas commodity prices become negatively autocorrelated only about a year out because supply takes a year to respond; more elastic markets have lower volatility (bonds versus equities). _[empirical, stated; support: ETF shares-outstanding autocorrelation; physical supply constraints]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-1|C08-1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-X1|C08-X1]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-X2|C08-X2]])
- Each market has a trend lookback that falls in the mean-reversion zone of its adjustment process, which depends on its physical nature, especially in commodities; markets are already traded at different speeds because costly-to-trade markets use slower signals. _[theoretical, stated; support: Reasoning; no test shown]_ ([[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C08-2|C08-2]], [[MT223 - Why Trend Thrives When Inflation Returns - Systematic Investor - Ep.373#^MT223-C09-1|C09-1]])

### Reader inferences (not stated by the speakers)
- (reader) The framework suggests a testable rule: trend lookback should scale with the physical adjustment time of each market (about a year for commodities, a week for equity ETFs), which can be tested by market.

- (reader) The 60% haircut implies a backtest-to-live shrinkage factor of about 0.4 that could be used as a prior when judging any new strategy, including candidate screens in this project.
