# Prompt: find investors' own words online (letters, interviews, podcasts, talks, articles)

## Your task
You are a research assistant. For each investor below, find material in which the investor explains, in their own
words, how they pick stocks, value them, avoid mistakes, sell, and size positions. Search the open web. Report every
item you find in the table format at the end. Work through Tier 1 completely before Tier 2.

## What counts (in order of value)
1. **Written by the investor**: annual or quarterly letters to clients or shareholders, owner's manuals, published
   essays, op-eds, book chapters, lecture notes, conference presentation slides with speaker text.
2. **Full transcripts** of long interviews or podcasts (at least about 5,000 words, i.e. roughly 40+ minutes of
   talk) where the investor is the main speaker.
3. **Long audio or video** (podcast, conference talk, university lecture, fireside chat) of at least 40 minutes,
   even without a transcript. Give the link so a transcript can be made.
4. **Short items** (articles quoting them, short interviews, profiles) only as leads, and only if nothing better
   exists. Third-party summaries ("lessons from X") do not count as the investor's words; list them as leads only.

Prefer items that state **explicit, measurable criteria** (for example "we buy at 10 times free cash flow",
"we sell when ...", "no position above 10%", "returns on capital above 15%"). Older items matter: anything from
before 2015 is especially valuable, because it shows the method was stated before the recent results.

## Rules
- Give the exact URL of the original source (fund website, SEC filing, podcast host, university, conference). If an
  item is only on a re-posting site, give that URL and say so.
- Record the date the investor said or wrote it (not the date it was re-posted). If unsure, say "date uncertain".
- Do not use pirated copies or paywall workarounds. For paid books or paywalled articles, give the citation only.
- Do not summarise or rewrite the content. Your job is to locate and list. Where a free transcript exists, save it as
  a plain text file named `Investor - Title - YYYY.txt`. Where a letter exists as a PDF on an official site, save the
  PDF as `Investor - Title - YYYY.pdf`.
- Check that the speaker is really the named investor (podcast titles and captions sometimes misname people). For
  firms, first identify the founder or portfolio manager, then search for that person.
- Skip anything already listed under "Already have" for that investor.
- Flag duplicates (the same talk posted in several places) and keep the most complete version.
