## 1. What he buys and why

He buys companies with high barriers to entry, which he calls the most important thing for his kind of investing (HOH2-C002, HOH3-C005). He wants barriers that last, ideally several of them ("maybe five") with no alternative option (HOH2-C004, HOH3-C017, HOH3-C024, HOH3-C027), and barriers that are obvious (HOH3-C021). He gives his reason as "Competition kills profits" (HOH2-C005, HOH3-C003). He also requires an essential product or service, which he also calls most important, and he does not like discretionary things (HOH2-C015, HOH2-C016, HOH3-C028). His companies usually have recurring, predictable revenue (HOH2-C018) and pricing power above inflation, which he calls the test of a moat (HOH2-C023, HOH2-C024, HOH3-C018). He wants confidence that the business will still be around, in a dominant position, in 20 or 30 years (HOH3-C006, HOH3-C047, HOH3-C052). Growth alone does not matter (HOH2-C001, HOH2-C019, HOH3-C004). He looks at valuation only after the barriers satisfy him, using a long-term DCF (HOH2-C045, HOH2-C053). He holds for the long term (average 8 years, private-equity approach; HOH2-C051, HOH2-C052, HOH3-C008), keeps 10 to 15 stocks (HOH2-C071) and acts as an owner (HOH2-C082). He avoids banks and a long list of other industries (HOH2-C033 to C039), fast-changing technology (HOH3-C026) and shorting (HOH2-C084, HOH3-C040). He calls permanent loss of capital "what kills you" (HOH2-C066) and puts risk first (HOH3-C001).
