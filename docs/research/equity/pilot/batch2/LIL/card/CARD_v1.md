## 1. What he buys and why

He buys a stock as part ownership of a business (C001, L2-C017). He checks four things: is it cheap, is it a good business, can he trust management (because they are good or because outside checks are enough), and what has he missed and why is the chance being offered to him (C007, C008, C078). Valuation comes first. If the valuation does not fit, he goes no further (C013). He wants a large margin of safety because what he thinks the value is may not be there (C002, L2-C020, L2-C021). He measures a good business by pre-tax, pre-interest earnings against the unleveraged capital it needs (C017). That capital is working capital plus fixed assets, with goodwill left out (C042). His ideas start from new-low lists, not new-high lists (C012), and from reading Value Line page by page (C010, C011, C037).

Over time he moved from cheap companies to "quality yet cheap" companies (L2-C012, L2-C013). He says cheap stocks give ordinary returns (C052, C071). The big returns come from a handful of insights into businesses whose advantage keeps getting stronger, bought at the inflection point (C053, C086, C090). He holds those businesses for 10-20 years and does not sell them on valuation (C085, C092, L2-C043). His stated aim is to own "the most dynamic creative companies in the world's most vibrant economies" to protect purchasing power (L2-C007, L2-C005, L2-C035). He thinks mispricing lasts because few people compete for it or their institutions cannot act (C027, L2-C031, L2-C033).

- **New lows** (RB2): no window (52-week? multi-year?) and no depth.
- **Strengthening advantage / inflection point** (RB4): no measure. One possible proxy, return on capital above the industry and rising (L2-C026), is our proxy, not a rule he states.
- **Quality yet cheap / dynamic companies / vibrant economies** (RB5, RB6): no definitions; the universe and countries are not stated.
- **Management trust** (RB1, RP7): judged in person only; no computable stand-in (insider ownership, tenure) is stated.
- **Ever needed capital** (RP5): 10-15 years of history, but "needed money" is not defined (equity issued? net debt up?).
- **Analyst coverage** (RP4): no count; "no coverage" is called good only in a principle (C019).
- **Size**: no position weights, no number of holdings, no cap. "A shitload" (C030) and "handful ... no more than number on the two hand" in a lifetime (C053) are not portfolio rules. Only fund leverage = 0 is stated (RZ2).

- **Avoid**: none verified; there is no company debt limit and no cyclical-peak filter beyond the L2-C065 principle.

- **Dates**: every rule was said in 2020 or 2026. The claims date nothing earlier, except anecdotes about 1993-2000.
