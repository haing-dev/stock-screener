## 1. What he buys and why

He stays in financials, where he says he has an edge (PIL1-C021, PIL1-C022), and buys cheap financial stocks
(PIL1-C088, PIL2-C005), focusing on the better businesses: higher ROE, managements compounding tangible book value,
well-run banks (PIL1-C033, PIL2-C068), while ignoring mediocre, low-ROE regional banks that grow by M&A (PIL1-C030,
PIL1-C031). An idea must offer, on his own thesis, a double in three years, his hurdle rate; ideas offering about 20%
are passed over so that capital is not tied up (PIL1-C013, PIL2-C013, PIL2-C014). Cheap is not enough: the stock has to
start moving, and he will pay up a little for confirmation rather than catch a falling knife (PIL2-C016, PIL1-C019,
PIL1-C018, PIL1-C045; principle PIL2-C015 "value plus momentum"). He likes names that are less trafficked and not
controversial (PIL1-C012), buys two or three lagging names each December for the January bounce (PIL1-C092,
PIL1-C093), and uses tender offers as an idea source (PIL2-C100). He sells when real evidence shows he was wrong
(PIL1-C036, PIL1-C037), lets winners run (PIL2-C022), holds 30-40% of names as enduring and treats 60-70% as for sale
at the right price (PIL2-C070, PIL2-C071). He runs about 40-45 longs at about 3% each (new positions about 4%), adds
rarely and only to winners (PIL1-C061, PIL1-C003, PIL1-C005, PIL1-C006, PIL1-C007, PIL1-C009), is long-biased with
net exposure cut from 75 to 50 and a little leverage (PIL1-C066, PIL1-C073, PIL2-C058). He calls these guidelines, not
strict rules, but says breaking them loses him money (PIL1-C010, principle).
