## 1. What he buys and why

He buys hard-asset, land and royalty companies whose value does not show in reported earnings: "dormant assets ... potentially productive, but ... dormant at the moment" (STA3-C039), which the market discounts "at a very, very high rate" (STA3-C040) and which act as a "call option on everything" (STA3-C056, C053; Franco Nevada STA1-C025, C027). He wants "scarcity value of a hard asset encased in scarcity value of corporate structure", meaning a "rare company that's not likely to access the capital markets" (STA3-C047, C048). Land is durable and cannot be made technologically obsolete (STA2-C011), is not marked to market and so is "not going to be well understood" (STA2-C012, STA1-C024), and no ETF or fund is dedicated to it (STA2-C013). Royalty and land firms need no reinvestment, so shareholders keep the earnings (STA1-C022, STA3-C054). He likes firms "not included in any index" that "keep buying back their stock" (STA3-C080; TPL STA3-C074, C075, C023), and more broadly the "less scrutinized" company that large, short-horizon, liquidity-bound managers do not take the time to understand (STA2-C009, C006, C007, C008, C001). Owner-operators, where management owns more equity than anyone else, are underweighted by indexes and trade cheaply, but this is a principle and not a verified rule (STA3-C012, C029, C036, C037, C038). His reasoning is that indexation is a price-insensitive buyer that leaves such firms unanalysed and has cut the number of undervalued securities (STA3-C010, C013, C014, C019), and that short grading periods make long-run payoffs undervalued (STA2-C002, C003, C004, C005). After decades of disinflation, indexes hold almost no hard assets (STA3-C057, STA1-C014, C015); hard assets were the bulk of wealth for most of history and "we're returning ... to the world of hard or tangible ... assets" (STA2-C035, C038), and he expects inflation to come back (STA1-C001, C004, C005). He also buys where a dividend cut or a lack of production has pushed the price down (STA1-C030). Because indexation has muted the IPO market, he sometimes buys private companies, but few qualify (STA2-C041, C042, C043, C046). He starts positions small, under 5% (STA2-C014), and lets winners grow into the biggest positions rather than trimming them (STA2-C016, C018, C019). He holds for a very long time: he wants a compelling reason before he sells (STA3-C082, C005, C002), and he sells only to fund something better or when a big position has lost its appreciation potential (STA2-C021, C022, C023, C025).

## 6. Gaps (what a screen needs that he does not state)

- Rbuy1 dormant assets: no measure or threshold for "dormant". Only one example figure (Franco Nevada, 2/3 to 70% non-producing assets, STA1-C025). No definition of "hard asset".
- Rbuy2 scarcity: no measure of scarcity or of "not likely to access the capital markets" (for example, no equity issuance over N years is never stated). STA2-C011 names durability (cannot be made obsolete) but gives no measure.
- Rbuy3: no list of which indexes count, no size ceiling for "too small to be in an index" (STA3-C043; the note on STA1-C029 gives SJT at about $150 million as an example only), no minimum buyback rate (TPL's ~3% a year, STA3-C075, is an example), and no measure of "not that many owners".
- Rbuy4: "less scrutinized" is measured only by the proxy analyst coverage (STA2-C009); no coverage threshold is stated.
- Rbuy5, Rbuy6, Rprice2 (private companies): the valuation hurdles are not stated (STA2-C043 note); "very limited competition" and "proprietary" have no measure (C045). These rules cover private companies and do not enter a listed-stock screen.
- Owner-operators: repeated five times (STA3-C012, C029, C036, C037, C038) but never a verified rule. The definition "own more equity than anyone else" (C036) could be computed (management = largest holder), but no ownership threshold is given.
- Spin-offs: the opportunity is in the parent (STA3-C041), as a principle only. No rule or measure.
- Long product life cycle (STA2-C010): principle only, proxy product life cycle length, no rule or measure.
- Price: no valuation multiple, discount or yield. He says to adjust P/E for the part of earnings that must be reinvested (STA1-C021, C023) and that land is not marked to market (STA1-C024, STA2-C012), but gives no formula.
- Rprice1: no size of dividend cut (zero in the example), no size of price fall, and "cost-effective" production is undefined.
- Sell: "compelling reason" (Rsell1) is now described by Rsell2 and Rsell3, but "superior prospective return" (STA2-C022), "appreciation potential" (C023) and "executing" a business plan (C047) have no measure, and Rsell3 gives no share to sell ("some", "gradually", C024). No holding period number (long-term only, STA3-C005).
- Size: start under 5% (Rsize1) is the only number; it is stated for the first purchase only. No maximum position, concentration or crypto weight beyond the 25 basis point Bitcoin start (STA2-C026) (13F needed).
- Avoid: no exclusion rule.
- Dates: all STA3 claims carry date_said 2026-09-08, but most were first said in 1995-2026 (per period_meant and notes). Rule dating must use those years, not the document date. STA2 claims have no period_meant and are dated 2026-03-11; the Bitcoin start (STA2-C026) refers to "more than a decade ago" but carries no year.
