## 1. What he buys and why

He is a thematic long/short investor in hard tech: semiconductors, semiconductor capital equipment, communications
equipment and tech hardware (ZLA2-C015, ZLA2-C043), because he understands it and few others focus on it (ZLA2-C016,
ZLA2-C011, ZLA1-C013). On the long side he wants to be an owner who buys and holds, for an extended period, companies
that drive and benefit from big inflection points (ZLA1-C001, ZLA1-C002, ZLA1-C003). His four long criteria are:
exposure to big inflecting themes; high market share with staying power, so margins stay high or rise; healthy balance
sheets and cash flows; and a multiple justified by his view of earnings two or three years out, "say 15-20" on that
earnings (ZLA1-C007 to ZLA1-C012, ZLA1-C044, ZLA1-C045). He is "not a slave to cheap companies" (ZLA1-C010). The why:
share prices follow earnings (ZLA2-C022); part of hard tech has separated from the old cyclical "trading vehicle" through
secular growth (AI, EVs, machine data) and rising barriers to capacity, which lifted margins (ZLA1-C014, ZLA1-C016,
ZLA2-C017, ZLA2-C044 to ZLA2-C050). On the short side he sells stocks that are not growing much, with EPS contraction from
threatened margins, holds them briefly and covers after a 20-30% fall (ZLA1-C004 to ZLA1-C006, ZLA1-C052, ZLA1-C053).
Risk is controlled by small bets, per-stock loss limits in % of the fund, and cutting gross exposure in high volatility
(ZLA2-C028 to ZLA2-C032, ZLA2-C037 to ZLA2-C039).
