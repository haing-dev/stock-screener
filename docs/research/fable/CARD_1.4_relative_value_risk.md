## 4. Rule layer

Common to all rules, as in cards 1 and 2 so the layers net (reader): checked at each month-end with data as known then (lags in section 3); PCEPILFE as first published where vintages exist, else latest, flagged; panel markets as excess returns; rules at equal risk; opposite positions net; four weekly tranches; every version run and averaged. All four are state rules: on while the condition holds, off otherwise, no tail. Reason: Graham's horizons are short (its quant programs: "our Quant programs typically run one to two month Horizon maybe three months", MT062-C03-4; "we can talk in a month and have completely different views", MT113-C07-5). Size is fixed while a rule is on (section 6).

**R1. Hawkish currency.** For the dollar and six currencies (yen, Swiss franc, mark then euro, sterling, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona) take the change in the 3-month rate over L months (series in section 3). Shape A: each of the six is long against the dollar if its change is above the dollar's, short if below. Shape B: long the one with the largest change, short the one with the smallest; if the dollar is one of the two, that leg is the other six as an equal-risk basket on the opposite side. Versions (8): L 6 or 12; shape A or B; activity gate off or on. He gives only a direction; 6 is the Compass window, 12 the yearly span of his counts (reader). The rate change stands in for hawkish (reader): it measures what was delivered, not what is promised. Activity gate: on only while the highest minus the lowest FEDFUNDS of the last 12 months is at least 0.50: two standard moves a year, between his quiet and busy paces (C1); reader. Claims: MT090-C07-1, MT066-C01-3, MT133-C05-1, MT214-C05-2. Hindsight: stated 2025-12; flagged. Not in the pods: Carry holds the higher rate, R1 the rising rate; Trend follows the price; Value would buy the cheap currency, as Calderini did. The pack describes Economic trend as growth and inflation surprises; if its inputs include short-rate changes, R1 duplicates it for currencies (step 3.2; D1). Cards 1 and 2 hold no currency position.

**R2. Steepener.** S = GS10 minus GS2. On while S is below the bar. Action: long US 2-year note, short US 10-year bond, sized to equal sensitivity to a 0.01 change in yield, so the pair gains when S widens (reader; he speaks of the slope in basis points). Durations: modified duration of a 2-year and a 10-year par bond at that month's GS2 and GS10, fixed now (reader). Versions (2): bar 1.0, from his figure ("The historical average since 1990 is about 100 basis points", MT092-C01-3; that the figure is the 2-to-10-year slope is claim text, so 1.0 is a reader's version), or the expanding mean of S (at least 120 months), which removes a number learned in 2025. No deficit gate: no deficit series on the sheet. THREEFYTP10 is not used: a model estimate revised with later data (reader). Claims: MT066-C04-3, MT089-C08-1, MT089-C08-2, MT090-C04-2, MT090-C04-3. Hindsight: first stated 2022-10; flagged. Ledger: three steepener rows. Tropin's of 2022 and 2023 (MT066-V002-2, MT089-V004-1): the slope rose by 0.14 and 0.20, under the 0.25 band, scored wrong. Calderini's of 2024 (MT133-V002-2) scored right, but that he held a steepener is ledger view text, not in his quote. Not in the pods: Trend trades each maturity alone; Economic trend and Value hold no curve spread (reader); Carry is probably short or small in the 10-year when the curve is flat, so that leg may repeat it (D1). Card 2's R1 shorts the 10-year on the same idea: they agree and overlap (D1). Card 1's R1 and R2 buy both maturities.

**R3. Cuts priced too early.** On when (i) the curve prices easing of at least 0.50 and (ii) the 12-month change of PCEPILFE is above the bar. Action: short US 2-year note. Versions (4): easing measured as FEDFUNDS minus GS2, or as 2 times (GS1 minus GS2), the fall priced from the coming year's rate to the following year's (reader); bar 3.0 or 4.0. The 0.50 is the easing in Tropin's example: "75 at year end", then "four and a quarter" (MT066-C04-X1; 4.75 is a reading of the garbled "4-3 now for uh 75"). Claims: MT066-C02-5, MT067-C07-X2, MT067-C04-3, MT089-C08-3, MT067-C02-2. Hindsight: 2022-23 only; flagged. ## 8. What would prove it wrong; when it should stop; disagreements

Wrong if: (a) a rule is not positive after costs or fails its placebo (Shelved); (b) R1's gate-on versions do no better than gate-off, against C1; (c) the pod loses on average in the worst tenth of S&P months, against the claim of negative correlation "about two thirds of three quarters of the time" (MT214-C03-3); (d) at the registered live checkpoint an analyst question scores worse than the base rate.

Should stop working: when central banks are on hold and guide the path (their account of 2010-21, MT089-C03-3); when positions are crowded (MT214-C06-X2), though McCann says the information ratio among their PMs is higher in times of higher correlation (MT067-C05-2), so the two disagree. Tropin expects any rule to decay: "there's no way you can do what worked 10 years ago and expect it to always work in the future" (MT089-C06-3), and edge was larger when "markets were less efficient" (MT066-C01-5). R2 switches off by construction once the slope is back above the bar.

Disagreements:
- Stocks. Tropin finds them dear in 2023 and 2025; Calderini in 2024: "I don't think that that is that much of a complete concern" (MT133-C03-2).
