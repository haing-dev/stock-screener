### 4.2 Fung and Hsieh 2001, QP09 (contradicts, qualifies or sharpens)

1. The payoff is option-like, and this cuts against judging the Trend pod by Sharpe alone. Sharpens.
   - "trend followers should deliver returns resembling those of a portfolio of bills and lookback straddles" (C04-5).
   - The primitive strategy "has the same payout as a structured option known as the 'lookback straddle'" (C04-3).
   - Both trend funds and the strategy "have strong positive skewness" (C25-3), while the strategy's portfolios have no significant mean: "whereas the PTFS portfolios and most of the individual PTFSs do not" (C15-1).
   - So the value sits in rare large moves. A Sharpe ratio, and a dSR bar, treats upside and downside variance alike and can favour a smoother rule that has shed its convexity. That is the U07-T1 shape-audit concern the charter dropped. Theory ASSERTED; skew SHOWN in Tables 1, 3, 4 (values summary only).
2. Compare with nonlinear factors, not linear benchmarks. Qualifies decisive test 1 (linear alpha against the Core book) and the Newey-West alpha.
   - "Linear-factor models of investment styles using standard asset benchmarks ... are not designed to capture the nonlinear return features" (C02-1).
   - "An investor using a linear-factor model on standard asset benchmarks would have concluded that trend followers had no systematic risk" (C16-1).
   - "the PTFSs are key building blocks for the construction of a performance benchmark for trend-following funds" (C26-2).
   - The F-test against the five strategy portfolios "is rejected at any conventional" level (C16-3b, SHOWN). The R2 of 47.9% vs 1.0% is summary only (Table 2).
   - Implication: report a Trend pod against straddle-type factors and by equity state, alongside the linear tests. The Fung-Hsieh trend factors (bonds, currencies, commodities, short rates, stocks) are published monthly from 1994 on David Hsieh's site (checker's knowledge, free). TESTING_PROTOCOL.md:24 already reports a "five-state equity 'smile'" for the book; the charter does not use it per pod.
3. Crisis behaviour: qualifies row 2 and D4 at monthly frequency.
   - Trend funds earned in BOTH equity tails. "the state-dependent beta estimates tend to be positive in up markets and nega- tive in down markets" (C03-3). "the returns of the PTFSs tended to be high during extreme states 1 and 5 for stocks" (C17-4). In the two worst world-equity periods (Sep-Nov 1987, Aug-Sep 1990) funds made "large positive returns of 13.0% and 10.2%" (C17-1, in quote).
   - Both episodes were deep falls (about 20% and 19%, summary only), so they do not contradict "anything less than 15% ... tends to be negative".
   - But they contrast with our own engine. Our 1987 crash loss "came from trend long equities (-6.4%)" (CHANGES.md:134-135). Our book's worst world-equity quintile earned -1.0%/yr in 1975-89 (protocol_diagnostics.md:17; design era).
   - So the option-like profile is a property of how the rule is run (entry, exit, reaction speed), not a given of "trend". D4 should be read with that in mind. SHOWN (1985-97).
4. Commodities give no crisis payoff: sharpens row 6 and the breadth item. "However, the PTFSs on commodities did not exhibit this option-like behavior" (C17-5b), though "primarily due to one outlier" (C17-5). So added commodities can raise long-run Sharpe but should not be expected to add crisis payoff. SHOWN (one sample, outlier caveat).
5. Where the payoff came from: qualifies Trend's four equal-risk classes. In 1989-97 the trend funds' capital sat mostly in bonds and short rates and in currencies: "41.2% trade bonds and three- month interest rate futures, 30.9% trade currencies, 15.5% trade commodi- ties, and 12.4% trade stock index futures" (C21-5). "Currencies appeared to be the instrument of choice, and stock indices attracted the least" (C22-1). "It is worth noting that no fund had any" significant stock-strategy exposure (C24-1; the completion is summary only). Equal risk to equities (our 18 indexes) is a choice the paper's funds did not make. SHOWN (regressions, 1989-97).
6. The lookback model is a perfect-foresight upper bound: qualifies charter s8 item 11 (c) on short shocks.
   - "which delivers the ex post maximum payout of any trend-following strategy" (C04-4); "This strategy delivers the performance of a perfect foresight trend follower" (C25-2).
   - In a trend followed by a reversal the lookback "pays out 30" while the standard straddle pays 0 (C26-6). Real trend followers with lags lose there (row 3).
   - At a 3-month horizon in monthly data the lookback and standard straddles "converge" (C08-2): monthly correlation 0.86 for the yen (C14-1, in quote). So monthly data cannot separate "trend" from "long volatility".
   - Hence F&H neither confirms nor refutes "no payoff in short shocks". It says the trend payoff is a long-volatility payoff whose capture depends on execution.
7. Autocorrelation (charter s8 item 11 (b)): qualifies both sides. F&H define a trend as moves "where price changes exhibit posi- tive serial correlation" (C06-1), but they assert nothing about whether markets trend (C06-4), and the strategy's mean is insignificant (C15-1). Reader: convexity (the crisis payoff) needs no autocorrelation, but a positive mean needs either autocorrelation or volatility that options misprice. U04's "no autocorrelation needed" (MT116-C14-2) is thus true for the payoff shape, not shown for the mean.
8. Sizing by current volatility (charter s8 item 11 (d)): cannot be verified from F&H. Their strategy holds a fixed notional straddle per market; vol scaling is not tested. Reader: a lookback straddle earns by keeping exposure while the range widens, so cutting exposure as volatility rises gives some of that away. This is consistent with U03 (static "40% or higher" long-vol correlation, MT195-C08-2) and with Santino (MT020-C06-3).
9. Data-mining honesty and an interest disclosure. "As the regressors were selected based on previous regressions, statistical inference is not reliable" (C21-2). "This article received research support from the Foundation for Managed Derivatives Research and John W. Henry & Company" (C01-4), a trend-following CTA.
