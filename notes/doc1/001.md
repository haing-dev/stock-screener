### 1. Li Lu — Columbia Business School lecture (2006)

*Founder of Himalaya Capital.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY)

**Takeaway:** Value investing suits a minority temperament (\~5% of assets). The work is investigative journalism, and the big money comes from a handful of deep lifetime insights you're willing to bet on heavily.

- Graham's three attitudes are owner mindset, a big margin of safety, and treating Mr. Market as separate from you. All three follow from one fact: you own a small piece you can't control.
- Be comfortable alone. "You're right because your reasons and evidence are right, not because others agree."
- The job is mostly research. Read Value Line page by page, starting with the new-lows list, until you can "smell" an opportunity in minutes.
- Checklist: Is it cheap? Is it a good business? Can I trust management? What did I miss? Why is this being offered to me?
- Timberland (1998): \~5x earnings, no analyst coverage, a 98% family vote. He read every court filing and checked the family's character. The stock rose \~7x in 2 years; he sold when 50–60 analysts filled the room.
- Korean holding company: market cap $60M against \~$70M cash plus \~$100M of listed stock. Think in whole-company numbers, not per-share.
- When you've done the work and see no downside, bet big rather than in 25–50bp positions.
- His biggest mistake was an omission: monthly-return pressure kept him from buying a company that later rose 50–100x.
- Expect 5–10 deep insights over a 50-year career; cheap Graham stocks are the "bread and butter" at 10–15% a year.
- Rarely sell rare compounders: deferred tax is an interest-free loan from the government.
- Moats like Bloomberg's come from learned workflows and network effects, and once an inflection is crossed the winner takes all.
- Shorting forces a trading mindset. Holding cash is fine, but never let a year pass without learning something.
