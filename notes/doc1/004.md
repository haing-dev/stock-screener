### 4. Sir Chris Hohn — In Good Company (NBIM, May 2025)

*Founder of TCI and the Children's Investment Fund Foundation (CIFF).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M01NZc2QlDk)

**Takeaway:** Barriers to entry trump growth. Buy the \~200 businesses with durable moats, hold \~8 years, and let intrinsic-value compounding beat whatever multiple you paid.

- Moat types:
  - irreplaceable assets ("they'll never build a second Madrid airport");
  - complex IP (no new jet-engine entrant in 50+ years);
  - installed base;
  - network effects;
  - switching costs.
- Growth without barriers is worthless: airlines grew \~5% a year for a century with minimal profit.
- Pricing 1% above inflation at a 20% margin grows profits \~5% faster than revenue. Price-led growth beats volume growth.
- Regulation: barriers too low invite competition; too high invite regulators. The ideal is weak, rational competition.
- Avoid list: banks, autos, retail, insurance, commodities, airlines, telecoms, media, traditional asset managers.
- Average holding period is \~8 years. He bought Moody's at 10x earnings in the financial crisis and now holds it at \~$400–500.
- Edge = fundamental, long-term, concentrated (10–15 stocks) plus intuition. "Very few things matter."
- Hardcore activism rarely pays now, and shorting is brutal: Wirecard rose 20–30x before it collapsed.
- CIFF gives >$500M a year to climate and child health.
