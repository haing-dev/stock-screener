### 5. Sir Chris Hohn — Investing & philanthropy (Money Maze, Nov 2025)

*TCI and CIFF.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wPNs8DZ0FvE)

**Takeaway:** Start with risk, not return. Competition and disruption are "the whole thing," so own businesses with several barriers to entry and an essential need, and hold them like a private-equity owner.

- Most of a company's NPV lies 20–30 years out, so ask whether it will still exist then.
- "Good companies stay good and bad companies stay bad," but only if you judged quality right.
- Moats are being worn down faster by AI.
- The thesis must be obvious. Avoid fast-changing tech and discretionary goods ("never understood what makes one handbag better").
- He prefers engagement to activism. GE invited TCI's analyst to address its top 300 managers.
- A great manager can't fix a bad business, and arrogance is a killer.
- "Do you need to change your wife every year?" Investors chase what's new; Peloton went to $50B and then nearly zero.
- Philanthropy: pick high-leverage, underfunded causes (contraception at \~$10 per averted unwanted pregnancy) and look past KPIs to root causes.
