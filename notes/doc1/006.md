### 6. Paul Singer — Elliott Management (NBIM, Feb 2025)

*Founder of Elliott (\~$72B).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zKRNT_h9n5E)

**Takeaway:** Never lose serious money, so you keep your judgment and capital for rare crises. Activism means acting like an owner at a time when fewer investors do.

- Activism is needed because index funds now hold a plurality of equity and sell-side coverage has shrunk. The best setup is an "open door" where part of the board already agrees. "We're dealmakers, not crusaders."
- About 70% of campaigns lead to meaningful action. Success means a lasting re-rating, not a 20-minute pop.
- His worst trades came from misclassifying risk, e.g. a Japanese inflation-linked bond arbitrage that fell \~30% under leverage.
- He lost 88% of his parents' money on margin in 1974. Losses wreck judgment, while intact capital lets you exploit crises like 2008. "Make a buck, keep it."
- Absolute return, no benchmark. Constant risk-aversion also prevents burnout.
- Argentina: 15 years of litigation because the country never negotiated.
- Early 2025: "about as risky as I've ever seen" (leverage, deficits >6% of GDP, AI "over its skis").
- Advice: get a broad liberal education rather than business courses.
