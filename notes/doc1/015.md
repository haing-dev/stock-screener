### 15. Robert Carver — Ex-Man AHL, now trades solo (Odds on Open, Jul 2025)

*Former head of fixed income at Man AHL; author of* Systematic Trading. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y39Zf3GPpxE)

**Takeaway:** In systematic trading, skepticism beats brilliance. Avoid implicit overfitting, use realistic metrics, trade slowly enough that alpha decay doesn't matter, and step back from your P&L.

- His worst week was a 10–11% loss (about $1B). Think in percentages; he turned off his daily P&L email.
- "Nearly every error I've seen was overconfidence": trusting backtests, assuming clean data, lookahead bias.
- No metric is ideal. Sharpe assumes symmetric returns, and LTCM's Sharpe was >3 *including* its blow-up. Its data was simply too short to contain the killer outliers (the "peso problem").
- Explicit fitting can be done properly. The danger is *implicit* fitting: swapping models after seeing the full backtest.
- High-frequency strategies decay within 1–2 months; slow ones decay over decades, indistinguishable from noise. Diversify across many rules.
- His rules across 200+ futures: trend, carry, breakout, slow mean reversion, relative momentum, skew. Returns are mostly risk premia, not inefficiencies.
- He adds about one rule a year. "Better to fail than to overfit."
- AHL's research is collaborative (shared library, seminars, CIO veto), unlike siloed pod shops. Hire people for whom money is secondary.
