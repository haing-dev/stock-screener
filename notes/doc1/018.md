### 18. Vinesh Jha (ex-PDT, ExtractAlpha) — How small trading firms can still compete (Odds on Open, Aug 2025)

*Ex-PDT Partners; founder of ExtractAlpha (owns Estimize).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=994x6I-XDos)

**Takeaway:** The 2007 Quant Quake showed that everyone using the same data builds the same crowded trades. Lasting quants pair paranoid risk control with constant evolution of data, models and many intuitive signals.

- In August 2007, PDT lost \~$500M in \~3 days while markets looked calm. A forced liquidation of a common factor portfolio snapped back on days 4–5.
- In a crisis, first decide whether you still trust the model. An explainable failure means the model is wrong; an unexplained one means the dislocation is more likely temporary. Your capital base shapes how long you can hold on.
- The root cause of crowding was everyone buying the same Compustat, I/B/E/S and price data, which led him to alternative data.
- Signals: an earnings-call model trained to predict returns, a refined sell-side estimates model, and Estimize's crowdsourced estimates, which are closer to true expectations.
- Signals crowd more slowly than people expect: either a gradual fade to "table stakes" or a sudden liquidation.
- Small firms can compete above \~$100–200M of assets: they are nimble and can trade less liquid names.
- Survivors share factor paranoia and continual evolution. Complacency gets punished.
