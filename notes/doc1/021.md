### 21. Agustin Lebron (ex-Jane Street) — How to find edge in markets and life (Odds on Open, Jul 2025)

*Former Jane Street trader; author of* The Laws of Trading. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UmCRYENRp7Y)

**Takeaway:** Edge is something you know or can do that the marginal participant can't. Build it incrementally and prove it statistically. In your career, find your own edge empirically rather than chasing status.

- Great firms start with one simple thing done well and then colonize adjacent markets. At Jane Street even the weakest capability is "pretty decent": if you're exploitable, you'll be exploited.
- You never *know* you have edge, only statistical bounds, which pushes quants toward higher frequency. Judge discretionary edge by the quality of decisions, as a poker coach would.
- Crises are great times to trade if you aren't forced to.
- Market-maker profits rose with disintermediation of banks, post-COVID retail and the "gamblification of everything."
- Good quant seats are few, and many students want them for status rather than interest. Use \~3 deliberately different internships to discover your edge. No question is too stupid.
- On Sam Bankman-Fried as an intern: "people who say they're risk-neutral are actually risk-seeking."
- Go where learning is fastest: AI in San Francisco, manufacturing in China, drones in Ukraine. The world is higher-variance, so sitting at the median is no longer optimal.
