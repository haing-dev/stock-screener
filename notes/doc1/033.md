### 33. Emma Walmsley — CEO of GSK (NBIM, Apr 2025)

[Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=00y-iTXE6bk)

**Takeaway:** Turning around a 300-year-old company took radical capital focus (spinning off Haleon, nearly doubling R&D) and treating culture as the CEO's job, not HR's, with single-point accountability and relentless repetition.

- Pharma "replaces itself every decade" because of patents. GSK expects 14 launches this decade with >£2B peak sales potential each, including antibody–drug conjugates ("biological missiles") and a functional cure for hepatitis B.
- \~90% of drug assets fail. AI is used across the R&D chain; RSV trials closed \~2 years early. The process is moving from linear to "lab in the loop."
- Pricing vs access is "and, not or." Access gaps exist in rich countries too: 40% of EU-approved drugs aren't available in Norway.
- Vaccine hesitancy: shouting "trust us" fails; answer questions transparently. Antimicrobial resistance is "the slow pandemic."
- Spinning off consumer health, a unit no analyst asked about, freed the balance sheet.
- Culture: ambitious for patients, accountable for impact, do the right thing. Prefer single-point accountability over committees.
- Making culture stick: never stop repeating it (a third of staff are new), and promote, pay and fire for it. She spends 55% of her time internally.
- "Hire people better than you." Her own weakness: not saying thank you enough.
- Advice: "Be kind, be brave, be truthful, be curious"; "what matters is the company you keep."
