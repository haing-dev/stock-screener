### 34. David Ricks — CEO of Eli Lilly (NBIM, Feb 2025)

[Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xl5-ZG3X1v0)

**Takeaway:** Speed is a management choice. Lilly roughly halved drug-development time through parallel work, pre-set go/no-go criteria and "beat the last team's record" continuous improvement.

- GLP-1s took three generations of molecule design. Obesity is a survival bias from our ancestors meeting a world of abundance.
- Excluding Lilly and Novo, pharma trades at \~12x earnings because R&D ROI is \~zero. "The biggest cost is failure."
- Preclinical work now takes \~2.5 years (from 4–5), and first dose to approval \~5.9 years (industry \~10).
- Lilly scales up manufacturing before animal data, pre-specifies the results needed to advance, and kills projects earlier at a higher bar.
- AI fixed a production bottleneck for +8% GLP-1 output, worth hundreds of millions of dollars. "Turn on a computer and invent drugs" is hype.
- Europe: patients get access \~2 years after approval. China cut its drug lag from 7 years to <12 months.
- Value-based pricing: drugs "go to zero for us and infinity for society" once they turn generic.
- Lilly had been "too nice," so it added structured conflict: a contrarian team argues against every Phase 3 advance.
- Bureaucracy check: when people escalate, ask what they've already done with their peers.
- "Put all your wood behind one arrowhead." Stay open to surprise; he joined Lilly only because his wife studied in Indiana.
