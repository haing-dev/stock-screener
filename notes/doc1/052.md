### 52. Chris Davis — All investing is value investing (Bloomberg Wealth with David Rubenstein, Jul 2025)

*Third-generation head of Davis Advisors (chairman since 1998) and Berkshire Hathaway director. $10,000 invested at the firm's 1966 start grew to \~$4.3M vs \~$2.8M in the S&P 500, \~100 bp a year better.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VTrE0PgZyvI)

**Takeaway:** "All investing is value investing": growth is part of value, and the real opposite is momentum. Davis tests a mega-cap by asking what growth and margins a buyer of the whole business would need to earn 10%, and pays up only modestly (75 cents for a dollar growing 15%) for durable growers he can hold a decade or longer.

- The 10% test: ask what must happen to earn 10% a year if you bought the whole business at today's price. Some Magnificent Seven names need \~20% annual growth for a decade with margins above 50%.
- Base rate: "a 10th of a percent of companies in the S&P 500 have kept margins above 50% for more than a decade." Valuations that assume such margins persist are betting on a historical rarity.
- Nvidia (mid-2025): its valuation needs a decade of durable advantage, Intel-in-PCs style. He admits "sour grapes because I missed it," but Porter's forces signal pressure from customers designing their own chips and from TSMC as sole fabricator.
- Labels like Magnificent Seven, FANG or BRICs lump together very different business models that share only current popularity.
- Early-internet lesson: everyone knew the winners were Cisco, AOL and Yahoo. Google wasn't public, Amazon had gone from 80 to \~6, and Facebook hadn't listed.
- Investing means expecting more back from "an assessment of the cash flows and the probability of that assessment being validated." Anything else is gambling or speculating on changing psychology.
- Growth vs value is "a strange distinction": profitable growth adds value. The true contrast is momentum, buying because the price rises, which "maybe" characterized the last ten years.
- Munger's refinement of Buffett's 50-cent dollar: buy something worth "many, many dollars" later. For a dollar of value growing 15% a year, "it may be worth paying $0.75 for that dollar."
- Holding period is "a decade or longer," so the businesses need durable, resilient growth. "I have the patience to be out of favor for a decade."
- Recession (Jul 2025): "I'm certain we're going to have a recession. I'm just not sure when." Build a portfolio for sunny days and storms; timing recessions is "a dangerous game."
- Hit rate matters less than "how much you make" when you're right.
- Investors' "behavior penalty" (buying after rises, selling after falls) swamps gains from picking great stocks or managers. Use an advisor who curbs it, or invest systematically.
