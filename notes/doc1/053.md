### 53. Chris Davis — High-conviction value investing (Excess Returns, Jan 2024)

*Chairman and portfolio manager of Davis Advisors; Berkshire Hathaway and Coca-Cola director. His financials fund, started 1989/90 and 100% in financials, beat both the S&P 500 and the financials index.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s9Cfd-dEsmA)

**Takeaway:** Value is the present value of all future cash, so look through GAAP: Amazon expensed its growth while Walmart capitalized it, and Meta was \~9x earnings excluding metaverse spending. Davis's costliest mistakes were selling compounders like Costco as their multiples rose, so he now treats fair value as a wide range and lets positions reach 8–10%.

- "No business is worth more or less than the present value of the cash it's going to produce": new-economy firms need no new metric. GAAP sometimes reflects value "and sometimes it's massively distorted," so value the business as an owner would.
- Amazon vs Walmart (his \~2010 study): each took 17 years to grow sales from $1B, to $100B and $70B. Walmart reported profits but consumed cash; Amazon reported losses yet generated cumulative free cash flow.
- Amazon was "expensing the cost of that customer acquisition rather than capitalizing it." Davis normalized its retail margin at 5% and found each customer cohort growing more profitable.
- Walmart's cash burn was rational because growth capex earned \~17–18%. The question for any cash-burning grower is whether customer-acquisition spending will ever be monetized; WeWork was the end point of copycats.
- Meta (\~mid-2022): \~9x earnings excluding metaverse spending (\~14x reported), a market cap below Home Depot's, 2.5B users and rising engagement despite TikTok fears. It tripled in 18 months; they trimmed.
- His biggest mistakes are valuation sales: Costco was bought at \~14x (17M shares in a day at 27, after a 40 close) and fully sold by \~27x. It's \~30x now on far higher earnings.
- Munger called the Costco sale "asinine": it underrated 20–30 years of advantage, and you pay taxes, then buy "some mediocre piece of crap." The risk is to "cut the flowers and water the weeds."
- Same error elsewhere: Amazon bought \~2002 at \~$6 and sold at $19; Apple ignored for years out of inertia after he missed it.
- Sizing: he and co-manager Danton Goei raised his father's \~5% soft cap to 8–10%, which "really did help" with Amazon. Fund rules and clients' life savings still force trimming that a personal account wouldn't.
- "Fair value is a very broad range"; inside it, take no action. Never anchor on past prices: "where a stock was has nothing to do with its value."
- Sector labels aren't risk; macro exposure is. In 2007 Countrywide, Toll Brothers and Lehman were one housing bet, while Chubb, Wells Fargo, BNY and Julius Baer run uncorrelated businesses.
- 50-year durability study: the top 10 churn, ranks 10–50 are steadier. Long-run winners (Exxon, Philip Morris, J&J, Coca-Cola, Colgate) are "non-obsoletable" with share counts that didn't rise.
- Big Tech are the "blue chips of tomorrow," with scale advantages like 1950s national-TV brands. Apple is most vulnerable: glasses or goggles could replace "this little box."
- Boards are overrated in 95% of cases: "a good board cannot make a great company, but a bad board can destroy a company." Directors should own stock; Philip Morris had the most heroic board he has seen, and not owning it was a mistake.
