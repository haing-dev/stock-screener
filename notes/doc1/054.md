### 54. Chris Davis — On Berkshire Hathaway and stock-picking (At Barron's, Jan 2025)

*Chairman and portfolio manager of Davis Advisors; Berkshire Hathaway director. His financials fund, started \~1990, beat the S&P 500 while the financials index lagged it.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bg2kLX2LlqE)

**Takeaway:** Davis values every company with one discounted cash flow; only the timing and certainty of the cash differ, and zero rates plus techno-optimism distorted both for \~13–15 years until 2022. His late-2024 sweet spot is durable franchises priced as fragile, above all banks at 10–12x earnings vs utilities at 20x, with twice their pre-crisis capital.

- Banks (late 2024): "utilities trade at 20 times earnings Banks trade at 10 or 12," yet banks hold twice their pre-crisis capital. Having come through COVID and the rate shocks, they are durable but "valued as if they're fragile."
- Precedent: after the 1930s bank runs, perception shifted from fragile to boring, and by the 1950s banks traded at 15x. "I dream about the day when people view them as boring."
- Banking, "making a spread on money," is hard to make obsolete: it survived money market funds, asset-backed securities, the internet and the ATM.
- Largest bank holding: Capital One, "the original fintech company"; Wells Fargo is also held. A well-run financial is, in his grandfather's phrase, "a growth stock in disguise."
- One method for all: "we didn't use a different version of discounted present value for valuing Amazon versus valuing Wells Fargo." Only the pattern of cash flows differs; bond investors don't split into growth and value either.
- Value lagged \~13–15 years after the financial crisis. "For the first time in all of recorded history the cost of money was free," breaking the discounting math, until \~March 2022 \[?\].
- Techno-optimism added to it: investors treated network-effect cash flows (Amazon, Google) as near-certain, then extended that certainty to other companies.
- Value the whole business like a private equity buyer: count senior claims (debt, unfunded pensions) and look for hidden assets (real estate at cost).
- Quality means durable competitive advantage and decent ROE sustainable for long periods. "We're not so worried about if they're lumpy... we're worried about whether they're durable."
- "Disconnected world" (late 2024): big transitions (money that costs something, tech disruption, the end of "magical thinking" on deficits) meet "enormous complacency." A segmented, highly valued market is "a dangerous recipe."
- Meta (\~late 2022): market cap below Home Depot's, 2.5B users, rising engagement, while the narrative said TikTok was killing it. Now trimmed to \~half the shares at about the same position size; augmented reality has gone from a free option to priced "in the money."
- Patience: "We studied Costco for 15 years before we owned a share." They bought \~15M shares in one day when it closed at 42 and opened at 26.
- Governance signal: "the executive compensation for the leadership at Exxon vests 10 years after retirement," visible in proxies. Standard Oil was the most valuable company for 12 decades, though nobody recalls its third or fourth CEO.
- Berkshire: "the inside is the same as the outside," and directors are paid $800. He frames succession through Standard Oil: durable assets and a culture that rejects short-termism matter more than the next CEO.
