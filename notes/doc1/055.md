### 55. Connor Haley — Alta Fox's process, activism and shorting (POWERS Pod, May 2022)

*Founder of Alta Fox Capital Management, a Fort Worth global long/short fund launched in April 2018 with \~$10M as a one-man band; previously \~4 years at Scopia Capital.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNGGcxZeBic)

**Takeaway:** Buy exceptional, moat-protected businesses the market misunderstands, and underwrite each to a probability-weighted 3–5-year internal rate of return (IRR) on normalized earnings and free cash flow (FCF). A scored framework sets position sizes, and where a misaligned board blocks value creation, Alta Fox forces change through fact-based activism.

- Sizing system: a 3–5-year IRR from normalized earnings and FCF, probability-weighted across bull, base and bear cases. Five factors scored 1–5 (risk, catalyst, quality, conviction, management, among others) then suggest a size, not followed "like gospel."
- Pipeline: a portfolio of 15–20 stocks, an "ideas list" of \~100 fully worked names with IRRs, and a monitor list. Each analyst juggles 3–5 ideas and kills them in days to weeks.
- Fast kills: outside the circle of competence, glaring governance issues, or 70% customer concentration, which scores poorly even at 90% confidence it's fine. Growth without a moat is another: "you can actually destroy value you know through growth."
- "If the facts are not changing but the price is going up then our irr is by nature by definition coming down." Shift capital from such run-ups to names that fell while facts held steady or improved.
- Start from exceptional businesses with real moats (network effects, cost, brand, switching costs), not cheap stocks to flip. Alta Fox holds little in financials, energy or biotech, where durable, understandable moats are rare.
- Management: they speak to nearly every team before buying, and the team talks to 15+ CEOs or CFOs a week. Favorite question: how they weigh internal reinvestment vs buybacks vs M&A.
- Killer CEOs hold a material share of their net worth in stock and don't sell (XPEL's Ryan Pape). Red flag: calling your stock cheap while buying businesses at higher multiples instead of buying back at an attractive FCF yield.
- Activism is a last resort and fact-based, focused on pay and capital allocation. Collectors Universe, the first campaign, paid dividends despite "amazing reinvestment opportunities."
- The shift to passive makes activism "more important than ever": passive holders give management comfort and rarely challenge pay. Governance is the neglected letter in ESG.
- Shorts: Kodak's 10–13x jump in 24–48 hours showed a 1% single-name short "could potentially blow up your entire fund." Alta Fox moved to broad-based and thematic hedges.
- Micro caps have been Alta Fox's worst market-cap bucket since 2018; its quality bias skews it toward small, mid and large caps.
- COVID (Mar 2020): they re-underwrote everything, made certainty the top variable and used web traffic to buy improving businesses. They sold a cheap restaurant stock that had just levered up for an acquisition; it survived, but he defends the sale.
- Macro (May 2022): with high inflation, cash is "a pretty guaranteed negative return." Hide in businesses with pricing power that protect a high return on invested capital (ROIC) and can succeed in most environments; a recession may be needed.
- Options are now routine on owned names: sell expensive volatility, buy cheap volatility, e.g. selling puts on a high-conviction, volatile stock for "an unusually large premium."
