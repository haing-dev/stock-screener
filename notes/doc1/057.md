### 57. Connor Haley & Garrett Finn \[?\] — The makings of a multibagger (MicroCapClub, Sep 2020)

*Haley founded Alta Fox Capital, then focused on small and micro caps; Finn \[?\] is the Alta Fox team member who supervised the study. A webinar on Alta Fox's study of 104 multibaggers.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7mSv0G2ZzA)

**Takeaway:** The 2015–2020 multibaggers were mostly already-profitable small and micro caps with mid-40s gross margins and room for operating leverage, bought at a median \~10x forward EBITDA. About two-thirds of the median return came from re-rating, yet Haley won't underwrite a thesis that depends on multiple expansion.

- Study screen: developed markets excluding energy, materials and financials; ≥350% five-year total return to June 2020; positive trailing EBITDA; $150M–$10B market cap; >$200k daily value traded. Result: 104 companies.
- Study: 88% were EBITDA-positive five years earlier, with median gross margins in the mid-40s% and EBITDA margins just over 10%. They were proven concepts, not cash burners diluting shareholders.
- Study: median revenue grew 21% a year and EBITDA 28% (25th percentile: 14% and 18%). EBITDA margins rose 753 bps as SG&A fell 416 bps of revenue.
- Finn \[?\]: "roughly one-third of the return came from ebitda growth and two-thirds of the return came from multiple expansion." 93 of 104 re-rated, from a median \~10x forward EBITDA to \~25x.
- Study: most winners started below \~3x forward sales and \~30x forward P/E. Haley: re-ratings came in bursts as stocks went from undiscovered to discovered, often at the swing into operating leverage or new coverage.
- Haley (Sep 2020): "if your entire thesis rests on significant multiple expansion maybe it's not a great thesis." Multiples were elevated, and profit growth alone would have made most of these work.
- Finn \[?\]: the US was 44% of the universe but \~29% of winners. Norway was 2.7% vs 7.7%, and Australia had over twice its share; Italy, Switzerland and Spain had none.
- Haley: "you have to sort of have an international investing strategy." Similar quality trades cheaper abroad, leaving room for re-rating, but verify that a foreign small cap's products are real.
- Acquisitions drove growth in over half the winners (Finn \[?\]). Haley: \~80% of deals fail to create per-share value, but skilled, aligned acquirers are over-represented in the right tail of returns.
- Study: most started micro or small (84% below $2B), yet median returns were similar (\~400–500%) across size buckets. Small size raises the odds of a multibagger, not the median return.
- Study: tech and healthcare were over-represented; consumer discretionary was 26% of the universe but 21% of winners. The mid-2015 to mid-2020 window was a bull market.
- Haley: "we really like to invest in category leaders": in growing niches, fragmented industries or new industries. "The bigger player will crush them" is lazy; XPEL out-innovated 3M.
- Haley on Zynex, the top returner: he passed \~2 years earlier, asking "if a market's attractive why is the second player leaving the market." Its main competitor exited over regulation, and EBITDA went from about −$2M to \~$12M.
- Insider ownership showed no clear signal in Bloomberg data, which can miss indirect holdings, so check it by hand (Finn \[?\]).
