### 58. Dan Sundheim — The art of public market investing (Stripe, Oct 2025)

*Founder of D1 Capital (\~$25B AUM, about two-thirds private, Oct 2025), which invests in public and private companies; formerly at Viking. He makes "95% plus" of the decisions.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRh0KTutZis)

**Takeaway:** Most of the money is made from multiple expansion when the market's doubts about a business model prove wrong, so the costliest mistakes are selling compounders too early on too-low exit multiples. Since GameStop, risk is controlled only before the fact, through sizing: \~40 small shorts instead of \~8 concentrated ones.

- "The multiple over the long term is a function of the perception of the stability of the cash flows," and re-rating can take years. Priceline grew 40% a year at 9x earnings while called "a flimsy business model"; it now trades at \~3x that multiple on slower growth.
- Underwrite \~2x in 3 years (a "20-something percent" internal rate of return, or IRR) over a 3–5-year horizon. Gains come early, so rotate when forward IRR falls, but IRR "is totally dependent upon what you assume the exit multiple is."
- "The biggest mistakes are selling the Costcos too early," almost always from too-low exit multiples. Netflix: the thesis was "exactly right," but he sold after the media analyst left.
- Netflix template: a huge fixed investment (content) with very high incremental margins; borrowing in the high-yield market to outspend rivals built the moat. Don't reject worsening free cash flow if spending builds such an asset.
- Sizing is "entirely an art": compare +50%/−50% bets with +25% low-risk ones. Avoid a book made only of +100%/−40% names or only of +20%/−3% names.
- "Planting seeds and then we're harvesting": staggered positions mean this quarter's returns reflect decisions made 12–18 months earlier. D1 buys before the \~1.5-month memo is finished.
- Earnings: a 5–10% drop rarely changes the long-term thesis; down 20% means a full re-underwrite. "Sometimes we're buying, rarely we're selling."
- GameStop (Jan 2021): stocks rose 400% in two weeks, and he "covered everything at the worst time and I knew it was the worst time." "I was right on the shorts but I was wrong on their risk management."
- Risk management happens only before the fact: size shorts so you never have to cover. Moving from \~8 to \~40 smaller shorts gave short alpha "as good or better."
- Short types: retail story stocks (keep small), frauds (mostly $1–2B caps), no-terminal-value decliners (already cheap), share losers (activist risk), cyclical over-earners (least attractive). No derivatives, no macro, no quant.
- Turnarounds re-rate fast in the US (3M within one or two quarters of a new CEO) and slowly in Europe and Japan. Rolls-Royce's plan was visibly working after year one; its profit comes from the predictable aftermarket.
- Oct 2025: Siemens Energy and GE Vernova have \~the same revenue and products, yet GE Vernova trades at 2x the enterprise value. Gas turbines stay short; he expects US power demand to grow \~4% a year for 20 years after 0%.
- 10-year hold: not tech. Clean Harbors owns most US incinerators, protected by local (NIMBY) opposition to new ones, with earnings growth "well into the teens for 10 years."
- AI bubble checklist (Oct 2025): debt-financed capex, circular financing, bad companies at crazy valuations; the trigger is disappointing training returns. NVIDIA at "20-something times" isn't expensive; it may be 1996–97, and "We should have owned more AI stocks."
