### 61. Joel Greenblatt & Matt Mark — Columbia Lecture 3: LEAPS on Lear and Grupo TMM distressed bonds (The value investing channel, Oct 2005)

*Greenblatt: founder of Gotham Capital, teaching his Columbia special-situations class. Guest Matt Mark: founder of Jet Capital (special situations and distressed debt, "hundreds of millions"), with 95%+ of his net worth in the fund.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TMM1M2ZUDr4)

**Takeaway:** The edge is a researched opinion, expressed through whichever instrument the market misprices. A Lear LEAP could turn $8.20 into $25–35, and Grupo TMM bonds bought at 60 cents doubled in \~18 months.

- Greenblatt: use options only when you "actually know something": a researched thesis or a dated catalyst that volatility models ignore. With a catalyst such as a spin-off, go out about nine months.
- Greenblatt: "So call is stock with a put": Lear's Jan-07 $25 LEAP at $8.20 = $4.53 intrinsic + a $3.70 put. Interest saved (\~$1.12) roughly offsets missed dividends ($1.25).
- Greenblatt, relaying Pzena: Lear's normalized EPS is $5–7 vs a $29.53 stock (Oct 2005). At $5 × 10 = $50 the $8.20 call is worth $25; at $60, $35.
- Greenblatt: Pzena sees Lear's problem as temporary. Small-car price concessions met an oil-driven SUV slump, contracts reprice yearly, and peer Johnson Controls had no comparable downdraft.
- Greenblatt: he defaults to an in-the-money LEAP as a "stock equivalent". The price gap between strikes is the implied spread's cost: $2.25 for a $5-wide 25/30 spread is good, \~$4.55 is not.
- Greenblatt: "People think options investing is risky, but you can only lose 100%." Kelly logic still applies: never put 100% into a bet that can go to zero.
- Greenblatt: the magic formula ranks on EBIT/EV and EBIT/tangible capital, trailing 12 months. Over 17 years it beat the market in \~95% of three-year periods and roughly doubled its return.
- Greenblatt: screens surface "aberrational things", so one real idea from a list of 25 is "a big home run". Demo screen: market cap ≥$500M, P/E 5–15, ROA ≥25%.
- Mark: view every distressed bond "both ways": as a bond whose coupons cut your cost, and as the price you pay for the equity. "It's never just a bond."
- Mark: convert the bond price into an implied enterprise price. TMM bonds at 60 meant \~$110M for half the TFM railroad: \~5x EBITDA and \~7x FCF vs 8–9x for US rails.
- Mark: TMM (2003): a Mexican injunction dropped the bonds to \~60, and an exchange gave 120 of 12% secured notes per 100. Notes reached \~par after Kansas City Southern re-cut its deal.
- Mark: game theory decides how you get paid; the outcome "is usually kind of what makes sense at the starting point". Hire your own banker, organize holders, and use small issues to get a seat.
- Mark: "In a sense, I'll buy anything for a price." Refco's bonds slid from 90 to 80, then 20 (Oct 2005): the question is always "is 80 cents low enough?"
- Mark (Oct 2005): credit spreads are "pretty tight", so distress is a needle in a haystack vs the 2001–03 Enron/WorldCom wave. "80% of what you need to know, you can learn your first 20% of your work."
