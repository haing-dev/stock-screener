### 63. Joel Greenblatt & Rob Goldstein — Columbia Lecture 5: paying 21x for Moody's and the magic formula's losing streaks (The value investing channel, 2006)

*Greenblatt: founder of Gotham Capital and author of The Little Book That Beats the Market. Goldstein: Greenblatt's partner at Gotham since \~1989, credited by him with much of its success.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cFls55S5DsM)

**Takeaway:** Paying up for a great business can be quantified, and sound strategies still lose for years. Goldstein derived 21x for Moody's (2000) from Buffett's Coke purchase; Greenblatt's magic formula doubled the market but lagged for 6.5–8 of \~18 years.

- Goldstein: at the Dun & Bradstreet spin-off (early 2000), Moody's cost ≤$20.25 on $0.95 pro forma EPS: 21x forward. Gotham normally bought at \~10x and sold at 14–15x.
- Goldstein: assume 12% growth for both, then adjust Coke's 1988 13x: × 1.15 for Moody's higher returns on capital ≈ 15x. Add × 1.4 for the slack Buffett had ≈ 21x.
- Goldstein: reinvestment rate = growth ÷ return on capital. Coke at 12% growth and 60% ROE reinvested 20%; Moody's, with \~$50M of PP&E and prepaying customers, kept every $1.
- Goldstein: moat test: no third rating agency could enter. Issuers won't bother, markets must trust it first, and conflicts block banks; Moody's and S&P hold \~40% each.
- Goldstein: Moody's compounded revenue 15% and operating profit 17% over 19 years, with one small revenue dip. Excess returns come early: Coke rose 185% in three years, then \~18%/yr.
- Goldstein: a stock double should pay managers \~10x their annual salary and bonus. Usually "management's rosy outlook is based on some shaky assumptions"; Moody's was a rare exception.
- Goldstein: Gotham sold Moody's at +50% within \~13 months. Profits then grew 40%, 35% and ≥25%/yr for 6+ years, and the stock rose 6–7x.
- Greenblatt: rank \~2,500 stocks on return on capital and on EBIT/EV using last year's earnings. Add the ranks 50/50, buy \~30, hold a year, and exclude financials (\~20% of the market).
- Greenblatt: 1988–2004/05 backtest: 30.2%/yr vs \~12% from a 3,500-stock universe; 22.8% vs 11.6–12% for the largest 1,000. Through 1H 2006, 22.6% vs 11.5%.
- Greenblatt: deciles fall in order, and these are "the most powerful two factors that we've seen published". Held a year, the top-minus-bottom spread was \~17 points vs 5.63 for Haugen's 65–71-factor model.
- Greenblatt: the formula lagged the equal-weighted universe in 20% of one-year and 9% of three-year periods. Its worst rolling three years was \~−0.7% vs −42% for the universe.
- Greenblatt: non-overlapping losing streaks ran 46 + 39 months vs the universe and 53 + 36+ vs the Russell 1000 Value. "you have underperformed for four years you're done".
- Greenblatt: time arbitrage persists: "this opportunity is not going to go away because people are going to suffer severely even during wonderful periods".
- Greenblatt: Gotham puts only "a portion" of its money in anything formula-like. It values a few predictable businesses on normalized earnings years out ("the whole art"), since last year's returns may be a cyclical peak.
