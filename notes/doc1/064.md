### 64. Li Lu — Global value investing in our era (The Value Vault by Raj, Dec 2024)

*Founder of Himalaya Capital (1997); Charlie Munger's friend, partner and student for 20 years, and Munger invested in his fund. Keynote at Peking University Guanghua's value-investing course.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mu-7J-14vW4)

**Takeaway:** Accept the macro and act on the micro: wealth is your share of total purchasing power, his proposed "sixth principle". So own the most dynamic companies in vibrant economies, unlevered, and know them well enough to hold through 50–80% drawdowns.

- Sixth principle: in a compounding economy static wealth shrinks, so equity, not cash or land, keeps your share. China's nominal GDP rose >340x in 40+ years; British nobles who kept only land grew relatively poor.
- Application: a global fiduciary should "find the most dynamic creative companies in the world's most vibrant economies". Individuals should hold purchasing power in the economy where they will consume.
- BYD, held 22 years, grew sales from 1 billion to nearly 1 trillion yuan but fell >50% at least six times, once >80%. "Each significant price drop tests the boundaries of your circle of competence."
- Four reasons to sell: a mistake (sell at once), a clearly better risk-reward, bubble-like overvaluation, or forced redemptions. Don't sell a superior compounder on valuation alone; its growth can far exceed your estimate.
- Never borrow, so the portfolio can survive a 50% drop. In his \~30 years the US market fell >50% several times, Berkshire 3–4 times, and Amazon 90% in 2001–02.
- Munger's "fish where the fish are": pick the small lakes a local expert knows, not the biggest one. "Lack of competition is an important reason for mispricing"; he sees many mispriced Chinese companies (Dec 2024).
- The "holy grail" is an enduring competitive advantage, vast growth and excellent returns on capital. Thoroughly researched and also cheap, he has seen it only "a few times" in 30 years.
- Path for young investors: start with the cheapest stocks so you can hold while learning, then move up to excellent companies. "There's no need to rush."
- His first 10-bagger (1993–94) had a $300M market cap vs $500M of book, $400M of it a listed TCI stake. The rest proved to be valuable wireless licenses, found only by researching after buying.
- Before trusting a low P/E, check whether earnings are at a cyclical peak, include one-time items, or are genuinely stable and sustainable.
- Crises make the returns: Himalaya launched into the 1997 Asian crisis, with markets down 70% or more. Graham, Keynes and Templeton also built their records amid depression and war.
- China (Dec 2024): consumption is \~40% of GDP vs India's 60% and the US's >70%, and savings rose to \~50%. Supply-side stimulus can't fix a demand problem, though policy has shifted notably since Sept 2024.
- Value investors are "definitely not parasites": they move prices toward long-term value and carry growth companies through crashes. He says BYD might otherwise have faced "a capital chain break".
