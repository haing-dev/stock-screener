### 67. Peter Thiel — Competition is for losers (The value investing channel, Feb 2022)

*Co-founder of PayPal and Palantir, founder of Founders Fund; a \~2014 lecture for Stanford's How to Start a Startup course, introduced by Sam Altman.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nSc0GoVRqtE)

**Takeaway:** A company's value is the value it creates (X) times the share it captures (Y%). The two are independent, so only monopolies keep lasting value, and durability, not growth, dominates that value.

- Airlines vs Google (2012): US airlines had $195bn of domestic revenue vs Google's \~$50bn. Yet cumulative airline profits over \~100 years are \~zero, and their market cap is about a quarter of Google's.
- There are only perfectly competitive businesses and monopolies, with "shockingly little" in between. "capitalism and competition are antonyms".
- Monopoly's financial signature: very high profit margins and cash piling up year after year (Apple, Google, Microsoft, Amazon). Screen on profitability and net cash, not revenue size.
- Market definitions lie: Google (\~66% of search) calls itself \~3.5% of \~$500bn advertising, while non-monopolists shrink their market to look unique. Measure share in the actual market.
- Four monopoly traits: proprietary technology \~10x better than the next best thing, network effects, economies of scale (high fixed, low marginal costs), and brand. Google had all four; now "three and a half".
- DCF math: PayPal (Mar 2001), growing 100% at a \~30% discount rate, got \~3/4 of its value from 2011 onward. Tech firms get 75–85% from 2024 onward (\~2014 view).
- So Silicon Valley overvalues growth rates, which are easy to measure, and undervalues durability, which is qualitative. Ask why it will still lead in 10–20 years.
- Be the last mover, not the first: Microsoft in operating systems, Google in search. "the better framing is you want to be the last mover you want to be the last company in a category"
- Start small and expand in concentric circles: Amazon in books, eBay in Pez dispensers, PayPal with 25–30% of \~20,000 eBay PowerSellers in 2–3 months.
- Red flags: big-market pitches (2005–2008 cleantech, "energy"), crowded categories (the 10th thin-film solar firm) and large existing markets such as \~$1trn restaurants.
- Fast innovation by others kills incumbents: 1980s disk-drive makers were 10x better, then replaced within two years. Consumers won, founders didn't.
- Historically Y ≈ 0 for science, railroads and textile mills (5–7% yearly efficiency gains, 1780–1850, competed away). Money came from vertically integrated monopolies (Ford, Tesla, SpaceX) and software.
- Software suits monopoly because its marginal cost is zero and adoption is fast. In a small market with slow adoption, rivals have time to enter.
- Crowds signal insanity, not wisdom: \~20,000 people move to LA yearly to be movie stars and \~20 succeed. He is skeptical of lean startup; great firms made a quantum improvement.
