### 68. Ryan Tolkin — Building Schonfeld into a global multi-manager (Generating Alpha Podcast, Dec 2025)

*CEO and CIO of Schonfeld Strategic Advisors (\~$17bn, Dec 2025); ex-Goldman Sachs corporate credit desk; became CIO at 27.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-S_S4tYymjk)

**Takeaway:** Scale makes a multi-manager work: outside capital pays for technology, data and overseas infrastructure and draws PMs who want bigger capital pools. But it took several hundred more people than the three or four Schonfeld expected.

- Three reasons for taking third-party capital (\~2015): fund technology, infrastructure and data; port US strategies overseas; attract risk-takers who want larger pools. All three have "largely played out".
- The miss was infrastructure: "we'd probably need, you know, maybe three or four additional people". They were off by several hundred, growing from one family's money to \~$17bn.
- 40–45% of the firm's risk is now outside the US, alongside big quant trading and macro businesses.
- Talent competition comes from quant firms, macro funds, multistrat peers, banks, and now big tech hiring engineers for AI.
- "our long-term talent retention strategy is our talent attraction strategy". The host says Schonfeld's PM turnover is among the industry's lowest; no figure given.
- Stress checklist for multi-managers: the GFC, the euro debt crisis, Q1 2016 factor volatility, COVID, and GameStop 2021, which exposed concentrated short books.
- Risk technology is an edge. On the Lehman weekend (2008), Goldman's Strats saw how its risk would shift, giving a huge Q4 2008 while rivals were still mapping exposures.
- Four ways to win among the giants: attract great people, add people who create scale, and decide unemotionally. Build resilience from lived experience of crises.
- Edge now lies in separating signal from noise, not in data access. AI cuts data-organising work that once took many people to a fraction of the time.
- Traders are both born and made: skill, drive and a lifelong ability to win matter, but mentors and repeatable process "meaningfully change the probabilities".
- No PM risk limits, capital-allocation rules, stop-outs or payout terms are disclosed.
