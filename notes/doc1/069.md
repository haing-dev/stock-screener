### 69. Bihua Chen — Inside biotech investing (Insightful Investor, Jul 2026)

*CEO and portfolio manager of Cormorant Asset Management (Boston), \~$2.5bn in a public long-short biotech strategy plus private funds (sixth just closed); molecular biologist investing in biotech since 1998.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Al14lbGkfA)

**Takeaway:** Long-term biotech value is "actually easy" (\~4x peak sales of the major drugs). The edge is beating consensus on whether a drug works: causal biology plus a technology that truly delivers it.

- Rule of thumb: biotech trades at \~4x peak sales potential of its major drugs. Pharma acquirers pay 2–3x to 6–8x, depending on uniqueness, patent strength and length of exclusivity.
- Mature drugs earn 80–90% gross and up to \~50% operating margins; compare the resulting free cash flow with S&P 500 names. Cross-stage comps ("project it out, discount it back") are the "less value added" part.
- Stocks already price the odds, so you must differ from consensus. If you put probability of success at 70% and the market at 30%, you make money when right.
- Rank hypotheses: an inherited missing gene is strongest, single-driver cancer genetics strong. Picking the key cytokine in inflammation is risky: "There you could really go wrong."
- "Every company will tell you something is causal, and most time it's not." Validation only comes in humans: an Alzheimer's antibiotic theory built on a correlation failed an adequately sized placebo-controlled trial.
- Ask whether failure came from wrong biology or technology that fell short (resveratrol misses blood levels; 99.999% protein binding leaves little free drug). A sharper molecule on proven biology becomes "an engineering problem", her favourite kind of project.
- Peak sales grow with time on drug, patients treated and price justified by value. Cancer drugs are billed monthly: \~$200,000 for one year on drug, \~$1M for five.
- Know the ceiling: ALK lung-cancer patients go \~7 years without progression, EGFR patients only \~1.5. Winners surprise on speed, probability or size of benefit, e.g. moving EGFR to 2–3 years.
- Of ten stocks that fall "from 20 to 2" on data, \~8 deserve it and \~2 don't (one later reached $40). Threshold-and-checklist sellers can't tell an imperfect early trial from a bad molecule.
- Weigh the time and capital needed to de-risk to the next round. Slowing a slow disease needs long placebo-controlled trials; she passes when capital is short and distrusts post-failure "retrospective signal finding".
- What doesn't work: baskets, connections, university ties or a CEO's past success. "Knowledge is different from judgment": a science degree is neither necessary nor sufficient; be skeptical but not cynical.
- Short-term prices follow sentiment, FDA views and rates, since biotechs are long-dated, cash-burning and financing-dependent. Valuations soared then crashed in 2020, yet for companies that delivered big drugs 2020 wasn't the price peak.
- Private entry is cheaper: pre-money $20–50M early, $300–500M later. Pharma may pay \~$10bn before approval for a drug that could sell $5bn a year, worth $20–25bn to the buyer.
- Once a pathway is validated, \~20 companies flood in. First-wave AI drug discovery "did not deliver" and its impact "has not been felt yet" (2026); the real bottleneck is slow trial enrollment.
