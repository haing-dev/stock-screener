### 70. Jim Flynn — Building a healthcare platform across public, private and venture (Value Investing with Legends, Jun 2026)

*Managing Partner of Deerfield Management, a $16bn healthcare investor with \~200 people; joined in 2000 and has led it since 2002 (officially 2005); earlier a Furman Selz pharma analyst and Alpharma corporate-development VP.* [Watch](http://valueinvestingwithlegends.libsyn.com/jim-flynn-building-a-healthcare-investment-platform-across-public-private-and-venture)

**Takeaway:** Deerfield underwrites everything, from public stocks to early discovery, with "long-term cash flow based probabilistic models". It stays instrument-agnostic, choosing and then changing how it holds each idea (equity, secured debt plus warrants, company creation, outright control) as conditions shift.

- Public or private, every position is valued the same way, which lets Deerfield compare risk and return across security types. Even long-term drug discovery "is really in a value context."
- Favourite structure is senior secured debt plus warrants: one part "protects your basis", the other keeps the upside skew. Split downside protection from upside whenever possible.
- Melinta: COVID nearly bankrupted this hospital anti-infective borrower, so Deerfield credit-bid, ran it six years and sold it for >$300M. Sales rose from $60M to $125M and EBITDA \[?\] from −$30M to +$25M.
- New antibiotics are "a terrible business model" because hospitals hold the best ones in reserve. Melinta was reframed as a hospital company, adding products (e.g. an antifungal) sold through the same formulary channel.
- Venture biotech "had been bad for decades": \~90% of bets lose ≥$50M, often hundreds of millions, as fixed-cost companies chase the next idea. Deerfield's Cure/3DC labs charge funds marginal cost, because "if we can fail cheap, this is the best business model ever".
- Stack probability of success: the right genetic association doubles it (per ex-GSK genetics head Matt Nelson). Antibodies are twice as likely to work as small molecules, so together \~4x, at marginal cost.
- Nuvalent: $2M to test-tube proof, Deerfield-only through animal proof, then a \~$400M round and $800M IPO as the bubble ended. Best-in-class ROS1/ALK \[?\] lung-cancer drugs filed; \~$10bn company, +$2.5bn to funds (Jun 2026).
- The public side's biggest question is obsolescence ("What's coming behind it"), which the private team answers. Public analysts track every potential acquirer (pharma, medtech, insurers), Washington and the conferences.
- Nursing homes (host: Deerfield's second-largest position): COVID cut occupancy to \~60%, but \~1M people turn 80 yearly and supply is flat to down. Brookdale rose from 64% to 84%; above 80–90% occupancy, pricing jumps.
- "If someone comes up with something transformative, people pay for it." With 8M Alzheimer's patients costing $200,000 a year each, a $100bn drug could still save the system money.
- Generics take 98% of volume within a month of patent expiry, so branded revenue at expiry is close to all-or-nothing. The 20-year-patent-then-almost-free US system is "amazing".
- Software is "hard to invest in" because its obsolescence risk can't be modeled; hard-to-replicate data is the moat. Example: the HSS partnership (millions of records, 25 years of follow-up, 4M scans).
- AI already helps earliest-stage discovery but has "yet to really transform" clinical trials, the costliest step. In care delivery, LLMs on rare disease will make "the consumer the dominant force".
- Stay instrument-agnostic, since rates flip from 10% to 0% and markets from value to bubble. "Science is just a language"; the core skill is good probabilistic assessment and choosing what to learn next.
