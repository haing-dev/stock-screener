### 72. Dave Johnson — Evolus and Caligan's life-sciences activist playbook (Yet Another Value Podcast, Sep 2022)

*Founder and CIO of Caligan Partners, a small/mid-cap life-sciences value investor that engages as an activist; ex-private equity; largest holder and 13D filer in four companies.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HHH7uJVaURw)

**Takeaway:** Caligan avoids scientific risk: it buys life-sciences companies whose approved, durable assets exceed enterprise value (EV), then pulls activist levers. Evolus's aesthetics-only toxin Jeuveau escapes Botox's government-pricing limits and grew \~50% vs a 12–13% market, yet traded at \~2.5x forward revenue (Sep 2022).

- Caligan's rule: "we don't take a lot of scientific risk". Therapeutics must be at Phase 3 or later, approved durable assets should exceed EV, and pipelines count only as call options.
- Targets have good assets and balance sheets but underperform for reasons fixable in 1–2 years. Three levers: capital allocation, strategic direction, and board or management changes.
- Capital went from "seemingly infinite" (\~2020 raises at 52-week highs) to "dried up" (Sep 2022), so "focus on your winners starve your losers". Forced management change is rare but was "transformative" at AMAG \[?\].
- Management is told "companies are bought not sold": build profitable growth around a differentiated product rather than angle for a sale.
- Pricing edge: Botox's 30+ therapeutic uses, many paid by Medicare/Medicaid, mean Allergan "can't charge anybody less than you charge the government". Aesthetics-only Jeuveau can discount, run loyalty rewards and co-brand billboards with practices.
- Practice economics: Jeuveau lists at about $375 vs Botox $500–550 (about $325 after rewards). Practices earn \~$400 vs \~$350 per treatment, \~80% vs \~65–70% margin, helped by free-vial volume discounts.
- Durability without patents: each toxin comes from an unreplicable trade-secret cell bank, so there's no generic cliff (typical pharma loses \~90% of revenue). The price is lower margins than small molecules.
- Royalty catalyst: an ITC trade-secret case (Daewoong vs Medytox/Allergan) once pulled Jeuveau off the market. The settlement royalty steps from mid-teens to \~5% in Q4 2022, adding \~1,000bps of gross margin.
- Numbers (Sep 2022): \~$150M revenue pace, \~50% growth, in \~25% of potential accounts, at \~2.5x forward revenue vs \~4x for aesthetics peers. A 20% share means \~$500M revenue; profitability "quarters away".
- SG&A rising with revenue isn't a red flag if it is a fixed, guided base: \~$35M a quarter, marketing ≤15% of sales. New accounts pay off with a lag; cash-flow neutral by Q2 2022.
- Pharmakon's $125M non-dilutive loan ($75M drawn for settlement payments, $50M delayed draw for M&A) got Caligan comfortable. Bolt-ons must be differentiated, not me-too fillers.
- Recession: in 2008–09 the category fell only low single digits as patients stretched intervals. Trade-down favours Jeuveau (four treatments for the cost of three Botox); \~40% of a quarter's patients are repeat users.
- Read rival labels closely: Daxxify's six months is a median at double the dose, tested against placebo, not head-to-head. Its smaller molecule spreads more ("Frozen face"), so expect a slower aesthetic launch than the market does.
- Entrants grow the category rather than start a price war, since rivals with therapeutic uses share Botox's pricing constraint. US penetration is mid-single digits vs mid-20s % in Korea; "toxins are beachfront ... real estate" that consolidators need.
