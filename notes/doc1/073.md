### 73. Dave Johnson — MorphoSys: royalty model turned US-style biotech (Yet Another Value Podcast, Jun 2023)

*Founder and CIO of Caligan Partners, a small/mid-cap life-sciences value investor that engages as an activist; Caligan owned 3%+ of MorphoSys.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0m_AiBEd7Mk)

**Takeaway:** Swapping a European royalty model for US-style biotech drove out MorphoSys's holders and pushed its EV negative (Dec 2022). At \~€27 (Jun 2023), cash, approved drugs and milestones covered over half the price, so Phase 3 pelabresib cost <13/share vs \~45 risk-adjusted.

- Pipeline value per share = peak sales × precedent deal multiple × probability of success (PoS) ÷ shares. Pelabresib: \~1.1bn × \~2x (average of four myelofibrosis deals) × 78% gives \~45/share.
- Peak sales build: a price of multiple tens of thousands of dollars per month and only half of newly diagnosed patients. Duration copies Jakafi's dropout (\~30% in year one, \~50% by year two), with no credit for longer durability.
- Floor = net cash after burn to the readout + approved-product NPV + royalties + probability-weighted milestones, covering >50% of the price. Caligan wants \~3:1 up/down: "you're paying 13 for something that we think is worth 45".
- Mispricing came from investor-base mismatch: Europeans liked diversified royalties, US investors like binary pipeline risk. Selling the royalties ($1.4bn, 2021) to buy a US biotech drove out \~16 of the top 20 holders.
- Sell-side PoS sat at 50–60%, set by analysts from the royalty era. Caligan's 78% is a haircut on the \~97.5% implied if Phase 2 replicates; even analysts' own inputs gave values above their targets.
- Regulatory milestones ($535M from partner Incyte) pay on approval whatever the drug sells, and Incyte funds 55% of development. Monjuvi swung from \~$20M quarterly losses to profit (Q4 2022) after an expense reset.
- Read Phase 2 against standard of care (SOC): pelabresib plus Jakafi hit 68% spleen response vs \~40% for Jakafi alone. The confidence-interval lower bound (\~57%) beat SOC, and an add-on must bring no extra anemia or platelet toxicity.
- Trial signals generalists miss: Phase 3 upsized from 300 to 400 to "overpower" the endpoint, and enrolled \~430 six months early during COVID. Also check years-long Phase 2 durability and call investigators.
- Two risks: your drug fails, or "competitors drugs maybe work better". AbbVie's rival navitoclax rests on a 32-patient Phase 2 with \~1/3 grade 3/4 low platelets and no real symptom gain.
- Patent-cliff M&A: big pharma has $140bn of revenue losing patents in the late 2020s, so boards make managers "justify not doing deals". The natural buyer is the partner facing expiry: Incyte, with Jakafi off patent in 2028.
- Launch lesson: Monjuvi was the first new second-line lymphoma drug in a decade, but five more followed, including same-target CAR-T. It ended in a third-line "knife fight", not a $1bn blockbuster.
- Activism via a formulaic A/B/C deck: have you underperformed, why, what to do. Within \~6 weeks MorphoSys bought back \~25% of its discounted convert and cut early R&D, "a lot less work than running a proxy fight".
- Convert math: the zero-coupon bond (€120 conversion price) traded in the low 60s, and buying it back at 72 earns a 22% IRR. The company bought \~a quarter at 64.
- Between readouts stocks sit in a "catalyst desert" and wake \~6 months before data. Phase 3 topline to approval takes \~1 year (Dec 2023 data, approval \~end-2024).
