### 74. Panel: Jim Flynn, Rod, Eli and Annie — The healthcare system of tomorrow (Greenwich Economic Forum, Nov 2021)

*Jim Flynn (Deerfield Management); Rod Wong \[?\] (RTW Investments), therapeutics investor who created Rocket Pharma; Eli Casdin \[?\] (Casdin Capital), public-private life-sciences investor (Sema4, EQRx); Annie Lamont \[?\] (Oak HC/FT), private healthcare-services investor.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PoNXB85e_uU)

**Takeaway:** As of Nov 2021 the panel agrees biology, data and care delivery are converging but splits on drug pricing. Flynn's base rate (academic ideas succeed \~2.5% of the time) argues for marginal-cost discovery with fast kills, funded by savings from value-based care.

- Flynn: \~95% of drug ideas fail and \~50% of academic data won't reproduce, so academic ideas succeed only \~2.5% of the time.
- Flynn: discovery must run at marginal cost (25 institutions, 35 senior scientists), killing failing projects fast. Otherwise, "if you create a company around it it's going to want to live".
- Flynn: managed-care members turn over \~every 3 years, and generics reach 95%+ substitution within 6 months of patent loss. Pay innovators for 20 years and the inventions stay "with us forever for almost nothing".
- Flynn: admits "abuses" in drug pricing; TNF-inhibitor class prices "inch up not down" despite competition, and Congress is eyeing such classes. Flag growth that relies on price rises in old, crowded classes.
- Flynn: biology keeps splitting diseases into smaller niches, creating "lots of small solutions that cost huge amounts of money", which he finds frustrating.
- Flynn: practices must be bought and converted to value-based care, since their own economics don't allow it. Deerfield is buying cardiology practices and rolling up primary care for BCBS North Carolina (\~60% share).
- Rod: getting drug-pricing reform wrong would "break the innovation ecosystem in the u.s and it can absolutely go somewhere else." About 3/4 of breakthrough therapies come from US companies.
- Rod: drug modalities went from 3 to 6 to 9, and fast projects need under half the old 15 years. So investors will span the whole life cycle, as with Rocket Pharma, built from orphaned academic gene-therapy programs.
- Rod: talent is the binding constraint; investors add value by seeing "more reps at less depth." A former AI skeptic, he is now convinced by closed-loop insulin pumps (Tandem, Beta Bionics).
- Eli: four US PD-1 drugs are priced within 5% of each other; China has ten at a tenth of the cost. That deflation is "coming here" via EQRx-style payer partnerships for late-to-market equivalents without sales forces.
- Eli: "i don't think drug pricing actually is the problem"; hospital care and mismanaged late-stage disease are. He backs only products "better cheaper to the system", where a price premium lowers total cost.
- Eli: public biotech is "all early stage investing"; without private stakes you hear "half the story". Biotech is a young industry, so big-pharma managers often struggle in small biotechs.
- Annie: "technology is not the issue"; payment reform and behaviour are. Primary care drives \~70% of spend, \~80% of care could happen at home, and hospital-owned primary care costs \~10% more.
- Annie (Nov 2021): Medicare Advantage, with an 8-year average member life, is the "playpen" for value-based care; direct contracting's regional benchmark resists up-coding. But CMMI is "frozen" and hospital systems are politically hard to challenge.
