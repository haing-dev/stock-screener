### 75. Stanley Druckenmiller — "It's always about liquidity": the Fed, emerging markets and how he sizes (Real Vision, Nov 2018)

*Macro investor; ran Duquesne and was George Soros's partner at Quantum, now runs a family office; 30%+ compounded over 30 years with no down year and 5 losing quarters out of 120.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G-MlrpoMig0)

**Takeaway:** Liquidity drives his calls: in 2018 he expected central-bank buying, falling from \~$1 trillion a year to zero, to break markets, starting with emerging markets. He bets big only where he can get out fast.

- The creed: "it's never been about politics it's always about liquidity." He measures liquidity as the rate of change of global central-bank balance sheets (Fed + ECB + BOJ).
- 2018 puzzle: central-bank buying of \~$1 trillion a year was heading to zero within 12 months, with Fed runoff at $50bn a month. Margins were at a record and valuations at the top of their range except against rates.
- Tightening breaks things in order: emerging markets first ("where it always starts"), then credit, then developed-market equities. there was "no more egregious recipient of free money than emerging markets"; Argentina sold 100-year debt at 7% (2018).
- Credit was the next shoe (2018): "hardly any bankruptcies" in the most disruptive period since the 1880s–1890s, with many zombies. Policy "tripled down on what caused the crisis", so the next one could be bigger.
- Big bets only in very liquid, ideally 24-hour markets (bonds, currencies), where he can change his mind. Beyond his $800,000 start, he never put 30–40% of a fund in one equity; \~70% of his profits came from currencies and bonds.
- Entry rule: start with \~1/3 of a position, add on a technical signal, and be in "no later than the second inning". Euro 2014: started short at 1.39, got braver below 1.35.
- Exception, go big at once when the market hands you a mispricing: in 1989 the consensus said reunification would hurt the Deutsche mark. He bet on the inflation-obsessed Bundesbank and bought very big after a 3–4% drop.
- Exception, fundamentals over price (Sept 2000): oil, the dollar and rates all rising, and a regression put S&P EPS at −25% vs the Street's +18%. Fifty gloomy small-business calls confirmed it; he bought very large 2- and 5-year Treasury positions, adding on every hawkish Greenspan speech.
- Know whether you're hot or cold: Las Vegas gamblers lose by betting big when cold. When down, he "had not earned the right to play big", overriding that only for once-in-a-lifetime set-ups like 2000.
- His 2018 book was long disruptors, short the disrupted. Cloud at 10x sales de-rating to 6x loses money even if forecasts are met, and Alibaba beat every estimate yet fell 210→165.
- He held Google at \~20x earnings (\~15x after cash), Campbell's Soup's multiple, as the AI leader "under earning" on bets it could switch off (2018).
- Algos broke his 35-year discipline of price action versus news: pharma went from worst group to best in May 2018 with no news. So he cuts risk sooner and uses machines only as an alert, citing Kasparov's human-plus-machine.
- Fed rule: "just sneak one in every time you can", hiking every meeting until financial disruption. With unemployment at 3.8%, a visitor from Mars would guess Fed funds of 4–5%, not 1.75% (2018).
- Zero rates remove capitalism's hurdle rate and fuel debt, and Greenspan's late-1990s restraint was "the original sin". Free money "pretty much squished" his bond and currency edge, so he refocused on equities.
