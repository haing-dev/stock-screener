### 76. Stanley Druckenmiller — "The Fed should pause": his leading-indicator dashboard (Bloomberg Television, Dec 2018)

*Macro investor; ran Duquesne at \~30% a year for 30 years and says it predicted the last four recessions; no outside clients for \~8 years. Recorded Dec 2018, uploaded Jan 2020.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kH01CNISeQ)

**Takeaway:** In Dec 2018 the long-time Fed hawk argued for a pause, not a cut, because his leading indicators had turned "amber". A wrong hike would cost "five to ten X" a wrong pause.

- His dashboard, in order: the inside of the stock market, the yield curve, credit, liquidity (QT, M2), autos and housing, then commodities. He discounts coincident GDP and treats unemployment as lagging.
- "the best economists I know is the inside of the stock market": in Dec 2018 a −13% S&P hid autos −30%, builders −35%, banks −25%. Small caps and retail were down over 20%, while utilities, staples and pharma rose.
- Curve and credit (Dec 2018): 2s/5s slightly inverted (2.69% vs 2.68%). GE credit default swaps (CDS) went from 50 to 200bp and IBM's from 30 to 80bp; AIG fell 65→35 and Blackstone 25% in a month.
- Liquidity (Dec 2018): $50bn a month of QT tightens even without Fed selling, because deficits mean constant bond issuance. M2 growth fell from \~7.5% to 4%: "not even the level it's the rate of change".
- Debt diagnosis, 2010–2018: corporate non-financial debt rose \~60% ($6T→$9.6T) and corporate earnings 27%, yet S&P EPS rose \~60% via debt-funded buybacks and M&A. Distrust per-share growth funded by rising net debt.
- The asymmetry: a wrong pause costs a little credibility; a wrong hike on top of QT costs 5–10x more. His "sneak one in" rule applies only in booming financial conditions, aiming for \~3%.
- Drop forward guidance "completely": it ties the Fed's hands, and "You build your credibility by being data dependent." Low unemployment usually precedes recessions, so it is a poor policy indicator.
- Outlook (Dec 2018): S&P returns of 0–5% a year for five years. A global bear market of \~a year could run 3–5 years sideways or down; worried about, but not predicting, a recession.
- His bear-market money comes from Treasuries, not stock shorts: the 2-year went from 6.04% in fall 2000 to \~1.5–2%. In Dec 2018 he was long 2s, 5s and 10s at \~2.68%: "I like the risk reward".
- Pairs (Dec 2018): short credit hedged by long Treasuries, as high yield paid 5–6% for paper that "normally would be eight or nine". Short financials, because they need rising rates, and no longs that merely fall less.
- Long cloud (Microsoft, ServiceNow, Workday, Salesforce) as 20–30% growers "almost better than a bond" in slow growth, bought on October 2018 limit orders. A 9x→7x sales de-rating still cost him 1% while 20–30% net short.
- Take only asymmetric bets: the 1992 pound was flat or +15–20%. Brexit was ±10% and he couldn't call it, so he passed (Dec 2018).
- "I've always believed markets are smarter than I am", yet algos broke price versus news. Facebook fell from 131 to 116 after great earnings and hit \~220 a year later, so he leans on 8–9-month signals and fundamentals.
- Industry view: 9,000 hedge funds charging 20%, only 10–20 great, and $3 trillion too much chasing alpha. Good managers need open minds and courage to buy at lows while "scared to death".
