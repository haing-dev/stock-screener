### 77. Stanley Druckenmiller — Sizing, streaks and a bear market without the bond hedge (Sohn Conference Foundation, Jun 2022)

*Runs Duquesne and formerly ran Soros's Quantum Fund; 45 years as a CIO, \~30% net a year in his client years and higher returns in bear markets. Interviewed by John Collison of Stripe.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-7sWLIybWnQ)

**Takeaway:** Sizing is "70 to 80% of the equation": concentrate, press when hot, and buy a promising idea before the research is done. Jun 2022 view: a bear market with "a ways to run", a likely 2023 recession, and no bond hedge, so he stepped aside.

- "sizing is probably 70 to 80% of the equation." What he learned from Soros was not currency forecasting but how much you make when right and lose when wrong.
- Concentrate: 15–20% of assets in one position, or 200–300% in macro positions, forces "ruthless discipline" and daily re-checking. The real danger is "stale longs or stale shorts", not concentration.
- Invest, then investigate: since \~2000 the industry is crowded, so waiting 2–3 weeks can cost "60 or 70% of the move". If an idea fits his macro view, he may buy a very large position first.
- The analyst then has 2–3 weeks to do the work: add if the story holds, exit if not. It's asymmetric, since with no story priced in a wrong call exits near flat after \~10 days.
- Size up other people's streaks too: follow hot technicians, fade badly cold ones, and judge analysts' runs in their area. When hot, "turn the dial way up" rather than coasting after +40%.
- Read the economy inside the stock market: "stocks tend to lead the fundamentals by somewhere between 6 and 12 months." Housing and retail lead; capital goods lag. In Jun 2022, homebuilders were −50% and trucking −40% despite record earnings.
- Invest in change: picture where prices will trade 18–24 months out, because "The present is not what moves stock prices. Change moves them." His biggest equity gains came from companies far better placed 3–5 years out.
- Capacity cycle, from his chemicals days: buy when an industry loses money and shuts capacity over 2–3 years; beware flush industries announcing expansions. Margins mean-revert within \~3 years.
- Over-earners made his best short-selling period: COVID-boosted retailers with 15 years of no growth whose stocks quadrupled, and trucking. In Jun 2022 it was ocean shipping on "massive margins"; Big Tech wasn't the true over-earner.
- Macro (Jun 2022): there has never been a soft landing after inflation above 4.5%, and inflation above 5% has never been tamed without recession. With Fed funds at 0.75% and CPI \~8%, he "pretty strongly" expected a 2023 recession.
- No bond hedge (Jun 2022): he usually buys Treasuries in bear markets, but 8% inflation with 3% yields was "an analog with no precedent". He cut shorts after 60–70% falls in quality stocks and waited for "a fat pitch".
- Would re-short after a 15–20% rally, since "6-month bear markets preceded by asset bubbles don't exist historically" (Jun 2022). Rising oil, rates and the dollar together, his 2000 earnings warning, looked "a little familiar".
- Positions (Jun 2022): long energy on a 5–10-year supply shortage, though it was no longer "a classic Duquesne play". Also long copper and, "mistakenly", gold; he expected to short the dollar within 6 months.
- He manages a known bearish bias: macro investors profit from chaos, yet great short sellers make 90% of their money on the long side. Bitcoin suits an inflationary bull market, gold a bear or stagflation market.
