### 79. Stanley Druckenmiller — Second-wave inflation, a bond short and the 1992 pound (NBIM, Nov 2024)

*Macro investor, 71; ran Dreyfus funds, Soros's Quantum Fund (\~$7.5bn in 1992) and Duquesne, with no down year despite significant drawdowns within years. Interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-5Weeox0Xus)

**Takeaway:** In Oct 2024 he flipped from fearing recession ("completely wrong") to fearing a 1970s-style second inflation wave, shorting bonds the day the Fed cut 50bp. His record rests on changing his mind when wrong and betting really big when convinced.

- "I think my record is mainly because when I'm wrong I changed my mind not that I'm always right." If the reason for owning a stock is gone, he sells regardless of cost, and will buy back higher.
- Cost basis is "absolutely irrelevant". Chart resistance is just holders who bought at 60 waiting years to get back; "once I'm out I'm out".
- Soros's lesson: "when you have conviction you should bet really big", especially in liquid markets. Druckenmiller had the higher batting average; Soros had the higher slugging percentage and more courage on size.
- The 1992 pound: a peg tying a booming Germany to a failing UK was a one-way bet costing 0.5% over 6 months. He started at $1.5bn (20–25% of Quantum's \~$7.5bn), well before any catalyst.
- When the Bundesbank head said the currencies should no longer be linked, he went to 100%; Soros pushed for 200%. They got \~$7.5bn done before the peg broke, within hours.
- Follow the dominoes: he then bought gilts, against the academic view that a weak currency forces rates up, plus UK stocks, and both rose. The same peg playbook worked in the krona and the Thai baht.
- Biggest regret: a massive 2-year Treasury short from 15bp in 2021, mostly covered at \~150bp because he anchored on where yields had been. Yields went to \~500bp.
- Macro from the bottom up (Oct 2024): companies saw no weakness except housing, and financial conditions stayed loose. Cutting 50bp "with credit spreads tight gold at new highs equity's roaring" made him nervous.
- The 1970s template: inflation fell from \~8 to 3 and then rose again, and the month count put the bottom "right about now" (Oct 2024). Tariffs, deregulation and lower immigration under a Trump win could re-ignite it.
- The 10-year can trade to nominal GDP: 6–7% if inflation hits 4–4.5% with 2.5–3% real growth, versus \~4.5% then (Oct 2024). He was \~25% of NAV short 10-year equivalents and "should have been much bigger".
- Fiscal (Oct 2024): deficits of \~7% of GDP at full employment can't last, and only reserve-currency status shields the US. Corporate debt rolls over in 2025–26; his best-guess trigger is a failed auction.
- Equities (Oct 2024): no bear market has started without narrowing leadership, so it was "a yellow light it's not a red light". Apple at 25–30x earnings without 25–30% growth was rich.
- AI (Oct 2024): very bullish but neutral, as 4–5 model builders spend heavily and he sees no winner-take-all. He sold Nvidia at 800–900 once it passed $2tn and got no second chance, though its 4,000 software engineers gave it staying power.
- Lilly: weight regain after stopping obesity drugs made it "a razor blade business"; he sold in the high 700s. His horizon is "two to four years stuff", not 20-year holds.
