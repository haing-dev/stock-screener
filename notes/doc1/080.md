### 80. Stanley Druckenmiller — Hard lessons: invest, then investigate (Morgan Stanley, Feb 2026)

*Ran Duquesne Capital Management at \~30% annualized with no losing years (1981–2010); now runs the Duquesne family office with his own capital; 30 years on Memorial Sloan Kettering's board.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z_pk4eBDaLA)

**Takeaway:** "My advantage is not IQ, it's trigger pulling." He bets on how investors will come to see a change: Teva re-rated from 6x to \~12x, and Nvidia was bought small, then doubled twice.

- Teva (late summer/fall 2025): a generic drugmaker at 6x earnings whose new CEO, Richard Francis, was rerunning his Sandoz playbook. He cut costs and pivoted to biosimilars and a non-generic drug.
- The shareholder mismatch was the opportunity: value investors sold because of the growth strategy, and growth investors waited for proof. The stock went 16→32 in \~6–7 months, re-rating to \~11.5–12x with little news.
- Screen for change, not cheapness: "If you look at today, you're not going to make any money." A low P/E plus a proven new CEO and a mix shift toward growth lines is the quantifiable part.
- Nvidia sizing path (late 2022): "I bought a not big position in Nvidia, but enough to get hurt on or to make some money on." He doubled after ChatGPT \~2 weeks later, then doubled again after an analyst said macro was "missing the forest".
- He bought without knowing Nvidia's earnings, on "total trust in my partner" and on Stanford students shifting from crypto to AI. Specialists' enthusiasm is "as important to me as the actual facts".
- His holding failure: at \~390 he said it "can't not go up for at least three years". He sold at \~800 because "I couldn't stand seeing success"; it hit \~1400 five weeks later.
- Biotech pivot (2025–26): AI had become "disturbingly heated", rhyming with 1999–2000. Biotech had been down \~4 years with momentum turning, and AI's best use is drug discovery and diagnostics (his MSK view).
- Horizon is 18 months to 3 years, yet "I put on a 3-year trade that 5 days later I'm out of and I've reversed". Volatility against a long-horizon view is an entry point.
- "Contrarianism is overrated": per Soros, the crowd is right 80% of the time. Crowding doesn't matter "if I think the thesis is right and the trend is with me", except for entry points.
- His old tools were "loved to death": technical analysis is "about 20% as effective today". Price versus news faded after \~2000 once "every wise guy learned" it; there's no silver bullet.
- Macro (early 2026): stimulus is making a strong economy stronger, a Fed cut is likely, and US valuations are near historic highs. Macro, "dead for 10 or 15 years", is back.
- Book (early 2026): eclectic equities with AI no longer the engine, plus big Japan and Korea positions. He is short the dollar (foreigners "way, way overloaded"), long copper on 8 years of tight supply, and holds gold as a geopolitical hedge.
- Short bonds as a hedge "matrix" (early 2026): break-even if growth stays disinflationary, profitable if the Fed cuts into a boom and inflation takes off. It lets him hold the risk assets.
- Nerve and emotion: he has more wisdom but less courage than in his 30s–40s ("Mr. Taco ... it's Daco"). Mistakes recur, so stop torturing yourself after \~48 hours and move on.
