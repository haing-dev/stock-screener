### 81. Jan van Eck — Macro forces shape markets (Insightful Investor, Jul 2026)

*CEO of VanEck ($230bn AUM), founded by his father in 1955; the first ETF firm to file for a Bitcoin ETF (2017).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Luz1PUx2nI)

**Takeaway:** Outside forces (deregulation, fiscal and monetary policy, technology, war, demographics) drive markets, so read them first and use valuation only at extremes. In Jul 2026 that means mostly equities, \~10% real assets instead of some bonds, and more international exposure, especially India.

- Four macro signals: deregulation and rule of law, fiscal stance, monetary stance, and technology, the hardest to read. A 6%-of-GDP deficit is "putting two feet on the gas pedal".
- Stay fully invested in equities through cycles. Cut exposure only when the Fed is slamming the brakes (2022, 1% to 4%); he expected no 2026 cuts as inflation leaves no room.
- Allocation (Jul 2026): 60–70% equities, \~10% real assets, the rest bonds, with "very very little science". He is biased against 60/40 and rebalances quarterly or on price triggers.
- Rebalancing paid off: in 2025 VanEck moved gold profits into oil at $55–60, which worked in 2026.
- Gold is his best hedge against US government spending, but not against a liquidation. If 10-year rates jump 200 bp with lost confidence, nothing goes up, not even gold.
- Valuation only at extremes: Nvidia at 30–50x sales, and SpaceX's IPO at \~100x sales ("there's no valuation support for that stock"). Cheapness alone, as in international markets, usually doesn't work.
- Valuation as support: listed alternative managers went from \~10x forward P/E at IPO to \~40x (summer 2025). They are back at \~10x, with Blue Owl \[?\] below its IPO price.
- Disruptive technology plus rising prices isn't automatically a bubble; market structure decides (railroads, Nvidia's ecosystem moat). The AI cycle will still have booms and busts.
- AI second-order plays must show a moat and visible profits 3 years into the trend: nuclear and power, and supply-constrained copper and metals.
- Theme stocks with no meaningful P/E or P/S, like drone names, get a toe-in-the-water position at most.
- Check true diversification: the Mag 7 top growth, value, momentum and quality ETFs alike, and private software marks follow public SaaS.
- India is the most underappreciated 10-year shift (2026). It has the fastest GDP growth, is underweight in indices, has a non-dollarized rupee, and is cheap after the Iran war and US tariffs.
- The secular fall in US long rates, from the high teens (early 1980s) to 1% (COVID), is over. 40-year backtests carry a falling-rate bias.
