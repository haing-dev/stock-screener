### 82. Alex Sacerdote — Riding S-curves at Whale Rock (Capital Allocators, Mar 2025)

*Founder of Whale Rock Capital Management, a Boston tech investor started in 2006 with $8B AUM; former Fidelity tech sector PM.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qe4MawRkrxs)

**Takeaway:** Find a technology at its S-curve's "mainstream takeoff" and own the one or two firms with airtight moats, valued on earnings 3–5 years out. Unit growth plus rising margins makes earnings grow exponentially, so a stock that looks expensive on next year's P/E can be "crazily cheap".

- "when you get scurve and a rising margin your earnings don't grow linearly they grow exponentially". Tesla (end-2019/2020) and Nvidia (Jan 2023) were bought at \~4x the earnings they made 2–3 years later.
- Takeoff starts when adoption barriers fall: price, complexity, a missing ecosystem or inertia. The $200 iPhone took smartphones from 1% to 50% of people in 4–5 years.
- Penetration typically runs 1% → 3% → 6%, and growth turns from exponential to linear at \~30–40%. The curve shows how long you can hold, so "sometimes it's okay to be late".
- Moats: the operating system, network effects (LinkedIn), e-commerce scale and logistics, platforms, and critical IP (Qualcomm's 3–4% royalty, ASML). Without one "you're a zero", like RIM, Palm and Nokia.
- "the biggest reason people blow up in Tech is they miss on revenue". Companies rocketing up the curve usually over-deliver on sales, so big positions at 10/10 conviction reduce risk.
- Four tenets: S-curve, competitive advantage, underappreciated earnings power, plus a "super leader" with an innovation culture. Few firms jump to the next curve (Tencent, Amazon), so those deserve a premium.
- AI (early 2025): invest in infrastructure first, because it inflects first, is needed "no matter who wins above", and its winners are analyzable. Models and apps resemble Internet 1.0's 7–8 search engines.
- AI infrastructure was only \~14% penetrated and \~3–4% within the cloud (early 2025). Reasoning models' up-to-1,000-to-1 inference ratio, cemented by DeepSeek, is bullish for compute.
- Shorts work at every stage: mature and disrupted (media vs Netflix), or in-curve but moatless (most EV makers). Or too early, like a battery maker that built capacity "10 years too early".
- Don't reflexively short old tech. Disk-drive duopolies "hang on" and may be priced for extinction with \~15 years left.
- EV lesson: US adoption stalled at \~10% against a 50–60% thesis. Tesla went from 10/10 to 0/10 conviction as its earnings power went "from being really cheap to expensive"; they exited.
- Cloud sizing: they assumed 50% deflation on $600B of legacy IT spend when AWS had $1B of revenue. No deflation came, so the market is \~$600B with 5–10 years to run (early 2025).
- Mag 7 concentration reflects winner-take-most platforms, not a bubble (early 2025). Amazon at 25x next year's GAAP EPS was cheaper than Walmart and Costco; Meta \~22x, Microsoft \~26x.
- Portfolio: up to 10% at cost, allowed to run to 15%; top 10 = 60%; 4–6 S-curves, AI infrastructure maybe 20%. Privates \~10% of capital, mostly Stripe, Canva, Databricks and Revolut.
