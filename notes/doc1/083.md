### 83. Alex Sacerdote — Why the AI boom is just getting started (Invest Like The Best, Jun 2026)

*Founder of Whale Rock, a Boston tech investor running long/short, long-only, private and new Mega Cap Tech funds; former Fidelity internet analyst.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZt1DDmMNGk)

**Takeaway:** AI is an "L curve": enterprise application AI is "less than 1% penetrated" and compute is already sold out (spring 2026). Whale Rock owns the three-player model oligopoly, led by Anthropic, and hardware suppliers that are no longer commoditized, and is net short application software.

- Anthropic, his highest conviction: Whale Rock passed at $60B (negative gross margins), then invested at $180B in Aug 2025 after meeting Dario. Revenue went $100m → $1bn → \~$9bn; "a total home run".
- Models are a "three-horse race" (Anthropic in enterprise, OpenAI in consumer, Google), not commodities. Critical IP and a "harness" ecosystem build lock-in, like AWS in 2013; \~50–60 model startups died.
- Coding alone is about $0.5trn: about $100 a day of tokens (about $20–30k a year) × \~20m coders worldwide.
- Spring 2026: infrastructure \~10% penetrated; advanced users \~10 bp of knowledge workers, expected to reach \~15% in four years. Anthropic has "half of what they need" in compute.
- AI "rule of 40" for chips: AI share of sales + market share in that category (30% + 30% = 60). Rising share (10% → 30%) matters most because it accelerates growth and margins.
- Hardware is decommoditizing: AI workloads grow \~10x a year, versus 25–40% that Moore's Law used to absorb. Every part is pushed to physical limits, so units grow and innovation is forced.
- Celestica, \~3 years ago: the sole Google TPU server supplier at \~8x earnings, with 50–60% of cloud Ethernet switches. On a $200–300k liquid-cooled server it is "like selling a critical part on a plane".
- AI servers need \~40-layer circuit boards vs \~10, from few suppliers (Whale Rock owns laminate maker Elite Materials). Their growth jumped from \~5% to a 35–50% CAGR for four years with rising margins.
- On shortages in boards and memory: "So even if it is a commodity it's going to be a great cycle." Corning gains as GPU links move to fiber; power-supply ASPs rise \~40% a year (Delta, Advanced Energy).
- Software went from 40–50% of the portfolio \~5 years ago to net short entering 2026. Red flags: AI at 1–2% of sales (Salesforce \~$40bn vs $500–700m AI ARR), budgets shifting to tokens, seat cuts.
- Sell at \~30–40% penetration. Selling Apple in 2012 at \~50% US smartphone penetration was a mistake: it kept compounding \~20%, though the big gains came at 0–50%.
- At inflections "you can't trust the data" (Andy Grove), so use anecdotes, like AWS packing Gartner's ballroom. If the curve is tall, "It's okay to miss the first 100%."
- Risks (spring 2026): backlash ("Maine just banned data centers"), a plateau causing an open-source "race to the bottom", or a failed lab stranding compute. Chips are less exposed to a plateau.
- Endowments are "massively underweight" mega-cap tech, so large caps still offer alpha. His new fund picks 12–13 of the top 30 global market caps.
