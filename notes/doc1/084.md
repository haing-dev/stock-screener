### 84. Alex Sacerdote & Leon Shaulov — Intelligence as infrastructure: how AI is rewiring the economy (Sohn Conference Foundation, May 2026)

*Sacerdote founded tech investor Whale Rock Capital; Shaulov runs Maplelane Capital (semis and macro); moderated by Leslie Picker (CNBC) at Sohn New York.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YMGbOx6HAog)

**Takeaway:** AI is a compute-starved "L-curve" that has barely started, so chip, semi-equipment and hardware-supplier estimates are "like 50 to 70% too low" (May 2026). Horizontal application software is the loser.

- Shaulov: wafer-fab equipment (WFE) spending goes from the \~$120–130bn expected to \~$300bn within 3–4 years. Chipmakers, TSMC included, "all of which under spent" after a decade of discipline (May 2026).
- Shaulov: long-term agreements give 3–4 years of capex visibility, so semi equipment is less cyclical and may re-rate. Customers earn 70–80% gross margins and toolmakers \~50%, which gives pricing power.
- Shaulov: his favourite is memory-levered Lam Research, with \~$55bn of revenue in view and the Street "50 to 70% too low" (May 2026). Winners "may not screen as the cheapest".
- Shaulov: an industry with weak profitability-to-capex and growth-to-capex ratios is underinvested, which signals a coming capex surge for its equipment suppliers.
- Sacerdote: supplier "earnings algorithm": units +50%, ASPs +20–100%, gross margin +300–500 bp, 3–4 years of visibility. That means earnings +100% a year for four years; "It's all been earnings."
- Sacerdote pick, TTM Technologies: circuit boards went from 10 to 20–40 layers (even 120), with few capable makers and rising ASPs. It just won Nvidia; \~40% of the business is defense (May 2026).
- Sacerdote pick, Google: "They've won AI." It is the only public company with a foundation model, has strong TPUs and accelerating search, and looks "very cheap" with \~50% upside.
- Sacerdote: "This is an L-curve, straight up, and it's just starting now." Only \~10 bp of \~1bn white-collar workers use AI deeply, burning \~1,000x the tokens; Claude Code has 14m daily users, heading to \~500m.
- Sacerdote: Anthropic's revenue went $100m → $1bn → $9bn → a $45bn run-rate, "maybe 100 billion". Tokens grow \~14x a year, outpacing chip efficiency, so the industry has "half of what we need" in compute (May 2026).
- Sacerdote \[?\]: Anthropic + OpenAI reach \~$200bn of revenue by end-2026. Compute locked at fixed cost while token prices rise gives "enormous incremental margin", which should settle the capex-ROI debate.
- Shaulov: analog semis (Infineon, Texas Instruments, Renesas \[?\]) could tighten like memory; favour names with an AI power angle. Memory is in its first NAND upcycle in \~a decade.
- Shaulov: "first you kind of get a sticky inflation", from soaring chip input costs and software-engineer hiring up \~18% in a month. Later AI is "highly highly deflationary" (May 2026).
- Software: Shaulov says horizontal apps are in trouble but data and infrastructure names like Datadog can win. Sacerdote calls the decline "largely justified" and expects no bounce soon.
- Shaulov: some semis have gone "100 miles an hour in a 60 mile an hour zone", so expect accidents. But "the next 50 to 100 is up" (May 2026).
