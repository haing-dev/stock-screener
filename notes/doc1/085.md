### 85. Glen Kacher — Investing for the AI boom (Bloomberg Podcasts, Sep 2026)

*Founder and CIO of Light Street Capital, a Palo Alto tech hedge fund and private investor (2010); ex-Tiger Management; returns per host −26% (2021), −54% (2022), +46% (2023), +59% (2024), +37% (2025).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E4t-jXvBTMs)

**Takeaway:** Put capital where innovation is fastest and own the leader, which today means the AI accelerated-computing oligopoly of TSMC, Nvidia, Broadcom and AMD. As of Sep 2026 demand still outruns supply, and the build-out is only "a third of the way through" its first leg.

- The #1 player gets \~two-thirds of a market, #2 20–25% "tops", and "everyone else fights for the scraps". In tech, the leader earns higher margins and compounds by reinvesting more dollars than rivals.
- Three-phase cycle: infrastructure \~5–10 years, platform \~years 6–16, applications \~years 11–21. As of Sep 2026 AI is about a third through the first leg.
- Computing stacks are rebuilt every \~15–25 years, and a new architecture takes 10–15 years to reach a quarter of capacity. AI builds a custom answer each time, far more compute-intensive than "search and retrieve".
- The overbuild bear case is wrong for now (Sep 2026). Hyperscalers are "playing catch-up", memory and TSMC are bottlenecks, and "today demand is still running way ahead of supply".
- Nvidia and TSMC numbers are "significantly better" than the Street expects (Sep 2026). Bottlenecks keep AI pricing "higher ... than it needs to be", so favour capacity-constrained suppliers.
- The core four were \~40% of the public portfolio; Nvidia (\~85% share) is underrated as an inference innovator. Agentic AI favours AMD's CPUs, Broadcom's Google TPU record won OpenAI work, and TSMC wins "no matter who wins".
- 2000s consolidation (Avago under Hock Tan) turned semis into an oligopoly suited to AI, a moment like the mid-90s, not the late 90s. Inference will outgrow training, much of it on cheaper XPUs.
- The Nvidia stake was built in H2 2022, when a crypto crash hid the AI inflection. A fading segment masking an inflecting one is a classic mismatch.
- In 2022–23, asking AI startups which vendors they used pointed to Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom and Marvell "long before people were talking about it".
- Variant perception: AI is cast as an overspending "evil empire" while users adopt it daily. "that will reverse, but you have to figure out when."
- The SaaS-apocalypse view is a "huge simplification": "core systems don't tend to get swapped out", so software selloffs create bounce-backs. Longs and shorts are independent bets, not pairs.
- Microsoft is back in (Sep 2026) after it "fumbled" its OpenAI lead. It is the largest security company as AI opens new holes, Azure has repositioned well, and it cut gaming spend.
- Open-source vs closed frontier models is the "real battle", and $100-a-day programmer spend drives open-source demand. Agents use \~5x the tokens of search-style AI.
- Data-center opposition and power are "an emerging problem"; the fix is power "behind the meter". It is "still early" to buy non-tech stocks for their AI plans (Sep 2026).
