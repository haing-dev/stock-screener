### 86. Ben Silver & David Tykocinski — Maverick Capital co-CIOs on finding the AI winners (Goldman Sachs, Jun 2026)

*Co-CIOs of Maverick Capital's public funds, successors to founder Lee Ainslie; Tykocinski covers TMT, Silver healthcare and cyclicals.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOZTXu_s_CE)

**Takeaway:** The AI trade has paid those following the bottleneck upstream to fabs, tools and Japan-listed materials, where low-margin firms see the sharpest "revision torque". Maverick now expects value to swing back downstream to AI services and applications (Jun 2026).

- Tykocinski: "to monetize the trade, it's been about following that migration of that bottleneck further upstream." The biggest earnings revisions come at capacity-constrained suppliers that started from very low margins.
- Tykocinski: next, value moves downstream as agents plug into existing enterprise workflows rather than LLMs sitting on an island. "that's what we're heavily focused on now" (Jun 2026).
- Tykocinski \[?\]: at the equivalent dot-com point, capex ran \~200% of operating cash flow; the AI build-out is "well under 100 percent". But a multi-trillion-dollar company just raised equity, so the gap is narrowing.
- Tykocinski \[?\]: the key risk is an "air pocket" as training build-out growth slows before broad knowledge-worker productivity apps arrive. Coding is the only clear pickup so far (Jun 2026).
- Tykocinski: "root hardware and materials is more subject to commodification over time." Lasers, optics and analog semis are ripe for Chinese competition, a risk he thinks investors underrate.
- Tykocinski: in secular sectors, multiples "rerate in the same direction as the revisions", so momentum persists. Elsewhere, returns come from management decisions or the macro cycle.
- Silver: as capital leaves healthcare for AI, life science tools are "left for dead" but win from AI and reshoring. A drug-manufacturing equipment capex boom should drive revisions within 3–6 months (from Jun 2026).
- Silver: there is an M&A floor. Three to five big consolidators make $5–10B tools companies takeover targets, just as pharma buys biotech within \~6–12 months of a selloff.
- Maverick deliberately moved away from near-term valuation metrics back to first principles. A 20+ page risk report shows each position's idiosyncratic and factor exposures.
