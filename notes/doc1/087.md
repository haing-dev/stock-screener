### 87. Dan Sundheim — Inside his bets on Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX (Invest Like The Best, Feb 2026)

*Founder and CIO of D1 Capital Partners, a public long/short fund with large private stakes in SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic; formerly CIO at Viking.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mBtmTmCC0ZM)

**Takeaway:** Frontier AI labs are Netflix-plus-Spotify businesses: a huge fixed training cost, sold at high incremental margins and made sticky by personalization. Their real risk is capital intensity, not commoditization, and the next AI trade is knock-on effects (early 2026).

- LLMs are not a commodity (early 2026): gross margins are "quite high" and labs pick lanes, OpenAI consumer and Anthropic coding. Snowballing capital needs leave room for only 4–5 long-term players.
- The real risk is capital intensity "to a degree that we've never seen before". That adds financial and operating leverage, so labs can't afford 2–3 years of slow adoption.
- Netflix plus Spotify: spend heavily to train, then sell widely at high incremental margins. Models are similar, so the moat is personalization: the more a model knows about you, the stickier it is.
- Hyperscalers are "a worse business model going forward" even as AWS and GCP growth accelerates (early 2026). Demand concentrates in 4–5 labs that will insource compute within 5–10 years.
- There were "basically no shorts prior to... 2026"; now AI creates many shorts, software first. Systems of record with strong distribution are protected for years: the labs buy an ERP rather than build one.
- Anthropic was a "second player" bet based mainly on Dario's essays, as Bezos's 1997 letter was the clue in Amazon's "sea of red". "Dario just did that better than almost any CEO I've seen since Bezos."
- OpenAI was first bought at the $125B round with only \~65–70% confidence that models would be moated. The bet was on the lopsided payoff; he also urged OpenAI to adopt ads early.
- Focus beats breadth: companies rarely win in several end markets at once. Anthropic went all-in on enterprise coding and is now seen as "winning".
- The bear case, scaling laws stopping, is "a really low probability assumption". Even then, \~3 years of businesses absorbing AI would bring profound economic change.
- The best business is a durable low-cost producer whose low cost drives volume: SpaceX launch, Costco, Amazon e-commerce. Monopolists "tend to get lazy".
- SpaceX was bought at a revenue multiple with little cash burn; Starship and Starlink now make global telecom its market. Rivian fell short because autos are capital-intensive and its ramp was slow.
- Fraud check from his 2002 short of Orthodontic Centers of America: rebuild management's unit economics against the filings. Capitalizing expenses was "the simplest form of accounting fraud".
- Passives, retail, quants and multi-managers make short-term moves overshoot intrinsic value, leaving "endless" fundamental shorts. Europe is "always the most inefficient", with Japan and Korea close behind.
- Macro (early 2026): AI could deliver growth with falling inflation, "nirvana for markets". The biggest risk is a China–Taiwan clash over chips, possibly "akin to the Great Depression".
