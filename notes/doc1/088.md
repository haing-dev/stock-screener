### 88. Sander Gerber — Inside the Gerber Statistic, Masters in Business (Bloomberg Podcasts, May 2025)

*CEO and CIO of Hudson Bay Capital, a multi-strategy, arbitrage-oriented hedge fund; ex-AMEX options market maker; its risk structure has run since 1998.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXp32_0ziJ8)

**Takeaway:** Historical correlation, regression-based factor models and normal-distribution risk are not real risk tools. Hudson Bay tags every idea with a "deal code" and measures diversification with the patented Gerber statistic, which ignores sub-threshold moves as noise.

- The Gerber statistic is rank-order and counts only moves above a threshold; a 10bp S&P move is noise. Correlation must use every data point.
- Papers with Markowitz show "better performance with less risk by replacing historical co-variance with the Gerber statistic". Hudson Bay holds six patents on its diversification process (2025).
- Historical correlation isn't predictive, and Markowitz agreed. His 1952 paper meant forward-looking judgment by practical men, not mined historical data.
- Factor math (Barra/Axioma): 34–40% of a stock's move is factor-driven and 85% of that comes from five factors. The other 21 explain \~6%, which looks like 60% at 10x leverage.
- Only market and sector factors matter; the rest are nonsense. Rough split of a stock's move: stock 20%, sector 30%, market \~50%.
- Momentum and value work because everyone crowds into them, and they break in dislocations. Use models to trade, not to manage risk.
- "Risk management is not about not losing money. Risk management is about unexpectedly losing money." Know each position's reasonable worst-case loss.
- A deal code tags each idea with its hedge, risk and worst-case loss. It gives each PM a batting average of ideas won vs lost; Hudson Bay made money in 2008.
- Machines can't price risk from a new event, so every strategy targets an event, catalyst or change. Multiple strategies are needed because each goes in and out of favor.
- Floor edge (early 1990s): track vega month by month, since earnings months like July can't offset quiet ones like August. Model events as up-a-lot or down-a-lot barbells.
- Volatility is his friend: the Q1 2025 dislocation proved the standard models wrong.
- Credit (spring 2025): rates stay higher for longer. Government-guaranteed banks (\~4,300 in the US, 10–20x leverage) will cede credit to private lenders, "a golden age for real estate credit".
- Office: A buildings get high rents while others empty out. Check rent rolls and weighted average lease, and weigh work-from-home and AI cuts to junior jobs.
- AI is the biggest change of his lifetime (2025) and works like a junior analyst. Yet markets are behavioral, so Hudson Bay is in the human-judgment business.
