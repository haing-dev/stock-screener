### 89. Sander Gerber — Lessons from three decades of investment innovation, Lunches with Legends (Prime Quadrant, Jun 2023)

*CEO and CIO of Hudson Bay Capital (\~$20bn, soft-closed, 27 PMs in 2023); traded only his own capital 1995–2005 before co-founding Hudson Bay in 2006.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QiB3Xm9Wlmw)

**Takeaway:** Hudson Bay tries to be the casino "house": every idea has limited loss, is independent and carries a slight edge. Each is hedged on its own for market, credit or rate beta, and leverage is set from worst-case loss to keep risk constant.

- Hedge each position's beta individually, not with an overlay on a big soup of ideas. In 2008 and March 2020 this showed exactly what was working.
- Peers say hedging every position kills alpha. "if we're generating an alpha we should be able to hedge the position and still generate the alpha"
- Hedges include shorting an ETF or stock, rate futures and CDS. The 27 PMs pick them under risk-team supervision and are paid a share of their own earnings, with a path to partner.
- Returns come from catalysts, events and change, often via option overlays whose loss is capped and upside is open-ended.
- "we want to maintain a constant risk profile". Size leverage from each idea's reasonable worst-case loss, rather than dialing it up and down to hit a return target.
- Multi-strats born on bank prop desks lever to the hilt on cheap capital. Hudson Bay is a bank customer that could be shut down, so it runs low leverage.
- Risk management is integrated with portfolio construction, not a back-office review. In placid markets anyone can look like a risk manager.
- Biggest mistake: financial transmission rights (2007), where a transformer outage sent prices limit up and only 2 of 13 participants got it right. Rule: expand slowly, only into adjacencies.
- SVB case (Mar 2023): a Fed report showed banks had lost 20% of equity capital on marketable securities alone. Screen banks' unrealized securities losses against equity and held-to-maturity reclassifications.
- Macro (Jun 2023): inflation isn't transitory (called in summer 2022). The 10-year at \~3.65% is below its \~4.5% \[?\] average since 1871 outside Fed-targeting periods.
- Leveraged structures that can't refinance at higher long rates will break, commercial real estate first (Jun 2023). Hudson Bay waits in distressed debt, structured products and credit.
- The S&P was powered by seven stocks while the other 493 were collectively down (mid-2023).
- Onshoring, as China becomes a competitor, brings jobs back to the Midwest and adds inflation. So rates are "not going lower anytime soon" (Jun 2023).
- History: deal codes began in 1998 while he traded his own money, starting with FDA Phase 3 option overlays. A friend's "trading account not a business" remark led to the 2006 launch.
