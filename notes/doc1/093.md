### 93. David Einhorn — Solvay ("Solv-AI") and the death of the value-investing industry (Sohn Conference Foundation, Apr 2024)

*Founder of Greenlight Capital; his 20th Sohn presentation, pitching an existing Greenlight position.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I7S9c1RhHlA)

**Takeaway:** Passive flows have gutted the value-investing industry, so cheap stocks are cheaper than they should be. Solvay (Apr 2024) trades at \~7x trough EPS, yields almost 10% and should earn its market cap in free cash flow within 5 years.

- "value investing industry is dead however value investing and the value investing industry are two different things". As "trillions" moved to passive, value analysts were fired, so bargains are the most abundant of his career outside bear-market bottoms.
- The edge has flipped since he started against "thousands of experienced professionals": "This may be how Mr Buffett felt in the 1950s."
- Setup: the Dec 2023 spin-off of specialty chemicals (Syensqo) left the essential-chemicals business as the new Solvay. It has €4.9bn of revenue, a 26% EBITDA margin and the #1 position in all its markets.
- Valuation (Apr 2024): €2.7bn market cap, 1.6 turns of debt, and 2024 EPS above €4 on Greenlight's numbers vs €3.62 consensus, \~7x. Normal 2028 earnings in the high-€6s imply 3.7x: "it seems too cheap at twice the price it wouldn't seem expensive".
- Cash return: €4bn of free cash flow over 5 years, split equally among essential capex, dividends and discretionary uses. "just the dividend will return half the market cap or around 10% per year" (2023 dividend €2.43).
- Trough, not midcycle: 2024 should "look a lot like 2019", but 2019 was midcycle and management agrees 2024 is the trough. Prior peak EPS was over €6.
- Sell-side tunnel vision is the edge: Morgan Stanley's 21-page and BofA's 20-page reports covered no product but soda ash beyond a pie chart. Yet \~70% of revenue is other products, and "the Bears seem intent on completely ignoring that reality".
- Freight moat: soda ash "doesn't cost much but it weighs a lot", so transport adds 30–100% to cost and markets are regional. That makes the bears' Inner Mongolia supply threat largely irrelevant to Europe and North America.
- Soda ash (\~40% of sales; largest producer at \~11% share) held \~$250/t in Europe and \~$180/t in the US through 2010–21. The 2022–23 spike is "normalizing" in 2024, and ex-China capacity grows only 1.7–2.7% a year.
- Soda-ash demand should grow a little above GDP on renewables and EV batteries (each ton of lithium needs \~2 tons). Utilization is \~83% after destocking, and flat glass should recover with construction.
- Peroxides (13% of revenue, mid-30s EBITDA margins): #1 globally and the lowest-cost producer in all regions. Its "very stable take or pay contracts" bring 40% of revenue, and 10% is semiconductor-grade.
- Management is "above average... for a European chemicals company", targeting €300m of cost cuts by 2028 with carbon permits hedged through 2030. Greenlight thinks Coatis, the lowest-margin unit, should be sold.
