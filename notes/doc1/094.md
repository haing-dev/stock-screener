### 94. Ricky Sandler — The evolution of long-short equity investing (Capital Allocators, Oct 2024)

*CEO and CIO of Eminence Capital ($7 billion across long-short, long-only and 150/50 funds), founded \~26 years ago after he co-founded Fusion; Eminence fell 19% in 2008 vs a market down \~40%.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbR-mPsuBk4)

**Takeaway:** Prices are now set by passive, quant, pod and thematic flows, so short-term price action is "much more meaningless than we used to think". Eminence keeps its core of quality longs and alpha shorts, but studies each stock's "setup", trades around noise and caps shorts at 2%.

- Market structure (2024): passive went from \~25% to \~50% in 2019 and \~60% now, and pods, quants and thematic ETFs replaced bottom-up price setters. Stocks now move because a model ranks them highly or a pod expects an 8–10% analyst-day move.
- Trade around noise: since end-2019 turnover is up 40% on longs and 70–80% on shorts. Trim after a 30% rise "for no reason we could identify" and add after an unexplained 30% fall.
- About 20–30% of research time goes to the "setup": the narrative, the holder list and sell-side tension points. Ask analysts "who's calling you, what questions are they asking, what's the tension point".
- Short sizing: "we don't allow shorts to grow above 2% of capital", and a stable $40bn name with little short interest can be 150bp. A $3–4bn name with 15% short interest in the top 50 Reddit mentions gets at most 85bp, sized to survive a 100% squeeze.
- Pre-size, then let shorts float: trimming after a non-fundamental squeeze, or pressing after a non-fundamental drop, "locking in negative alpha". Balance short types (meme, earnings miss, share loss, melting ice cube, fraud) so they don't move together.
- Quants bucket on trailing numbers: Zillow (2022) looked like "unprofitable growth" though it was profitable after exiting home flipping. "what quants can't do is look forward."
- Low stated margins from gross-vs-net revenue, or low return on capital after acquisitions, also get good businesses mis-bucketed. "we were playing a different game than the people setting prices"
- "the stock isn't going to go from 12 times earnings to 18 times earnings because you think it's worth 18 times earnings". It moves when the next investor believes something different, so since 2010–11 he also differentiates from buy-side consensus.
- Judge a style against its long history: in mid-2022 growth looked cheap at pre-COVID multiples, but those weren't cheap. By late 2022 a 50-year study showed growth "finally at a discount to its long-term premium".
- Data science tests multi-year theses with card, web and parking-lot data, e.g. Dave & Buster's remodeled stores (10 of 200) against the cohort. Real-time key performance indicators are "table stakes": a 3% quarterly miss explains weakness without breaking an 18-month thesis.
- Incentives: in early 2014, after 2013's 30% S&P rise, working lists were almost all longs. "you show me the incentives and I'll show you people's behavior", so short alpha got double weight in pay and a dedicated short seller.
- Mistake reviews look back three years, not one: with 18 months' more hindsight, the error is often selling after a temporary miss. Group mistakes into biases, such as falling for cheap stocks with leverage.
- Business: "hedge fund investors want you to beat the market and they want you not to lose money", which no portfolio can do. After 2008 assets halved to $2.5bn; allocators now barbell, so he launched a 150/50, now $1.4bn and always 100% net.
