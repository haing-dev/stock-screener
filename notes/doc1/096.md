### 96. Murray Stahl — Letting winners run (Inside Active, Mar 2026)

*CEO, CIO and co-founder of Horizon Kinetics and portfolio manager of its Paradigm Fund, a long-term contrarian value strategy with very low turnover; one of his last interviews before his death in Apr 2026.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kISrPZid3_Y)

**Takeaway:** Professionals are graded on the calendar year, so returns beyond one year are undervalued. Start positions under 5%, let the best become the biggest, and sell only for something better: trimming swaps a possible drawdown for certain taxes.

- The "equity yield curve" is very steep because bonuses, evaluations and fees are paid on a one-year grading period. "things get overvalued in the short run and... undervalued in the long run", hence the firm's name, Horizon.
- Hunt the under-scrutinized, not the misunderstood: managers running huge fee-paying sums need liquid securities. A smaller or patient investor can own what "people don't take the time to understand".
- Texas Pacific Land is the model: land can't become obsolete and can be made more valuable, as is happening now. US accounting doesn't mark it to market and there is no land ETF, so "it's not going to be well understood".
- Sizing: start "relatively small", under 5%, and let only appreciation make a position big. "you would like your highest return to be your biggest position. The only way you achieve it is to leave it alone."
- Concentration vs taxes: trimming to avoid a possible drawdown guarantees one in taxes, \~1/3 of profits for New Yorkers plus city tax. The funds are built for taxable investors.
- Sell for a better idea, or when a big position has lost its appreciation potential: "no company is an extraordinary company forever". Sell gradually, and "we don't throw out dirty water until we have clean water".
- Switching math: even a portfolio manager right 70% of the time faces 0.7 × 0.7 = 0.49 odds selling A to buy B. That's red or black at roulette; if a laggard keeps executing, the market moving on "just makes it a better opportunity".
- Bitcoin: a 25bp position bought over a decade ago risked going to zero, with "rather limited" damage, against "enormous" upside that came through. He argues it has fundamentals: each four-year halving doubles the work, servers and power needed per coin.
- From the June 2007 iPhone, the Mag 7 earned extra searches and ad clicks with no extra capex, an unprecedented era. AI is high-performance computing needing enormous capex and power, so markets are returning to hard, tangible assets (Mar 2026).
- Alphabet goes from quasi-monopoly to "challenged" by OpenAI and Anthropic. When capex needed to defend dominance shrinks free cash flow, the market pays "a lower price earnings multiple because there's higher risk".
- Index rules (Mar 2026): an OpenAI IPO would enter the new Bloomberg 500 almost immediately, while the S&P 500 has trading and seasoning rules. If SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic list, the two indexes' tops will "look very very different".
- Privates (Jan 2026 letter): indexation muted IPOs for \~25 years, so liquidity-starved private equity owners sell at valuations "that would never have been possible". He buys few: robust cash flow, or else little competition or a proprietary lead.
- AI won't change research: "just because the data is available doesn't mean the people know how to interpret it". The data flood has shrunk investors' historical frame to "maybe a few decades".
