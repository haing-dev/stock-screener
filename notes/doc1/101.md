### 101. Eli Casdin — Building a public/private life-sciences firm on the molecular cost curve (Capital Allocators, Jul 2022)

*Founder and CIO of Casdin Capital (\~$3.5bn across public and private funds, including a pre-IPO lockup fund; 100+ private investments; \~40 staff); ex-AllianceBernstein. Interviewed by Joel Wittenberg, ex-CIO of the W. K. Kellogg Foundation and a Casdin LP.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w63Ibo1XpEM)

**Takeaway:** Sequencing a genome fell from \~$3bn and 13 years to $500 and a day, turning biotech from "lottery tickets" into predictable secular growth. Casdin exploits it with long-duration capital, public-plus-private stakes, over-capitalized companies and a strict management filter.

- Catalyst-strength finding: their factor data show high-impact catalysts 1–3 months out are negatively correlated with returns, and low-impact ones positively. Calls were right, but expectations had moved: "now it had to be 15 to beat".
- Enter on "the front lawn", not in "the garage", then over-capitalize: companies burn $100–150m every 12–24 months. Scale in over time rather than front-load and "fight dilution on the way down".
- Nine times out of ten, sector selloffs over the past decade had nothing to do with the industry ("inflation most recently", 2022). Buy well-financed companies with intact innovative products on such dips.
- Biggest mistake: building M&A into his valuation and sizing up on it. Assign probabilities only to what you understand, or "you can lose a lot of money without even realizing it".
- 70/30 rule: \~70% of teams he judges bad turn out bad, while \~30% surprise. "I'm comfortable missing the 30 so that I miss the 70", because FOMO "dominates growth investing".
- Management test: suggest a change; "Oh, that's a good idea" is a red flag, while "6 months ago we instituted a program" is good. "A's hire A+ people", so meet staff down to junior scientists.
- Long-duration capital is his biggest edge: LPs commit "philosophically and contractually" and "No one ever calls". So the firm can step in when others are anxious.
- One decision-maker, with each idea scored on management, market, return profile and risk to justify "why something was 1%, why something else was 5%". An in-house data scientist tests which factors drive returns.
- Reimbursement lags technology: payers used to "five bucks for a blood test" balked at Foundation Medicine's $3,000 tumor test. Drug developers adopted precision tools first, and their need for diagnostics "pulled forward the payers".
- Precision trials can show efficacy in \~100 genetically selected patients and skip phase 2, but markets shrink to tens of thousands. That suits small biotechs, the source of \~70% of innovative drugs, more than pharma.
- SPACs (with Keith Meister) are "a one-off private equity-like vehicle" that let a company sell its 3–4-year future. In an IPO, companies "can only talk about next year and what's happened before".
- AI is a tool: the Anton supercomputer alone "didn't produce anything" until Relay paired it with drug developers. "technology is not going to cover up crappy businesses"; AI works best on images today (Paige AI).
- 10-year view (2022): investors can "compound at a 20% CAGR" with high volatility, as allocators lift life-sciences weights from \~1–2% to 5–10%. His therapeutics weight falls from \~55% to \~30% as data and tools rise.
