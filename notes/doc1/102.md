### 102. Stanley Druckenmiller — Hard-landing odds, the curse of the reserve currency and a "200 foot tsunami" (Sohn Conference Foundation, May 2023)

*Founder, CEO and chairman of the Duquesne family office; 45 years in the business since starting Duquesne in 1982; \~27–28 years on Memorial Sloan Kettering's board. Interviewed by Kiril Sokoloff of 13D Research.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bMAm2S1M_IU)

**Takeaway:** In May 2023, his "most challenging" forecasting period in 45 years, he rated a hard landing far likelier than a soft one. He ran \~60% gross and flat net to keep capital and a clear head for the "unbelievable opportunities" he expected afterward.

- Two rules point to a hard landing: the worst outcomes follow asset bubbles, and "don't fight the Fed". Soft landings since 1950 all followed "Proactive rather than reactive fed policy", which this wasn't.
- Edward Chancellor's 500-year finding: rates below 2% have generally been followed by "difficult Economic Times". The 10–11-year bubble's finale was $5tn of COVID spending, 60% financed by the Fed.
- His hard-landing template: corporate profits down 20%-plus from near-record margins, unemployment from 3.4% to above 5%, and bankruptcies rising from "astonishingly low" levels.
- Anecdotes lead (May 2023): trucking, his firms' "Guiding Light" with a 6–8-month lead, was "extremely weak", while housing, travel and restaurants were robust. Recession may have begun in Q2 2023.
- Regional banks: the median holds 43% of loans in commercial real estate, \~40% of it office, with securities yielding 2–2.5% against 5% funding. They are impaired before any loan losses.
- Positioning (May 2023): \~60% gross, neither net long nor short, since no fat pitch was in sight. The model was 2021's 2-year at 15bp with money growing 40%: downside \~10bp, upside \~200bp.
- His usual bear-market trade, owning Treasuries, was "off the table" with the 10-year at \~3.5% and Fed funds at 5% (May 2023). He held a dollar short that was not "massive" but "will move the needle".
- Long gold and silver "against the history", as authorities are "at the end of their rope" and autocracies shun dollar reserves. Caveat: "don't go out and buy gold I could change my mind in a week or two".
- Ways out of a hard landing: copper, the tightest market he's studied but avoided going in, and US housing, short of single-family homes. Also biotech after 3–5 lean years, and AI, held via Nvidia and Microsoft.
- Inflation (May 2023): money supply fell \~6% y/y after \~40% growth, so the money stock stays high; he expected 3–3.5% within 6–9 months. Easing "like Arthur Burns" before the 2024 election risks stagflation.
- Fed independence: he expects it to survive but puts a 5–10% chance on losing it in a really bad hard landing. The risk is the highest in 50 years.
- Fiscal: the debt ceiling is a 30-foot wave beside a "200 foot tsunami" of entitlements plus interest, which exceed taxes by 2040 (CBO). The US fiscal gap is 7.7% of GDP, France's 2.4%.
- The "curse of the reserve currency": markets shut down Truss's UK but don't check US policy, and the dollar rose through 2021–22. Keep digging and "you lose the privilege".
- At 20x earnings with high margins he expects flat US stocks for 10 years (May 2023). But 1968–82 shows a flat market need not be a lost decade for stock pickers.
