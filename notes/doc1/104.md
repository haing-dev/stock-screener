### 104. Karen Karniol-Tambour — Record US corporate profits at a turning point (Sohn Conference Foundation, Jun 2022)

*Co-CIO of sustainability at Bridgewater and a member of its executive and investment committees; formerly ran its Daily Observations group.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b5q1l3aJ-_s)

**Takeaway:** Record US profits came from households spending more than firms paid them in wages, funded by borrowing, falling rates and finally COVID checks. In Jun 2022 every support was reversing, yet equities had priced only higher rates.

- Profit identity: "everyone's spending is somebody's income". So economy-wide margins rise when spending is funded by something other than wages: household borrowing or dissaving, borrowed capex, or deficits.
- Before 2008, globalization, deregulation and weaker unions held wages down while EM and oil-producer dollar saving pushed rates lower, funding the borrowing. QE then mostly lifted assets held by the wealthy.
- COVID checks more than replaced the bottom 60–80%'s lost wages: "at the same time the economy collapsed corporate profits actually surged to new highs".
- Four margin pressures (Jun 2022): checks are gone and households are dissaving, and the labor market is the tightest in decades. Inflation is concentrated in fuel and food, and capex is shifting to supply-chain resilience and decarbonization.
- Politics is turning against the "peak pro corporate environment", since "we've already globalized every good we could globalize". Corporate taxes, antitrust and wage pressure all point up.
- Few listed inflation winners: commodity producers were \~30% of the market in the 1970s and \~3% now. Non-oil firms selling to lower-income consumers struggle to pass on costs.
- Pricing (Jun 2022): +1.5 points on the 10-year cuts the value of 10 years of earnings \~15%. Equities fell about that much: they "have basically fallen in line with the fact that rates have risen that's it".
- Not priced (Jun 2022): an earnings slowdown, or a 1970s-style higher risk premium; profits were good then, yet equities had their "worst decade ever". The US is also priced to keep beating the world, inviting disappointment.
