### 105. Rob Citrone & Karen Karniol-Tambour — Modern mercantilism and where to take risk outside the US (Sohn Conference Foundation, May 2025)

*Citrone founded Discovery Capital Management, with \~70% of risk outside the US and half in emerging markets; Karniol-Tambour is at Bridgewater ("we're fundamental we're also systematic"). No moderator.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GNDpVJAx6uA)

**Takeaway:** The paradigm that made "a simple S&P 500" the best holding is giving way to modern mercantilism, which markets have barely priced (May 2025). With global portfolios concentrated in US assets, both take most of their risk outside the US.

- Citrone: America Movil, his top single name, spans Latin America with wide moats, a 10% free-cash-flow yield and \~4x EV/EBITDA \[?\]. It grows \~10% and buys back 7–10% a year: "you can trust Carlos" (Slim).
- Karniol-Tambour: under modern mercantilism, "the state's job is to really maximize that country's its national wealth, its strength, its self-sufficiency". Trade deficits become wealth transfers, and this is "only starting to be priced".
- Karniol-Tambour \[?\]: market-cap weighting leaves investors heavily in the US; Nvidia lost "two Mexicos" of value the Monday after DeepSeek. Her top advice: "take geographical diversification seriously", in countries with different growth, inflation and policy.
- Karniol-Tambour: non-US government bonds are her top conviction, a once-every-10–15-year signal (May 2025). Other central banks can ease into a slowdown they didn't cause, and Treasuries started with no real risk premium.
- Karniol-Tambour: short the dollar "not against everything, but euro, yen, kind of the big crosses". So far fast money moved it; big holders "take a while to work through governance".
- Karniol-Tambour: Bridgewater's China and Asia ex-China funds were its biggest winners by trading bonds and currencies, not just cheap stocks. China's weak economy made its bonds a good buy and weakened its currency.
- Karniol-Tambour: private credit hollowed out institutional bond alpha teams and carries "equity-like risk". Neglected at zero rates, "duration and currency are great alpha spaces" again.
- Citrone: "we look at countries like we look at companies", management and policies first; Milei's Argentina is the template. Four Latin American elections within 18 months could bring "substantial change" (May 2025).
- Citrone (May 2025): the US 10-year hits 5–5.25% this year with no Fed cuts, then a 2026 boom on tax cuts and reshoring. US and Japanese bonds are "the two clear shorts".
- Citrone: play rates that "can come down thousands of basis points, at least hundreds" in Brazil, Mexico, Argentina and Turkey. He likes Nigerian and Turkish currencies after devaluations, with rates at 30% and 45%.
- Citrone: "US exceptionalism in the private sector is still here" in technology and capital markets, so money will leave slowly. He still uses shorts and stays "dynamic".
- Commodities (May 2025): Citrone bought oil at $55–58 on Iran/Israel risk, as a near-term trade only. Karniol-Tambour prefers gold tactically and calls commodities ignored after years without inflation.
