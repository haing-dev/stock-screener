### 106. Murray Stahl — Inflation, hard assets, royalties and Bitcoin (Hedgeye, Oct 2024)

*Chairman, CEO, CIO and co-founder of Horizon Kinetics, whose INFL inflation-beneficiaries ETF holds \~$900m; interviewed by Hedgeye's Keith McCullough.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PXpNuay7PoY)

**Takeaway:** Four decades of disinflation rested on "two and a half miracles" that are now over, so inflation "can last decades" (Oct 2024). Own the hard assets the S&P 500 lacks: royalties with dormant optionality, land and water for gas-powered data centres, and fixed-supply Bitcoin.

- Disinflation rested on Soviet resources flooding world markets, \~3bn new low-cost workers from China and its imitators, and multinationals entering newly opened countries. "Those are the Miracles and that's over."
- The inflation trend is "just starting" and deficits everywhere "can't continue" (Oct 2024). Austerity or a Volcker-style shock is impossible given ≥$5 trillion of private equity ("essentially leverage Equity") and far bigger bank balance sheets.
- The S&P 500 holds "next to nothing" in tangible assets, unlike in the 1970s. When the world's biggest capital pool rebalances toward them, it will be "very disruptive in an equity sense".
- Texas Pacific Land, a royalty and land company, has no reinvestment requirement, so shareholders truly own its earnings. Adjust other firms' P/E for required reinvestment, and remember GAAP doesn't mark land to market.
- Franco-Nevada: 2/3–70% of its assets produce no gold until prices rise; then output "goes to the bottom line because it's a royalty". The bad part: a Panama referendum left its copper mine's metal in the ground.
- Dividend cuts can be the opportunity: San Juan Basin Royalty Trust (\~$150m, 99.8% gas) cut to zero to fund operator Hilcorp's capex pro rata. The operator is in effect lending the trust money at no interest and with no recourse.
- Natural gas is "the vehicle of choice" for AI data centres: coal is unlikely, nuclear too slow, solar and wind intermittent. US power use has been flat since 2007, and data-centre spend is \~$250bn "conservatively" over 12 months (Oct 2024).
- LandBridge, a new holding (Oct 2024): Permian/Delaware Basin land with water and water-pipe rights of way, where off-grid data centres can burn local gas. Texas gas is negative-priced in places, and "modestly positive" prices would swing some companies' profits sharply.
- CPI under-measures: if beef rises from $4 to $5, the substitution method assumes you eat more chicken, and the $17 basket shows no inflation. Hedonic adjustment counts pricier health premiums as "getting more for your money", but lower earners and retirees can't substitute further.
- China's living standard is \~1/5 of America's and India's \~1/10. Both reaching 1/3 would lift demand for all commodities, and supply takes years or decades to develop, "so we're way way behind".
- Spending dwarfs the headline deficit: a \~$7 trillion federal budget, almost $4 trillion of state and local spending, plus Fannie, Freddie and Ginnie Mae. Those agencies "create money" with combined balance sheets about the Fed's size.
- Bitcoin is "the most conservative investment there is": its blockchain solves the "authentication problem" blocking Hayek's denationalized money, with a fixed 21m supply. Gold isn't fixed: at $5,000/oz, more would come out of mines and jewelry.
- Small payments may need other coins: Litecoin's dollar volume rivals Ethereum's at a fraction of its market cap. Merge mining makes it nearly free to accumulate, which has depressed its price in the short run.
