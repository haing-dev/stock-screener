### 107. Sam Altman — Open source, AI regulation and capital-efficient growth (Sohn Conference Foundation, May 2023)

*OpenAI CEO, also working on Helion, Retro and Worldcoin; interviewed by Stripe co-founder Patrick Collison.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1egAKCKPKCk)

**Takeaway:** Open source will stay "a couple years behind" the giant-cluster frontier, so only the frontier needs a global, nuclear-style regulator (May 2023). AI should make capital allocation far more efficient, yet Silicon Valley has forgotten how to fund nine-digit, pre-product bets.

- Open models will be "very capable" and enough "for many super economically valuable things" (May 2023). But superintelligence, cancer cures and new physics will come from the biggest models first, so value sits at the frontier.
- Regulate through an International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) for AI covering "extremely powerful AI training systems": audits and safety evals before release. "The best way to implement this would be a capabilities threshold, but that's harder to measure", so compute is the easy proxy.
- AI has unusual levers: a big energy signature and few top-GPU makers, which could flag chips "talking to more than 10,000 other GPUs". Many who say China won't join have "never been to China".
- AI should make the economy more capital-efficient, not more capital-intensive, because human capital allocation "is so horrible" (May 2023). If AI picked one right $500M-a-year cancer project, against \~$50–100B of yearly spending, "That would be a great efficiency gain."
- Strategy: "I am a believer as a business strategy in platform plus killer app", and ChatGPT makes OpenAI's platform better. Still, being the best research lab matters more than any productization.
- Given the choice of a 10x gain, pick inference efficiency over training efficiency. Optimize total lifetime compute (training plus all inference), where inference should be the dominant term.
- Beneficiaries outside AI labs (May 2023): Microsoft "will transform themselves across almost every axis with AI". Some investing vehicle will use AI for "crazy outperformance" like RenTech \[?\].
- Google is "doing a good job", and search will change but survive unless Google does nothing (May 2023). Facebook's AI strategy was "confused at best", but it will be "a surprising new real player".
- Deep-tech capital gap: OpenAI, Helion, Retro and Worldcoin each "raised minimum nine digits before any product at all". The Valley "over pivoted" to capital-light startups, though the last cycle's tech billionaires now fund long projects.
- Keep capital structures normal: "you should innovate on like products and science and not corporate structures." OpenAI's odd structure was forced on it and a "time suck".
- Data won't stall progress: running out of internet tokens is "not the plan", and models past "the synthetic data event horizon" make their own. A stall would come from architectures that can't reason reliably.
- Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) isn't "the right long-term solution"; reliable interpretability would cut everyone's fear of AI catastrophe. Back small technical teams, since \~six months turns a physicist into a productive AI researcher.
- Talent idea: pay the "100 most talented people" $250K a year for \~10 years, investing on predefined terms if they start companies. Work with people known "a decade plus", plus some new energy.
