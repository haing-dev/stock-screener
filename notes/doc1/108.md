### 108. Sam Altman & Satya Nadella — The OpenAI–Microsoft deal and compute economics (Bg2 Pod, Oct 2025)

*Altman is CEO of OpenAI; Nadella is CEO of Microsoft, which owns 27% of OpenAI (\~$135B) on \~$13.5B invested; host Brad Gerstner founded Altimeter Capital.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gnl833wXRz0)

**Takeaway:** AI demand outruns supply, and Nadella's binding constraint is power and "warm shells", not chips (Oct 2025). Altman warns "there will come a glut for sure"; Nadella's defence is a fungible, busy fleet, a short backlog and the "agent factory" layer.

- Nadella: "I don't have warm shells to plug into", so chips can sit in inventory for lack of powered buildings (Oct 2025). Microsoft is supply-constrained, and Azure's 39% growth could have been 41–42%.
- Altman: a glut will come in "two to three years or five to six", and "Some people are going to get really burned". Triggers: cheap mass-scale energy undercutting long contracts, cost per unit of intelligence falling \~40x a year, and local on-device models.
- Altman: compute demand is a price curve: a 100x cheaper unit of intelligence would raise usage "by much more than 100". Scaling is roughly "log of intelligence equals log of compute".
- Altman: OpenAI's revenue is "well more" than the reported $13B, and \~$1.4T of compute commitments bets on steep growth (Oct 2025). Growth legs are ChatGPT, an AI cloud, devices and automated science; critics should "short the stock".
- Nadella: a cloud needs an efficient "token factory" plus high utilization, so the fleet must be fungible across workloads, regions and chip generations. Hence Microsoft declined some OpenAI demand for a single-site, multi-gigawatt training build.
- Nadella: the $400B of remaining performance obligations (RPO) averages \~2 years and is diversified, so capex is spent "with high certainty" (Oct 2025). OpenAI's \~$250B commitment is longer-dated and excluded.
- Nadella: vendor financing is old, but some deals take "exotic forms" that "need to be scrutinized by the investment community". Microsoft swapped compute for equity, and "circularity ultimately will be tested by demand".
- Nadella: Microsoft can't lose more than its \~$13.5B, yet holds a $135B stake, so look past consolidated OpenAI losses. Royalty-free IP for seven more years is "like having a frontier model for free".
- Nadella: investors are "overly fixated on the Azure number"; the Copilots are the high-margin businesses. M365 Copilot is priced above anything else Microsoft sells, yet is deploying faster with more usage.
- Nadella: "agents are the new seats". Low-ARPU (average revenue per user), high-usage software that accumulates data is protected; high-ARPU, low-usage software has "a little bit of a problem".
- Nadella: software now carries "a real marginal cost", and each chat burns far more GPU than search's amortized index. Consumer monetization is "murky", while "enterprise monetization is much clearer."
- Nadella: "nothing is a commodity" at scale. Hosting OpenAI's huge workload cuts Azure's costs fastest, a challenge for levered new entrants like Oracle, CoreWeave and Crusoe (Gerstner's examples).
- Altman (2026 outlook): agents go from multi-hour to multi-day tasks, plus "very small scientific discoveries in 2026". An IPO will come "someday" with no date; asked about $100B+ revenue by 2028–29: "How about 27?"
- Gerstner: public software trades at \~5.2x forward revenue vs a 10-year average of 7x (Oct 2025). He calls this "the golden age of margin expansion", with headcount growing slower than revenue.
