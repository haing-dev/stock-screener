### 110. Orlando Bravo — What's next for software (Sohn Conference Foundation, May 2026)

*Thoma Bravo, a software private-equity firm doing buyouts since 2000, manages \~$200bn with \~200 people across 77 portfolio companies.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb84Ua4UDvs)

**Takeaway:** Markets overlook "AI as software": domain-leading incumbents with long-tenured leaders will bring agentic AI to enterprises, so Thoma Bravo is doubling down on them (May 2026). Medallia, a 2021 "big mistake", taught it not to overpay for growth or replace domain leadership.

- Buy the #1 player in a domain, "not the number three or four", as Thoma Bravo did through the software, SaaS and cyber waves. Teams are vertical: "We don't invest behind a horizontal technology because technology comes and goes."
- AI automates human judgment: domain leaders that automated 5–10% of a function can now "automate all of it". Founders and 20+-year domain leaders move fastest, and "Quality leadership in an organization is being a bit undervalued".
- "There never was" much of a moat in software; owning the data or being the system of record is mostly comfort. What matters is the best product, a 5-to-1 customer ROI and real usage.
- Disruption risk (May 2026): "I'm extremely worried about it", yet retention rates are holding and "We're not getting surprised". Retention is the first warning signal to watch.
- Medallia (2021): moving fast with shallow domain expertise, "we paid too much cuz our growth didn't materialize". Replacing management to go from −10% to 40% margins lost the "innovative capability"; reports say \~$5bn of equity may be wiped out.
- Keep existing leaders, now a "100%" bias, backed by 4-hour monthly operating reviews, rather than the PE habit of swapping management. Founder-run or long-tenured companies "need less help from us".
- "this is exactly the wrong time to buy a mediocre software company at a bargain price", even at a 7–8% day-one yield. Buy top domains with domain leadership and AI progress visible "in the numbers".
- Credit (May 2026): refinancing a \~$3bn loan, software leverage fell from \~7–7.5x to \~5x and spreads widened \~250 bp plus fees. Pay down debt now, so leverage is low when the refinancing wave hits in years 2–3.
- Incumbents vs AI natives is "not either or": Thoma Bravo bought \~30 AI-native add-ons, and AI is \~35% of portfolio bookings (May 2026). LLMs and hyperscalers can't do it all: "if you try to do it all, you'll get to none."
- Agentic re-platforming (May 2026): Proofpoint, Anaplan and Dayforce are there; Ping Identity and Coupa nearly; others lag, and ROI is unproven. Most agents deployed today come from these software companies.
- Software re-rates if \~20% growth continues with steady margins and retention: "These are really really good businesses." Exits are hard in 2026, and a record \~$15bn distributed in 2025 buys time.
- Cyber: AI excels at removing vulnerabilities as code is written, which threatened former holding Veracode. Identity and email security stay key because attacks need "the human factor", and agent identity is a new field.
