### 113. Mike Alfred — AI, Bitcoin, digital credit and the future of investing (True North, May 2026)

*Self-described "high-conviction, long-duration" value investor with a single LP, targeting 30–50% CAGRs; founder of Alpine Fox, IREN director and former software entrepreneur.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bJwPOlpyXew)

**Takeaway:** Alfred (May 2026) sees Bitcoin-backed preferreds ("digital credit": Strive's SATA, Strategy's STRC) at \~11.5–13% yields winning share from income products. He warns they are equity, not credit, and could unwind if Bitcoin falls far enough for long enough.

- Balance sheet first: "if I can't understand how I'm not going to lose money in something... I won't get involved." IREN and Cipher were bought in Q4 2022 at \~1/3 of estimated site value, a floor even if the businesses failed.
- Concentrate: 75 stocks at 1–5% can't deliver 30–50% CAGRs; "You sometimes going to have to be 40% or 50% long one idea." Accept 30–50% drawdowns, perhaps several per cycle, while pod shops, cutting risk at 3%+ drawdowns, sell as he buys.
- Buy/sell rule: well below intrinsic (or 2-year) value plus "really negative" sentiment is "always a buy"; near target with very bullish sentiment, reduce. Sentiment comes from the VIX, options positioning and a private list of X accounts "absolutely terrible at inflection points".
- Treasury entries: Strive was bought in fall 2025, once premiums to Bitcoin had "largely gone away". At the Nov 2024 MSTR top (\~$541) he warned that paying $2–4 per $1 of Bitcoin "didn't make rational sense".
- Digital credit (May 2026; "not an endorsement"): he owns 50,000 SATA shares at \~$90–92. Yields are \~11.5% for STRC and 13%+ for SATA below par, versus <10% for safe traditional income.
- His payment case: yields look unsustainable in dollars, but issuers can keep paying if Bitcoin compounds >10–15% a year for 10 years. Skipping payments would wreck the treasury model's one "real sustainable flywheel".
- Risks he gives: these are "a form of equity" above common, with no amortization or payment guarantee, so the "digital credit" label may mislead. If Bitcoin "falls enough for long enough", doubts about payments could set off a self-reinforcing unwind.
- Resilience (his reading): as Bitcoin fell from \~125k to \~60k, the preferreds held near par, and intramonth dips keep getting shallower. Issuers seem to need a \~$1bn+ market cap to compete.
- Suited to "people who are already rich and they want to stay rich", not the young, who should buy Bitcoin and growth stocks. His mother moved part of a \~3–4%-yielding dividend basket, trimming Altria and British American Tobacco, into SATA at \~12.5–13%.
- Private credit's assets, much of them software, went unremarked in 2022 while public peers fell 30–99% (Carvana \~98%). Bitcoin, by contrast, trades 24/7.
- Cycle (spring 2026): no traditional bull market since 2021 after 500 bp of hikes, and the ISM purchasing managers' index only just topped 50. He expects broadening (equal weight, small caps, international) and euphoria before any serious bear market.
- Late-'90s analogy: "we might be in '97 right now... We might even be in mid-'99." AI data-centre capex is the top barometer: in 2000 it "turned over right around the time that the stocks turned over".
- Gold has beaten Bitcoin for \~18 months, and when that stops Bitcoin "tends to go into its bull cycle". He sees Bitcoin at $1m "probably by 2033 or 2035" (May 2026).
