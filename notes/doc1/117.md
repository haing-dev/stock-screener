### 117. Lars Theill — Crescat's biotech playbook: late-stage catalysts and buying the overshoot on bad news (Crescat Capital, May 2026)

*Biotech adviser to Crescat Capital for three years (PhD), on the 2026 biotech backdrop, Crescat's strategy and recent trades. The strategy is up 95% gross (\~62–63% net) since 1 Jan 2023, vs 43% for the average of the XBI and IBB indexes.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oCxV_2PuKPk)

**Takeaway:** Informed investors can get good odds on late-stage binary events, but crowds overreact both ways: on average small biotechs give back their gains after good phase 3 news and rebound after failures. Crescat holds \~30 late-stage and early-commercial names, avoids the "valley of death" before phase 3 data, and buys selected overshoots after bad news (May 2026).

- Why biotech: it is both non-cyclical (constant need for drugs) and a growth sector (ageing populations, "hundreds of millions of people" entering the middle class, and AI speeding up biology). The toolbox keeps widening: antibodies, bispecifics, radioligand-armed antibodies, off-the-shelf cell therapy, gene therapy and mRNA.
- FDA tailwinds: one pivotal phase 3 now suffices (previously two), which he later ties to rare diseases, and accelerated approval on earlier data is more available. Earlier revenue helps the developer survive while a confirmatory trial runs. By early 2026 the 2025 fears about the new administration's FDA and drug pricing had faded, but a "revolving door" of FDA staff makes some decisions less predictable.
- Edge and risk: knowing the disease, the competition and the earlier data gives "pretty good odds" on a phase 3 outcome, with "tremendous upside in a short time". A wrong call can "drop 80% overnight", so diversify.
- What Crescat buys: late-stage clinical and early-commercial biotechs with first- or best-in-class drugs for a significant unmet need in a reasonably large population, or with promising phase 2/3 data. "We are not so much interested in me too drugs." Areas: inflammatory and autoimmune disease, oncology, neurology and metabolic disease, including weight loss.
- Diligence covers the modality, the disease and market, management and competition: will the drug be first or best in class, or "overridden by competing products"? The portfolio typically holds \~30 names; commercial companies get larger positions and make up the bigger part of the book. Crescat also uses a network of experts.
- Timing: a phase 2 bet needs an effect too strong to be chance. Usually Crescat enters during phase 3, "somewhat toward the end", before everyone focuses on the readout. It avoids the 2–3-year "valley of death" between phase 2 and phase 3 results (heavy spending, few catalysts), sometimes rides the pre-readout "bio run-up", and may exit fully or partly before the result.
- Success has traps too: crowded optimism often leaves the stock suffering after good data "until the product is actually approved". Approved drugs can still fail commercially, and the FDA can reject good phase 3 data for reasons outsiders can't see.
- Evidence for Buffett's "fearful when others are greedy": a research paper (published 2025) on \~20 small-biotech binary events found good phase 3 news lifts stocks \~10%, but 100 days later they sit below the pre-data price on average. Bad news cuts them \~50%, then they rise \~50% in 100 days, so profit comes from being "able to pick the right losers". Crescat sometimes sells before a binary event when the good news looks "baked in".
- Capricor (Duchenne muscular dystrophy; allogeneic, off-the-shelf stromal cells that are anti-inflammatory and help tissue regenerate): the FDA turned down its first filing and wanted a bigger phase 3. Amid short reports the stock fell to \~$5; positive data sent it up about fourfold overnight, to the high 20s. Crescat "ignored the short reports" because Mesoblast's similar cells had worked in another disease. The FDA decision date is Aug 2026, and Crescat still holds some.
- Mesoblast: its similar cell therapy was approved at end-2024 for graft-versus-host disease in children and has sold more than $100 million. It is late-stage in adults, in phase 3 in lower back pain and inflammatory heart disease, and starting phase 3 in Duchenne (a future Capricor rival). After failed trials since the early 2000s, the stock rose from "maybe a couple of dollars" (2023) to \~15, about tenfold. He calls it a "portfolio drug" (one therapy, many diseases) and a recent, long-term holding. The prize is replacing steroids, which fade after months and cause severe side effects.
- IO Biotech (melanoma): the stock rose from 1 to more than 2 in 2025 into a pivotal phase 3 that "missed by a hair", halved, returned to 2 on a strong pre-specified subgroup, fell to \~50 cents when the FDA demanded a new trial, and bounced above $1. Partly due to weak senior management it is now "basically going out of business", yet it doubled three times within \~6 months.
- Replimune (RP1, an oncolytic virus given with anti-PD-1 to melanoma patients who failed checkpoint inhibitors): 32% partial responses plus durable complete responses, but no real control arm, since re-giving anti-PD-1 could not help. The FDA declined approval and the stock fell from $8 to $1.50. Crescat judged the pipeline (including pancreatic cancer) worth more than the price even without approval in melanoma and bought at $2. Three weeks later it closed at $3.47, and Crescat sold part "for a prudent risk management"; the company has cut half its workforce.
- Day One Biopharmaceuticals: bought in late 2025 for its approved paediatric low-grade glioma drug and successful launch, and taken over within a few months at an 80–90% premium. Crescat looks for companies whose products make them takeover targets for big pharma, which faces a patent cliff and is buying to refill pipelines.
- 2026 backdrop: Morgan Stanley sees M&A, cash-rich commercial companies and a calmer FDA favouring small and mid-cap biotech, whose revenue it projects to grow 14% a year, from 57 billion (2025) to more than 100 billion by 2030. The survivors of the 2021–25 shake-out are higher quality than the COVID-era "science projects". Spring 2025 was the "final capitulation"; the sector then beat the S&P 500, small caps beat large, and the XBI was up 7.7% in 2026 vs a flat IBB. It is still "just approaching levels of fair valuation overall" (May 2026).
