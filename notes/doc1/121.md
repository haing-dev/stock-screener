### 121. Peter Kolchinsky — Pharma's soul: builders vs landlords and contractual genericization (The Long Run with Luke Timmerman, 2020)

*Managing partner of RA Capital Management ($6.8bn in public and private life sciences at the time) and author of The Great American Drug Deal, on his nonprofit No Patient Left Behind. Recorded in 2020, weeks before the US election; uploaded Jun 2025.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKn1Pq98MZs)

**Takeaway:** Drug prices aren't too high; "some drug prices are too long". Patent gaming and uncopyable biologics keep old drugs branded for decades, rent that does little for innovation once discounted. Pay well for 10–15 years, then force a near-cost price by law ("contractual genericization"), and cut patients' out-of-pocket costs.

- Diagnosis: drugs "aren't going generic when they're supposed to", through patent gaming or because complex drugs are "natural monopolies because they're uncopyable" (gene therapies). Biosimilars never got "confident interchangeability", so biologics don't fall off a patent cliff: "a market failure". The industry is "rediscovering our roots as landlords, as rent extractors".
- Discounting argument: to an investor funding a Phase 1 trial, billions earned decades later are little incentive "when you discount that back to year negative five". So "every high margin dollar that society spends for branded drugs should be relatively early in the life of that drug in its first 10 to 15 years".
- Enbrel (host): when Amgen bought Immunex, the deal model needed "$3 billion a year out of this product by 2005" and assumed "patent expiration 2012". A later process patent extended it, and it now makes "six, seven, eight billion dollars a year". Paying for that tells the industry to "keep pulling this trick".
- Humira: AbbVie's non-stinging reformulation won a patent "that helps keep humor biosimilars at bay". Worth having, but "a quantum of incentive for a quantum of innovation" means \~six more months, like a pediatric extension. Humira "collects like $15 billion a year from the US", so six months is \~$7.5bn.
- Prilosec to Nexium: AstraZeneca's Prilosec sold "like four or five billion dollars a year". Nexium took three modest upgrades (a pure enantiomer, double the dose, once-daily dosing); six months each would have paid "six to eight billion dollars more", and "we would have had generic Nexium in like 2003."
- AbbVie "has already reconciled itself" to Humira going "off a cliff" once biosimilars launch, if payers drive competition. The next test is Merck: as Keytruda nears the end of its "mortgage period", follow-on patents could add "another 10 or 15 years". Enforcing them would make Merck "yet another landlord".
- The risk grows as gene and cell therapies age (e.g. Novartis's Zolgensma): many companies could have "their builder souls hijacked by long-term rents".
- Size of the rent: "if I had to guess maybe 10% of branded drug revenues. So that's like 25 billion". Yet the industry's "profit margins as a whole are just 10%" once you count the loss-makers: "Beyonce makes millions", but include "all the street performers".
- Me-too drugs aren't the problem: they are "the consequence of hustling to try to be first or best", then compete on price for share. "That's good for society."
- Pledges can't fix it: if Biogen promised to cut a drug to "slightly north of the cost of production" after 13.5 years, "activist investors could ultimately take over the company, change up the board" and keep it expensive; "arguably it would be their their duty". It needs a change in law.
- RA Capital's website said for over a decade that it likes "small molecules that do the job of biologics because small molecules go generic", although "technically it doesn't maximize our returns" (since removed).
- Patients: out-of-pocket costs "are corrosive because they deter appropriate utilization". Give the preferred drug zero co-pay: if Lilly offers the biggest rebate on Humalog, Novolog can carry a high co-pay or no coverage.
- Politics (2020): some incumbent Democrats signed HR3 as "a harmless shot across the bow", but it "miscalculated the impact on innovation". Price controls on "big bad pharma" also hit venture-backed drugs that "will someday be some pharma's drug". His pitch: "shift your hand from the heart to the tumor" of rent-seeking.
