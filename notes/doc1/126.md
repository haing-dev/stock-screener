### 126. Rod Wong — Tariff drill, obesity's second wave and a five-year biotech bear market (Killik & Co, Jun 2025)

*Founder and CIO of RTW Investments (founded 2009; \~80 staff in New York, London and Shanghai; \~$6.5bn AUM per the host), which also runs the London-listed RTW Biotech Opportunities; interviewed by Gordon Smith on Killik & Co's Investor Insights podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D90GUjZUT1w)

**Takeaway:** By mid-2025 Rod judged the tariff and FDA scares past their peak and drug pricing the last big policy risk, yet biotech valuations still priced in fear after a record fifth straight year of lagging the S&P. RTW had sheltered in late-stage and commercial names and was starting to shift back toward clinical-stage ones; obesity is its biggest theme.

- Tariff drill: when "Liberation Day" dangled drug-specific tariffs, no one at RTW "knew really anything about tariffs". The new government affairs team studied what might come, and after a weekend with outside experts the six analyst sub-teams talked to "every single portfolio company" in two days. Most biotechs had limited exposure; RTW sold the one holding with significant risk, then repeated the sweep outside the portfolio and found "a couple companies" worth shorting as a hedge, "all in the span of less than a week".
- Universe: "less than two dozen" giant multinational pharmas, then \~600–700 US-listed therapeutics companies, "most of those development stage" and very few with revenue and earnings. Add 100–200 meaningful companies abroad and a couple hundred listed medtechs.
- Where to look (Jun 2025): "the biggest opportunity is actually in smaller but mid to late stage development or early stage commercial publicly traded companies". In private markets "you have to be very careful right now" and very selective.
- Focus areas, in order: cardiometabolic ("The whale within that is obesity by a mile", with MASH, or fatty liver, a multi-billion-dollar market just emerging); immunology, where innovation is shifting from new targets to combinations of them and coming more from smaller companies; neuropsychiatry, with new mechanisms after a near two-decade gap since SSRIs; and rare genetic diseases.
- In cancer, the focus has moved to making chemotherapy better: antibody-drug conjugates steer the toxic payload to the tumor, "a huge wave of innovation for the next several years". Medtech "remains a policy safe haven" and is likely where AI models first reach real products.
- Obesity: GLP-1s already reach about two-thirds of the type 2 diabetics who need them, but obesity is "probably in the second inning of the eight or nine inning game". Today's weekly injectables have poor GI tolerability: "half of patients come off those drugs within half a year or so". The first true oral (a small molecule from Eli Lilly) comes next year, and a second wave hits around the start of 2028 as the market fragments.
- Only 10 smaller public biotechs focus on obesity; given an opportunity that is "the biggest ever in our industry", he thinks "that number should be 30" or more. RTW backs private contenders to become the next listed launchers (2028–2030+): two of RTW Bio's largest positions are private obesity companies, one a better injectable and one a true oral. Metsera \[?\], one of the year's biggest IPOs, trades at "north of $2.5 billion market cap" while early stage, showing "the supply demand imbalance".
- Tariffs (Jun 2025): exposure sits with the multinationals and hardly any biotechs. More than half of the multinationals have committed to US manufacturing investments since Liberation Day, so drug tariffs may never come, and if they do they "will probably be quite modest".
- FDA: fear built through RFK's nomination to run HHS, a cut of "over 10,000 employees" and the exit of Peter Marks, prized by investors for championing "regulatory flexibility" at the biologics division, in favor of Vinay Prasad, who has talked of a higher bar. Now it is "well past peak uncertainty": Makary and Prasad promise continued flexibility and target bottlenecks such as manufacturing and trial times.
- Drug pricing is "still the biggest remaining uncertainty". After a Trump executive order the week before, he expects pricing to be folded into trade talks so US patients pay a little less and other developed countries a little more. MFN pricing in the US would face the courts and need Congress, so it is "more of a negotiation tactic rather than a real policy risk".
- Bear market: the XBI had never lagged the S&P for more than one year in a row "except for this time. And now we're into the fifth year in a row." Tilting to late-stage and commercial companies protected capital, and many launches beat Street expectations, while pure VC funds had "a hard go". With "a lot of fear reflected in the valuations" and the bear market in "the later innings", RTW is shifting some capital back to clinical-stage companies.
- Psychedelics are his highest-conviction new area: about "three times a bigger effect" than SSRIs, and a session of a couple of hours may last several months. Data are strong in treatment-resistant depression, with proof of concept in anxiety and PTSD. RTW Bio holds GH Research (inhaled, short session) and Compass Pathways (psilocybin; longer session but closest to market).
