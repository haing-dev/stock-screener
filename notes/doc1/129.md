### 129. Steve Levine — Psilocybin on the verge: launching COMP360 for treatment-resistant depression (RTW Investments LP, Jul 2026)

*Psychiatrist and Chief Patient Officer of Compass Pathways, who founded the Actify Neurotherapies ketamine clinics in 2010, interviewed on The RTW Podcast about bringing COMP360 psilocybin to treatment-resistant depression (TRD).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t8FNmVNVu3c)

**Takeaway:** The first FDA-approved psilocybin therapy could reach the market within a year (Jul 2026), and COMP360 can launch through SPRAVATO's existing clinics, staff and billing codes. Its edge is durability: one or two doses plus a few re-treatments a year vs 25–50 SPRAVATO visits, in a TRD population that is mostly untreated.

- Market: TRD affects "four million people", yet SPRAVATO (esketamine) is the only drug being prescribed for it, reaching "only about 100,000" (Jul 2026). Many of the 50+ approved antidepressants, and many drugs built for TRD, failed in it. So the plan is not to switch SPRAVATO patients; "a rising tide raises all ships" by raising awareness.
- Durability is what clinicians praise "loudest", per Compass's medical science liaisons. SPRAVATO takes "Somewhere between 25 and 50 three-hour appointments per year"; COMP360 may need one or two initial doses, then re-treatment "on average, two to four times per year in total". The COMP005 and COMP006 trials tested one or two 25mg doses.
- Ready-made channel: "A SPRAVATO room is a COMP360 room." Drug handling (assuming Schedule III), rooms and staffing are the same: one prescriber (psychiatrist, nurse practitioner or physician assistant) plus nurses, medical assistants and technicians running several rooms at once, often with CCTV. About 7,300 sites deliver SPRAVATO, with prescriptions concentrated in a subset (Jul 2026), so Compass knows who is "ready operationally".
- Reimbursement first: "No one is going to prescribe COMP360 if they lose money on it". SPRAVATO launched without a CPT billing code, so Compass and specialty societies applied to the AMA for codes covering monitoring on the day of a psychedelic dose. They went live in January 2024 and bill hour by hour.
- Scheduling: psilocybin is Schedule I, which complicates trials. Approval removes the "no approved medical use" basis; FDA's controlled-substance staff then run an eight-factor analysis and recommend DEA rescheduling, base case Schedule III. About half of states have trigger laws and the rest need administrative or legislative action, so Compass's government-affairs team is already working with those states to avoid "undue delay".
- FDA: breakthrough therapy designation brings "robust dialogue" and help with trial design, and the psychiatry division "has remained largely intact" despite leadership turnover. Compass wants the same standards as any TRD drug: "We don't want corners cut."
- Referral bottleneck: TRD patients are "locked in the permafrost of primary care". There aren't enough psychiatrists, only about half accept insurance, and those often have long waiting lists. He wants primary care to refer straight to interventional psychiatry.
- Delivery is "monitoring and support", not psychotherapy. At Actify many refractory patients rejected therapy ("I've had decades of therapy. It never helped me"). COMP360 uses specially trained providers, and the launch will need provider training.
- Trial patients: typically a woman in her forties, in an episode lasting at least a year (often two or three), with six to seven prior episodes on average and moderate-to-severe depression at baseline.
- Next: a 22-participant open-label PTSD Phase 2 (mainly safety and feasibility) was well tolerated with "a really exciting efficacy signal", so Compass is starting a registrational Phase 2b/3 in about 300 participants. 13 million live with PTSD, served by "two old generic SSRIs" with no approval this century. "We don't intend to be a single asset company forever": OCD and bipolar depression show signals in investigator-initiated studies.
