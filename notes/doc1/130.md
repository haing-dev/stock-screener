### 130. Connor Williams — Psilocybin: from counterculture to cutting-edge psychiatry (RTW Investments LP, Jun 2026)

*Senior research analyst at RTW Investments covering disorders of the nervous system; an RTW Podcast episode on psilocybin and Compass Pathways' COMP360 after its Phase 3 results.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B_g4x_s6aq4)

**Takeaway:** COMP360 beat placebo in all three trials Compass ran, a record "very few psychiatric drugs" have, and it can slot into the clinic network built for J&J's Spravato (over $2bn run rate, Jun 2026). New psychiatric drugs tend to add to the market rather than take share, so there is room for both.

- Base rates: "There are innumerable approved psychiatric drugs that failed many, many trials before they ultimately got the one or two required for FDA clinical approval." Spravato's pivotal program had five trials and only two hit. COMP360 separated from placebo in its Phase 2b and both Phase 3s.
- Phase 3 design: over 800 patients with documented treatment-resistant depression (TRD: failed two or more prior drug lines). The first study tested one 25mg dose vs placebo. The second gave two doses three weeks apart at 25mg, 10mg or 1mg; the 1mg arm is an "active placebo", too low to work, so every patient gets a dose and dose response can be compared.
- Market proof: Spravato, the only drug on the market for TRD, is "big business" for J&J at "over $2 billion a year in run rate", and J&J estimates over $3 billion in 2027 and 2028 (Jun 2026). It shows a perception-altering drug given in clinics can sell at scale.
- Competition: in mental illness, even when several drugs enter in a short period, "none of those drugs really pull down the sales of the other drugs", because patients need "two, three, even more options" before one works. "no one drug can change this disease."
- Scalability drives trial design: underground use pairs psilocybin with a therapist, but therapy is "very difficult to scale". Sponsors now test without concomitant psychotherapy: "If the drug is able to have its effect without psychotherapy, that means we have an intervention that's much more scalable." It won't be take-home "any time soon"; dosing happens in interventional psychiatry clinics.
- Durability: Compass's 005 data showed the effect "could last well out to 26 weeks". Spravato maintenance is weekly or every two weeks; COMP360 patients may return an "order of magnitude less frequently", perhaps two or three times a year, and some may need one course for years.
- Rapid onset: depression scores (MADRS) drop at the first time point measured, and trial psychiatrists say patients report feeling better right after the session. But no one can predict responders: "do we know who will respond? No."
- Safety diligence: RTW "spent an enormous amount of time" on the psychosis risk as it ramped up investing in psychedelics. Controlled studies show hallucinogen persisting perceptual disorder "isn't really a massive issue in clinical trials", and psilocybin is not addictive. Acute hallucinations and nausea make these "impairing drugs" that must be given in a clinic, and Compass targets severe illness with no other options, where the benefit-risk is right.
- Old research as evidence: for psychedelic companies RTW invested in, it went back to old research (e.g. Alexander Shulgin's account of 5-MeO-DMT in one of his books). Academic studies "give us incremental confidence as investors" that the effects were real and the drugs safe, against war-on-drugs-era literature that called them dangerous.
- The trip matters: psychedelics "with more intense perceptual effects, tend to have greater efficacy". Whether 5-HT2A agonists without a trip will work: "We don't know." Such a drug would be "an even more scalable intervention. I wish those companies luck."
- Infrastructure and policy: COMP360 "gets to slot into" Spravato's clinic model (nurse and CCTV monitoring), "not a paradigm that has to be reblazed", and the heads of HHS, the VA and the FDA have spoken favorably about drugs for PTSD and TRD (Jun 2026). Open items: FDA labeling, a REMS, and Compass scaling delivery.
