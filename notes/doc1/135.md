### 135. Rod Wong — Full-life-cycle investing through the 2022 biotech crash (BIOS, Sep 2022)

*Co-founder and managing partner of RTW Investments and an adjunct at NYU Stern teaching healthcare financial analysis; interviewed on BIOS's Ventures series in the middle of the 2022 biotech sell-off.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RttLux_Ax7o)

**Takeaway:** In Sep 2022 small-cap biotech was down \~70% from its peak and a record share of companies traded below cash, which on valuation made it worse than the genomics bust. Rod saw a cleansing that would reward survivors and the investors able to fund them, and argued the next decades' drugs will come from refining today's technologies, not inventing new ones.

- Why full life cycle: "science doesn't care what stage a company is in and so we figure we shouldn't either". Meeting science where it is, sometimes a private company or a project still in a university, meant building venture and company-creation skills. In-house company builders let RTW take a more active role than most hedge-fund-structured firms.
- The crash in numbers (Sep 2022): measured peak to trough from 2021, small-cap biotech indices are down 70%, second only to the genomics bubble's 85% fall and "way worse already than the financial crisis" (down 46%). On valuation it is already worse than the genomics bust: the share of companies "trading below cash is the highest in history", and price-to-sales is "meaningfully lower than the bottom set post the bursting of the genomics bubble".
- Two causes. 2021 brought healthcare-specific problems: "a record number of ipos of very young companies some with questionable business plans" that then hit setbacks, COVID-related FDA slowdowns and a front-page drug-pricing fight in Congress. In 2022 macro forces (interest rates, inflation, the war in Ukraine) hit all growth stocks and "overwhelmed all the healthcare specific issues".
- What comes next: "a gradual cleansing process" of the excesses, and "for those that can survive or navigate this period of time it's also going to be a period of tremendous opportunity". He tells his team that the hardest periods are when you find "the most amazing opportunities" at attractive valuations, and when companies "need us most" because capital is hard to get.
- Being a good partner in a crash starts with "a steady hand", then capital when it is needed most, in creative mixes. RTW is adding instruments: royalties, licensing deals ("specifically geographic ones") and SPACs.
- Beyond the odds of success and what each possible end state is worth, the swing variable differs by case: a clinical trial, a regulatory call, a patent dispute or the size of the treatable population. RTW matches expertise to each question, and repeating the exercises builds "institutional memory" of what works.
- Track record (Sep 2022): eight of RTW's top 10 investments life to date "resulted in successfully developed products", which he ties to staying with companies through setbacks. RTW has "something like 22" MDs and PhDs, each focused on a different area.
- Technology: "the next cycle of the most exciting tech" is "persisting on the current cycle". Gene and cell therapy, bispecifics, ADCs, protein degraders, gene editing and RNA are past their first proofs of concept and in a "growing pains phase" (safety issues, manufacturing, regulatory standards). With no new technology for one to two decades, refining these would still "generate more drugs than we've seen in the last half century".
- Stay inside your competence: RTW is wary of areas "where we're not smart enough to understand" or where the science is "still a little bit mysterious", such as the microbiome.
- Personal lesson: in medical school he traded the inheritance meant for his tuition ("degenerate gambling"), multiplied it several fold and was briefly a millionaire "for a day" at 23. He round-tripped most of it in the bust, finished school with loans and credit card debt, and swore never to invest again "unless i really understood what i was doing".
- From his NYU Stern course: "the financials actually work out" when great science becomes an innovation that changes patients' lives; the finance "is kind of the sideshow".
- The London-listed RTW Venture Fund (ticker RTW) traded at roughly a 20% discount to NAV (Sep 2022), after trading "pretty consistently at a premium" before the correction. Its book is about a third private companies, a third public companies first bought while private, and a third public positions started in public markets.
- The RTW Foundation is building a nonprofit drug-development institute for ultra-rare diseases, whose therapies often get no industry funding "because the commercial opportunities aren't big enough". Its first program is a gene therapy for Christianson syndrome, a severe central-nervous-system disease in infants.
- Screen implication: below-cash biotechs are most plentiful at the bottom of a crash, exactly when capital is hardest to raise, so a floor-value screen needs its cash-runway test.
