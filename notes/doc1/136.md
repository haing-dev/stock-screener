### 136. Krishna Guha — Warsh's Fed, a 5% 10-year and Bessent's failed buyback gamble (The Real Eisman Playbook, Oct 2026)

*Evercore's Fed watcher, interviewed by Steve Eisman (Ep 78) on new Fed chair Kevin Warsh's first hike, the jump in long-term yields and the Treasury's bond buybacks.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PUiD8NpknW8)

**Takeaway:** Guha blames the 10-year's jump to \~5.25% (Oct 2026) on a "perfect storm": AI-hyperscaler borrowing on top of a 6%-of-GDP deficit, a higher neutral rate from the AI boom, and oil, made worse by Warsh dropping forward guidance. A 5% handle already strains the non-AI economy; the thing to watch is whether stocks can take it, because consumer spending rests on wealth gains at the top.

- Why Warsh hiked (Oct 2026): inflation has been above the 2% target for "more than 5 years" (pandemic, tariffs, then a Middle East war and oil), and he fears it gets locked into wages and prices. Guha expects inflation to keep falling (tariff effects dropping out, no lasting oil impulse) but, with a solid jobs market, sees the hike as reasonable "insurance".
- Warsh sees himself as a "change agent" and a supply-sider, breaking with \~20 years of new-Keynesian Fed thinking. He thinks QE was "massively overdone": right when markets seize up, wrong as a way to keep pushing growth after a crisis.
- Balance sheet: \~$6.5–7tn, down from \~$9tn at the peak. One of Warsh's five task forces covers it, but he will wait for yields to come down before shrinking it, since dumping bonds now would "drive long-term interest rates to the sky".
- QE debate: Eisman thinks QE "did absolutely nothing to help the actual economy, but inflated asset prices". Guha disagrees: buying government bonds also cuts corporate borrowing costs, because "a company's borrowing rate will be the government's borrowing rate plus some kind of spread".
- The yield jump: the 10-year went from \~4.5% around July to \~5.25% (Oct 2026). AI hyperscalers' debt issuance competes with Treasuries while the deficit runs at 6% of GDP. Bond investors also see a multi-year AI boom, "a huge demand to invest chasing a limited pool of savings", so the normal (neutral) rate is higher.
- Oil: since the US–Iran ceasefire broke down in early summer, diesel is at all-time highs and has roughly doubled in a short time. Refined products rose far more than crude or gasoline; Guha likens the diesel move to oil "closer to 200 bucks".
- Forward guidance: Guha agrees with Warsh that hard promises ("raise rates three times not more not less") belong only in emergencies. But dropping the broader explanation of the Fed's playbook removed the "guard rails" and "has contributed in my view to rates markets running rampant over recent weeks"; it is "deeply inefficient" for markets to guess how the Fed weighs a decision.
- Bessent's buybacks (announced Aug 2026): the Treasury buys back long bonds and funds them with bills, a kind of "operation twist", at $4bn and then $6bn per operation. Eisman: in a \~$40tn market that is a "BB gun". Guha: "you've absolutely brought a knife to a gunfight"; Bessent wanted to give leveraged Treasury shorts two-sided risk, "bit off more than he could chew" and was right to back off.
- Net effect may be negative through credibility: "serious governments like the US government tend not to intervene in their own debt market. That's the sort of thing you find more often in struggling emerging markets". Eisman's verdict: "it seems like it's clearly failed".
- Eisman's "bazooka" idea: loosen big banks' liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) so they buy Treasuries. Guha: the administration and the Fed are reworking liquidity rules so banks hold fewer Fed reserves (helping Warsh shrink the balance sheet) and more government debt, though banks may prefer bills to long bonds.
- Where yields bite: "a story of two halves". If an AI investment "could yield a return of 500% or 10,000%", "it doesn't make much difference whether the borrowing cost is 6 or 7 8 or 9%", but old-economy and small businesses feel it. A "five handle" already strains the non-AI economy; housing is "completely bombed out because mortgage rates are 7%". Hurting AI capex itself may take a "six handle or something" (Oct 2026).
- What to watch (Oct 2026): AI capex largely buys imported chips, while consumption is spent at home and held up by wealth gains at the top. So "the one thing to watch right now is whether the stock market can withstand, you know, 10-year yields in this zip code". Eisman: markets "simply move with oil prices and the yield on the 10-year".
- Fed report card: Greenspan "maybe even a 10 out of 10" on the macroeconomy but "three or four out of 10" on regulation. "Bernanke's Fed stood between the US economy and a return to the Great Depression". Yellen kept rates low longer to heal the labor market. Powell's COVID backstop of private borrowing averted a 2008-scale crisis; he was slow on inflation but caught up for a soft landing, and the missed last mile is "more fairly placed at Trump's door than his".
