### 137. Crescat Capital — Gold, silver and fiat money: history's case for metal-exploration stocks (Crescat Capital, May 2024)

*A Crescat Capital presenter (not named in the captions) walks through the history of metal and paper money to pitch Crescat's activist metals-exploration strategy; its exploration expert, Quinton \[?\], has 40 years in the industry.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tImUmy4KKGc)

**Takeaway:** Debt and deficits make inflation pressures "intractable" and fiat currencies prone to debasement, while metal supply lags demand, so Crescat expects a precious-metals bull market like 1973–74. Junior explorers lag gold badly (May 2024), but most are little more than a claim, so picking the right ones is "really critical".

- Three reasons the timing "has never been more compelling" (May 2024): developed-economy debt and deficits leave fiat currencies more exposed to debasement than at any time since the 1970s; metal supply is constrained as US infrastructure, reshoring and green-energy spending lift demand; and an activist exploration strategy, drilling for economic discoveries, can outperform metal prices.
- The lag: gold has broken out to new all-time highs, but the exploration index (the TSXV composite, \~51% exploration-focused miners) needs \~7x to regain its 2007 high.
- Selection risk: 3,600 mining and metal stocks are listed globally, and most "have mining claims and a dream of a big deposit". Crescat leans on its expert's 40 years of exploration experience to pick.
- Crescat's companies expect 114 drills turning over the next 12 months, more greenfield drilling than all the gold majors combined (Barrick, Newmont, Agnico Eagle). The majors "have abdicated exploration really to the Juniors".
- M&A thesis: the majors have underinvested in exploration and new mines for so long that they will have to buy juniors to grow production, so "there is an m&a cycle coming".
- Paper money began in China's Song Dynasty (\~960–1279 AD) and worked partly because notes were exchangeable for metal coins. The Mongol dynasty then printed excess paper to fund wars, depleted its silver and suffered hyperinflation.
- Supply can hinge on one deposit: Spain's American mines produced over 150,000 tons of silver in the 16th–18th centuries, over 80% of world supply. Cerro Rico at Potosí (modern Bolivia) alone gave over 37% of world output from 1545 to 1810.
- Mark Twain's lesson from Nevada's Comstock silver boom: miners worked hard for little while "the mine owners who were trading paper were getting rich". Many Comstock stocks were "total scams insider trading frauds based on bogus Discovery stories", hence "a gold mine is a hole in the ground with a liar standing on top of it".
- Newmont: William Boyce Thompson founded it in 1921 and built it by "hiring the very best Prospectors geologists engineers accountants and legal advisers". 100 shares bought when it went public in 1925 at $40 became 4,330 shares worth about $140,000 by February 1972, a "35 bagger".
- 1973–74: after Nixon cut the dollar's link to gold, Barron's gold-mining index rose 5x in two years while the S&P 500 fell 50%. Rising inflation and rates "decimated" the 1972 Nifty Fifty growth bubble. Crescat calls today's mega-cap tech, AI and crypto excitement a "highly speculative world" born of fiat money printing.
- Bre-X (from 1993): a small Canadian miner claimed over 200 million ounces in Busang, Indonesia, which would have been the largest gold discovery ever. The samples were "salted", and Freeport-McMoRan found the whole thing was a fraud.
- Canada's response, NI 43-101: a qualified geologist or engineer signs off technical reports, resources and reserves are reported on a standard basis, and independent technical reports are filed at various project stages. Canadian exchanges now list most of the world's miners, yet the industry's record of fraud is still "an extra hurdle" that keeps it depressed.
