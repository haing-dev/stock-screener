### 141. Jim Rogers — Commodities, disasters the world needs, debt and why hot tips ruin you (Generating Alpha Podcast, Jan 2025)

*Co-founder of Soros Fund Management and the Quantum Fund, creator of the Rogers International Commodities Index and a former Columbia Business School finance professor, now based in Singapore; interviewed by two high-school students.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nw-jo_UM6DU)

**Takeaway:** Know as much as you can, from copper and wheat to technology, invest only in what you understand, and look for disasters in things the world needs, such as agriculture (Jan 2025). Money printing and record US debt are long-run dangers, and China is rising again.

- First big trade: he bought puts expecting a crash, it came four or five months later, and he sold the puts on the day the market hit bottom. Four or five months after that he "lost everything": his shorts were in companies that later went bankrupt, but they went "through the roof" first.
- The lesson: "there was a lot I didn't know especially about emotion in the market". He saved up and started over.
- Quantum's vision with Soros: "we wanted to make money". Hedge funds, reputedly started by A.W. Jones (probably 1949) as long/short funds taking a share of profits, are "an efficient way to manage money and get paid if you're good at it".
- Find your own method: Roy Neuberger of Neuberger Berman knew every tick in 50 or 100 NYSE stocks and "didn't care what the companies did". Rogers needed to know the companies and work out what the future would be.
- Know everything: Quantum invested in "stocks bonds currencies commodities all over the world long and short", and he read the Wall Street Journal's commodity page daily when most people didn't know it existed. "if you're building power plants you better know the price of copper".
- Look for disasters in essentials: "agriculture has been a disaster", and with something "the world needs and it changes you'll probably be very successful". He expects agriculture to get better; if none of the hosts' classmates want to be farmers, they "probably should be farmers".
- China is the only country in history to rise to the top again after collapsing, several times ("three or four", later "four or five"). In the 1980s he told people to teach their children Chinese while everyone said "Japan Japan Japan".
- Uzbekistan (Jan 2025): he is "extremely bullish" and invests there, though "it doesn't mean I know what I'm doing". Decades ago he didn't know the country and it didn't matter; the world keeps changing.
- Cutting losses: some sell after a fixed fall such as 12%, but "my pathetic answer usually is I just take the pain until I realize oh I made a bad mistake and finally take the loss".
- Money printing: central banks treat it as the easy fix, but "there are very few countries that have become successful by debasing the currency".
- Debt: the US was a creditor nation until about 1987 and is now "the largest debtor nation in the history of the world". Britain, the richest country 100 years ago, kept spending and borrowing and 50 years later needed an IMF bailout: "that could happen to the US".
- Hot tips "will ruin you": if you don't know why you bought, you won't know what to do when it rises or falls. "only invest in what you yourself know a lot about", and "don't count on luck because Luck's not going to save you".
