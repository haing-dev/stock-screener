### 144. Kenneth Tropin — Why macro is thriving: busy central banks and missing duration risk (Bloomberg Live, Oct 2022)

*Founder and chairman of Graham Capital Management (\~$19bn: \~$12bn quant, \~$7bn discretionary), interviewed on stage at a Bloomberg Live event in a banner year for macro; the host put Graham's funds up \~20–50% in 2022 and some macro managers up 200% or more.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FSzs-eP5oGw)

**Takeaway:** Macro opportunity tracks how much central banks move, and in 2022 they moved more than in the whole previous decade. Tropin (Oct 2022) expected inflation to stay above target until 2025 or later, so long bonds that paid nothing for duration risk looked mispriced.

- Central-bank moves drive the opportunity: they "equate to FX volatility" and very often to equity volatility. The Fed, ECB and Bank of England made 13 quarter-point-equivalent hikes in 2010–21 combined, 26 so far in 2022, with 44 priced for the year (Oct 2022).
- Divergence adds trades. Japan held its 25bp yield-band ceiling with USD/JPY at \~146; if it loosens the band, "there'd be a lot of opportunity on the downside in dollar Yen". Governor Kuroda's term ends in April 2023, "an important ... Catalyst to watch", and Japan had just intervened.
- Inefficient markets pay macro: the 1980s–90s had more "overshoots" and momentum events, while the efficient, near-zero-inflation 2010s left little to work with. Graham still "generally generated positive returns" then, so diversifying into macro "didn't cost you money".
- Inflation lesson from the Volcker era: the central bank eased early "because they thought they had it under control and then they had to raise rates again". Supply chains and energy prices are beyond the Fed's reach; the market saw easing from end-2023 ("I'm skeptical"), and "it may be 2025 before we get to Target if then".
- He sides with the Summers camp over Siegel's: "inflation's so poisonous" that the Fed should err on getting it "comfortably down to Target", even at the price of a recession: "when is kind of the key question".
- Two inflations: Europe's is supply-driven (Netherlands \~15%, power "hundreds of percent higher"), while the US adds labor. Some of his 180 staff are 35 or younger and have never seen a recession; a workforce used to "constantly higher compensation" feeds inflation, and "that Trend will reverse it won't reverse quickly".
- Equities: after "a straight shot up in equity prices for 12 years", "we may be in a trading range for equities for some number of years".
- Bonds (Oct 2022): with the 10-year at 3.95%, the old premium for duration risk is "completely missing at the moment". If inflation lasts three to five years, he would want the 10-year 50–100bp higher, and the slightly inverted curve "should get steeper".
- Liquidity first: most of Graham's risk sits in "very liquid markets" so it can "exit positions without a great deal of expense". He wants to be "the guy who gets out of the market sooner than later not later at greater expense".
- 2022 winners: FX (EUR/USD from 1.20 to \~0.97, sterling, USD/JPY), energy (the most profitable commodity), and short bonds across durations and regions. The dollar is "definitely expensive" with a path of least resistance a little higher, but "we're pretty light"; he was neutral on sterling.
- Two speeds: discretionary traders "can be long a market today and within a few days have the other side", while the quant systems turn over weeks and months.
- Energy keeps inflation sticky: green policies, which "maybe they really need to be", come "at a cost of not having a lot of energy to use today", and crude was back in the $90s after Saudi Arabia's snub a week or two earlier.
- Stay where you have an engine: Graham's traders and systems can trade bitcoin, but positions are "very very very small" because "we try and stay away from things that we really don't have an engine for". Gold, down "10 or 15 percent" in 2022, "has been ineffective as a hedge"; Graham's quant models were short and made a little.
