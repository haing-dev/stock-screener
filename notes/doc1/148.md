### 148. Steve Eisman — Weekly Wrap: is OpenAI the Achilles' heel of the US economy? (The Real Eisman Playbook, Sep 2026)

*Steve Eisman's weekly market commentary, recorded Sep 3, 2026; he "predicted the end of the world once" (the 2008 crisis) and is in no rush to do it again.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RhLLwfwlNAU)

**Takeaway:** US growth rests on hyperscaler AI capex, which rests on two loss-making labs, Anthropic and OpenAI. OpenAI looks weaker (slowing revenue, surging costs, departures), and its failure could push the US into an almost immediate recession; Eisman isn't calling it yet (Sep 2026), but he is preparing.

- Rates (Sep 2026): the 10-year "almost brushed against 4.8%" and "at some level of interest rates, the market will correct". He thought 4.5% "was the Rubicon and I was wrong". Heavy AI debt issuance adds to the pressure, and Treasury Secretary Bessent's attempt to cut long rates "appears to have already failed".
- "the entire US economy hinges on the success of AI": if the spending stopped, "the economy would go into a recession almost immediately". Still, "right now the data does not support the end of the AI story, at least not yet."
- Two known worries needn't be fatal if AI pays off ("and it's a big if"). Hyperscalers' cash flow "has evaporated" under hundreds of billions each of AI capex, and some raise debt and equity to fund it. Nvidia-led circular financing (loans or equity stakes that customers spend on Nvidia chips, e.g. Poolside) "will probably turn out to be okay" if AI succeeds.
- The chain: Wall Street reports put \~70% of hyperscaler AI revenue on Anthropic and OpenAI, \~25–35% of total hyperscaler cloud revenue (he can't confirm). Oracle's \~$600bn backlog is \~50% OpenAI, and OpenAI is over 20% of CoreWeave's contracted order book.
- Hyperscaler capex of \~$700bn this year (2026), and more next year, is around half of the 2% GDP growth projected for 2026. So the economy depends on that capex, and the capex depends on Anthropic and OpenAI.
- OpenAI is the weaker lab and is losing senior people: chief revenue officer Denise Dresser quit in Aug 2026 after eight months (probably giving up unvested options), and former COO Brad Lightcap after eight years. Leaving as an IPO nears "is an important data point".
- OpenAI's June quarter (2026, per the Wall Street Journal): revenue $6.7bn, up "only 18%" on March, versus Anthropic's $11bn+, up over 100%. Costs reached $12.3bn, up $3bn: "in 3 months, revenue increased 1 billion, but costs surged 3 billion."
- Strategy error: OpenAI chased consumers by giving its product away or underpricing it, and now chases business customers late, behind Anthropic. Ed Zitron says it must raise $100bn a year for several years, and for an unprofitable company with "an insatiable need for capital, appearances matter more than anything".
- Oracle is the preview. Its S&P rating is BBB- ("barely above junk") after a big rise in debt. When RPO (backlog) jumped over $300bn in the Aug-2025 quarter to $455bn, the stock went from $230 to $330 in days, then gave back all the gains and more within months once analysts noted half was OpenAI. Now \~$145, down over 50% from the peak.
- If OpenAI failed (not his forecast, "at least not yet"): hyperscalers would cut capex and tech would correct, Nvidia included. So would banks and investment banks at "peak valuations" in an AI-fed deal cycle, and AI industrials (GE Vernova, Quanta, Eaton, Rockwell).
- Where to hide: "This is not a stock picking question, but a reallocation question." Safety sectors (healthcare, consumer staples) and safety subsectors such as P&C insurance within financials; he flags the LVHD, SPLV and KBWP ETFs. "I'm not making a major call. Not yet. I'm just preparing."
- Nvidia's July 2026 quarter: revenue growth over 100%, up from 85% in the April quarter. But note 7 of its 10-Q shows five direct customers at 22%, 14%, 13%, 11% and 10% of receivables (70% in all), and their AI revenue rests on "two companies that currently lose billions".
- OpenAI's ad business reached "$1 billion" of annualized run rate (ARR), cheered by the press, but "What exactly is being annualized?" It barely dents the losses, and falls far short of the $2.4bn of 2026 ad revenue OpenAI projected earlier in the year.
