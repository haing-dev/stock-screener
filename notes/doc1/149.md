### 149. Kenneth Tropin — How a macro fund lasts 30 years: experience, a daily risk meeting and many alpha sources (Graham Capital Management, Mar 2023)

*Founder and chairman of Graham Capital Management, interviewed by Bilal Hafeez on the Macro Hive podcast; recorded \~Mar 2023 (he says the SVB bank run came "two weeks ago") and posted Feb 2025.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6_6EjXnrh3I)

**Takeaway:** A macro fund lasts by pairing experience of crises with a steady, conservative management team, a culture that keeps talent, daily risk discipline and alpha sources that don't depend on one style. In Mar 2023 he expected 36–60 more good months for macro, leaned short equities at just under 19x forward earnings, and read SVB's failure as proof that duration risk was unpriced.

- Find your edge early: "you start focusing on something that you have a bit of an edge in". He was "one of the early people in the field" of CTAs (Dean Witter 1982–89, John Henry 1989–93) before starting Graham in 1994 with Tudor's backing, trading systems he had designed. Graham broke even in its first six months, then had a "great 95".
- Macro means trading equities, FX, fixed income and commodities, directionally or as relative value, though it "very typically is a directionally oriented strategy". Early hedge funds were meant to diversify away from beta, "hence the name hedge fund".
- Crisis record: in the 1987 crash his CTAs were "wrong-sided in foreign exchange" but short equities, then long bonds as yields fell; 2000–03 was good for CTAs as the NASDAQ fell; "the second half of 2008 was a great period for macro". 2010–21 was "a really quiet and somewhat dull period".
- Forward guidance killed the trade: when the Fed signaled no hikes for years, Graham's star Fed watcher Fred Levin \[?\] retired, saying "there's nothing to do for several years I must retire".
- Allocations: macro was half or even 70% of hedge-fund assets in the 1990s; CTAs and macro now run \~15–18% of a \~$4tn industry. With a good opportunity set he expected modest increases, e.g. a 10% macro allocation "maybe it'll go to 15", not a sea change (Mar 2023).
- SVB is the duration lesson: "their duration risk wasn't properly priced and that's why ... they went bankrupt", holding a large bond book with a \~10-year average duration at a \~1.5% yield. With an inverted curve and "no term premium", stock-and-bond investors should look for diversifiers, and macro funds help "because they're just not correlated".
- What a conservative institution should look for in a macro fund: experience (Graham has lived through "counterparties failing" and "liquidity disappearing"), managers who are "conservative and are steady", the best people (pay plus a culture that attracts and keeps them) and risk management.
- Daily risk meeting: started in 2007 as counterparties got into trouble, at 9:30 every morning, covering "every Traders positions liquidity and counterparty risks and stress tests". It pays off over time, not in any given moment, "because you're just more attentive to it".
- Don't be a "one-trick pony" that only earns when momentum works: Graham added discretionary trading 24 years ago and recently long-term quantitative equities, hunting uncorrelated alpha "where we feel that we have an edge and it's synergistic". "there's no way you can do what worked 10 years ago and expect it to always work in the future".
- Pod shops: he respects their net returns, but some clients dislike pass-through fees ("the costs end up getting pretty high") and five-year lockups. Graham started its own macro-focused multistrategy fund, because offering "the same economics" as the best competitors is "essential to compete for the top people".
- Equities (Mar 2023): with recession the base case, a forward P/E "just under 19" should normalize at 16–17. With 4–5% risk-free rates "there is an alternative", so he leans short, though 15 years of "buy every dip" psychology "probably hasn't changed yet".
- FX and rates (Mar 2023): the dollar "looks a little weak" as EUR/USD tries to break above 1.10, with the ECB seen as more hawkish than the Fed. Rates are "priced about right", but the 2s10s curve (inverted as much as 100bp, now \~60bp) should steepen in a recession, and the market is "slightly optimistic" on US cuts (30–40bp priced for Q4).
- No 2008-style crisis, "although we came pretty close two weeks ago"; the Fed handled the bank run well, and the debt ceiling is "always ugly until it isn't" (Mar 2023).
- Careers and hiring: work closely with someone who "inspires you", and look for talent where others don't, since "if you fish in the same Pond as everybody else" the pickings are thinner. Read Reminiscences of a Stock Operator and Jack Schwager's Market Wizards.
