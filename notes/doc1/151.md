### 151. Kenneth Tropin — Stocks and bonds both look vulnerable, and macro is the hedge (Graham Capital Management, Aug 2025)

*Founder and chairman of Graham Capital Management, on the Risk and Return podcast with Charlie Gasparino (Fox Business) and Bob Sloan (S3 Partners), who add their own segment after the interview.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6VCqo84_CI)

**Takeaway:** Tropin (Aug 2025) can't recall a time when investors were more exposed: stocks and P/Es near records (\~24.5x forward), a thin term premium, and a 40% stock–bond correlation that may stop bonds hedging a selloff. Macro is one of the few hedge-fund styles that tends to work in an equity correction, while money locked in illiquid private equity and multistrat funds may not.

- Term premium (Aug 2025): the 2s10s curve pays only "about 40 basis points"; the "historical average since 1990 is about 100 basis points", and it has been as high as 300bp. With a growing deficit and "no plan that I've heard about" to cut it, "it's a concerning time if you're a fixed income investor".
- Equities: "I can't really recall a set of circumstances" in which "you're more vulnerable than you are today". Prices and valuations are at records (\~24.5x one year out), and stocks and bonds are 40% correlated (normal is zero or slightly negative), so investors "may not get diversification from bonds". Geopolitics are "as unstable as I can recall", and tariff inflation limits the Fed's room to ease.
- AI carries the market: valuations rest on a pro-business administration and "a huge bet on AI", and "there's no reason for equities to be at record highs unless you believe AI is going to create a lot of ... earnings momentum and growth and new jobs". His colleagues split on whether AI destroys or creates jobs, and fast job creation would bring wage pressure; stagflation is "a possibility".
- April 2025 Treasury selloff after the Liberation Day tariffs: "a panic in the fixed income markets", caused by an "almost unprecedented change in policy" that left markets "really really illquid", so moves were exaggerated. Reactions have since grown "more muted each time"; a manager wants opportunities, but not ones so noisy they are "untradeable". The VIX says risk is low, which he doubts.
- Trump's tariff numbers are "his way of negotiating": giant opening demands, then a compromise, which won a "pretty favorable deal" from Europe. Markets aren't used to it: neither the Obama and Biden administrations nor Trump's first term acted like this on trade.
- Fed (Aug 2025): the year began "expecting four cuts this year. Now maybe we'll get one", and the Fed is split between tariffs-slow-growth and tariffs-are-inflationary camps. He expected a cut in the last third of 2025. Pressure on Fed independence upsets markets, but "this is all going to go away" as Powell terms out and Trump picks someone capable on "the accommodative side of the ledger".
- Deficit risk: when markets get spooked is very hard to predict. SVB's problems: "Nobody was thinking about them until they were"; the tech bubble and 2008 turned the same way, because "psychology is one of the most unpredictable" parts of investing. At Shearson in 1980 he had clients whose bonds "went down 45% in value".
- Multistrats such as Citadel and Millennium have done "a magnificent job", with consistent, high-Sharpe returns, but helped by a good equity market: "22 was not a good year for the multistrats", so there is "some correlation" with equities. Investors accept lockups to get those returns.
- The setup to fear: an equity correction while much money is tied up in illiquid private equity and multistrat funds "could be pretty provocative". Then "macro is one of the few" styles that tends to perform; "Equity long short and credit and other things, not so much. Private equity, not so much."
- Dollar and diversification: the dollar "sold off 3 or 4%" after the EU tariff deal and should move into a trading range. Hold some assets outside the US and outside traditional investments (crypto has been "evergreen", gold has done reasonably well, EM bond spreads are "really tight"), but "I'd rather invest in the United States" for its regulation, resources and transparency.
- Crypto: asked whether this is a David Swensen-style moment, "I'm starting to ... believe that"; "a lot of the infrastructure behind crypto is even more compelling than necessarily just betting on the price" of bitcoin.
- Bob Sloan (S3 Partners) separates "battleground" stocks from meme stocks by active long versus short interest. True meme stocks (AMC, GME) have $150–200M of long holdings, and "most of the time, the short interest in those stocks is bigger than the long interest", so retail can swing them. Kohl's has \~$0.75bn of active longs and a ratio of \~1, so longs book profits into rallies.
- Gasparino's items (Aug 2025): Citi is "snooping around for a brokerage firm", perhaps Stifel (\~2,500 brokers, \~$11bn market cap) or the much bigger Raymond James (\~8,000 brokers, \~$38bn). He calls Warner Bros. Discovery "an interesting counterintuitive story": near "penny stock levels" ($6–7) in 2024, roughly doubled since, cutting debt, and split into two units that are "much easier to sell".
