### 152. Jeffrey Hirsch — The calendar edge: midterm bottoms, the Best Six Months and the January barometer (The Real Eisman Playbook, Apr 2026)

*Author of the annual Stock Trader's Almanac, which his father Yale Hirsch started in 1966; interviewed by Steve Eisman (Ep 56) on the seasonal patterns the book tracks.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mLJLmvDFD4)

**Takeaway:** Markets follow the calendar because people and institutions repeat the same behavior: fund year-ends, window dressing, 401(k) inflows, holidays. Some patterns have passed out-of-sample tests; for 2026, a midterm year, history points to a bottom around Q2–Q3 and a "sweet spot" from Q4 2026 to Q2 2027, but patterns decay, so Hirsch tracks which ones stop working.

- Midterm year, the "bottom pickers paradise": \~11 of the last 16 bear markets bottomed in a midterm year, and Q2–Q3 is the weak spot of the four-year cycle (2022 was "a nice textbook October midterm year bottom"). From the midterm low to the pre-election-year high the Dow has gained 46.3% on average since 1914, the NASDAQ 66.6%.
- The "sweet spot" runs from Q4 of the midterm year to Q2 of the pre-election year, i.e. Sep 30, 2026 to Jun 30, 2027: on average 19% for the Dow, 20% for the S&P and 29.3% for the NASDAQ. Pre-election-year highs cluster near the last trading day of December.
- Best Six Months switching strategy (in the Almanac since 1986): own the Dow and S&P from the end of October to Apr 30 (the NASDAQ's best eight months run to June), timed with Gerald Appel's MACD, which is "not something you use in a vacuum" but a confirmation of another reason to buy or sell. "you can't sell in May and go away if you didn't buy in October"; in May they don't go away but get defensive and sell losers and underperformers.
- Gerald Loeb's rule: "go against your instincts", sell losers fast and let winners ride; too few investors sell their losers. Hirsch likes to "sell half on a double" or take some profits after a big gain.
- Evidence: around 2008 David Aronson (Evidence-Based Technical Analysis) tested 6,200-plus black-box systems against chance and "They all failed". Tested from 1987, after it was published, the Best Six Months was "the only one that had predictive power and was not the result of chance".
- Why it works: mutual funds' Oct 31 fiscal year-end creates "a vortex of action", September is the worst month with end-of-quarter window dressing, then funds chase year-end numbers and bonus and holiday money arrives: Nov–Jan are the best three months. Eisman: a fund dumps a stock down 50% because "they don't want their investors to to know that they ever owned it", when it maybe should be doubling down.
- Patterns change with the flows: the old month-end "five-day bulge" has faded, and a mid-month spike appeared from biweekly 401(k) contributions that funds must invest. The small-cap January effect now starts in the last two weeks of December, and the September reverse barometer is in the "indicator graveyard".
- Intraday: weak open, a low around 10–10:30, a late-morning rally, a drag around 2–2:30, then the closing rally ("the smart money"). Algorithms haven't killed it: "who programmed these algorithms? People." Hirsch sets buy limits at prices analyzed while the market was closed and waits for the afternoon lull.
- Sector seasonals (Apr 2026): copper and energy stocks (XLE) tend to bottom in December and top in April–May; utilities (XLU) are their trade for the market's weak March–October stretch, helped now by data-center power demand. He layers five disciplines: "Seasonality, fundamentals, technicals, monetary policy, and sentiment".
- January indicators: the full-month January barometer, the first five days and the Santa Claus rally (last five days of the year plus the first two of the new year, usually \~+1.5% for the S&P). With all three up, the market rose 90.6% of the time (29 of 32 years). In 2026 two of three were up (Santa Claus rally down): historically the S&P then rose over the next 11 months 87.5% of the time (12.2% gain).
- A seasonal failure is a signal: if a market doesn't rally in its bullish season, "there are other forces that are stronger", and they "will really have their say" afterwards. Bitcoin's weak Q4 tells Hirsch "there's something up there"; "the luster's worn off". Eisman: Bitcoin "acts opposite to its thesis", trading like a leveraged NASDAQ, and stolen coins have no recourse.
- Moats outlast hype: DeFi was supposed to wipe out card companies, yet they are "bigger and better than ever" (Hirsch). Eisman: "Visa connects 200 million global merchants to billions of human beings"; think "what it would actually take to invest to actually just hook all that up from scratch".
- Super Boom: Yale saw 500% moves after war and inflation (Dow 3,420 from the 1974 low). Jeffrey's 2010 forecast of Dow 38,820 by 2025, made with the Dow near 10,000, has been passed (\~45,000, Apr 2026). A 500% move from the Oct 2011 end of the secular bear gives 62,430, reachable around 2030 at the Dow's \~8.5% average annual gain; AI "feels a lot like Windows 95".
- War, Trump and credit (Apr 2026): crises tend to bottom within about a month of their outset; oil goes up "like a rocket" and comes down "like a a feather". Trump's "taco trade" cycle (only five years of data) has a late-March/early-April double bottom, then about +15% on average. Eisman: almost 100% of US loan growth since the GFC has been private credit, a tightening credit cycle will slow the economy, and "a few bad players" haven't "blown up yet".
