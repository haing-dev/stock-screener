### 155. Pablo Calderini — Resilient strategies: macro as a tailwind in left-tail scenarios (Graham Capital Management, Jun 2024)

*President and CIO of Graham Capital Management, in a talk given in spring 2024 on the risks behind calm markets and what macro adds to a 60/40 portfolio.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9lb78PgOHEg)

**Takeaway:** Markets in spring 2024 priced "a perfect world" (a record risk-adjusted equity rally, very low volatility) while geopolitical, inflation and stock-bond correlation risks were rising. Macro helps because it cuts or shorts equities in bad markets: since 1990, adding 20% to a 60/40 raised returns, cut volatility and trimmed the worst drawdown.

- Regime: in 2010–2020 central banks eased at any sign of trouble, so "bad news is good news": "a phenomenal time to be an asset owner" but a hard one for macro traders, with volatility suppressed. The 2022 inflation shock forced the opposite: US rates went from 0–25bp to 5.25–5.5%, outside the Volcker era the biggest and fastest tightening, with "the largest increase in real rates that we've ever seen". Macro did very well in 2022; 2023 was "more of a cross-current year".
- The December 2023 pivot: the Fed and every developed central bank except the BOJ turned dovish while growth was decent. The S&P, which bottomed near 4,200 at the end of October 2023, rose 1,000 points in four months; in Q1 2024 equities had an information ratio of 4 vs a long-run 0.6, "at best 0.7".
- Calm pricing (spring 2024): the VIX bottomed at 13 and sits at \~16, the MOVE index of rates volatility made a low near 100, and FX volatility is very low. "we are, according to this, living in a perfect world", at odds with the economic and geopolitical outlook.
- Geopolitics: "maybe we are living in the most dangerous geopolitical environment of the last 100 years since World War II". A Chinese blockade or invasion of Taiwan "would be the biggest shock financial markets has ever seen", maybe worse than March 2020. Ukraine is unsettled, and while Iran's attack and Israel's response weren't "true acts of war", a line has been crossed: "there's always the potential for something going wrong".
- Back to reality: "inflation for us is the biggest shock". At the mid-2022 peak, monthly PCE prints annualized to \~15% headline and \~8% core (core PCE is the Fed's 2% target measure). After falling, core has bounced to levels "not in line with a 2% inflation target"; if recent prints persisted, core PCE "goes back to 5%", which "would actually trigger another Fed hike". Not Graham's view, but "one of the major tail risks".
- Valuation is a lesser worry because earnings grew too, though part of the rally is P/E expansion. "AI is a reality", unlike the internet in 2002 "when companies made absolutely no money from the internet"; AI players now make "multi-billion dollar profits". Still, when stock prices rise this much "they always create the left tail": "They just went up a thousand points, they can easily move back down that much."
- Stock-bond correlation: the 60/40 is "predicated on" negative correlation. Over 100 years the average is zero, but it is "almost never at zero": strongly positive from 1960 to 1995 (sometimes 60%), strongly negative for the last 20 years. Growth shocks make it negative (bad news lets the Fed cut); inflation shocks make it positive (a hot PCE print sinks both). "we are back to a world of positive correlation", which is "another bad development for asset owners".
- Where macro sits: between passive indexing, tactical allocation and smart beta at one end and pure arbitrage at the other. It has low long-run correlation to asset classes, yet "on any given day, we proactively seek to have exposure to markets". Main inputs are GDP, inflation and central banks ("When central banks are in action, we typically perform really, really well"), but anything that moves macro markets counts: Nvidia "looks like a micro issue" but became a macro one as AI's poster child.
- Positions: in 2022 Graham was "extremely short interest rates globally" (rates needed to reprice 400–500bp), short stocks and commodities, and long the dollar. In spring 2024, with less conviction: still short rates, US curve steepeners, short Japanese rates ("Japanese rates will go up a lot") and long the dollar, as a strong US economy limits Fed cuts while the ECB nears its "independence day" of cutting before the Fed.
- Conditional correlation: Graham has no long or short bias, but "we are good at forecasting bad markets for equities": macro's correlation to the MSCI World is positive in good periods and turns negative as it cuts or shorts equities in bad ones. Macro index vs MSCI World drawdowns: 1990 recession +6.7% vs -24%; 2000–02 almost +13% vs as much as -46%; 2008 a gain vs almost -54%; 2022 +10% on average vs -25%, and Graham made "more than double that".
- Not a put: "We are not in the business of always being short", and macro misses: in the Q1 2020 COVID sell-off it fell almost 2%. Good results in bad equity markets are expected, "but we are not guaranteed".
- Portfolio math (1990–Dec 2023): adding 20% of the macro index to a 60/40 lifts annual return from 6.7% to 7.2%, cuts volatility from 10.2% to 8.7%, raises the information ratio 30% (0.65 to 0.83), trims the worst drawdown from 36% to 29% and cuts beta to the MSCI World from 0.7 to 0.55. The reasons: macro runs at lower volatility than equities, with almost zero correlation.
