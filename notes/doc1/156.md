### 156. Jason Trennert & Chris Verrone — A rotational market, yields that haven't bitten yet and the AI capex bill (The Real Eisman Playbook, Aug 2026)

*Strategas founder Jason Trennert and its market strategist Chris Verrone, who cover the whole market rather than one sector, in an early-August roundtable with Steve Eisman (Ep 73).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hg2yuB7bsXA)

**Takeaway:** Despite war, a new Fed regime and 30–50% drops in AI leaders, money hasn't left equities: breadth improved and the economy is strong, so they now think the 10-year must rise well above the 4.5% they once saw as the limit before stocks break (Aug 2026). The open question is whether the hyperscalers' capex, now draining their cash flow and needing debt and equity raises, earns a return.

- Concentration (Aug 2026): Trennert is "personally nervous because the market is so concentrated": the top 10 S&P 500 holdings are \~39% of the index and tech \~36%, over 50% with tech-adjacent names like Amazon.
- Leverage lesson: the Situational Awareness hedge fund blew up with the VIX below 20 and only a 15–20bp rise in long rates. Trennert: picture 1900, long every auto stock and short every buggy-whip maker, four times levered. The thesis is right, but "you're long X and you're short Y, but it's the same thing", so one scare that reverses the trade wipes you out, as with Long-Term Capital.
- Rotation, not exit (Verrone): leading semis and hyperscalers fell 30–40%, some 50%, from mid-May to early August, yet "Money doesn't want to leave the asset class of equities". At the Jun 2 S&P high \~50% of stocks were above their 200-day average; now 75% are. Had Cisco or Sun fallen 40–50% in 8 weeks in 1999–2000, the indexes would have dropped a lot.
- The yield that ends it is "higher than most people think": they thought 4.50% on the 10-year, where 2023–25 corrections began. In bubbles yields rose a lot (JGBs 4→8% in Japan's 1989 melt-up, US 10s 4→7% in 1999), and with nominal growth at 6.5% "It's not 470" (Verrone). Trennert: stocks rising while long rates climb is "the point at which you have the most risk". Verrone: the 10-year's 85bp range over 400 days is its narrowest ever.
- Economy and Fed: Trennert says PMIs show rapid expansion and the One Big Beautiful Bill lets companies write off all US capex. Verrone: the Fed may "have to tighten because of growth", not inflation; the backup in yields is in real rates. Trennert backs Warsh's "old regime" of less guidance: markets got "very used to being spoon-fed", and from here "There'll be more volatility. Good companies will still get rewarded."
- 2026 is "about the E, not the P/E": the P/E is down two or three turns this year, making it an "alpha market" where stock picking beats passive (Verrone); State Street, T. Rowe and Invesco lead asset managers. Private equity, the biggest winner from zero rates (Trennert), now takes \~7 years to monetize; holders "ain't getting their money back", and endowments heavy in PE and venture (Notre Dame \~50%, he believes) must tighten their belts.
- AI financing: Trennert was fired by a client for writing that "it's going to be hard to continue to grow AI investments without further dilution of shareholders". Google raised $85bn of equity (its first since the early 2000s), hyperscalers are issuing debt, Oracle was downgraded, and hyperscaler cash flow "looks like it's going to be negative". Latest cash flow: Meta $785m for the quarter; Microsoft, the best, \~$19bn but down \~25%; Amazon negative over 12 months.
- Positioning (Trennert, Aug 2026): market-weight tech; "I wouldn't short it" since AI demand exceeds supply, but he doubts hyperscalers earn an appropriate return and is more bullish on semis, as hyperscaler spending may become "a little bit of a race to the bottom".
- Verrone: investors long saw hyperscalers as "cash flow machines" and are searching for a new way to value them, which has "been mistaken for some deterioration in AI fundamentals". Sentiment hit "a fever pitch of negativity" (Oracle near 120) before Google returned to its highs and Amazon made new ones. Give AI build-out names (Quanta, EMCOR \[?\], Caterpillar) the benefit of the doubt until they "attempt to rally, don't make new highs, and fail".
- Eisman: OpenAI and Anthropic are "totally negative cash flow", and one report puts "something like 70%" of hyperscaler AI revenue on those two, so "if Open AI and Anthropic ever get in trouble, the ecosystem is in trouble". Trennert: "everything right now is a big bet on productivity".
- Meta (Eisman, Aug 2026): revenue grew 28% but expenses 55%, "we want the reverse", and depreciation (\~$4bn to $6bn, heading to 8, 10, 12) will weigh on earnings. Verrone respects the stock recovering a 10–12% post-earnings drop in 3 days. Trennert: Meta faces hundreds of social-media suits; IBM's \~12-year antitrust case was won "quote unquote" but distracted it so much that it missed personal computing. Lobbying "can be an alpha generator".
- Oil and metals: Trennert expects oil to keep a bid because supply problems persist "whether the war ends tomorrow or or not". Verrone: Europe is more sensitive to oil because it lacks the AI capex story; in the US "It is still the CapEx spend". Copper, zinc and tin are at new highs while Chinese iron ore hit 52-week lows, so metals demand is "AI and CapEx", not China.
- Banks: global banks "carry the football", BB spreads hit cycle lows and deregulation is "extreme" (Verrone), though he "can't think of too many times" every bank stock is at new highs on the verge of a big problem. Trennert: private credit was "largely a regulatory arb"; a bust "won't be that systemic", but "it could be big"; he expects a steeper curve and minimal credit losses.
- Regions and defensives: Europe is "cheap for a reason" (Trennert); its Euro Stoxx high rests on AI build-out names like Schneider Electric and Siemens Energy plus banks. Trennert prefers Japan, which now chases "return on equity" over market share, and Verrone reads higher JGB yields as reflation. Among defensives health care leads (life-science names like Thermo, Danaher and Illumina "put in major major bottoms"); Kraft Heinz and Hershey are "worth a look"; regulated utilities make new lows as politics hits the sector.
