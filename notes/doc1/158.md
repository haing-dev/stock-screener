### 158. Todd Sohn — One trade, hidden concentration and the leveraged-ETF boom (The Real Eisman Playbook, Jun 2026)

*Chief chartist at Strategas, who also runs its ETF research; he walks Steve Eisman (Ep 66) through stock charts and his ETF chart book in mid-June 2026.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mQXXlrL13Gs)

**Takeaway:** In June 2026 the market is one trade: semis are \~18–19% of the S&P, tech ETFs took \~$27bn since the March low while all other sectors saw outflows, and even "quality" funds now hold the same names. Sohn's advice is to look under the hood, because index and factor funds are far less diversified than they look; Eisman: if tech reverses, "there'll be no place to hide".

- Reading charts: RSI is "a guideline. It's not a rule". A new high without being as overbought is a warning; a new low without being as oversold can mean selling pressure is easing. The trend is the slope of the 200-day average: when it flattens and rolls over, "something's changing". Strategas prints the S&P 100's 100 charts as "a homework assignment" to read the market quickly.
- Semis (Jun 2026): a massive run since the Mar 30 low leaves them "Super overbought", at 18% "going on 19%" of the S&P vs 2% ten years ago; the market "lives and breathes by this index". Since 1990 only tech hardware (to \~30% in the tech bubble), energy (mid-2000s) and software (late 2010s, ended by a 2018 GICS reclassification) passed 15%; "Two out of three of those times have ended up with not so great results".
- Software: after a terrible year it has "mean reverted" to its 50- and 200-day averages, "A reprieve", not a buy. If you "take a flyer", "respect your stop loss levels" or use options so the premium is your whole risk.
- Strong charts (Jun 2026): GE Vernova, which Eisman has owned for a long time, is a strong uptrend consolidating after being very overbought, so "buyable". Google has the best chart of the big names, extended but likely viable after some profit-taking. Amazon, which Eisman owns, is "middle of the road".
- Weak charts: Meta "looks more like a short": the S&P, equal-weight S&P and small caps make new highs while it has "not made a new high in months", and its 200-day is flat to down (Sohn admits he wrongly wrote it off in 2022). Microsoft is "Similar position as Meta, if not weaker", retesting its April lows; Oracle is "messy". Eisman: the laggards carry software or AI-capital-intensity exposure.
- Concentration: info tech was 38% of the S&P that morning, and with Google and Amazon "well over 50%" (Eisman). The S&P 500, Russell 1000 Growth and thematic funds all load up on Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta. Eisman: "you own an index, but don't think you're diversified". Korea or Italy are more concentrated, but far less money tracks them.
- Flows since the Mar 30 low: \~$27bn into tech ETFs vs \~-$4.4bn for all other sectors combined, mostly into semis and hardware rather than software. Eisman: "One game". Broad equity ETF flows are still "lukewarm", "Not a not a signal that we're at a real top": "Tech is the extreme part."
- Factor funds hide the same bet: mega-caps throw off so much free cash flow that they score as quality, so the "S&P has kind of morphed into a quality index". A large quality ETF has a "near perfect" R-squared to the S&P 500 and momentum funds are full of semis: "Quality is wasting space in your portfolio."
- ETFs vs mutual funds: ETFs do \~30% of US exchange volume on an average day and 40–45% in stress. Cumulative mutual fund flows since 1984 are now negative, with -$5.1tn from active equity funds; money is moving to active ETFs because "They're cheaper. They're transparent."
- Leveraged ETFs: \~$200bn across a few hundred products, at record usage, charging \~1% fees. They reset daily, so they are "not buy and hold instruments", and end-of-day rebalancing adds volatility. Single-stock versions began in 2022 and now reach ever-smaller names, "kind of like a lotto ticket" (2x SpaceX funds launched that day). Sohn thinks it's getting frothy, "but who knows?"
- Thematic ETFs: the average 3-year drawdown is 32% (cannabis \~80%) and 70% have a Sharpe ratio below one. Flows into a hot theme reflect "performance chasing" and "herding behavior".
- Sector flows (Jun 2026): nobody wants financials (private-credit headlines), which Sohn reads as bullish. Health care shrank from 16% to 8% of the S&P, "its own GLP-1", with huge outflows; he expects reversion to the mean, though he said the same "a year ago". Small-cap ETFs had outflows last year, the first since 2011. Equal-weight discretionary is in the bottom decile, as if in a recession (Eisman), which Sohn ties to the K economy; hotels and a couple of cruise lines are good and restaurants are starting to come back.
- Staples were 19% of the S&P in the early 1990s and are 4.5% now; Nvidia is double the whole sector. Covered-call and buffered S&P ETFs now compete for staples' income and low-volatility role: "It will take a market freak out for algorithms to buy consumer staples again". REITs, under 2% of the S&P, rose during the 2000 tech bust.
- Cross-asset (Jun 2026): $8tn in money market funds, \~25% of the bond Agg, is elevated. Gold failed to rally on the war and inflation fears after a "blow off from metal mania"; Verrone's rule is to "pay attention when stuff that is supposed to behave one way does not". Outflows from gold and Bitcoin ETFs mean "the bar's low for both". Sohn's closing advice: "Pay attention to what you own."
