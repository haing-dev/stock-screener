### 160. Kenneth Tropin — Why macro now: expensive stocks, "kind of scary" bonds, and the bond market as the canary (iConnections, Jun 2025)

*Founder and chairman of Graham Capital Management (founded 1994, \~70% macro), interviewed on stage at an iConnections event.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSpiLKOdY-Q)

**Takeaway:** In Tropin's words, "stocks are really expensive and bonds are kind of scary" (Jun 2025), so investors want returns uncorrelated with both, and Graham holds 3–4 times as many client meetings as five years ago. Macro is not reliably negatively correlated, but it trades tactically and goes short in an extended decline; the risk Tropin watches most is investors getting spooked by bonds after 25 years without a duration premium.

- People first: "it's all about having the best possible people", so invest in their careers, "their health, their well being". Of 235 employees, 40% have been there more than ten years, and all five of Graham's first clients (Geneva investors who took a chance in 1994) are still invested: "we look at our investors as partners, not clients".
- Why macro demand is up: equity valuations are at record highs, geopolitics have "never been more unstable", and bonds, the usual diversifier, face a deficit "the biggest that's ever been" with "No obvious effort" to shrink it.
- Uncorrelated is not negatively correlated. Macro did well in 2008, 1987 and 2003, but "there is no way that I can say to clients, we're always going to do that because we don't"; it can be long equities as they fall. Graham aims to be negatively correlated when clients need it most and manages it roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of the time: "If markets have some kind of extended decline, we're going to go short."
- Trend following needs trends: "If there are a lack of trends, probably not", and reversals hurt. The 6–8 weeks of volatile policy before the talk were "tough for a medium term time horizon", while high-frequency and short-term tactical strategies did better. With midterms coming, "It's inconceivable to me" that the tariff whiplash accelerates, and calmer policy should help the models.
- Beyond price: he favors quant strategies "not just based on price, but also that are based on fundamentals" (yield, carry, relative value), which are complementary "If the architecture is really well done". Adding economic signals to trend following is "additive", in pursuit of a better information or Sharpe ratio.
- Learn from bad cycles: in 45 years, every cycle that hurt Graham became a reason to "roll up our sleeves" and handle that market character better next time. Trend followers were loved in 2022, but "it's hard to be loved if you're not performing".
- AI: a new Graham task force tests AI for both discretionary traders and quant models. It can "expedite research" and idea testing, but "What we don't necessarily have a lot of confidence in at this time is just asking the computers to design, trading systems"; it must be done in "a pretty closely supervised fashion".
- Quant plus discretionary: Graham began as a quant firm in 1994, and in 1998 Tudor warned that quant alone would be "hit or miss", so it added discretionary trading. Around 1999–2000 it launched Proprietary Matrix, half discretionary and half quant, to "replicate how I invest proprietary capital". Today \~$12bn of \~$20bn is quant and \~$8bn discretionary, which relies on people and "is harder to scale".
- Versus multistrats: they deliver consistent returns with "phenomenal risk management", but a CIO who wants macro gets perhaps "a 20% sleeve" there. Graham won't "copy what the big multi strats are doing"; it builds custom mandates (discretionary or a quant blend, a chosen leverage and volatility target) rather than "one size fits all".
- Culture as a recruiting edge: at the largest platforms culture "gets a little watered down", and PMs get capital and risk limits but are "maybe not even encouraged to collaborate", since the platform wants the "lowest correlation between the portfolio teams". Graham takes the opposite approach.
- Its discretionary teams' correlation averaged \~10% but runs higher now, with "crowded positions". Mixing very short-term tactical traders with one- to two-month horizons keeps it down, and in big moves (bitcoin, a weak dollar) "you don't want to miss it", so the balance is being in the moves without getting too concentrated.
- The Fed (Jun 2025): tariffs are inflationary, so it "can't really be as dovish as they'd like to be", and the rumored next chair, Kevin Warsh, is "no dove", more hawkish than Powell in his view.
- The canary: "the thing that's most on my mind is, do we get a situation where people get spooked by bonds?" With a huge deficit and tariffs, "the canary in the room to watch is, is the bond market", and a bond scare would hit equities as well as fixed income.
