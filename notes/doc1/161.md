### 161. Steve Eisman — Weekly Wrap: why this market melt-up is making him nervous (The Real Eisman Playbook, May 2026)

*Steve Eisman's weekly market commentary, recorded May 14, 2026: froth signs, inflation, private credit and the week's earnings.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FQNexJivqL0)

**Takeaway:** After a fast rally from the late-March low, the market feels "bubbly" (May 2026): semiconductors and megacaps dominate, defensives have shrunk and retail call buying is the heaviest since 2021. Eisman isn't calling a recession, but he has lightened up on his high flyers.

- The melt-up (May 2026): at the end-of-March bottom the S&P was down 4% and the Nasdaq 7% for the year; now they are up 9% and 15%. Part of the rally assumes the Iran war is over, but Trump has just rejected Iran's peace proposal: "I'm just not sure that is right."
- Froth signs: semiconductors are over 18% of the S&P, up from 14% at the start of 2026. Nvidia is bigger than the whole healthcare sector, and Micron is in the top 10. Healthcare, staples, energy and utilities together are only 19% of the S&P 500, down from 31% at end-2022.
- Retail traders are the most bullish since the stimulus-check days, buying calls on Cboe's "Mag 10" (the big seven plus AMD, Palantir and Broadcom) at the heaviest 10-day pace since 2021. Of new positions, 52% were call buying and 17% call selling.
- His move: no recession call ("there is no evidence that there is one on the horizon"), but "This week, I lightened up, especially on my high flyers, because this market is feeling bubbly".
- Inflation: the producer price index rose 1.4% in the month (0.5% expected; March revised up to 0.7%) and 6% on the year, the biggest gain since Dec 2022. The 10-year is "flirting with 4 and 1/2%, which in the past has been a Rubicon for markets."
- Private credit (his premium masterclass): "We have not had a credit cycle since the Great Financial Crisis", and almost all lending growth since 2008 sits in private credit "behind a veil of privacy". He expects losses and perhaps a recession, but "not a total disaster" because banks are well capitalized.
- FSK, a $3bn public BDC with rising loan losses, is down 27% this year after Moody's cut it to junk in March. KKR will add $150m of equity and spend $150m buying out sellers, but JP Morgan-led banks cut its credit line by $650m (14%) and raised rates on the rest: "that is always really, really bad". MSCI says private credit funds have marked down more than 1/10 of their loans by at least 50%.
- Payments outside Visa and Mastercard are hard to own long term: "investing in the space is like rolling dice". Toast has halved from \~49 last summer on competition and AI fears and fell 17% on weak Q2 EBITDA guidance. Bill (cutting up to 30% of staff for AI) is still down 26% this year; Block, up 13% after firing over 40% of staff, has been range-bound since mid-2022.
- The Trade Desk: Amazon crept into its business and revenue growth fell from 20% two years ago to 8% guided. Now "growth investors are leaving, and value investors are not prepared to step in"; the stock is down 46% this year.
- CoreWeave's revenue more than doubled, but June-quarter guidance of $2.45–2.6bn missed the $2.7bn expected and the stock fell 8%. It doesn't make money, and "when you were losing money, you can't disappoint on any metric, especially forward revenue guidance."
- Other prints: Cisco beat (EPS $1.06 vs $1.04 expected, +10%; revenue +12%) and guided higher on strong AI orders, rising double digits. Constellation Energy beat ($2.74 vs $2.54 expected) but fell on unchanged $11–12 EPS guidance. Coinbase posted a surprise 17-cent loss after crypto fell in Q1.
- Meritage (long): "buying a home builder below tangible book value, which is where the stock is now, when the builder is growing book value, usually works out. It's just a question of time." With long-term rates rising, he has to keep waiting.
- FICO (short): it has raised prices 1,500% over the last 5 years and is losing its monopoly in a pricing war; if it does, mortgage costs should fall. Being short "does not mean I am biased, that means that I have an opinion."
