### 164. Tim Draper — Persistence, deal flow and Bitcoin: lessons from Hotmail and Skype (Generating Alpha Podcast, Mar 2025)

*Founder of Draper Associates and co-founder of Draper Fisher Jurvetson (DFJ), interviewed by a high-school student about his career, deal flow and Bitcoin.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WTGcKXx4PmM)

**Takeaway:** Venture returns are slow and lumpy: his first fund looked dead and had its government loan called months before five IPOs rescued it. Deal flow and founders with total conviction drive the business; he is an outright Bitcoin bull (Mar 2025).

- Early portfolios look dead: after \~3 years his first fund (\~$6 million borrowed from the SBA at age 26) held \~40 companies, none "worth much on paper", and \~8 had failed. The SBA called the loan; he argued "they take a long time to percolate".
- About six months later the IPO window opened and five companies he had seeded went public. One, Parametric Technology (PTC), repaid the SBA and the $2 million his father had put up.
- Fundraising "as an onion": start with family and friends, ask them to introduce richer people, and repeat until you reach investors who can fill the fund. The DFJ fund with Steve Jurvetson "hit the internet just right" and was "the number one Venture Fund in the world at that time".
- Judging founders: he asks "why are you doing this what are you thinking", and "then the true founder comes out". The great CEOs he backed were "purely focused" and "100% sure it was going to work".
- Hotmail: the founders chose to give email away free, which he "thought was crazy", so he pushed for a footer ad on every message, "P.S I love you get your email at Hotmail". They dropped the "PS I love you" but kept the ad, and one email to a friend in India brought 100,000 Indian users within 3 weeks, "before there were 100,000 computers in India".
- Skype attacked fat incumbent margins: long-distance carriers earned "about $3 a minute in the US and as much as $50 a minute internationally" (his figures). Skype made user-to-user calls free and charged "something like two cents a minute" to phone numbers.
- Deal flow is still "the key to becoming a great venture capitalist". Early on he drove past new office buildings and knocked on doors marked "software" or ending in an X, which found a photonics company.
- Today's funnel: Draper University (6,000 students from 104 countries, \~1,500 companies started, five unicorns), contestants and viewers of the Meet the Drapers show, and AI that filters 250,000 companies to "five a week that I want to look at".
- Warning on cycles: when venture returns are good, investment bankers say "I'm a venture capitalist too", "lose their investors money and then we all get a bad name".
- Bitcoin (Mar 2025): he holds and is "buying more"; the interviewer cites 29,656 BTC he bought at a 2014 US Marshals auction. Bitcoin's share of crypto rose from \~40% to 50% to 61% across boom-bust cycles, and other coins are "fun sandboxes".
- On volatility: "one Bitcoin is worth one Bitcoin"; the dollar has fallen against it from \~$4 to \~$82–84k. He expects retailers to stop taking dollars and "the run on the Fiat Banks", "probably in my lifetime and definitely in yours".
- Advice to founders: don't hide the idea; tell everyone "this is what I'm doing" rather than asking "should I", and adjust the pitch to their reactions.
