### 167. Anthony Scaramucci — Failure, outsiders and risk-taking: lessons from Goldman, the White House and SkyBridge (Generating Alpha Podcast, May 2025)

*Founder and managing partner of SkyBridge Capital, a fund of funds that has managed up to $10 billion (per the host), and founder of the SALT conferences; a career interview with a 15-year-old host about failure and resilience.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ug3dk-C6_JU)

**Takeaway:** A talk about failure, not markets: expect setbacks, take the job you will be good at, and stop caring what others think so you can take risks. His investing lesson, from Buffett: he sold too soon and would have done better holding longer.

- Investing regret: he "devoured everything that Warren Buffett had ever written" but sold holdings when he needed money for a house or his kids' tuition. He admits "if I had held things longer in my portfolio, I would have done better"; he would have been better off "just holding onto the assets, probably even borrowing against them".
- Take the job you will be good at: Goldman fired him on 1 Feb 1991, 18 months into real estate investment banking during a real estate recession ("Frankly, I sucked at the job"). It rehired him on 28 Mar into another area, which he calls luck. Don't pick a job because it seems "cool" or will "impress your friends".
- Expect failure: everyone is born with the same "punch card" of setbacks and loss; Bill Gates, then the world's richest man, could not save his mother from breast cancer. People who accept this "can ride through failures more easily" than those with "very high and unfair expectations about life".
- Lean into public failure: after his 11-day White House stint ended in his firing (2017), late-night TV lit him up (Stephen Colbert called him "Tony Soprano on the Potomac"). He went on those shows and was a good sport: "I went towards it, not away from it." When Goldman fired him he joked he lacked "Patriot missiles to block the incoming Scud".
- A "comfortable outsider" (no establishment ties, no family money) built his own firm so "I'd always have a job". Not caring what others think lets you "take exogenous risk", such as choosing to "flip your fund of funds into crypto": "You're thinking about the upside", not "the downside failure".
- Management: SkyBridge staff (now "a couple billion dollars under management", May 2025) "work with me. They don't work for me." You get more out of people "rowing the boat with you" than through command-and-control "over bossing", one reason he "couldn't really get on with Trump inside the White House".
- To understand people, read the Iliad, the Odyssey, Marcus Aurelius' Meditations and one Shakespeare play; you would be "ahead of just about everybody else". "Superman is a representation of how we'd like to see ourselves"; "Batman is actually who we are", able to fight crime "because he thinks like a criminal".
- Most impressive people (May 2025): Trump is "the most important political figure of the first 25 years of the 21st century" and "way smarter than people want to give him credit for", though he dislikes his direction. Elon Musk is "singularly the most impressive person that I've met" for work ethic and could "probably be our first trillionaire" if he goes back to making money.
- Musk's standing as a data point: in 2016, 71% of Americans knew who he was and 70% of those viewed him favorably; after he went into politics, 91% know him and "only 33% of them like him" (May 2025).
- Role models need not be rich: David Rubenstein (Carlyle) has a "Rather be working" sign on his Nantucket house and bought the Baltimore Orioles at \~73–74. His late father-in-law, a parking attendant, never judged "human frailty so harshly".
- Habits: forgive yourself and others, and "Be useful" (the title of an Arnold Schwarzenegger book). Each day do one thing for yourself (for him, reading a book), one for your body and one for your career, and you'll "grow a great big portfolio and a great big success for yourself".
- Advice to a 15-year-old: read everything, "be comfortable taking risks" early, and ask "What would I be if I knew with 100% certainty I'm not going to fail at it?" Then go do that. "Don't live in fear."
