### 172. Roger Altman — Evercore's no-jerks culture and a finance industry that keeps reinventing itself (Generating Alpha Podcast, Jun 2025)

*Founder of Evercore (1995), earlier a Lehman Brothers partner, Blackstone's fifth employee and a US Treasury official under Carter and Clinton; a career interview.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yiho7XMpflc)

**Takeaway:** Culture is "the most important thing in a business by 100 miles": Evercore's veto on corrosive hires has kept its senior turnover at none, at \~$3.5bn of revenue. Finance keeps reinventing itself, and private credit may soon matter more than public credit markets and bank lending (Jun 2025).

- Lehman in 1969 was old guard: "a bunch of very talented individuals who didn't like each other much and didn't work with each other at all", with "about 200 people and no capital"; Goldman was the same. After a coup purged the old guard, one year there were only 12 investment-banking partners, so he made partner early.
- Lehman later ousted chairman Pete Peterson "in a midnight raid". The division that followed led to the 1984 sale of the firm, which "Really shouldn't have happened" but was "the only compromise people could agree on".
- He joined Blackstone the day his three-year non-compete expired, as its fifth employee, about a year after it started. Schwarzman reminds him that the difference between founding it and joining a year later is "only about $35 billion". "There were a lot of mistakes, but they made the right decisions generally."
- Peterson was "Mr. Outside" and Schwarzman "Mr. Inside", "a absolutely brilliant dealmaker". The three raised "an $835 million fund in 1988 when none of us had ever invested a nickel". Schwarzman also chose his successors well (Tony James, Jon Gray).
- Seeing Blackstone grow from five people showed him founding a firm is "not the equivalent of climbing Everest without oxygen". Evercore's first three or four years were very hard; after \~30 years he calls it "the third largest investment banking firm in the world".
- Culture: "the most important thing in a business by 100 miles is culture" ("culture eats strategy for breakfast"). At "three and a half billion of revenue, give or take", Evercore has no senior turnover (someone you want to keep leaving for a competitor), in an industry "infamous for high turnover rates".
- The "no jerks rule": no senior managing director is hired if any one of three or four people thinks the person will be "corrosive internally", "no matter how productive or celebrated the person may be".
- Size is no excuse: Evercore has 2,500 people vs JPMorgan's 250,000 and Goldman's 35,000. Culture survives growth if you are paranoid, "fanatical" in his word, about enforcing it.
- Finance keeps reinventing itself (Jun 2025). A Warner Bros. deal he had just worked on used capital-markets techniques "you wouldn't have even heard the language 5 years ago". Private credit "may become more important than the traditional public credit markets and traditional bank lending", all "in the last few years".
- Evercore has "a great big business" in continuation funds, yet "Nobody heard of a continuation fund five years ago".
- Government is "a five-sided equation" (Congress, White House, press, interest groups, sometimes states and cities), so no one acts alone. The 1993 Clinton economic plan passed the House by one vote and the Senate on Vice President Gore's tiebreaking vote.
- His edge: "never any good at math" and unable to do the numbers, he can read an 80-page client deck with 60 pages of numbers and quickly grasp what they mean, and he communicates well.
- Fate beats planning: Lehman meant to hire his Chicago classmate Howard Marks, but the decision-maker "confused us and thought I was Howard" and offered Altman the job. His advice: "do not over plan your career"; "The role of fate will be larger than any planning you make".
