### 177. Kevin Hartz — Talent over category: how A\* picks founders (Generating Alpha Podcast, Aug 2025)

*Co-founder of Xoom (remittances) and Eventbrite, former Founders Fund partner and general partner of early-stage firm A\* Capital, interviewed by a high-school host about his career and how he picks founders.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6Z5dlUz-fc)

**Takeaway:** At A\* the founder matters more than the category: the brightest people find big opportunities others have cast aside. He judges founders within \~10 minutes from their background and drive, and thinks investors underrate relentlessness.

- A\* is "very talent focused": the category is "practically immaterial". "the brightest people find the biggest opportunities", and these "usually are opportunities that are overlooked and kind of cast aside".
- Contrarian example: a Yale founder's industrial-biotech company, a sector "incredibly out of favor" with investors after most of a first generation failed. A\* backed the founder and the "why now", not the narrative.
- Founder meetings are "not an employment interview", so he asks about family, parents' jobs, siblings and where the drive came from. He looks for "competitiveness to thrive" and grit, because investors decide in a "very short amount of time for many millions of dollars".
- He usually knows "before 10 minutes in". Quiet talent exists too: Pinterest's Ben Silbermann was "a quieter force" but "an absolute brilliant product person".
- Co-founders are preferred ("It is hard to do this on your own") but "it's not a rule": he'd back solo founder Ethan Thornton of defense-tech Mach Industries, who left MIT at 19, "a hundred times over".
- Overrated: requiring an engineering background, since "some of the best founders of our time don't have engineering backgrounds". Underrated: the "relentless founder who keeps going", e.g. Perplexity's CEO bidding $34.5 billion for Chrome (Aug 2025).
- Platform lesson: an investor told him on PayPal's IPO day that PayPal was "only in like the second or third inning" and "a great company will create industries upon industries". Xoom was built on the PayPal API (he was "the first developer on the PayPal API"), much as apps are built on OpenAI today.
- Xoom regret: it went public and was sold to PayPal, "a great exit", but he wonders if it should have stayed independent. Small rivals "far behind us" are "now doing multi-billions in net revenue" in a market that grew to a "staggering" size.
- His lessons from Xoom: perseverance "year in and year out", and "look for a big market" and steer towards it. Remittances had been "really backward", run by Western Union and MoneyGram.
- Mentors: Pierre Lamond (co-founder of National Semiconductor, longtime Sequoia partner, in his late 90s) made him "make my own recommendation and then challenge that". Roelof Botha, a former actuary, sat on the Xoom and Eventbrite boards for almost two decades.
- Network: Peter Thiel recruited "brilliant misfits" at Stanford (Reid Hoffman, Keith Rabois, David Sacks) long before PayPal. Hartz's lesson is to stay close to such people; he angel-invested in PayPal and built both his companies on its API. Advice: find your own Peter Thiel.
- He tries not to make predictions and tends to "follow the evidence because it always surprises us". He hopes fusion delivers cheap, plentiful energy; A\* is a small investor in Fuse.
