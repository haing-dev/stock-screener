### 181. Michael Anders — ICONIQ as the "caddy": a client network as an information edge (Generating Alpha Podcast, Sep 2025)

*Founding partner of ICONIQ Capital, the wealth manager and investor for Silicon Valley founders such as Mark Zuckerberg and Jack Dorsey (over $100bn under management, per the host); a career interview.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kXOgackmCKg)

**Takeaway:** ICONIQ was built by listening: a curated network of founders and CEOs gave it candid information, deal access and a structuring edge, while the firm stayed anonymous as the clients' "caddy". His earlier start-up failed because wealth managers push what pays them, not what clients need.

- Incentives beat signed contracts: his start-up white-labelled donor-advised funds for big private banks (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Northern Trust, Credit Suisse, Smith Barney) and signed almost all of them. Yet "the really hard part was getting the attention of the wealth managers".
- Their "job is to sell stocks and bonds and funds", and the charitable product "didn't really pay them". It "sat on a shelf and collected dust", and the company was sold "for peanuts".
- The lesson: relationship managers were "more interested in selling hedge funds that pay them lots of money than engaging their clients in a conversation about impact" ("at least back then, generally true of the industry"). It "left a scar" and shaped ICONIQ.
- Founding: he joined Divesh Makan, then at Goldman Sachs, despite vowing to stay out of wealth management: "I would continue to show up skeptical and leave inspired".
- Tenets: "listen more than you talk", build relationships without a transaction in mind, create goodwill and "always be learning". Wealth management "can be a mile wide, but an inch deep", and "being an inch deep is you become a commodity and you become transactional".
- Information edge: a curated client base (founders, CEOs, star athletes and entertainers) whose "collective wisdom" shaped ICONIQ's view of the world. "because we were managing everybody's wealth, their incentive was to share the whole truth", and their brands "opened up a lot of interesting unique investment doors".
- The caddy: if clients are "the Tiger Woods", "we were the caddy and we should be anonymous". Staying below the radar lets the firm act for them more effectively.
- Bets of time, not capital: years of pro bono trust, estate and tax work for unknown founders with "vision and drive", since their equity would need planning if the companies worked. He credits "an enormous amount of luck" over the "20-year arc".
- Client mix as a portfolio: a "jigsaw puzzle" of growing industries where ICONIQ lacks representation (crypto and AI didn't exist at the start); new clients must add to the network. Investors now see its capital as value-adding and strategic, which brings unique deals and "a structuring advantage".
- Calls from the network: CEO clients in 2007–2009 informed its view of consumer spending, real estate and rates. After the financial crisis ICONIQ was "very early" in private credit, "because you were getting paid so much to lend to these middle market businesses", and middle-market private and structured credit "served us really well" in COVID.
- Now (Sep 2025): "anybody who speaks with certainty and conviction I would run away from". He sees the fastest transition since the Industrial Revolution ("over the course of 150 years"): geopolitics, tariffs and AI. ICONIQ tracks how traditional CEOs adopt AI, hire and retool jobs, and carries tech CEOs' lessons to consumer and luxury companies "becoming techified by the day".
- Hiring: an extensive, multi-interview process that screens for humility, curiosity, "uncommon care" and listening; ICONIQ has close to 600 employees, with offices in Palo Alto, San Francisco, New York, London and Singapore.
