### 193. Yoni Assia — eToro: copy trading, track records and "inevitable truths" (Generating Alpha Podcast, Feb 2026)

*Co-founder and CEO of eToro, the social investing platform he launched with his brother in 2007 and now a public company; on copy trading, risk management, crypto and where finance is heading.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a3JYG0zvxbs)

**Takeaway:** Assia built eToro on the belief that talented retail investors can beat the market, so every user's record is public and can be copied; he weighs tenure and down-year results above headline returns. His own edge is spotting "inevitable truths" 5–10 years out, such as tokenization and app-based finance, while diversifying against his own flaws.

- Origin: his brother, who had designed Bloomberg's screens, concluded that financial firms keep software ugly on purpose: complexity attracts professionals and "pushes the retail investors out of it". The Bloomberg terminal "still looks exactly the same today as it did 18 years ago", and wealth managers avoid letting clients decide in the app "because then he's not reliant on us".
- eToro bets the other way, that daily engagement drives retention. It is building "an investment super app": stocks from 25 markets (he says it is "the only brokerage" doing so), \~200 crypto assets, options, futures, and multicurrency accounts with a debit card.
- Every user's name, record and portfolio are visible, "not like Reddit where you have no idea who you're talking to". Copying mirrors each trade "at the same time, the same price, and the same proportion". The top Popular Investor (of 5,000) has $300M copying him after six years at \~30% average return; the second has 12 years, 29% and $200M. Both earn from being copied.
- Judge tenure and bad years: 29% a year for 12 years "it's better than maybe somebody with 32% for 6 years", and 10–15% in 2022, "significantly beating NASDAQ and S&P", is "a very important data point".
- Buffett dinner (2020, bought by Justin Sun): the crypto guests tried to convert Buffett, but Assia knew there was "zero chance" given his view that gold "doesn't generate value". His takeaway, "the most important thing in investing is track record", led him to hire a professional value investor the next day to build a value investor club.
- Three ways to value: value investing is "find the lower bound if something trades below its lower bound buy it". Growth investing bets on double-digit growth for 10 years, so "current profitability doesn't matter" (Amazon, then Tesla). Crypto and 2021's meme stocks rest on "a hypothesis of what is the crowd going to do", closer to trading and riskier with no lower bound.
- Cycles: in 2003 he built AI agents that saw only an asset's price history ("Minority Games"). Their markets "go up up up up up slowly and then crash", so bubbles need not relate to the underlying asset. Crashes teach investors that markets "don't always go up in a straight line".
- 2008 showed markets can "stop functioning": even euro-dollar trading shut and you waited to see whether your bank would open the next morning. That, plus T+1/T+2 settlement, convinced him finance should run 24/7 on a shared ledger, as Bitcoin does.
- Crypto: eToro bought Bitcoin at \~$5–10 and turned "$50,000 into $50 million" from 2012 to 2017, then sold. In 2017 funded-account openings rose from \~200 a day to 20,000 a day at the peak (Nov or Dec 2017). Listing Dogecoin a week before Elon Musk hosted Saturday Night Live (2021), ahead of Robinhood and Coinbase, brought \~$5M of revenue in its first month.
- Risk: "we're all in the business of selling risk", and risk management is "about managing risks", not just reducing them. Finance has frequent "one to zero" moments: "if you did not manage as a one to zero risk, you're done." eToro's deputy CEO was chief risk officer of Israel's largest bank.
- With \~$20bn on the platform (end-Sep 2025), a $1M client "is worth like a thousand customers of $1,000" and leaves after one glitch, so quality must approach "99.99999%". In crypto he stayed "very conservative", starting with Bitcoin and Ether before adding long-tail coins.
- Scale: alpha strategies hit capacity, but where users make their own choices "there's no limit to scale". 5,000 Popular Investors could become 10,000, each managing \~$10M, and eToro aspires to "a trillion of assets".
- His edge: only after 40 could he say "yes, I'm a great investor": 30 years in markets, a \~30% average on eToro, and a knack for spotting "inevitable truths" early (crypto, tech stocks). He still copies 100 people and holds \~50 smart portfolios to diversify "from my own flaws". Unlike Buffett, he thinks many investors "consistently beat the markets because of their talent".
- Inevitable truths: everything investable (real estate, bonds, stocks) gets tokenized, "probably $400 trillion dollars", and first movers capture the audience. Wealth passes to a generation that calls "an app the financial product", so product-led firms will lead "old stack financial institutions". Bigger still: AI.
