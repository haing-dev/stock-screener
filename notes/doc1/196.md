### 196. Christian Angermayer — RNAi, psychedelics and the Enhanced Games: betting where 99% disagree (Generating Alpha Podcast, May 2026)

*German serial entrepreneur and founder of Apeiron Investment Group (his family office, which also sponsors funds; over $7bn under management per the host), atai Life Sciences and the Enhanced Games, interviewed the day after the Enhanced Games listed on the NYSE.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=guroiwEC1qU)

**Takeaway:** His biggest wins (RNAi, psychedelics, the Enhanced Games) came from ideas he bumped into, not planned ones, where the science backed a view that "99% of the people" rejected. He leads with gut feel backed by data, starts only unique companies, and calls biotech's orphan-disease playbook "not ambitious".

- First company: as an economics student he asked two professors, his tutors, what would be a medicine "in 20 years from now". Their idea, now called RNAi, could switch off the genes behind genetic diseases, so "that's a multi-billion dollar business". He handled fundraising; in 2004 it listed in the US through a merger, keeping the name Alnylam (he calls it a SPAC merger), and he was a multi-millionaire at 25.
- Conviction came from both the people (among "the smartest people in biology") and the idea, pitched so simply he could grasp the market: nothing then treated the root cause of genetic disease.
- Don't stop at the first win: in Europe the bar for success "is super low". Peter Thiel complained that "in Europe, people make a 50 million exit" and retire; Angermayer's family told him to bank his millions and live on \~100,000 a year of interest.
- Apeiron does three things: invests in companies from seed (Compass) to very late stage, including "a very big sort of public equities book"; invests in third-party funds for diversification; and starts companies itself, where he spends most of his time.
- His bar for starting a company: no "me too companies". Ideally "a really unique idea"; otherwise one that fits Apeiron's network so well it makes sense to be the entrepreneur, not just the investor.
- Contrarian signal: twice (psychedelics, the Enhanced Games) he found something "where literally 99% of the people very deeply even think the opposite". In 2014 psychedelics were seen as just as bad as heroin (still technically Schedule I), and even friends known as contrarians thought it too crazy.
- Gut first: "I'm kind of intuitive", with data backing it up. He has "bumped into all of the biggest opportunities" of his life, none of which "could have come out from a top-down brainstorming".
- Rwanda (2008): Rwanda was "literally the Singapore of Africa" while investors rated Africa low, an arbitrage, so he made "an emotional buy" of a small bank being privatized. Run by a former BCG banking head, it bought banks in 11 countries and became, he says, the third-largest bank in Africa, sold about six years later.
- "Blissful ignorance": "every single big success I had, had I known all the problems which will come my way, I would never have done it." The best combine years of craft and knowledge with a young person's novelty seeking.
- Psychedelics: a 2017 meeting with Mike Novogratz, whose sister had just met Compass's future founders George and Katya in Bali, led him to fund Compass at once. Compass is psilocybin-only; atai (Nasdaq IPO 2021 at a $3.2bn valuation, per the host) covers the whole suite.
- Outlook (May 2026): helped by a presidential order, he expects the first psilocybin therapy, taken with a clinician, by early next year at the latest. He says "1 and 1/2 billion" invested (with atai and Compass \[?\]) has proven psychedelics work at fairly low doses. He opposes recreational use, alcohol above all.
- Biotech's ambition gap: the playbook of an orphan disease, small trials, quick approval and a \~$1m price makes "a great two three billion dollar company... but hey it's not ambitious. That's not how you build a trillion dollar company". He has always looked instead for ways to fight aging and enhance performance.
- Enhanced Games (NYSE debut May 2026 at over $1bn market cap, per the host): Aron D'Souza's idea, which every other investor rejected. Athletes may use anything "you can do with a doctor and get in a pharmacy"; the training camp runs "like a very proper clinical trial" to build "the biggest data set ever", which he expects to show the drugs mainly help bodies recover from the damage sport does.
