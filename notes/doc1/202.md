### 202. Tom Gayner — Markel's capital allocation, AI durability and the four-part test (GabelliTV, May 2026)

*CEO of Markel Group, the Richmond-based specialty insurer and holding company whose equity portfolio he has run since 1990; interviewed at Gabelli's 17th Omaha Value Investor Conference (1 May 2026).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dfCkzZrYNDE)

**Takeaway:** Markel runs Berkshire's playbook: insurance generates the capital, and each dollar goes wherever it will be "treated best", with "no religion, there's no formula". Every investment must pass four tests: good returns on capital without much debt, managers with talent and integrity, reinvestment or capital discipline, and a reasonable price.

- Scale (May 2026, moderator's figures): \~12.5M shares at \~$1,800, a \~$22bn market cap, \~1.2x his \~$1,500 book estimate, and a public equity portfolio above $13bn. Gayner: the market cap was "30-some million dollars" at the 1986 IPO, and most capital since has been generated internally.
- Unrealized gains on the equity portfolio were $9bn at end-2025 and \~$8bn on 30 Mar 2026, which he says "probably is the second largest unrealized gain in the country" for a company with a public equity portfolio. Markel has bought controlling stakes in businesses (Markel Ventures) since 2005.
- Few companies have copied Berkshire, and "Wall Street analysts don't seem very comfortable with hybrid business mixes" of insurance and industrial businesses. To Markel, as business people, the mix "seems logical".
- Capital goes in this order: (1) proven internal managers who have earned good returns on capital ("they're at the front of the line"); (2) acquisitions, often found by the people running Markel's 150 products, who "know people I don't"; (3) public stocks or bonds; (4) buybacks of Markel stock.
- Buybacks were the largest use over the past year (moderator: $2.8bn of operating cash flow in 2025) and total \~$1.5bn over 3 years. The share count is down \~10% in 5 years, and at current prices "I don't think it'll take us 5 years" to cut the next 10% (May 2026).
- AI: in 5–10 years "we won't even be talking about AI", any more than "telephones or kilowatt hours or rail lines". Markel's underwriters already process a vastly larger volume of documents. Agentic AI is in "early days": "I don't think the intermediate chapters have been written".
- Durability test: AI will make BNSF more efficient, as GEICO became under Todd Combs, "But, it won't change the fundamental position of Burlington Northern". He favours what the "HALO" label now going around describes, "heavy assets, low risk of obsolescence": insurance, houseplants, trailers, trailer flooring. Newspapers show the other side, and Markel tries not to be "on the wrong side of history".
- "Adapt, migrate, or die": Markel "tried hard at reinsurance" and "could not make it work", so it exited reinsurance in mid-2025 and is working its way out.
- Value is the present value of future cash flows, "a universal reality". Test 1: businesses "profitable and earn good returns on their capital without using too much debt". Profit is "a social stamp of approval that the world wants what you're doing".
- He doesn't do venture capital. A VC can win with 2 hits in 100; he wants at least 85 of 100 investments to work, because "for the composite returns to be good" there can be no zeros. Too much debt invites a day when "you either have to fund your business or pay your interest bill", "a death spiral".
- Test 2: management with "equal measures of talent and integrity because one without the other is worthless". A talented manager with "an integrity hole" may do well, "but you as their partner are not".
- Tests 3 and 4: can the business grow organically, make acquisitions that work, or return capital through buybacks or dividends? That reinvestment is how "real money is made and compounded over time". Then pay "something reasonable so that whatever intrinsic returns the business itself makes, your returns as an investor should match that"; overpay and a falling multiple can leave an outsider with nothing even if the business does fine.
- On Berkshire (May 2026): he expects buybacks to become "much more meaningful" there and calls a dividend "within the realm of possibility"; he "would not think of that as a negative signal". Berkshire could cut its share count "by a meaningful percentage" each year for a while and keep its flexibility.
