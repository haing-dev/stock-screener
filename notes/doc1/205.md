### 205. Leon Cooperman — Low equity returns ahead, machine-driven markets and Henry Singleton's model (PBD Podcast, Mar 2022)

*Founder of Omega Advisors after 25 years at Goldman Sachs, where he ran research and started the asset-management arm; on the PBD Podcast with host Patrick Bet-David and Danielle DiMartino Booth, mostly about markets, policy and philanthropy.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WQqzCbjZ1yE)

**Takeaway:** In Mar 2022 Cooperman expected years of very low equity returns: policy had been the most irresponsible in US history, inflation wasn't transitory, and a 2023 recession could take the S&P 40% off its high. His answer is to be a disciplined, cash-based stock picker who owns only companies that earn money.

- Outlook (Mar 2022): "we're in store for an extended period of time and very low returns in the equity market". He joined Goldman on 1 Feb 1967 with the Dow at \~1,000, and it was \~1,000 again in 1982. A big drop next year may recover, "but i don't think we're going anywhere for a few years". So be a disciplined stock picker, "a cash-based investor and know what you own".
- Fair value (Mar 2022): given the government's involvement in the system, "an 18 multiple in the s p is a reasonable number". At 18x $225–230 of earnings, the market is fairly valued at 4,000. Very accommodating monetary policy had kept it "in overvalued territory", "and that's changing".
- Recession (Mar 2022): not in 2022, but "a growing probability" in 2023. A recession would take the market 40% off its 4,800 high, to near 3,000. Still, the US is "the best game in town": Europe is too close to Russia and "fixed income is totally mispriced".
- Policy (Mar 2022): "the most irresponsible combination of fiscal monetary policies in our history". Potential real growth is \~2% (productivity \~1.5% plus labor-force growth \~0.5%), yet the economy grew at 2–4x that with rates at zero, pushing everyone out the risk curve. Federal debt rose from $20tn (2017) to $30tn (2021).
- Inflation (Mar 2022) is not transitory. The Fed had "a very very serious miss" and will take a few years to get it down, because labor is 64% of a typical business's costs "and labor is not coming down".
- Shown union approval at a 50-year high, he said "the power is shifting from capital to labor right now", one reason for his conservative view of the market.
- Market structure: brokers once charged 25–50 cents a share and committed capital to make markets, which the Volcker rule and thin commissions ended. NYSE specialists handled 80% of volume; now 80% trades off-board and in dark pools, and the 1938 uptick rule was dropped in 2007. The machines "know nothing about value they know everything about price", so the market "goes up too much" and "goes down too much".
- Energy was 20% of his portfolio (Mar 2022): oil companies are "coining money". Cooperman said ESG pressure and "sunset industry" talk make them afraid to invest, "so they're returning the money in the form of dividends and stock repurchases".
- Speculation: there is the S&P market, the FANG market and "the robinhood market". The Robinhood names are down 70–90% "and they still look expensive"; Robinhood's own stock went "from like 80 to single digits" and doesn't earn money. He buys only companies that earn money.
- Henry Singleton of Teledyne is his hero: never paid more than $1M a year, he did 130 acquisitions "intelligently", bought other companies' stocks, managed the capital structure and bought back 90% of the stock. Owning 20%, "he was totally aligned with the shareholders".
- Pay without performance: he owns a stock whose CEO made "a huge huge amount of money" while the stock is lower than a decade ago: "this is wrong". He keeps such positions small or sells. Buffett jokes that pay consultants should be called "ratchet ratchet and ratchet".
- Mistakes: in 1982 he wrote a seven-page letter to BusinessWeek defending Singleton against a cover casting him as Icarus. Warren Buffett wrote to praise it; Cooperman framed the letter, "but i never bought a stock which is a big mistake".
- Screen implication: Singleton's profile (a 20% stake, modest pay, 90% of shares bought back) is the owner-operator template. Owning only companies that earn money matches the screens' profitability rules.
