### 206. George Noble — Narrative dominance, AI's missing ROI and shorting SpaceX and Tesla (The Real Eisman Playbook, Sep 2026)

*Former Fidelity portfolio manager, now a podcaster and Substack newsletter writer, in a wide-ranging dialogue with Steve Eisman (Ep 76) recorded Sep 2, 2026.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tG8S5zpi9P0)

**Takeaway:** Noble says stocks now trade on "narrative dominance" and passive flows, so "there's no price discovery". He sees an AI boom with no visible return, funded by circular money and resting on OpenAI and Anthropic, while AI borrowing and fiscal dominance push rates up (Sep 2026). He is short SpaceX and Tesla, valuing each at a fraction of its market cap.

- Regime change (Noble): after the GFC, printing money "didn't really matter" in a deflationary world; now "binding constraints" turn stimulus into "real inflation because you got bottlenecks". The market "constantly overestimated inflation" before COVID and since 2022 has been "constantly underestimating inflation because we have fiscal dominance". Ignore monthly CPI noise and forward rates: "They're no better than the weatherman."
- Rates (Sep 2026): Eisman sees upward pressure "mostly because of AI, not so much the deficit". Treasury Secretary Bessent buying back debt against \~$40tn outstanding is "like trying to kill a whale with a BB gun", and if rates hit 5% "His credibility is shot". Noble: such "open mouth operations" fail, because intervention "only really works when the fundamentals are with you".
- AI bear case (Noble): "Show me the ROI." It is funded with "funny money": that Nvidia had to announce a "$250 billion package for Open AI" "tells me all I need to know". The boom is ".com and subprime all merged in one", and without a return "the funding is going to dry up".
- Commoditization (Noble): Chinese models arrive "every week" at "95% less" and are "good enough for like 95% of us at 95% less cost"; "Maybe the market's worth 50 or hundred billion dollars". He cites Julien Garran \[?\] that AI malinvestment is 24x the dot-com era's, and Peter Berezin \[?\] (BCA) that internet bulls were right (traffic compounded 43% a year for 25 years), yet Global Crossing still went bankrupt.
- The chain (Noble): Nvidia selling chips and hyperscalers buying them is fine, but "something like 70% of AI revenue of the hyperscalers is just from OpenAI and Anthropic", "about 25 to 35% of their entire cloud revenue". The ecosystem rests on two companies that lose billions, and OpenAI "I think is in trouble".
- Circular financing: Noble likens it to a homebuilder lending buyers money at 0–1% when mortgages should cost 7%, then "booking the whole thing as income". Eisman: if OpenAI and Anthropic became "insanely profitable" the loans would be good, but the circular financing "just tells me that there's probably something wrong". Nvidia's latest 10-Q (revenue up over 100%) shows "70% of accounts receivable is from five accounts". Healthy lenders would be lending, "but they're not doing it" (speakers unclear in captions).
- Yen (Noble): Japanese savers and insurers ran the carry trade into US assets, earning the yield pickup plus a rising dollar, as the yen fell from \~80 to 160. Japan's rates are too low for its debt: on Robin Brooks's debt-to-GDP vs yield chart its 10-year "should be around 7%" (debt \~240% of GDP, \~130% net of domestic holders). The US 10-year is 4.8% (Sep 2026); Eisman says markets price 6% ten years forward.
- Foreign money (Noble): Japan, "the world's largest creditor country", and European pension funds have poured money into US bonds and the Nasdaq. With the US-Japan 10-year gap "only 180 basis points", Japan may "Bring the money home"; it has been selling Treasuries "in recent months", China "for years". After \~$95bn of yen intervention in one week, the US lent Japan money through a swap line rather than see it sell Treasuries: "total insanity".
- "Narrative dominance" (Noble, coined "on September 2nd, 2026"): "Don't tell me about the fundamentals and the PE ... Tell me a story." Investors "don't have to read any 10Ks", just "look at the chart and hear the story", and index weight adds "the passive bid", so "there's no price discovery", which "creates threats and opportunities". Jeff Vinik found investors "just want to know the narrative".
- His own readers want brevity: Fidelity-style reports ran "six or eight pages" (SWOT, statements, catalysts, insider buying), and even three or four pages are "Too long"; they want a "Story and a page". But once "the cost of capital starts to matter", lenders will ask for a data center's economics and fundamental analysis matters again.
- SpaceX (Noble is short; his bear report came out in May, before the IPO): these are "shorts at scale", $1.7tn and $1.4tn market caps vs subprime lenders shorted at $500M. Beyond "90 times revenues", lockups are lifting the float from \~5% to "like 20 or 25%", with \~7% more every few weeks; "if nothing changes fundamentally" the "stock's going to go down" as the float heads to 100%. Universities whose stakes ballooned have to sell to diversify.
- SpaceX is "really an AI play" (\~$22tn of the $29tn addressable market in its S-1 is AI). Noble values Starlink at "200 billion 300 billion" and says the AI unit, bought for stock at $200–250bn, is "worth a fraction of that"; the parts add to $300–500bn vs a $1.7tn market cap.
- Tesla (Noble): sales "are imploding": US sales were "down 26%" in July (Gordon Johnson's data), Europe and China are down, and buying ahead of the end of tax credits flattered Q3 2025, so this Q3 "is going to be a disaster". Return on equity is "abysmal"; capex jumps to \~$30bn this year vs \~$6–8bn of depreciation, so "Tesla is going seriously cash flow negative". Austin robotaxi rides scaled to 350 million Americans value robotaxis at "like 800 million not 800 billion".
- Sum of the parts gives "30 $40 $50 a share" for both ("I'll give you 60") vs \~$360 for Tesla and 140 for SpaceX; Tesla is "unchanged over 5 years". Japan in the 1980s hit 60x earnings, with banks at \~100x and \~six times book: "once the worm turned, all you have to do is avoid that and just index everything else and you kill it."
