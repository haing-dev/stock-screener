### 209. Tom Gayner — The long game: fallow periods, Terra Nova and the Jana campaign (Guy Spier, Jun 2026)

*CEO of Markel Group; a short talk and Q&A at Guy Spier's VALUEx BRK 2026 gathering in Omaha.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhPoCLy1Q-E)

**Takeaway:** Markel's edge is its time horizon, not IQ: it has compounded "in low double-teens" for 39 years as a public company, in batches with fallow periods between. Facing activist Jana Partners (2026), he concedes insurance underperformed but defends the non-insurance businesses as diversification, and pledges to be "hyper-honest and hyper-communicative".

- Record (2026): IPO in 1986 at about $8 a share, "about $1,800 a share" now. Markel calls itself "a permanent capital compounder", built on Buffett's model of investing underwriting profits for the long term.
- Progress comes "in batches": stretches of "really bringing in the crops", then pauses to fix "real operational difficulties" before the next plateau. Markel went through a fallow period "a couple of years ago" and says it is "coming out of it pretty pretty nicely".
- Terra Nova: bought in 2000 as a known troubled international insurer (Markel "had a history of buying troubled companies"). The turnaround's J-curve ran longer than planned, and investors complained by 2005. Yet the stock roughly doubled from \~175 to \~350 over 2000–05, \~15% a year by the rule of 72.
- At the 2005 investor brunch, amid charges that Markel lacked international skill, Guy Spier said "the US has been a developing country for over 250 years", a line Spier credits to Tom Russo. The brunch grew from six people in 1992 to 2,400–2,500 in its final year.
- Choose your game: over 100m bet everything on Usain Bolt, but a race from Key West to Seattle "is no longer about pure athleticism": "It's about will. It's about determination." Thinking in generations is "an epic advantage" (he cites Luca Dellanna's Winning Long-Term Games).
- Jana Partners first engaged in Dec 2024 and restated its case days before the talk. Gayner agrees "We had underperformed in our insurance business" and says new insurance leadership is starting to show results. He rejects calling Markel Ventures a distraction: several income streams meant "cash coming in the door when the insurance business was not doing quite so well". The real gap is time horizon.
- Guardrails without a controlling holder (no Buffett or Prem Watsa figure, a questioner noted): be "hyper-honest and hyper-communicative". "That is the process which I can control. The outcomes I cannot control." Keep the "do say ratio" near one and aim for Munger's "seamless web of deserved trust".
- Markel, Berkshire or Fairfax: all long-term focused and all had fallow periods; the punchline for anyone who bought any of them 20 years ago is "Rich".
- War and Peace: Kutuzov beat Napoleon "by strategic withdrawal and letting Napoleon punch himself out", a reminder to hold to your principles when markets get "out of phase". To keep hearing the Omaha message without Munger, read about people who got it right, Munger's "eminent dead".
