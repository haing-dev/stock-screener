### 212. Brad Gerstner & Bill Gurley — Gulf AI deals, the China tariff truce, the reconciliation bill and leaving Delaware (Bg2 Pod, May 2025)

*Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Bill Gurley (Benchmark) on the Gulf AI deals after the diffusion rule's repeal, the China tariff pause, the budget bill and Invest America, Delaware litigation risk, stablecoins and Chinese open-source models.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K2cpSUpdIOs)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner says the market's 20% rebound (May 2025) came from the tariff truce with China and the Gulf AI deals, which put the US on a deal-making rather than isolationist path. Gurley adds that Delaware courts now carry a structural risk of outsized legal awards, so companies should reincorporate elsewhere.

- Market (Gerstner, May 2025): the market "was down 20% for the year has now bounced 20% in the last uh 20 trading days". He calls the China talks in Switzerland "the bigger catalyst", ahead of the Middle East visit.
- Two tariff paths (Gerstner): the "nuclear navaro path" of \~$2 trillion of tariffs would be "probably a 600 to 700 basis point headwind to GDP". The Bessent path is targeted reshoring plus modest tariffs, and the president leaned that way.
- Tariff math (Gerstner): the Liberation Day list added up to \~$800 billion on \~$3.3 trillion of imports. With China at 30% on strategic goods, 15% on non-strategic and the rest of the world at \~10%, it is \~$300 billion vs $77 billion last year, "a 4x increase". He expects about half to be paid by US consumers and businesses, half by foreign producers.
- Gurley: the China rate is still 30% (10% plus a 20% fentanyl tariff), so "if you're importing 80% of your you know cogs from China, that's still a big deal". Rare-earth and magnet restrictions were lifted in the 90-day pause.
- Gulf deals (Gerstner): \~$1 trillion of US investment pledged each by Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE. The 5-gigawatt UAE–US AI campus (with Nvidia, OpenAI and Oracle) is "almost like a global Stargate": "every gig is about 500,000 GPUs", so \~2.5 million GPUs.
- Gurley tests the power edge: cooling and power "might be 20% of COGS", maybe \~10% of revenue for token hosting. So "a two to one power advantage, you might pick up five percentage points of operating margin" or price \~5% lower. Gerstner adds latency (serving Europe, the Middle East and India) and labor cost.
- China chips (Gerstner): the H20 ban means "there are no chips currently being sold by Nvidia into China". Jensen Huang cited $15 billion of lost sales next year. Gerstner thinks it was on track to be "a $50 billion business" in two or three years and expects a downgraded chip may be allowed.
- Reconciliation bill (Gerstner, May 2025): it extends the 2017 tax cuts and adds no tax on tips, overtime and Social Security, plus immediate expensing of capital investment, "huge for business". Gurley stresses that the expensing is "from a tax perspective, not accounting one". Gerstner puts the package at \~$400-500 billion of annual stimulus.
- Deficit (Gerstner): a $2.2 trillion deficit and a $38 trillion debt. The bill will probably add to the deficit; at best it cuts it by $100-200 billion. Tariff revenue and DOGE cuts are not counted in budget scoring, so "people should not get their hopes up".
- Invest America (Gerstner): $1,000 in an S&P 500 account for each of the 3.7 million children born a year, which could reach \~$50,000 by 18 and \~$150,000 by 30. It is in the House bill, renamed the "MAGA account".
- Delaware (Gurley): Joe Grundfest's study (May 2025) of cases from 2009-2024 finds lawyer-fee awards of 7x or more are 23 times more likely in Delaware than in federal courts, and 10x or more 57 times. Two judges account for 55% of the 20 high-multiple cases. "any company that I'm involved with, I'm going to encourage to leave."
- Texas Senate Bill 29 (Gurley): it codifies the business judgment rule and lets a company require up to 3% ownership to bring a derivative suit. The Tesla pay case had a plaintiff with nine shares.
- Chinese open source (Gurley): Baidu is switching to open source, the fourth well-funded Chinese lab to do so after DeepSeek, Qwen and a third lab. Banning DeepSeek is whack-a-mole "because there's four, not one". Gerstner expects an OpenAI open-source model by the end of summer and says "the early leadership here probably does go to China".
