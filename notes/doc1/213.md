### 213. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Thanksgiving roundtable: China's chips, DOGE math, IPO ratchets, Pix and tariffs as anchors (Bg2 Pod, Nov 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) trade five topics each in a short holiday episode: China's tech progress, balancing the budget, ServiceTitan's IPO ratchet, the California EV credit, real-time payments, tariffs, open source and Invest America.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-7bJtS7koQM)

**Takeaway:** Gurley warns that China is out-innovating US attempts to block it, and that "dirty" late-stage terms such as IPO ratchets set investors against employees. Gerstner argues the budget can be balanced by 2029 with modest spending cuts, and that Trump's tariff threats are negotiating anchors, not "literary flourishes" (Nov 2024).

- China (Gurley): Huawei's Mate 70 runs its first non-Android OS, and reports suggest high-end chips made at SMIC, not TSMC. "I don't think blocking asml or blocking AI technology or blocking anything is working". Tariffs and protectionism "will only protect American industry for a very short window".
- Gurley, citing Elon Musk on China's work ethic and STEM investment: STEM is 35% of Chinese university graduates vs below 10% in the US. Gerstner agrees on running faster but warns full decoupling from "the second largest economy in the world" is "probably not wise".
- Budget math (Gerstner, Nov 2024): spending jumped from $5 trillion to $7 trillion with COVID. Cutting it \~3% a year for four years gets back to \~$6 trillion, the pre-COVID trend. With revenue growth of 3.5% a year the budget balances in 2029.
- Revenue scenarios (Gerstner): at the 20-year revenue growth rate (4.9% a year) the 2029 surplus would be \~$450 billion. At the post-2017 rate (5.8%) it would be \~$700 billion. Gurley cites Argentina's ETF (ARGT) up \~60-65% year to date as evidence cuts can work.
- IPO ratchets (Gurley): Meritech's breakdown of ServiceTitan's S-1 shows late rounds with a "compounding IPO ratchet". The investor is guaranteed a return at the IPO, and extra shares come from employees, founders and earlier investors.
- Why he hates them (Gurley): ratchet holders "actually want the IPO to be as low as possible because they'll they'll get more shares". Founders accept them to avoid a down round, but they concentrate risk. He urges founders to avoid any investor who writes such "dirty term sheets".
- Gerstner: "there is nothing wrong with a Down Round IPO it's simply a price at a moment in time". Take the bid and go public, since "you're building a company for a generation". Gurley notes the ratchet's annual step-up may itself be what pushes the company to list.
- Payments (Gurley): Brazil's government-run Pix reached \~43% of payments since mid-2020, like UPI in India and Faster Payments in the UK. Visa and Mastercard "have the two highest operating margin percentages in our markets", "over 60% and something's not right". He blames regulatory capture and backs FedNow.
- Tariffs (Gerstner): Trump's threatened 25% tariff on Canada and Mexico is an anchor, "a starting point" to be negotiated away once goals are met. Scott Bessent: "the Tariff gun will be loaded but rarely discharged". Gerstner told Jason Calacanis to treat them "as effective negotiating tactics not as literary flourishes".
- EV credit (Gurley): rather than an EV credit that costs the government money, "I would much rather see us use a gas tax", since "tax what's negative rather than trying to pay people". Gerstner backs Ro Khanna against a California credit that excludes Tesla, which makes over 550,000 cars at Fremont.
- Open source as a weapon (Gurley): Anthropic open-sourced its MCP data-connector protocol. He calls open-sourcing "one of the most Savvy competitive weapons you can bring to the field", like Google's Kubernetes or Meta's Open Compute Project.
- Invest America (Gerstner): 3.7 million accounts a year, one per child born, seeded with a small federal amount, with employers from Uber to Nvidia offering to contribute. Donors with \~$100 billion could add matching for families earning under \~$150,000.
