### 214. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — DeepSeek, open source, tariffs, DOGE and the "golden age of uncertainty" (Bg2 Pod, Feb 2025)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) a week after DeepSeek R1: what it changes for open versus closed models and China, then Trump's tariffs, DOGE and what all three mean for tech valuations.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j8chNXhA8o8)

**Takeaway:** Gurley says DeepSeek was a real innovation and that open source, "the closest thing to pure competition", is winning. Gerstner says the AI super cycle is "bigger than ever", but uncertainty over technology, tariffs and politics raises discount rates, so multiples should fall and investors should expect volatility (Feb 2025).

- What DeepSeek did (Gurley): it put more experts against a problem with smaller parameter counts, so it is cheaper and faster, and it "did innovate right versus just copy". It shipped open weights under an MIT license and went around CUDA to optimize down to the hardware.
- Cost (Gerstner): with power included, DeepSeek's total cost of ownership is closer to \~$1 billion, similar to rival models. The $6 million final training run is "also correct" and compares with \~$10-15 million for OpenAI's o1, so "they were 30 to 50% more efficient".
- Pricing (Gurley, citing Dylan Patel and Nathan Lambert): on the API DeepSeek charges about a twelfth of OpenAI. Gerstner says it could be running "well below cost"; Gurley notes Lyft "endured rides well below cost for a decade".
- Compute ceiling (Gerstner, from talks with the DeepSeek team): scaling is log-linear, so reaching o3's level needs \~10x the compute. Export controls deny them Blackwell, "2 to 3x Improvement on top of the hopper series". Their cluster is mostly serving inference demand, so this was "the closest it's ever going to be" to OpenAI.
- Open-source adoption (Gurley): 48 hours after R1 there were 500 variants on Hugging Face, and 1,300 by the recording. Enterprises such as Box (Aaron Levie) and Salesforce (Marc Benioff) back it because a commodity input beats "some proprietary piece they have to license".
- Licensing game (Gurley, from Benchmark's Red Hat investment \~25 years ago): open-source players "want the proliferation of openness but they want some kind of hook". Meta's Llama license has a usage clause, so Amazon has to pay Meta; DeepSeek's MIT license has none.
- Closed labs (Gerstner): Sam Altman said in an AMA, "I personally think we have been on the wrong side of History here". Gerstner expects frontier models may stay unreleased while labs open-source models "one or two generations behind".
- China (Gurley): China has long backed open source (Linux, RISC-V) because "you'd rather live in a world where there's no IP protection". In 1820 China was 33% of global GDP; by the end of Mao's rule, behind a closed wall, it was 5%. Tariffs on BYD won't make Detroit more competitive.
- Tariffs (Gerstner): the market consensus is the "bessent consensus", a "fully loaded gun but rarely discharge". His variant view: Trump may truly prefer tariffs to income tax, citing McKinley and pre-1910 federal revenue. Gurley: tariff successes look "very short windowed", and making "a $40 microwave in America is going to fail".
- Evidence (Gerstner): a Fed study of the 2018 washing-machine tariffs found higher prices even on untariffed dryers, and domestic makers raised prices under the umbrella, with "very few jobs" created. A tariff on Taiwan chips would be "a tax on chip manufacturers and on the end buyers" (Nvidia, AMD, Meta, Amazon) because the US cannot yet make 2–3 nm chips.
- DOGE (Gerstner): returning spending to the 2019 baseline plus 2.5% a year balances the budget this term, which means cutting \~$1 trillion off the COVID high. A credible plan would bring interest rates down.
- Market view (Gerstner): technological, economic and political uncertainty means "you and I have to take the discount rate up", so "multiples come down and asset prices have to come down". As a risk manager, "you don't do the 10th best idea right or the 11th best idea". Expect volatility for six months: "the Golden Age of uncertainty".
- Nvidia lesson (Gerstner): in fall 2022 all he had to figure out was "is the forecast for NVIDIA too low" given the coming AI explosion. Now the valuation "is much higher", so tariff and export risks matter more. Gurley warns that Nvidia bulls who claimed DeepSeek had more GPUs may provoke tighter sanctions that hurt Nvidia's revenue.
- Sovereign wealth fund: Gerstner says with $40 trillion of debt, \~$50 trillion of unfunded liabilities and 5% borrowing costs, the fund's hurdle rate is 5%. Gurley says the most successful sovereign funds are in autocracies; Gerstner points to Norway, Korea and Canada.
