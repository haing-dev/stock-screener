### 215. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Units of risk, the AI air pocket and the Google ruling (BG2 Pod, Aug 2024)

*Brad Gerstner (founder, Altimeter Capital) and Bill Gurley (general partner, Benchmark) on the August 2024 sell-off, the AI capex debate, the Google search ruling and "take-under" AI deals.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5LYZCoDysLs)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner cut net exposure from nine to three "units of risk" because prices had run ahead of earnings revisions while recession, tax, AI-capex and geopolitical risks rose. He calls the sell-off a healthy reset (Aug 2024); Gurley warns that long capacity build cycles make AI supply and demand easy to misjudge.

- Gerstner sizes net exposure by skew. In Jan 2023 he saw an 80% chance of +50% and a 20% chance of -10%, "5 to one or 4 to1 risk reward", and ran nine units of risk (e.g. $4.5bn net long on $5bn). By July 2024 he saw an 80% chance of 10–20% downside and cut to three units.
- Gerstner: "as price goes up mathematically risk reward gets worse right unless we're taking up our numbers". In 2023 Altimeter kept revising estimates up; in 2024 its numbers were only in line with consensus. He holds public stocks about three years but re-checks the odds as facts change.
- Gerstner's five risks (Aug 2024): expiry of the individual Trump tax cuts in 2025 (\~$150bn a year, \~60 basis points of GDP), earnings misses or "skinny beats", the AI air pocket, the yen carry-trade unwind and Iran–Israel escalation. More uncertainty raises the margin of safety needed, so multiples fall.
- Recession signals Gerstner tracks: Wayfair says its category decline now matches the 2008 home-furnishing slump; Airbnb and Disney parks softened. Consumers "defer your couch" first, then the family trip. With 80% of the S&P reported, 48% beat on revenue, the "lowest level since the third quarter of 2019", and the Sahm rule had triggered (unemployment 4.3%).
- Gerstner on the Fed (Aug 2024): fed funds 5.5% vs a 4% 10-year, and 10-year TIPS at 1.8% vs \~50 basis points neutral, is highly restrictive. The market expects 125–150 basis points of cuts in 4–5 months. "rate cuts that are preceding a recession" are often sold (2019; in 2001 the NASDAQ was down over 20%), so earnings matter more than cuts.
- Gurley on SaaS: people say it is "super cheap at six times revenue", but he has seen industries mature "where six ain't a stopping point". Investors must "get out of this Revenue mindset" and move to net income, earnings and cash flow.
- Gurley's PC-era lesson: with slow capacity build-outs (DRAM, hard drives) you "had to buy hard drive compy companies when their PE was the highest" and sell when the P/E was lowest, at peak earnings. Data centres also take long to build, so it is "easier to miss supply and demand"; cloud buyers' purchases are "Supply that's not demand".
- Capex (Aug 2024): the big four will spend \~$220bn this year; Sundar Pichai said it is "more dangerous to underspend than to overspend". Consensus has Nvidia data-centre revenue going from \~$136bn this year to \~$166bn next. The Microsoft and Meta CFOs said data-centre shells are 15-year assets they need not fit out if revenue lags.
- Gerstner: in the internet, cloud and mobile shifts "capex got way out ahead of early revenues", and each supercycle was "underestimated when you looked at them 3 to 5" years out. Gurley adds that incumbents moved fast this time (Microsoft), partly to make it harder for OpenAI and Anthropic to raise capital.
- Altimeter's usage data (Aug 2024): weekly/monthly users are over 80% for Spotify, Instagram and WhatsApp but \~40% for Claude, Gemini and ChatGPT. ChatGPT's month-one retention is \~65% vs over 90% for WhatsApp and Instagram; Gurley cites 35% 12-month retention. Gerstner: AI search is not the billion-user product; a personal agent with memory might be.
- Google ruling: a court found Google kept default status on the iPhone with deals worth over $25bn a year. Gurley: Google monetizes so much better than rivals that a choice-screen remedy "could be a nothing Burger"; Gerstner expects Google to keep \~90% of searches under consumer choice.
- "Take-unders" (Inflection, Character AI): license-and-hire deals that dodge merger review. Gerstner said Altimeter liked Character but found it "really hard to underwrite" because the likely exit was a sale to Google or Meta. Gurley flags double taxation (license revenue taxed at the company, then the payout taxed again).
- Japan: the Nikkei's 13% fall was its "biggest one-day drop since 1987" after the Bank of Japan's first hike in 17 years. JPMorgan put carry trades at \~20 trillion, half unwound in two days. Gerstner: the market could still "give up a 100% of the gains" year to date, another 10–15% down.
- Screen implication: Gurley's hard-drive rule backs buying cyclicals on depressed margins and normalized earnings, not on a low headline P/E.
