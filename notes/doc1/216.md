### 216. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Tariffs, free trade, export controls and the H20 ban (BG2 Pod, Apr 2025)

*Brad Gerstner (founder, Altimeter Capital) and Bill Gurley (general partner, Benchmark) on the April 2025 tariff war, China's rare-earth ban, the US ban on Nvidia H20 sales to China and how much of it the market has priced.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPfE5SS7fc0)

**Takeaway:** Both back narrow, tactical reshoring but see broad tariffs and the H20 ban as self-defeating: they invite retaliation and hand China's AI chip market to Huawei. Gerstner thinks S&P earnings forecasts have not yet absorbed the uncertainty, so he keeps risk in the "bottom third" of normal exposure (Apr 2025).

- Gerstner: the administration is attempting a lot at once: trade talks with \~100 countries, an effective embargo with China ("140% tariff" plus export controls), the tax bill, DOGE, Ukraine and the Middle East. Multiply the odds of success on each and a smooth landing is hard to see.
- Gurley (Santa Fe Institute): in a complex system "it's very hard to know which variables are dependent", and one unknown variable can flip the whole system. That is why he avoids macro, but tariffs now drive the fate of the tech companies he cares about.
- Gerstner's two camps: Bessent's "fully loaded gun rarely discharged", with targeted tariffs to reshore critical industries, vs the "nuclear Navaro" view of tariffing everyone to build an "external revenue service". Markets fell 15% after "liberation day" (Apr 2, 2025).
- Gurley steelmans free trade with a Reagan clip: tariffs raise inflation, reduce innovation, make protected firms less competitive and invite retaliation. He doubts government should pick winners, since the AI boom "has attracted more capital dollars than I've ever seen accumulated". His exception is energy: South Korea and China build fission nuclear at "1/4 the price" of the US.
- Rare earths: \~60% are mined in China, but "90% of them are refined in China". Some around the White House called China's Apr 5 export ban (magnets for EVs, phones and machines) a "killshot" to US industry.
- H20 ban: the H20 was a "nerfed chip", "two or three generations behind". Analysts cited (SemiAnalysis, Bernstein) say Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 rivals the GB200, and the ban gives Huawei "monopoly profits in China" without CUDA competition. Gerstner calls it a "very close call" but "selfdefeating"; he would still not ship GB200s to China.
- Power is no constraint for China: Huawei Ascend clusters may need "two or three times the number of chips" and power, but China has \~100 nuclear plants under construction. Gerstner: "Power is not their limit."
- Gerstner's H20 math (Apr 2025): Nvidia sold \~$12–15bn of H20s, worth \~$8–9bn of earnings (captioned "89 billion") and "two or three billion dollars" of US tax. Banning them widens the China trade deficit. Nvidia also takes a $5bn write-off, which Gurley calls minor for a company its size.
- Gurley: export controls are "external regulation" in a world the US doesn't control, and "internet arbitrage is undefeated". Russian sanctions failed; selling a product to 120 countries while barring one has "no effing way" of working.
- Gurley: AI is an "infinite game", so "win the AI war" has no meaning; bans push the world toward DeepSeek and Huawei. Gerstner: the KPI should be "do we widen the gap", by accelerating US companies rather than slowing rivals.
- Market (Apr 2025): S&P \~-10% and NASDAQ \~-20% peak to trough; Apple -21%, Nvidia -25%, Google -20%, Tesla -40%; VIX over 30; gold ahead of the S&P by \~40% this year. Gurley: Apple and Tesla have so much China revenue that "one plausible thing" is being "kicked out of the country".
- Gerstner: 2025 S&P EPS consensus only fell from $273 (+15%) to $265 (+12%), "only... a 3% adjustment" despite CEOs freezing decisions. United Airlines gave two guides: $7–8 in a recession, $12 without. The next leg down, if it comes, would be "around growth" earnings.
- Gerstner's base case: 5–10% tariffs on the rest of the world (\~$200bn) and 25–30% on China, still a "3 to 4x increase" on last year. He holds the "bottom third" of normal exposure and buys on big risk-off days. Gurley warns of reflexivity: firms start "decommitting capex" and capacity, which raises prices.
- Screen implication: single-country revenue or supply exposure (China sales, rare-earth inputs) can erase earnings overnight and needs a manual check; financial screens can't see it.
