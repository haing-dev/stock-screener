### 217. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Circular AI deals, the capex build-out and stablecoins (BG2 Pod, Oct 2025)

*Bill Gurley (general partner, Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (founder, Altimeter Capital) on the quality of AI revenues and the capex boom, state AI laws, stablecoins and Gurley's book; Gurley's last episode as regular co-host.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KX6q6lvoYtM)

**Takeaway:** Gurley sees vendor-financed and backstopped AI deals as historical red flags. They raise the odds of over-provisioning and hide the signs of a slowdown. Gerstner separates cash-rich, low-leverage financiers like Nvidia from the riskier neoclouds and startup chips, and watches capex as a share of cash flow (\~66% for the Mag 5 in 2025).

- Gurley counts "six different transactions" he calls non-normal. Describing them to ChatGPT, without names, led it to "Enron and WorldCom". He thinks AI multiples are "not that high" partly because investors see these red flags.
- Gerstner's test for circular revenue is a continuum. At one end is a "true sham transaction" with no underlying demand; at the other, a customer with real demand that also happens to take your investment. In the middle, ask whether the product would have been bought "but for this investment"; if not, revenue quality is in question.
- Gurley traces it to the original Microsoft–OpenAI deal: cloud credits given as an in-kind investment come back as Azure revenue, "That's a cashless transaction". Amazon and Google have since done the same with other AI startups.
- Off-balance-sheet risk: Gurley says Meta agreed to cover the failure of debt on a facility whose debt it doesn't own, "classic off-balance sheet financing". Nvidia has also promised (per a CoreWeave filing) to buy any CoreWeave capacity it can't sell. That backstop means investors may not see slowing demand at the purest AI-cloud play.
- Gurley: these deals are now competitive necessities ("if we don't do it, everyone else is doing it"). They raise the chance of over-provisioning while adding "virtual leverage" and delaying when we find out. Equity investment is riskier than Cisco's dot-com customer loans because the buyer never has to pay it back.
- Gerstner on Nvidia (Oct 2025): it can invest but needn't, and OpenAI can buy its chips but needn't (OpenAI just signed deals with AMD and Broadcom). Nvidia should generate "$450 billion of free cash" over 3 years and invests a small part of it. As a holder he is not concerned.
- Gerstner's red flag: "a chip that there's only one customer for", whose maker gives that customer $10bn to buy it. Expect "yellow flags" further out the risk curve, in neoclouds and startup chips with weak balance sheets.
- Build-out scale (Oct 2025): \~$3 trillion of capex over 5 years, \~60 GW, not all of it new. Sell-side has Nvidia going from \~$200bn of revenue this year to \~$350bn in 5 years (4–5 GW to 9 GW). Jensen Huang puts the odds of a glut as "extremely low" until all general-purpose computing turns accelerated.
- Gerstner: Mag 5 capex is \~66% of operating cash flow in 2025, which consensus sees as the peak, falling to 45–50% as cash flow grows 15–20% a year. Capex was $156bn in 2023 and $379bn this year: "another thing to keep your eye on".
- Why Meta's stock now tolerates heavy capex when the 2022 Reality Labs spend got it "obliterated": AI spending shows up in earnings growth, and "They don't have to hire a lot of new employees". Investors will accept "giving over half of their free cash flow" to build-outs while they see the payoff.
- Gurley: Howard Marks says multiples are too low for "bubble watch", but the capex numbers are unprecedented. The Mag 7 are now putting "the majority of their free cash flow" into capex. He thinks OpenAI is chasing "escape velocity" and daring rivals to match it.
- Gerstner: the OpenAI deals are "frameworks", "not contractual obligations", and AMD and Broadcom must still deliver working chips. Adding them up, OpenAI faces \~$150bn of capex in 2030, which needs at least \~$150bn of revenue. Gerstner thinks that is "more than plausible", but it makes scale very hard for anyone but the hyperscalers.
- Stablecoins: through a deal with issuer Circle, Coinbase pays \~4% on stablecoin balances as a "reward" (the GENIUS Act settlement allowed rewards, not interest), with instant, near-free transfers. The bottleneck is throughput: Visa and Mastercard handle \~50,000 transactions per second vs under 4,000 on Solana and Ethereum. Gerstner expects Amazon and Meta to enter because "money is a network effects business".
- Also covered: Gerstner wants federal preemption of state AI laws (Colorado's AI Act, California SB 243). Invest America ("Trump accounts") will give every child under two $1,000, with 65 million children eligible and accounts due by July 4, 2026. Gurley's book *Running Down a Dream* is due in late February.
