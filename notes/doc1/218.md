### 218. Dylan Patel, Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — The AI semiconductor landscape: Nvidia, custom chips and the 2026 reckoning (BG2 Pod, Dec 2024)

*Founder of SemiAnalysis, a semiconductor and AI research firm that tracks data centers, fabs and the hardware supply chain for hyperscalers, chip makers and funds, interviewed by Bill Gurley and Brad Gerstner.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QVcSBHhcFbg)

**Takeaway:** Patel argues scaling is not dead: pre-training is just logarithmically more expensive, while synthetic data and reasoning open new, compute-hungry axes, so Street capex estimates are too low (Dec 2024). Spending in 2025 looks firm; 2026 "is like where the Reckoning comes", and it depends on models continuing to improve.

- Nvidia's share (Dec 2024): over 98% of AI workloads if you ignore Google, "more like 70" once Google's in-house TPU workloads count. Its moat is a "three-headed dragon": software, hardware (newest technologies first, at volume) and networking (Mellanox).
- The software moat is smaller in inference. A big customer can "hire enough Engineers" to run a few models on other hardware; Microsoft runs GPT-style models on AMD. So Nvidia competes on performance per total cost of ownership (TCO): Patel says Blackwell pushes it \~5x in a year, against Moore's law's 2x every two years, and Nvidia has decided to cut its margin "somewhat".
- Scaling: pre-training needs \~10x more spend for each step, and text data is running out. But labs have spent only "tens of millions of dollars maybe on synthetic data", so they can "jump up two to three logarithms". Synthetic data works only where outputs can be checked automatically (code, math, engineering), not art or emails.
- Evidence against "scaling is dead": Meta's 2 GW Louisiana data center, multi-gigawatt builds at Amazon, Google and Microsoft, Microsoft's fiber deals to link sites, and the 400,000-chip Trainium cluster for Amazon and Anthropic. From tracking data-center power through regulatory filings and satellite imagery, Patel says the Street's capex estimates are "all far too low".
- Reasoning economics: an o1-style answer has \~10x the tokens (\~10,000 thinking tokens), and the long context cuts the users a server can batch to 1/4–1/5. So cost per query rises \~50x. Gurley: great for whoever sells the hardware, but margins compress unless passed on. Patel: they can be passed on because the model "literally could not do this before and now it can".
- Model margins (Dec 2024): Microsoft makes \~$10bn of inference revenue at 50–70% gross margins and rents few GPUs, since selling tokens earns more. Open models are a commodity: a 10x smaller model is \~15x cheaper to run, and competition strips the margin, so \~30x cheaper. "the margins here are bad"; a lab must have the best model "or you're no one".
- Cisco comparison (Gerstner): Cisco peaked at \~120x P/E and compressed \~70% to about 30x. Nvidia is at \~30x, so a repeat needs another 70% multiple drop or a 70% fall in earnings. Patel: Cisco's buildout was funded by private credit; today's comes from "positive cash flows of the most profitable companies".
- Memory: Gerstner frames it as boom-bust ("you always buy them at trough"). Patel: HBM, not TSMC, is Nvidia's largest cost of goods sold, and reasoning's long context needs far more memory. But HBM gross margins "have not been fantastic" because Nvidia pushes suppliers hard; ordinary server memory earns more. Samsung is squeezed by China's CXMT at the low end and has almost no HBM share at Nvidia.
- AMD: excellent silicon, but "AMD has no clue how to do software", won't spend on its own GPU cluster and lacks system design (it bought ZT Systems). It will still earn billions, but its share of AI revenue should fall in 2025, with less business from Microsoft and Meta.
- Google TPU: an unremarkable chip in an excellent system (scale-up to 8,000 chips, water cooling, reliability). It sells little outside Google: software kept internal and opaque pricing. Apple accounts for over 70% of Google's TPU revenue "as far as I understand". Amazon's Trainium 2 is an "Amazon Basics TPU": \~$5,000 per chip vs "North of$ 30,000" to \~$40,000 for Nvidia, with the most HBM per dollar.
- Broadcom: several custom-chip wins (Google, Meta, OpenAI, a small part of Apple's) and a rival to Nvidia's NVSwitch. Patel likes it long term but expects a slowdown in Google TPU purchases over the next six months because Google "literally" has no data center space. Gerstner noted a \~40% gap opening between Broadcom and Nvidia in 30 days.
- Neoclouds: SemiAnalysis tracks 80; "maybe five to 10 will" survive. H100 rental prices are "tanking": "like two bucks" for a one- or two-year deal vs "the $4 quoted". Hyperscalers are 50–60% of industry AI revenue; the rest is neoclouds and sovereign AI.
- 2026 test: Blackwell costs over 2x Hopper to make, so Nvidia can grow on flat units. Meta and Microsoft may take free cash flow "close to zero". Patel's "Pascal's wager": underspending is the worst outcome. A host replied that this logic raises the odds of overshooting. Gerstner, citing Satya Nadella: spending follows revenue, so 30% annual infrastructure growth needs inference revenue to grow at about the same rate.
- Screen implication: AI capex can push high-quality hyperscalers below cash-conversion thresholds for years, and component suppliers' revenue rests on that capex. Check who funds the customer's spending (cash flow vs credit) before trusting a supplier's growth.
