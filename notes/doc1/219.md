### 219. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — BG2 Rewind: 2024 year-end highlights (BG2 Pod, Jan 2025)

*A montage of clips from the 2024 BG2 episodes hosted by Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter), with guests including Jensen Huang, Satya Nadella, Dylan Patel and Bob Mylod.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoitlIIsOW4)

**Takeaway:** The year's themes in short clips: AI compute demand keeps growing as costs fall, agents may absorb the logic of today's business software, stock pay is a real cost, and too much capital makes companies worse.

- Gerstner's chart (10 years to 2024): tech stock prices compounded "a little bit faster than earnings" (the captions garble the exact rates), while non-tech earnings grew \~6% a year and prices \~8%. Tech went from 5% to 15% of global GDP in 15 years.
- Stock pay: Bob Mylod (Booking Holdings): "a dollar of stock-based compensation is actually not only should it not be ignored it's the most single valuable expense". A host on the asymmetry: an RSU granted at $20 is still worth $10 after a 50% fall, while a stock option granted at that price "has zero value" if the stock falls even 1%.
- Cheaper compute means more use: if a hot shower cost $100,000 few would take one, so as compute prices fall, total consumption rises (Jevons paradox, "a fancy way of talking about the elasticity of demand").
- Staying private does not fix a weak business: at $100 million of revenue growing 10%, a public valuation is "not going to be all that great", and in private markets "you're just fooling yourself". Excess capital distorts behaviour: "scarcity breeds Innovation". Every founder promises to stay frugal after raising, but "that never happens", and they do six things instead of the three that matter.
- Jensen Huang: "the data center is now the unit of computing", and Nvidia builds a new one every year. Gerstner quotes Jensen's forecast of "two trillion of data centers" within four to five years, all accelerated compute. Dylan Patel: Meta's 2 GW Louisiana site and multi-gigawatt builds elsewhere show the "is scaling over narrative" fails.
- Satya Nadella: business applications are "essentially crowd databases with a bunch of business Logic" (CRUD databases, misheard in the captions), and in the agent era that logic moves to the AI tier, which is "probably where they'll all collapse". He calls OpenAI the company of this generation.
- Gerstner on the deficit: federal revenue just over $5 trillion vs \~$7 trillion of spending, up from $4.5 trillion in 2019. Growing revenue 3–4% a year while cutting costs 5%, 5% and 2% would balance the budget; Vivek Ramaswamy and Elon Musk called that "not nearly ambitious enough".
- Gerstner on risk: when markets are at all-time highs with little upside and outside risks (the attempt on Trump's life), "you got to take down those units of risk".
