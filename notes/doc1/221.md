### 221. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Big-tech AI credits, valuations reset and the VC correction (Bg2 Pod, Jan 2024)

*First BG2 episode: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on big-tech investing in AI labs, public tech valuations, the venture correction and Milei's Davos speech.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wu_LF-VoB94)

**Takeaway:** Gurley argues that big-tech "investments" paid in cloud credits create low-quality, cashless revenue and distort AI valuations. Gerstner says tech multiples have normalized after 2023 (Jan 2024), so expected returns are lower, and founders must accept real valuations in 2024.

- Setup (Gerstner): VC investing by Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia and Google ("MANG") went from almost nothing six or seven years ago to \~$25 billion last year (2023), concentrated in a few AI companies.
- Gurley: credits that come back as the investor's own cloud revenue are "Flatout low quality revenue" and "cashless Revenue". A skeptic "would say you're using your balance sheet to to drive your your income statement". He does not claim it breaks GAAP.
- Gurley's thought experiment: a hosting firm whose only customers are startups it funded with credits has "tons of Revenue" and "zero cash flow whatsoever". It does not matter that it is a small share of a big company's revenue.
- Further distortions (Gurley): a cloud provider can bid up a startup's valuation to win its compute spend, so valuations "could be Superfluous". Startups holding credits may price "below the replaceable cost of the credit", which is "really a negative gross margin sale". Independent startups face "a sport of Kings".
- Gerstner's test: if the labs could raise the same money from arm's-length investors, the credits are only a substitute; if not, it supports Gurley's case. Altimeter has not invested in the model labs. The structures were too complex to understand "the nature of the security I was buying", and open source may "collapse the price of the market down to zero".
- Valuations (Gerstner, Jan 2024): public software traded over 100% above its 10-year average in 2021-22 and 30-35% below it at the start of 2023, when Meta traded at "six times earnings". The Nasdaq-100 now trades at \~36% above its 10-year average and non-tech at \~12% above. Microsoft, Nvidia and Meta trade at \~14% above, but their PEG ratios are in line with the last 10 years.
- Lower expected returns: after Meta went from \~$90 to $380 a share, Altimeter's target return is 20-30%, vs \~80% at the start of 2023.
- "increasing advantage of scale" (Gerstner): training on 600,000 H100s needs a business generating huge cash. He expects Microsoft, Amazon and Snowflake to accelerate growth in 2024: "accelerating your growth rate at that scale is unheard of".
- Long run (Gerstner): over 10 years tech earnings and stock prices compounded faster than non-tech, whose earnings grew \~6% a year and prices \~8%. Tech went from 5% to 15% of global GDP in 15 years. A common mistake is chasing unknown 10-baggers, leaving investors "grossly uh underleveraged to the largest companies".
- Gurley's caveat: the chart starts after 1999 and 2009. If your entry is 1999, 2009 or the end of 2021, "you're not in a good place". Gerstner agrees "price of Entry matters".
- VC correction: companies entered it with lots of cash and cut costs fast, so the reckoning is delayed. Track "months of cash" (cash ÷ burn). Shutdowns ran \~180 a quarter in the first three quarters of 2023 (\~800 a year), with little press.
- Founders should face their real valuation. Klarna went from a $46 billion round (2021) to $6.7 billion (2022), which Gerstner calls an honest board reset. Gurley: insider bridges rarely work ("peers", as in walking off a pier). Layoffs of "13 or 14%" were too timid: "do it all up front".
- Valuation discipline: Gurley calls price-to-revenue "one of the most naive ways you could value a financial asset". Gerstner: above a $1bn valuation "you sure as the hell better be building a model" of exit value at a normalized public multiple.
- IPOs (Gerstner): normally over 100 tech IPOs a year, but almost none for two years. He expects "the dam really break" in the second half of 2024 as companies need capital; 2024 is "the year of acceptance".
- Screen implication: revenue funded by the seller's own investments shows up as revenue without cash. A revenue-to-cash check matters more than revenue growth for companies that invest in their customers.
