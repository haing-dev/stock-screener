### 222. Bill Gurley, Brad Gerstner & Aaron Levie — Software multiples, the metrics that matter and AI's disillusionment phase (Bg2 Pod, Feb 2024)

*BG2 episode 2: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) with Aaron Levie, co-founder and CEO of Box, on software valuation, big-tech earnings, Google's AI problem and H-1B immigration.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xgvoYJvbNT8)

**Takeaway:** Revenue multiples and the Rule of 40 are crude shorthands for a multiple of future free cash flow. Gross margin, net retention and cash discipline decide what a software company is worth. Gerstner expects AI to hit a "zone of disillusionment" within \~four quarters (Feb 2024) before proving bigger than forecast.

- Gurley: Silicon Valley has "the crudest, kind of least intelligent view of valuation" because it rushes to price-to-revenue. Gerstner's charts (Feb 2024): the average SaaS multiple is six; growth-adjusted revenue multiples are back above their 10-year average, while free-cash-flow multiples sit near it because software got much more profitable after 2021.
- Levie: companies with 5-10% margins and 90-95% margins got the same revenue multiple, which is "diabolically crazy"; the underlying economics "could be literally a 100x difference". Box has always traded at \~4-8x revenue, with 70-80% gross margins and 100-120% net retention; its FCF margin (\~29%) is among the highest in software, on mid-single-digit growth.
- Levie on metrics: "your gross margin will ultimately determine your operating margin". At "40 50 60% gross margin" you will never have an operating margin like a software company. Next come LTV/CAC (do you get a sticky customer) and cash flow: "I've gotten religion on cash flow".
- Cash discipline: Levie says better billing terms bring cash in up front, and many small choices can be "the difference of of an extra five or 10% dilution". Gurley: few startups manage collections, so at 70-80 million of revenue "their DSO's are just slipping out".
- Net retention (Levie): "You can make, you know, any business model work if it's 90 or 100 or 110", but higher is better (Box hit 131-141% in hypergrowth). With \~90% retention, sales and marketing must "refuel the customer base" forever, so cash flow never converges with a 110%-retention peer at the same revenue multiple.
- Rule of X (Gerstner, citing Bessemer): growth is now valued at \~3x margin, vs an average of 2-3x over time. He values Snowflake or Palo Alto Networks on 2025 free cash flow × a multiple, discounted back: Rule of 40 and revenue multiples "are shorthands for getting at the multiple of free cash flow".
- Growth runways: Gurley sees first products peter out at $100-200 million of revenue. Box planned its "next act" at $300-500 million. Levie holds up Autodesk, Adobe and Ansys (\~$2 billion revenue, 30-40% margin) as the model: grow 10-20% with 30-40% FCF margins "for like literally decades".
- Big tech Q4 2023 (Gerstner): the Mag 7 is "the Mag 4", with Apple and Google flat and Tesla down year to date. Meta is up over 30%, video engagement up 25% and earnings per employee nearly tripled as headcount fell from 87,000 (Q3 2022) to 67,000: "flatter is faster".
- Meta's Reality Labs loses \~$20 billion a year, \~$6 a share. At a 20x multiple the sell side effectively subtracts \~$120 a share. Gurley objects that this holds only with "zero future free cash flow" from the unit; Levie says any other number would be made up.
- Cloud (Gerstner): AWS, Azure and Google Cloud re-accelerated in aggregate, AWS from a \~$100 billion run rate, with a record \~$15 billion of net new ARR in the quarter. Levie: no CIO he talks to is even 90% through the cloud move, and AI is still "in the experimentation phase" in enterprises.
- AI winners (Levie): incumbents with users, data and workflows win AI in their categories ("Salesforce as opposed to a startup"). Startups win where software replaces human work. He is less keen on model makers: a trained model is a "perishable asset" that can be leapfrogged or open-sourced. He puts coding gains at 30-50% at best.
- Disillusionment (Gerstner): Morgan Stanley data show super cycles \~40% underestimated at the start (internet users forecast 152 million for 2000, actual 361 million). Gurley adds the "Jaws" effect: imagination runs ahead of what can be shown.
- Google (Gurley): "a horrific problem". Ads let marketers pay "40, 50, 60% of first purchase" while transaction partners want \~5%, "a 10x reduction". Gerstner: hotels pay \~10% commission on a \~$100 room, but ad bidding goes up to gross profit, so answers that replace blue links shrink the profit pool.
- Screen implication: revenue multiples should be read with gross margin and FCF margin, not alone. The same EV/Sales means very different value at a 40-60% gross margin than at 80%+.
