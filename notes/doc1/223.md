### 223. Nick Turley — ChatGPT and the "super assistant" era (Bg2 Pod, Mar 2026)

*Nick Turley, head of ChatGPT at OpenAI (formerly at Instacart and Dropbox), in a BG2 guest interview (host named "Peru" in the captions \[?\]) on ChatGPT's metrics, growth, pricing, ads and compute limits.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MIKej1HCRW0)

**Takeaway:** Turley runs ChatGPT on long-term retention, not revenue, and says revenue follows. Growth has come equally from removing friction, product work and better models. The binding limit is GPUs: demand per user keeps rising even as prices fall (Mar 2026).

- Scale (Mar 2026): 900 million weekly active users, which the host puts at over a billion monthly; Turley says "about 10% of the world" uses it, "90% left to go".
- North star: given 100 points to spread across metrics, "I would put all my points there", on long-term retention: "the sign of durable values, whether or not people are coming back in three months". Revenue follows from that.
- Example: a model that had been behind the paywall was later given away free once it became cheap enough to serve. That proved "totally revenue positive and retention positive". The host cites third-party data showing ChatGPT's retention curves are "smiling", which is rare.
- Why retention rises: users need a "multi-month process" to learn what they can delegate. Search and personalization changed usage from "a pretty worky product", with dips on weekends and in summer, to mobile-first personal use.
- The host says he wrongly assumed Google's and Meta's distribution would win consumer AI: "clearly distribution was not enough". Turley splits historical growth roughly a third each among friction removal (e.g. removing the log-in wall), core product work (search, personalization) and model improvements.
- Next billion users: ChatGPT is still "too much like a computer terminal". It needs clearer affordances, proactivity (Pulse is a first step) and reasoning that works in the background. Chat should stay the input, with artifacts and outcomes as the output.
- Agents: domain-specific agents already work. In coding, many OpenAI engineers "don't open their IDE like ever". Quantitative knowledge work is likely next because "It's testable". The earlier ChatGPT agent was "slightly too early", so users never learned to trust it.
- Pricing: subscriptions began only "because it could shape the demand". An unlimited plan may make no more sense than an "unlimited electricity plan", so expect plans to change for power users (the host cites a $200 subscription).
- Ads pilots aim at access, because in many markets people don't pay for software by credit card. The principles include keeping answers independent and respecting privacy. The most common inbound question about ads is "how do I run an ad?"
- Compute: GPUs are "zero sum", so existing users come first. Ranking by "incremental revenue per GPU" is the "naive" business-school answer, because new products like deep research have unknowable demand. Token use per user keeps rising, so "demand keeps going up even as prices go down". Plan by "working backwards from GPUs".
- Competition: after Google shipped a strong model last winter, OpenAI declared a "Code Red", delayed ads, health and shopping work, and focused on reliability, latency and personalization. It exited the Code Red with the launch of models 5.3 and 5.4. Turley's moat claim: "anything we build will get copied", so the edge is a team that ships faster than it is copied.
- Partnerships (e.g. Apple and Reliance deals with Gemini): you can get users to tap on anything, but if the experience isn't great "people will churn".
- Where he would start a company: AI firms that do hands-on "professional services" inside customers, because labs solved math and coding first and other domains need people close to the problem.
- Timing: end-state products are fairly predictable, but "anything in between three months and eventually is is is difficult".
