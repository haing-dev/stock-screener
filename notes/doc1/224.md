### 224. Bill Gurley, Brad Gerstner & Sunny Madra — China's open-source models, the compute arms race and the tariff reset (Bg2 Pod, Jul 2025)

*BG2 episode: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) with guest Sunny Madra, COO of the inference cloud Groq.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fTqINzeudJ4)

**Takeaway:** Chinese open-weight models now give \~90% of frontier intelligence at a fraction of the price, which pushes the model layer toward commodity and value toward applications and lock-in. Compute demand still outruns supply, but Gurley warns that AI is priced for share, below cost.

- Madra: Chinese labs "compound" by building on and distilling each other's open weights (Kimi K2 as a "remix" of DeepSeek). A new 30-billion-parameter Qwen model performs "as good as" GPT-4o. Gerstner: Alibaba's Qwen has passed \~400 million downloads.
- Madra: the leading open models offer "90% of the quality in terms of intelligence, but at a 90% price discount", so developers and enterprises use them. He expects a US open-source model in the global top three by Q4 2025 or Q1 2026, because buyers want a vendor to hold accountable (the Linux/Red Hat pattern).
- Gurley on why Alibaba funds rival labs: "if you're not confident you're going to win on offense, you want to play defense". Commoditizing a threat is valuable; laggards such as Amazon or Apple would do better funding an open-source competitor than a closed lab.
- Gurley: open sharing raises the "fitness level" of the whole community but leaves "a lot less chance of a breakout monopolist". Zhipu \[?\] had raised $1.4 billion before he had heard of it.
- Demand evidence (Madra): Google went from 5 trillion to 480 trillion tokens a month, and has since crossed a quadrillion, "200x" in a little over a year. At Groq, "we fire up a rack it becomes fully consumed within a few hours". He sees no sign of supply outstripping demand.
- Scale (Jul 2025): xAI's goal of 50 million H100 equivalents is \~4 million GPUs and \~11 GW (Altimeter's Clark Tang); OpenAI's Abilene deal is 4.5 GW. Anthropic was reported to be raising $5bn at $170bn on rumored \~$5bn revenue; xAI at $150–200bn.
- Gurley names a "cost is no object" (CNO) group: OpenAI, Meta and xAI. OpenAI will "lose 7 billion this year", which Google would never allow itself; private companies can now be "more risk-seeking with capital than the public companies". Microsoft chose not to extend its capex budget.
- Gurley: in a game this aggressive the "pick and shovel" sellers win: Nvidia, Dell and "maybe" SK Hynix.
- Gurley's caveat: "no one's pricing the cost because they're pricing to take market share". "the thing that throttles demand is price, but no one here is raising price". Rumors say some of the best-known AI brands have "negative gross margin", which will cause a bump in the supply-demand curve once it is fixed.
- Steep price declines for older models let companies run at a loss today and switch later. Gurley's parallel: internet startups began on Oracle and Sun, then moved to Linux and MySQL once cost mattered. Madra: coding-agent companies are building their own models on open source to remove the cost of Anthropic's Sonnet "for 70% of use cases".
- Gerstner: OpenAI could use its high-margin consumer business to subsidize a cheap open model, as Amazon's retail business subsidized AWS share "for a decade". Gurley: OpenAI's best long-term edge is "switching cost and lock in" from memory, not staying ahead in the model race.
- Gerstner: the open-source race means "the model layer is being increasingly commoditized"; value goes to whoever owns the consumer. Google was the greatest business in capitalism for 20 years "because they owned the consumer". He expects ChatGPT to cross a billion weekly users "probably this year".
- Tariffs (Gerstner, Jul 2025): markets sold off on Peter Navarro's "two trillion" plan and recovered as Bessent's \~$300bn plan won (vs $75bn of tariffs in 2024). The NASDAQ fell 21% at the trough, then rose 30% in 60–70 days. He cites import prices rising slower than domestic goods in core PCE, the EU deal (15% on EU goods, 0% on US goods, $750bn of energy purchases) and June's first monthly surplus since 2015.
- Positioning (Gerstner): out of the market in March, "all back in" on May 2. With rates heading lower and GDP at \~3% he would have called at least +15% for 2025, and the NASDAQ is up 10% YTD, so "we've captured a lot of the return". Tariffs make onshoring investable: Altimeter led the Series A of an all-American rare-earth magnet producer.
