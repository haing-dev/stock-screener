### 225. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — China's Darwinian industrial policy, AI data walls, rare earths for chips, and markets (Bg2 Pod, Jun 2025)

*BG2 episode: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on China, AI talent, the rare-earth standoff, tariffs and corporate governance.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4qG_f2DY_3M)

**Takeaway:** China wins industries by seeding hundreds of competitors and letting most die, and it may not care about creating trillion-dollar market caps, which matters to anyone valuing Chinese stocks. In AI, platforms are walling off data, so businesses built on someone else's platform are exposed.

- Gerstner's dot-com lesson (Jun 2025): Amazon was $150 at the start of 2000, and Henry Blodget's $400 call was ridiculed as it fell to \~$26. Split-adjusted, it is up \~440x from the 2000 high and \~1,800x from the low; the NASDAQ is up 5x from its 2000 peak. "we overestimated what it was going to do in the short term", but the bigger surprise was "how dramatically we underestimated the long term".
- Gurley's "data walls": Reddit sued Anthropic despite a deal, and Anthropic cut off Windsurf after OpenAI bought it. Salesforce changed its terms so customers can query their own Slack data through an MCP connector but not train on it. He expects enterprise apps to be labelled "open data or closed data", with Salesforce's competitors choosing open to win customers.
- Gerstner, from a Zillow board debate: "you can't build a brand in the underbelly of Google". TripAdvisor fell from $20 billion to $1 billion in value once Google did its own recommendations. AI moves faster: OpenAI reached 400 billion annual searches "8 years faster than Google", so dependent apps (Windsurf, Cursor) are building their own models.
- Gurley on China: in industries it wants to win, it ensures "500 competitors or 1,000 competitors" (seeded at the provincial level) and lets the market whittle them down. China had over 500 EV startups; the US has "a handful".
- The model breeds optionality and deep supply chains. Chinese solid-state MEMS lidar costs "like $130 a car" vs "like $5,000" for Waymo's. US industrial policy picks winners late instead: of \~10 solar startups that raised over $500 million, three won DC money and Solyndra went bankrupt ("regulatory capture").
- Investor warning (Gurley): China seems to deprioritize the market caps of successful companies, so a Mag-7-style \~$3 trillion outcome "may not" be "part of the objective function of what a win is". BYD cut prices 30% in three weeks, possibly with government encouragement: "your margin is my opportunity".
- Gerstner: that belongs in "relative valuation comparisons between Chinese internet companies and US companies". Beijing also exerts control once winners emerge, through golden shares, veto rights, procurement and approvals (Jack Ma, a still-private ByteDance). Gurley: BYD was given \~$2 billion of subsidies.
- Talent: Marco Rubio's plan to revoke visas of Chinese students "studying in critical fields" worries both hosts. Gerstner cites a study that 40–50% of the best US AI researchers are Chinese. Gurley: skilled immigration has been stuck at "two to 250,000 a year for like 20 years".
- Gerstner: export controls "probably" caused Huawei to catch up so quickly. Gurley: "rather than build a wall around China, we're going to build a wall around America", and the US can't balance trade if its best products are off the table.
- Gerstner's proposed trade: rare earths for Nvidia's deprecated B30 (no HBM, no TSMC CoWoS). It keeps China's developers in CUDA, earns taxes and Nvidia profits, buys years to rebuild rare-earth supply, and lowers the risk of a move on Taiwan. Without it he expects "a massively disrupted economy over the next six quarters".
- Chip supply (Gerstner): \~0% of leading-edge chips are fabbed outside Taiwan today; the US could reach "upwards of 15 to 20%" by 2030. A TSMC fab in the UAE under some US influence could lift that to "almost 40 or 50%" in 4 years.
- Markets (Gerstner, Jun 2025): the NASDAQ is up 20% from its intraday low and \~flat YTD. The rally holds if China tariffs land at a blended 10–15% and the reconciliation bill passes. Without the bill comes a $4 trillion tax increase; with it, $300–400bn of new stimulus. If those fail, he expects the market "back down 10 to 15%".
- Rates (Gerstner): the 10-year has sat at 4–5% for 2 years despite Larry Summers' 7% call (end 2022), and he expects two Fed cuts in H2. Cutting $2 trillion of spending in one year would be "an 800 basis point headwind to GDP". "don't take yourself out of the game, but I think it is a wait-and-see approach."
- Governance (Gurley): Delaware risk is greatest for the highest-profile companies and also reaches private companies whose shares trade in secondary markets. ISS and Glass Lewis (97% share, per Senator Hagerty) lack "first principles" thinking on pay. Super-voting shares, often called weak governance, can be the way to "get away from them".
