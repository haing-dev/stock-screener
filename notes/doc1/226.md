### 226. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — China's tech surge: what Gurley saw on the ground (Bg2 Pod, Aug 2025)

*BG2 episode: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) reports on a recent China trip (BYD, Xiaomi, founders and VCs) and Dan Wang's book "Breakneck" with Brad Gerstner (Altimeter).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hUJz55AsUz4)

**Takeaway:** China out-builds through provincial hyper-competition and an engineering culture: Xiaomi went from no car business to 1,000 cars a day in about four years. The state favors employment and low prices over big market caps. Both hosts argue the US should compete and reform rather than decouple.

- Gurley on the AI mega-rounds: "you can use cash burn as just a proxy for risk because you get further away from knowing unit economics". The sums are bigger than the "Uber Door Dash wars" because investors who have watched network effects now bet ahead of the curve. Gerstner: the OpenAI and Anthropic deals are bigger "private IPOs" than any public tech IPO in 5 years.
- Gurley on "Breakneck": Politburo members are mostly engineers, Washington mostly lawyers. Provincial leaders compete "like divisions of a company", which brings hyper-competition but also overbuild. Ghost cities and failing EV and solar makers that "need to go bankrupt" survive because provinces protect jobs.
- Gurley: read China's coming five-year plan, "where they tell the provinces what's important to work on". The 14th plan already named open source.
- Gurley: "every founder and every VC in China studies the West at a nauseating level"; "The West doesn't do that of China." TikTok came first and Reels copied it.
- BYD (Gurley): the largest EV maker at \~4 million vehicles, it started in batteries and sells entry cars at "10 to 15 grand". On subsidies, its executive Stella Li said "come show me the money".
- Xiaomi (Gurley): a phone maker (\~3rd globally) that began cars around 2021. Its factory makes "a thousand cars a day with 2,000 employees", is sold out with a 30–40 week backlog, and charges $5k to join the waiting list. Two workers per car per day, vs "like at six in the US", means reshored car plants may bring few jobs. Ford's Jim Farley called it "the most humbling thing I've ever seen".
- Gerstner: the US is "about 14% of China's exports" and "about 3% of China's GDP", so "we're just not that important to China". He backs tariffs and industrial policy only for critical industries (rare-earth magnets, steel, pharmaceuticals). If the US needs 10 workers where China needs one, high protection is "unsustainable".
- Gerstner: Chinese EV makers on "razor thin margins" sell into Europe at $20,000–25,000. Gurley: a country like Mexico will buy the "10 to 20 grand" Chinese EV rather than a "50 grand" US one. Mercedes' CEO says "We need a reality check"; Stellantis' Carlos Tavares called EU tariffs on Chinese EVs a "major trap". Protection leaves consumers buying "inferior goods at inflated prices".
- Valuation gap (Gurley, Aug 2025): Baidu's Apollo robotaxi costs \~30K vs over 150K for a Waymo, partly thanks to solid-state lidar. Waymo is said to be worth $170 billion inside Google, yet Baidu has a "30 billion market cap, 30 billion in cash", so "you can buy shares of BYU \[Baidu\] for zero enterprise value".
- Why China stays cheap (Gurley): "I don't think they care about market cap"; Beijing cares about employment and global competitiveness, which leads it to "support low margin companies". Founders know "don't be the tallest tree". Western VC has thinned out (Sequoia and GGV split in half), and provinces now invest on terms US VCs see as "non-starters".
- China AI (Gurley): DeepSeek has the strongest "intellectual brand", Alibaba's Qwen matters because Alibaba is "like a 70% player" in cloud, and ByteDance is the consumer company to watch. With so many open models, nobody fears a monopolist.
- Gurley: public incumbents haven't "internalized" how much private markets will fund attackers. Should Google lose "five or 10 billion dollars" to defend its core (for example by open-sourcing Gemini more aggressively)? It did this before with Kubernetes against AWS and Android against Apple. Rich Barton fighting Opendoor at Zillow was "probably the right thing to do".
- Gurley's policy asks: allow JVs with Chinese leaders (as Western carmakers took 49/51 stakes in China), let Ford or GM buy or partner with a struggling Chinese EV maker such as NIO, and invite Korea, which "can build a nuclear plant for 1/4 the price", to build in the US.
