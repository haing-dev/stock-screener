### 227. Kevin Weil, Brad Gerstner & Bill Gurley — ChatGPT growth, iterative deployment and the "worst AI model that you'll ever use" (Bg2 Pod, Jun 2025)

*Kevin Weil runs product at OpenAI (ex-Instagram, Twitter); a short on-stage BG2 conversation recorded at Coatue's 10th East Meets West event.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IOoRXSyezBE)

**Takeaway:** Weil argues ChatGPT's opportunity is bigger than he thought a year in: usage is deepening, models step up every few months, and nearly every product will be rebuilt around AI. Gerstner frames the upside as \~$100bn of revenue and a possible "5 to10 trillion business".

- Valuation frame (Jun 2025): Gerstner cites Coatue's slide putting OpenAI at "$300 billion in enterprise value today" and \~1.6 trillion in five years, the ninth most valuable company in the world.
- Weil: OpenAI has "grown faster than anything I've ever seen", faster than Instagram, and usage per person is deepening, not just user counts.
- Metric choice: OpenAI goals itself on weekly active users, not monthly, because a once-a-month user means "we're probably not adding all that much value in their life". Over time he expects daily active users to be the natural measure.
- Gurley, "as someone who's gone from once a day to 20 times a day": usage expands as people learn what the product can do. Weil's advice is "just start using it", since "in most cases it can".
- Product rule: "the model that you use today is the worst AI model that you'll ever use for the rest of your life". So build products that only "kind of work": a feature that is 70% right today may be 95% right with the next model "in two or three months".
- Edge he cites: a tight loop between research and product, with "release early, release often" iterative deployment, and decisions pushed down into teams. OpenAI "moves faster than any company I've ever worked at".
- Disruption: "basically every product, service, device, etc. that we use will be will need to be reinvented". Incumbents can do it if they "move quickly and sort of overcome the the innovators dilemma", but history says not in every case.
- Regulation: Gerstner says it is "way too early to obstruct American AI", comparing it to regulating autos early: "It took us 40 years to realize we needed seat belts and airbags". Weil's line is that lawmakers should use the models and "co-evolve" with the industry.
- Gerstner (an OpenAI investor) asks about confidence in reaching "a h 100red billion of revenue". Weil gives no number: "the path is there and it's up to us to execute", against "a very serious set of competitors".
- Screen implication: Weil's view that most products and services must be rebuilt around AI supports the Core manual check on AI disruption exposure; it can't be computed from filings.
