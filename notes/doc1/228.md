### 228. Philippe & Thomas Laffont, Brad Gerstner & Bill Gurley — Coatue's East Meets West deck: AI super cycle, GPUs, IPOs and headcount (Bg2 Pod, Jun 2025)

*Philippe Laffont (founder) and Thomas Laffont (co-founder) of Coatue walk Gerstner and Gurley through slides from Coatue's 10th East Meets West conference deck.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JA7n0wTChw)

**Takeaway:** Coatue sees AI as the biggest tech wave yet, now showing up in hard data: ChatGPT subscribers use Google less, GPU allocation is reshaping cloud share, and AI-first firms grow revenue while cutting staff. Gurley's warning for slow growers: at low growth, revenue multiples can slide "from five to three to one".

- Philippe: everyone worries it is the peak, "so it's never priced in". AI builds on every prior wave (mainframes, PCs, internet, SaaS), and power and grid-equipment makers may belong with tech, like the "next semicap".
- Thomas (Jun 2025): the Mag 7 were "basically flat year-over-year" while value accrued to top private AI labs. AI pure plays such as CoreWeave are "very few in the public market"; Google "has a lot of good AI but also has some disruption threats".
- Google vs ChatGPT: Coatue joined data from "a 100 million credit card receipts a day" with email receipts. Google page views per user grow \~4% a year without ChatGPT. After a user starts paying $20 a month for ChatGPT, they fall 8% year-over-year (11% peak to trough) over almost two years. Philippe: "data is useless unless you can join data sets that don't speak to each other", and "you can't underestimate these small moves".
- Gerstner: ChatGPT held share as Meta, Gemini, Grok and Claude entered, like Uber's early lead. Unlike Twitter or TikTok, it "has no virality to it", and switching costs from "permanent memory" are only starting.
- GPUs vs cloud share (slide 27, numbers may be "off by, you know, five or 6%"): cloud revenue share is Amazon 44%, Microsoft 30%, Google 19%, Oracle 5%. GPU allocation is Amazon \~20%, Oracle 19%, CoreWeave 11%. Amazon has "half the share of GPUs than their share of AWS": either AWS is behind in AI or it is betting on its own chips.
- Philippe asks whether Nvidia GPUs are "a prediction of future cloud revenues". He sees a battle between those who "pay the Nvidia rent" and those with their own chips, and possibly "a dozen hyperscalers" (Stargate, Anthropic, sovereigns). Precedent raised on stage: internet startups "started with Oracle and Sun and 5 years later they weren't on it".
- Macro (Philippe): markets are expensive but were in the '90s too, and "tokens trump tariffs". Debt to GDP is at "100% debt to GDP on our way to 140", but productivity growth of "2 and a half to three and a half%" a year for a decade could bend it to 80–100%. Experts can be badly wrong: in 1993 debt to GDP was forecast to rise from 60 to 80 and "in fact went from 60 to 40".
- Philippe on bond risk: at 4.5% a 30-year bond "on its way to 6 or 7, you could lose 60 or 70% of your money". On Bitcoin (\~2 trillion vs \~450–500 trillion world net worth and gold at 15–20 trillion), Philippe says "I don't think we can afford to ignore it anymore" but doesn't know when or how to own it.
- IPO market (Thomas): the 2021 IPO cohort was down 40% a year after listing and is "down 50%" five years later (traditional IPOs, not dollar-weighted, excluding SPACs). Gerstner: "On the relative basis they're probably down 75%". Signals are moving from red to yellow, and the new cohort averages a rule of 40.
- Meta–Scale AI deal (Jun 2025): Meta paid \~$15bn for 49% at a $30bn valuation, in effect paying full value for the team. Thomas: it is \~1% of Meta's market cap for a "multi- trillion opportunity", and speed matters. Anthropic took about a year to reach its first $1bn of revenue, three months for the next and two months for the one after.
- Headcount (Gerstner): the Mag 7 grew over 20% compounded while opex grew 2%, "the golden age of margin expansion". Coatue's AppLovin slide shows revenue doubling since Q2 2021 while employees fell over 35%. Gurley: "a willingness to reduce headcount is a different level" of confidence in AI than lip service.
- Thomas's founder quadrant (25% growth × cash-flow positive or not). Growing and profitable: get IPO ready. Growing and burning: build a "fortress balance sheet" (OpenAI just raised $40bn). Slow but profitable: play offense, even back into losses if growth reaccelerates. Slow and burning: "time to reinvent", e.g. go all in on a small, fast-growing new product.
- Gurley: perhaps "a thousand of these" slow, burning companies at $50–100M revenue are playing defense. If they "stay low growth at this size, their multiple could go from five to three to one, right, times revenue": "they're protecting something that doesn't exist".
- Screen implication: Gurley's point that slow growth crushes revenue multiples backs the Red Flags pairing of a high EV/Sales with fading growth.
