### 229. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — The "quasi public" pre-IPO market, the AI hype cycle and "is software dead?" (Bg2 Pod, Jun 2024)

*Episode 10 of BG2: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on mega private rounds, AI hype and the post-earnings software sell-off.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nrDudJtKpiE)

**Takeaway:** Both now see huge late-stage private rounds as a permanent "quasi public market", with real costs to discipline. Gerstner argues the "software is dead" sell-off (Jun 2024) is an overshoot that rewards stock picking on growth and SBC-adjusted free cash flow. Gurley counters that software can't re-rate while AI hype says it replaces everything.

- Gurley's new view: abundant mega-round money is "a systematic trend and not a cyclical one". AI is "50% at least" of venture, and it would take a 2001-style shakeout to change. Examples: four coding-copilot startups, each about a year and a half old, have raised "over $200 million each". Gerstner adds Sapphire Ventures data: 2023 AI funding was \~$28bn "in over 700 deals", with about 65% going to five or six companies.
- Gurley on fees: "two and 20" on a $300–400M check with no board seat, charged yearly over 7–8 years, "could be 10% of that money". Gerstner says the largest rounds often carry lower fees, "like zero and 10".
- Gerstner (Q2 2024 data): 521 private-market funds raised $295bn, but fund counts fell 45%. Capital is concentrating in multi-stage funds, sovereigns (MGX) and private-wealth platforms, creating a "quasi public market" for $10–30bn companies, unlike VC's 7–20% stakes with a board seat.
- Costs of excess capital. Gurley: "you're not allowed to not play"; Opendoor's raise pushed Zillow into home buying. "You can't raise $400 million and not have a high burn rate", and with high burn "you're going to know less about your unit economics". Gerstner: "excess capital distorts company behavior" and "scarcity breeds innovation".
- Gerstner: SoftBank-funded ride-sharing competition blocked the natural 80/20 "winner take most" structure; once capital dried up, Uber's profitability soared. Gurley adds that staying private longer can become staying private forever, which hurts IRR and invites regulators.
- Late-stage outcomes come down to "stock selection" (Gerstner). Groupon's last private round was $19bn, and "two years later it's worth 1 billion". He calls OpenAI at $90bn "an incredible risk reward". Gurley: LP corrections can take "a 10 to 15 year window".
- Gurley on why it's structural: tech companies with momentum and network effects "go much further and longer and higher", so investors accept paying "30 or 40 times revenue for something if it's going to hyper growth for four or five years". Gerstner: late-stage rounds now draw "20 30 40 players" vs five or six before.
- Hype cycle. Gurley: AI is "unquestionably" in one, with leaders spouting vague hyperbole; past claims (crypto replacing the corporate entity, scooters claiming they would take 75% of Uber's rides) failed, and UBI talk is "La La Land". Gerstner: TSMC's chairman calling Sam Altman "too aggressive for me to believe" is a way to "undermine the sources of capital" of a would-be competitor.
- Gerstner on the "fog of war": the analyst's job is to "develop deep conviction at these moments in time and be right before everybody else". In the internet era, Ask Jeeves, AltaVista, Lycos and Excite went to zero, yet Google became a multi-trillion-dollar company. He expects the same pattern in AI, which is why Altimeter leaned to "picks and shovels".
- Software "triple whammy" (Gerstner, Jun 2024): a less predictable future raises the discount rate, slowing growth cuts multiples, and rates stayed higher than expected. Forward revenue multiples sit \~20% below the 10-year average. UiPath's growth slowed to 6%, Salesforce's to 7% and Snowflake's to 26%, with buyers citing deal scrutiny and longer cycles. UiPath trades \~20x and Snowflake \~36x forward FCF (4x and 9x 2025 revenue).
- Gurley on budgets (Battery Ventures CIO survey): only 8% of CIOs have budget increases over 10%, yet 85% are aggressively increasing AI spend. Since security spend rarely falls, vendors that are "not security or not AI" are most at risk on expansion dollars. Gerstner: buyers freeze big commitments to make sure they are "betting on the future not on the past".
- Who's in the bullseye. Gurley: areas where LLMs already work, such as customer support, HR hiring tools and RPA (forms, invoices). Gerstner uses Aaron Levie's three axes: degree of automation, cost of the work and its volume/frequency. UiPath "grew only 6% in the quarter", and "most of its free cash flow gets eaten up by stock based compensation", so "it's not even real free cash flow on a per share basis".
- SBC as the key adjustment (Gerstner). Salesforce has "13 billion of free cash flow" with \~$2–3bn of SBC, so it still converts over $10bn: "not that expensive" for a leader despite slowing growth. Snowflake must prove growth "higher for longer" and "get more fit around SBC" to re-expand its multiple.
- Gerstner's call (Jun 2024): software had "trumpets in the air" in 2021 at "22x revenue multiples". He thinks "end of software" headlines will look "silly" in "less than 24 months" as incumbents' AI products ship, though UiPath-type models must reinvent. Gurley disagrees: while AI is sold as replacing everything, the view goes "from half full to half empty for anyone that's not holistically AI".
