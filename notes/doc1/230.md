### 230. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Coatue East Meets West recap: AI bubble, the IPO drought and Tesla vs ISS (BG2 Pod, Jun 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) record live at Coatue's East Meets West conference, recapping Coatue's public- and venture-market presentations, with a short drop-in from Coatue founder Philippe Laffont.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xuurlc-V2Jg)

**Takeaway:** Coatue argues AI is not a bubble because Nvidia's earnings rose as fast as its stock. The hosts worry less about public markets than about \~1,400 overfunded private unicorns that cannot go public, and they argue that public-market discipline beats staying private.

- Bubble test (Coatue via Gerstner, Jun 2024): unlike Cisco in 1999–2000, whose earnings multiple more than tripled, "because of the earnings growth of Nvidia the earnings multiple has been flat despite the fact that the stock is up nearly 10x". If Nvidia misses, it will be because revenue growth stops or margins collapse.
- Concentration (Jun 2024): of the S&P 500's 14% year-to-date return, Nvidia supplied 5 points, the other Mag 6 another 5 and the other 493 companies 4; the equal-weighted index was up only 4%. A year earlier, 56% of the conference's poll said AI was a bubble; Nvidia is up 200% since.
- Payback math for \~$200bn a year of AI capex: Laffont's "Monkey Math" needs $1.8 trillion of benefit for a 25% return on invested capital, roughly a 5% labour-force saving. Satya Nadella instead pointed to global GDP growth rising from 1–2% to 3–4%.
- Gurley prefers the GDP case: AI tools are competitive weapons that rivals also get, "it's the Red Queen effect you got to keep running just to keep up", so "capitalism ultimately competes away margin" and the gain goes to users.
- Capex risk: Gurley notes that internet-era software firms bought little hardware, but AI is delivered from the cloud, so a reset hurts more "when you're putting all this into the ground" (Nadella: "a supply driven wave"). Gerstner: "you have to assume that there is going to be an AI winter", and "if you run out of money during that period it's all over".
- Software (Jun 2024): the trade Gerstner hears most is buying software at 5.5x forward revenue, 20% below its 10-year average. Gurley: some software is "more under threat from llms", namely workflow tools rather than the data repository.
- Venture reset (Coatue): $715bn went into venture in 2021 vs \~$250bn forecast for 2024. Year-to-date AI deals (200 deals, $22bn) averaged a $1bn valuation and $120M rounds, 5–6x non-AI deals.
- The other \~1,400 unicorns are mostly not raising. Their LinkedIn headcount growth fell from 75% to 10%, revenue growth slowed sharply, IPOs in 2021–24 trail 2008 levels, and venture secondaries trade "50 to 75% off the highs", like non-Mag 7 public stocks.
- Public beats private: Meta at $90 (fall 2022) forced Zuckerberg to "confront the brutal truth"; it cut from 86,000 to 69,000 staff faster than private unicorns move, and the stock has since quintupled. Gerstner: "investors are greedy", so a fast grower with great margins gets found even when small (Samsara).
- Gerstner: "if you're only growing 10% and you're marginally profitable or not profitable", public or private, "that's never going to fetch a big multiple". He expects 60–70% of the 1,400 unicorns never to go public.
- IPO bar: Coatue suggests \~$10bn market cap, about $1bn of software revenue. Gurley: "if you want a 10x multiple you got be growing 30 35%". Laffont's two tests are \~$1bn of revenue and "a break even or path to break even".
- Gurley on OpenAI (Jun 2024): credit-card panel data suggests \~2.1bn of a \~3.4bn run rate is the $20 consumer plan. For cohorts old enough to measure, churn is \~65% a year, with only 35% retained.
- Governance (Tesla vote): retail voted \~90/10 and institutions \~70/30 for Musk's pay package, and even Vanguard went against ISS and Glass Lewis. Gurley found ISS "mostly backward-looking" and calls Musk's package "the most shareholder aligned" he has seen; Netflix draws ISS criticism despite "Industry leading low dilution".
- Screen implication: the hosts contrast Musk's pay with GM, whose CEO "has made tens and tens of millions of dollars" while the stock went nowhere. Pay versus multi-year shareholder return is a manual check, not something filings score.
