### 231. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Mag 7 vs the rest, the "AI's $600bn question" debate and calcium CT scans (BG2 Pod, Jul 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) discuss market positioning before the 2024 election, the run of skeptical AI-spending reports (Sequoia, Goldman Sachs, McKinsey) and why calcium CT scans are not standard care.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-8rMn2pk5qU)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner argues mega-cap tech is not a bubble: earnings are rising and forward P/Es sit near, not far above, their 2021 peaks. Gurley thinks LLM scaling may hit diminishing returns. Both say the real AI risk sits with richly funded venture startups, not cash-rich incumbents.

- Setup (Jul 2024, Gerstner): the Nasdaq is up 20% on the year. Markets price a 60% chance of a Trump win, which would make permanent the 2017 tax cuts behind "20 to 30%" of earnings growth since then, plus a 73% chance of a rate cut before the election.
- Positioning (Morgan Stanley data via Gerstner): May and June were "two of the top three largest months of tech selling since 2010". Hedge funds are more underweight growth than at any point in 10 years.
- Gurley's counterpoint: only \~11–12% of trading dollars in large-cap tech are active, vs above 20% in 2010. So active selling may not move prices while passive 401(k) flows keep coming.
- Dispersion (Goldman data via Gerstner): Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Google and Meta grew earnings 38% over the last 12 months. S&P 500 earnings were roughly flat, and the other 495 companies' earnings fell 5%, which is why shorting the rest has worked.
- Consumer names (McDonald's, Nike, Lululemon, Papa John's) hit 52-week lows, and casual dining is down 30–40% on the year. Gerstner calls it a normalization after the 2021 pull-forward, when "everybody started valuing them at kind of peak multiples like they were growth stocks".
- Forward P/E at the 2021 peak / 2022 trough / now (Jul 2024): Apple 28/22/32, Meta 22/12/24, Google 24/17/\~23, Amazon 64/46/33, Microsoft 37/23/35. Nvidia was 66 at peak, \~20 at the start of 2024 and is \~40 now.
- Gerstner's read: they "could all be down 10% in the next 3 months" without being screaming buys, but none trade at "hundreds of times sales" like 2021 software. Altimeter's Mag 7 exposure peaked at \~70% (once 30% in Meta alone) and has been cut by almost half into cash.
- Gurley: Meta is the safest of the group because its core AI (ad matching, content ranking) is "not really dependent" on frontier LLMs, so it gets the upside without the LLM risk.
- Skeptics' case: Sequoia's David Cahn ("AI 600 billion dollar question") sees a gap between buildout and revenue. Gurley says Cahn's worry is that crazy money raises "a higher risk of a reset", with every AI-coding startup holding \~$400 million.
- Gerstner's rebuttal: big platform bets have always paid off late. Amazon spent over $100bn on AWS for over eight years before profit, and Reality Labs spent \~$20bn a year. The incumbents act like nation states investing surplus cash in strategic advantage, and OpenAI already earns \~$3bn from paying users.
- Gurley stays skeptical: LLMs are great at text but "horrible at numbers", and LLMs are "set up for diminishing marginal returns". Coding-productivity claims slid from 40% to 30% and 20%, and the open question is "cost reduction versus Revenue creation".
- Where the risk sits (Gerstner): if Meta or Microsoft overspend, the stock dips, but there is "nothing lethal" for firms "throwing off tens of billions of free cash flow a year". Venture-backed AI firms that raised billions live off capital markets and must show payback.
- Gurley on Adept's licence-plus-hire exit to Amazon (it had raised $415 million): "an alternative to a low price sale it's not a win". The hosts' rule: "valuations represent discounted future expectations they're not an award for what you've done in the past". Gerstner expects AI's "mortality rate" to be very high and the winners very large.
- Calcium CT (Gerstner): five times as many US women die of heart attacks as of breast cancer. The scan costs \~$150 (a Seattle mobile unit charges $55), and Stanford's David Maron calls it the mammogram for the heart; half of people with good cholesterol still have plaque. Gurley suspects a healthcare "industrial complex" that maximizes profit over prevention and notes insurers "make a percentage of what is spent".
