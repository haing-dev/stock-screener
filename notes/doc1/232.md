### 232. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Silicon Valley's political shift, Meta's open-source Llama 3.1 and the Wiz walk-away (BG2 Pod, Jul 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on tech's swing toward Trump, Meta open-sourcing its 405B-parameter model, OpenAI's economics, Wiz turning down Google, Tesla FSD, the CrowdStrike outage and a COVID postmortem.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o9e1SDPc6Pc)

**Takeaway:** Gurley reads Meta's free frontier model as a defensive use of open source. Gerstner says it forces closed-model labs to "compete with free" while spending billions a year for years. Both see a stalled M&A and IPO market as a problem for venture.

- Unrealized-gains tax (Gurley and Gerstner): it would be "catastrophic" for startups. "there's no way to value a private company"; Gurley calls 409A valuations "voodoo magic" quoted to two decimals with no confidence interval. Holders would need liquidity to pay, and a later price collapse leaves only an unusable tax-loss carry-forward.
- Regulatory capture (Gurley): $42 billion of FCC rural broadband, paid cost-plus, has connected "zero" people per Commissioner Brendan Carr. Rules written by landline incumbents disqualified Starlink, where an antenna might cost \~$250 at the margin. He says the largest closed-model AI labs now outspend startups on lobbying.
- Gurley's framework: over \~15 years, big tech learned to use "open source as a defensive weapon rather than as an offensive weapon". Examples: Android (to get carriers and handset makers to back a rival to the iPhone), Kubernetes (so workloads could leave AWS) and Facebook's Open Compute Project ("almost like a patent deflection move" that keeps hardware costs down).
- Why Meta did it (Gurley): AI labs' grand claims "awaken the Giants", and Meta would not depend on a third party for a tool it needs. Gerstner adds that Zuckerberg called Apple's veto over product changes "soul crushing", so this is personal, and a company with "tens of billions of dollars of profit in the back room" can fund models indefinitely.
- Gurley's old Android post: even if it never makes money directly, open-sourcing "creates a moat that's so wide around the castle". Gerstner: "not every company can afford a multi-billion dollar uh moat extender".
- Gerstner's VC read: closed-model start-ups must "compete with free", spend billions for a long time, and maybe cross a bubble. Zuckerberg says Llama 4 compute is already laid out (Gerstner expects \~Nov–Dec 2024), Llamas 5–7 are planned, and each model will cost billions.
- OpenAI traction (Gerstner, Jul 2024): since GPT-4o, web visits rose from 1.8bn to 2.6bn a month and daily users from 60M to 100M. "there's only one way ... either you got to get a consumer to pay you or you got to get an Enterprise to pay you". Meta's Reality Labs (\~$20bn a year) may be cut \~20%, freeing $4–5bn for AI.
- Gurley's credit-card-panel guesses for $20 consumer subscriptions: OpenAI \~$2bn, Anthropic \~$100M, Perplexity \~$25M. "at 12 months the retention ... even in open AI is like 35%". Models cost "$3 billion every year" to rebuild because they are "super quickly depreciating assets".
- Wiz (Gerstner): rejecting Google's reported $23bn offer, it would trade at 13–14x forward revenue if public, "13 or 14 billion not 23 billion". Gurley: "it gets exponentially thin at the top", and the board partner is usually "overly confident"; walking away is the clearest case of valuations as "discounted future expectations".
- M&A drought (Gerstner): for four years the hyperscalers mostly "bought back their own stock", started paying dividends, bought Nvidia chips and held headcount down, so they became far more profitable but bought few companies. Gurley: Sequoia recycling Stripe shares with its own money shows LPs' hunger for liquidity.
- Tesla (Jul 2024): Musk said "if you don't believe that Tesla is going to solve autonomy" sell the stock. Altimeter's tests found hands-off time rose from \~40 seconds on FSD 12.3 to up to 4 minutes on 12.4, with \~5x larger on-car models. Its analyst found Chinese rivals "still catching up to fsd1" but consumers quick to adopt.
- Gurley on where AI moves fastest: finite games (chess, Go, driving defined as "no wreck") progress faster than open-ended problems, so AGI is the slowest. Gerstner adds customer service and website conversion.
- CrowdStrike (lost 30% of its value): Gurley says the failure was process, not code, and asks "why didn't you stage gate" the rollout. He flags Matthew Prince's worry that Microsoft, now "one of the top four security companies", could use the outage to push rivals out of the Windows kernel.
