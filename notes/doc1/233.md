### 233. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner, with Maureen Zawalick — Diablo Canyon and why US nuclear costs \~4x China and Korea (Bg2 Pod, Sep 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) visit PG&E's Diablo Canyon nuclear plant, interview site vice president Maureen Zawalick, then discuss US nuclear costs, SMRs and policy.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TYCTfrec6FE)

**Takeaway:** Existing US reactors can run 60–80 years and should not be shut. New US plants cost \~$13bn per gigawatt vs \~$2.5bn in China and South Korea, mainly because of slow permitting, litigation and no repeat building. Gerstner wants AI hyperscalers to help fund new capacity; Gurley would rebuild the rules from zero.

- Diablo Canyon (Gerstner/Zawalick): two reactors, \~2.5 GW, online 1985. It supplies 8–9% of California's energy at \~6 cents/kWh, with a capacity factor "upwards of 90%" (units have run at 93–94%).
- Why it was nearly closed (Zawalick): 2015–16 forecasts saw no need for it, before AI data centers, electrification goals, the Ukraine war and the Aug 2020 blackouts. Replacement projects such as offshore wind came slower than the state expected. A 2015 bill (SB 350) "excluded nuclear and large Hydro" from "renewable".
- Plant life is a licence question, not a physical limit: 40-year original licences, 20-year renewals to 60, and \~a dozen plants applying to go to 80. California extended Diablo to 2029 and 2030 (Dec 2023); PG&E applied for a 20-year federal renewal although the state asked for five (Sep 2024).
- Waste (Zawalick): all spent fuel from the US's 100+ units would fit "in the size of a football field up to the goalposts", and reprocessing as France does could reuse \~80–90%.
- Cost gap (Gurley): Vogtle 3 and 4 in Georgia cost \~$30bn for 2.2 GW, "over 13 billion dollar per gwatt", against "2.5 billion per gigawatt" in China. Democratic South Korea matches China's price and exports, which makes it the more telling comparison.
- Gurley's four causes, from an anonymous industry and regulatory source: separate federal and state permitting (\~10 years; the NRC is fee-based, billed by the hour); litigation by special-interest groups; no learning curve after 30 years without building; and higher labor, steel and cement costs.
- Gerstner's version: US builds take "12 to 15" years vs 7–8 in Korea and China, and time is money. Repeat building matters most: "by plant five it was cut in half" and by plant ten cost fell 70%. Labor is only "a third" of the gap.
- Gurley doubts the US can cut cost by the \~70% needed to compete with gas: "it's hard to see". He says "landed cost of energy is the data point you should measure most", and the US should stop blaming China and Korea for using US technology and consider licensing from Korea.
- Utilities are the bottleneck (Gurley): they are "inherently conservative". The PG&E CEO said they have "no interest in investing in smrs" and will "let risk Capital invest" first. NuScale's utility power-purchase deal collapsed, and an SMR startup pays \~$60 million in NRC fees just to reach first approval: a "sport of Emperors".
- AI demand (Gerstner): data-center needs mean US baseload must grow faster than in two decades; "Power is the Primitive for AI". If hyperscalers spend "$250 billion a year on capex" on AI, they could form a consortium with Washington. Diablo's site was "provisioned for six" reactors, so four more could each serve one hyperscaler.
- Gurley's math: Diablo was built for $5.5bn in mid-1980s dollars, \~6.4 per gigawatt in today's dollars, "still half of 13" at Vogtle.
- Policy ideas: Gurley wants a Manhattan Project–style effort that rewrites NRC rules "from zero ground up", limits lawsuits, and creates an IP-free standard, "the Linux you know of SMR", drawing on the Navy's \~100 ship reactors. Gerstner wants no more decommissioning, restarts of closed plants, and nuclear incentives at least equal to wind and solar.
