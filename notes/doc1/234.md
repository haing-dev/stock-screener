### 234. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Nuclear update, OpenAI at 15x forward revenue, and overfed venture companies (Bg2 Pod, Sep 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on hyperscaler nuclear deals, o1 and inference demand, OpenAI's valuation and margins, the state of venture capital, and Big Tech multiples after the Fed's 50bp cut.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7ZKbMWUjsM)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner says OpenAI's rumored $150bn round (\~15x forward revenue) is in the same "valuation zip code" as Google and Meta when they were at that stage. Gurley says the open question is gross margin: compute may make AI a 10–20% gross-margin business. On venture, Gurley argues that force-feeding startups huge late-stage rounds harms execution and kills the push to IPO; Gerstner sees mostly reversion to the mean.

- Nuclear (Gurley): utilities are conservative buyers. Selling only to them is like "selling only to laggards" in crossing-the-chasm terms. Hyperscalers as customers (Microsoft–Constellation's Three Mile Island restart, Amazon's Talen data center deal) "may be like 10x better than selling just to utilities alone".
- Gerstner: Morgan Stanley estimates \~$2bn was spent on carbon offsets in 2020, rising to \~$100bn by 2030, mostly by hyperscalers. Buying clean nuclear power reduces their need for offsets, which helps them justify nuclear.
- o1 reasoning (Gurley): the charts used a logarithmic x-axis, so linear gains need "maybe 10x the amount of processing" at inference. That should shift compute spending from training toward inference. OpenAI admits extra iterations sometimes made answers worse.
- Gerstner: reasoning means "100x more inference" than single-shot prompts, and "the world is inference constrained". A $10bn, 300 MW data center should run inference whenever training isn't running. Nvidia claims GB200 improves inference 50x; others say 3x.
- Gurley: "inference constrained" "may be a financial problem" too. Advanced voice may cost far more to run than it earns, so pricing and business models are still unknown.
- Moat debate (Sep 2024): Gerstner says ChatGPT, with \~200 million weekly users and almost no advertising, is pulling away through a data flywheel. Gurley doubts that user data makes the model much better, since rivals' benchmarks are close. Memory is where "switching costs go through the roof", but Microsoft, Google and Apple hold the email and chat data.
- Valuation (Gerstner): OpenAI has \~$4–5bn of revenue growing over 100% a year, so a $150bn round is "about 15 times forward revenue". By comparison: Google's 2004 IPO at \~10x forward revenue, Microsoft's 2007 Meta investment at \~50x, and Meta's 2012 IPO at \~13x. OpenAI reached $5bn of revenue in \~2 years; Google took 2–3 years longer and Meta 6–7.
- Gurley: "all revenue is not created equal". OpenAI's gross margin may sit "in the 10 or 20% range versus the 57 and 81" in Gerstner's Google/Meta charts. If tokens are a variable cost, it looks more like an airline with fuel: is an AI business "inherently a 20% margin business"? On an AWS comparison, Amazon trades at \~3x.
- Gerstner: revenue doesn't set value; "the future cash flows that those revenues can produce" do. Watch for a permanent "layer tax" from Nvidia and compute. The bet requires scale plus falling costs: inference cost fell over 90% in 18 months, and Sunny \[?\] expects another 90% drop over the next several years.
- Venture (Gurley): IPOs are in the single digits vs 70–80 in average years. Mega-funds earn the same fee percentage on $200m checks as on $5m ones, so they compete by being founder-friendly. Founder and employee secondaries remove the main reason to go public: liquidity.
- Gurley's "gavage tube" point (the tube used to force-feed geese for foie gras): overfed companies burning "20 million dollars a month" lose discipline and fund low-return projects. At \~$240m a year of burn and \~50% gross margins, breakeven needs a \~$500m revenue run rate. "500 million in revenue or bust" means many more zombie unicorns.
- Gerstner's reply: venture grew from \~$100bn to \~$300bn a year over a decade, but much of it goes to "quasi-public companies" (Databricks, Stripe, OpenAI) that should be compared with public peers. Exits are \~$100bn a year, down from the $700bn 2021 peak. He thinks \~80% of the \~1,000 unicorns "will never get back" to their old valuations. Altimeter sold Lacework to Fortinet and may have four IPOs in 4–6 months.
- Both agree too much capital hurts companies. Gerstner: "too much capital does ruin corporate culture", raising burn and lowering returns, and it "leads to excessive SBC". He praises Meta's 2023 "year of efficiency" ("flatter is faster and leaner is better") and Zoom, Salesforce and Workday "starting to get sober about stock-based compensation" (Sep 2024).
- Markets (Gerstner, Sep 2024): Mag 7 forward P/E is \~31x vs a \~25x 10-year average and a 21x low in Jan 2023. Growth-adjusted P/E is below its 10-year average, but if growth misses, stocks go "sideways to down". Altimeter cut risk in Jun–Jul and is back to average exposure. Nvidia fell from 140 to 115; the bull case is \~6 million GPUs next year vs bears' \~4.5 million. Jan 2023 was "pocket kings or pocket aces"; now it's "pocket nines".
- Screen implication: for tech names, check gross margin and stock-based compensation as well as revenue growth. A high revenue multiple only holds if growth is delivered.
