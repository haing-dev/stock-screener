### 235. Jensen Huang, with Brad Gerstner & Clark Tang — Nvidia's moat, the "not Cisco 2000" case and inference scaling (Bg2 Pod, Oct 2024)

*Nvidia's founder and CEO, interviewed at Nvidia headquarters on 4 Oct 2024 by Brad Gerstner and Clark Tang of Altimeter, an Nvidia shareholder, ahead of Altimeter's annual investor meeting.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bUrCR4jQQg8)

**Takeaway:** Huang argues Nvidia's moat is a full-stack, yearly-refreshed computing platform whose advantage compounds, not a faster chip. Demand is not a Cisco-2000 overbuild: \~$1 trillion of existing data centers must be modernized for machine learning, and a new layer of "AI factories" comes on top. Inference, already over 40% of revenue, should grow far faster than training.

- Cost curve: Nvidia drove the "marginal cost of computing down by 100,000x over the course of 10 years"; Moore's law would have given "about 100x". The gain came from the whole stack: accelerated computing, new numerical precisions, tensor cores, NVLink, HBM memory and software.
- Scaling now has three engines: pre-training (compute up "by factor of four" a year), post-training and inference-time reasoning. A model may run \~10,000 internal inferences before it answers.
- Moat (Huang): what matters is "the flywheel of machine learning", from data curation to training to deployment. Amdahl's law means speeding up one step (e.g. 30% of the time, 3x faster) barely moves the whole, so Nvidia accelerates every step. Asked two years earlier whether the moat would hold in inference, he said it "would be greater".
- Parallel vs serial: Intel needed every transistor excellent. Nvidia would "rather have 10 times more transistors 20% slower". Every parallel algorithm must be rewritten for the architecture, so domain libraries (cuDNN and others below PyTorch) are the real lock-in.
- Installed base: new gear trains models and leaves "a trail of free gear" for inference that stays CUDA-compatible. Software updates make Hopper "two three four times better than when they bought it"; OpenAI only recently retired its Volta systems.
- Yearly cadence: "the data center is now the unit of computing". Each year Nvidia builds a new one with 2–3x more performance, 2–3x lower cost and 2–3x better energy efficiency. It tells customers to "buy a little every year", then splits the system up and integrates it into \~10 clouds and partners.
- On custom ASICs (Trainium, TPUs, Meta's MTIA): "Nvidia is a market maker not share taker" and never talks about market share internally. Nvidia shows its roadmap to hyperscalers years ahead; "they don't present their Asic road map to us". The host relays Amazon's Andy Jassy: "Nvidia is a huge and important partner".
- Forecasting lesson (a host): in Jan 2023 the \~25 analysts expected Nvidia to do "26 billion of Revenue" for 2023, and it "did 60 billion": "the single greatest failure of forecasting the world has ever seen". They were anchored on the crypto winter.
- Why not Cisco 2000 (Huang, from first principles): \~$1 trillion of CPU data centers must be modernized over 4–5 years (\~$150bn a year). With Moore's law "largely ended", a company with $50bn of capex would put "100% of the 50 billion" into generative AI. On top come AI factories running digital workers, a market that is unknowable but "probably a few trillion dollar".
- Scale (a host, Oct 2024): Nvidia will do \~$125bn of revenue this year against a multi-trillion market. Huang: "share takers can only be so big"; "market makers can be quite large". Nvidia invented the 3D gaming PC market too.
- OpenAI (hosts; Altimeter and Nvidia both joined the round): \~250 million weekly users, about twice Google's at its IPO. At \~$10bn of revenue next year, the \~$150bn valuation is \~15x forward revenue, "about the multiple of Google and meta at the time of their IPO". Huang: a model is "necessary but not sufficient" for AI. Every layer has opportunities, but not for everyone, so builders must know "what's a feature versus a product right versus a company".
- xAI's Memphis cluster went from equipment arrival to first training run in 19 days. Huang says a 100,000-GPU supercomputer would normally take "three years to plan" and a year to get working. 200,000–300,000-GPU clusters are here, but Nvidia's demand doesn't depend on million-GPU clusters: "distributed training will have to work".
- Inference: a host notes "over 40% of your Revenue today is inference" and that reasoning could raise it "by a billion x". Huang: "the growth of inference is going to be way larger than the growth in training".
- Efficiency (a host): Nvidia makes \~$4 million of revenue and \~$2 million of profit or FCF per employee, with 32,000 staff. Huang hopes for a 50,000-person company with "100 million" AI assistants. More productive companies post better earnings or growth, so the next CEO email is "not a layoff announcement".
- Policy (Huang): regulate AI mainly at the application level through existing agencies (FAA, NHTSA, FDA). Models should be "closed and open", not one or the other. Nvidia's Nemotron 340B is a reward model built to improve other models.
- Screen implication: the moat Huang describes (ecosystem, installed-base compatibility, yearly cadence) can't be computed from filings. Revenue per employee and FCF per employee are computable signs of the efficiency hosts praise.
