### 236. Bill Gurley, Brad Gerstner & Sunny Madra — Recap of the Jensen Huang interview: Nvidia's moat, xAI and AI assistants (BG2 Pod, Oct 2024)

*BG2 episode in which Bill Gurley (Benchmark), Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Sunny Madra (Groq) unpack Gerstner's 90-minute interview with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z77jZkYDpIE)

**Takeaway:** Nvidia's moat is a full system, strongest in the largest training clusters; it is weaker in inference and at the edge, where faster, cheaper rivals already exist. Gurley warns that hypergrowth hides normal economics, so record margins and the number of competitors may not last.

- Gurley on the numbers (Oct 2024): a \~$3.3 trillion company "still growing over 100% a year" with 65% operating margins; "there's only like five companies in the S&P 500 at that level and they certainly aren't growing at this pace".
- Gurley discounts private claims of "insane" demand but not Nvidia's: the company is "doing 30 billion a quarter growing 122%", so the demand is visible in reported numbers.
- Moat, per Huang: Nvidia is "not a GPU company", it is an accelerated-compute company. The CUDA library has over 300 industry-specific acceleration algorithms, and software gains are backward compatible, so older Hopper, Ampere and Volta fleets get faster too.
- Moat risk (Gurley, Madra): will fewer developers touch CUDA as optimisation moves into PyTorch and similar tools? Madra expects a "10 to1 or 100 to1" ratio of developers working above the metal, so the moat is "not as strong" long term. Gavin Baker, quoted by Gerstner: if the world standardises on Transformers and PyTorch, advantage shifts to custom ASICs.
- Gurley: Nvidia's advantage "is strongest where the size of the system is largest". That explains single nodes for rent "at or below cost" while big systems are scarce, Nvidia's interest in CoreWeave existing, and a risk that customer concentration "increases for NVIDIA over time".
- Inference is the weak flank. Inference is already 40% of Nvidia revenue, \~$50–60bn this year (2024). Yet the three fastest inference providers on public leaderboards (Groq, Cerebras, SambaNova) don't run CUDA, and Madra says inference has no CUDA tie-in.
- Gurley rejects Huang's argument that leftover installed base wins inference: it argues for cost, not performance. Startups moved from Oracle and Sun to Linux and MySQL within five years, "from 100% to to 3%", once building gave way to optimising.
- Rene Haas (Arm CEO): "nobody is going to assault the Nvidia Castle head-on", but an orthogonal competitor could take AI at the edge, where Arm has 300 billion devices installed.
- xAI stood up its 100,000-H100 cluster in 19 days, and Huang called it the largest supercomputer in the world. Gurley estimates it took \~10% of a quarter's supply, a sign Nvidia puts builders of big clusters at the front of the line. Huang sees clusters going to 500,000–1 million GPUs before power limits force distributed training.
- OpenAI funding (Oct 2024): over $4bn of revenue, likely $10bn+ next year, a $6.5bn raise and a $4bn credit line. Gerstner underwrote it at \~15x forward revenue, against \~13–14x revenue for Google and Meta at their IPOs. He expects consolidation, because VCs won't write multi-billion checks to labs without a hit product.
- Costs: inference cost fell 90% over the past year and should fall another 90% (Gerstner). Gurley guesses an o1-preview query costs 20–30x a normal ChatGPT search, so the open question is which problems justify the extra time and cost.
- Margins from AI (Gurley): Huang's plan to triple revenue with \~25% more staff fits Nvidia, an IP company with surging demand. For most firms the gains get "competed away": Delta's AI savings will go to Southwest and United because "bad Industries remain bad Industries".
- Gurley: "hyper growth tends to delay what you learned in microeconomics class". He recalls five public PC makers all growing 100%; hypergrowth margins and participant counts "may or may not be durable".
- Agent wager (Oct 8, 2024): Gerstner and Madra bet on a full-release agent that books a hotel with memory and actions within two years. Gurley takes the over: a demo is easy, but trust at scale with credit cards is the hard part.
- Screen implication: treat record margins earned during hypergrowth as unproven. Lasting margin gains from AI need a moat, so persistent ROIC matters more than one year of margin expansion.
