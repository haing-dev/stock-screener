### 237. Bill Gurley, Brad Gerstner & Jamin Ball — The state of venture capital and the AI capex race (BG2 Pod, Oct 2024)

*BG2 episode with Bill Gurley (Benchmark), Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Altimeter partner Jamin Ball, author of the Clouded Judgement blog, recorded during big-tech earnings a week before the US election.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tBU5UPI03Rg)

**Takeaway:** Mega venture funds now earn so much from fees that deploying capital can matter more than returns. The resulting over-funding removes the middle range of exits and leaves most 2021-era unicorns stuck. Big-tech AI capex is tolerated only while profit growth outpaces it.

- Ball's founder test: are you partnering with "a 2% or a 20% Venture firm"? A $100M fund that triples earns $2M a year in fees and $40M of carry. A $4bn fund earns "roughly 80 million a year" in fees alone, so GPs can get rich whatever happens to the companies. On the hosts' math, a firm raising $5bn every two years manages \~$50bn over 10 years, so 2% is "a billion a year" of guaranteed fees.
- Too much money hurts companies. Founders always promise to stay frugal after a big raise: "that never happens". They chase six priorities instead of three, and revenue built in markets without a real edge proves "less sustainable" when growth slows. Rivals then face a "prisoners dilemma" and raise just as much, so the "Natural Market winner" emerges more slowly.
- Ball: over-raising closes the middle exits. A sale for $100–200M can change founders' and employees' lives, but not after raising $100M too early, because the preference stack takes it all. Investors "removed a thick middle" of outcomes, leaving 10x or zero.
- Gurley: a high round is not a prize; "valuations represent discounted future expectations", so the next up round gets harder. Of \~1,400 pre-2022 private unicorns (a Coatue figure), the hosts guess "less than 10% it might be less than 5%" could raise an up round today (Oct 2024).
- Gerstner's market data: VC deployment peaked at over $700bn in 2021 and is back to \~$300bn, about 2017–18 levels. First-time funds have collapsed while platforms consolidate (General Catalyst raised \~$13bn in 24 months; Lightspeed \~$13–14bn in \~28–30 months).
- Ball: top-quartile funds need power-law outcomes, not evenly spread returns. A growth-stage fund has few companies that can deliver one, so a bigger fund must widen its aperture and takes more of the diluting long tail.
- Gurley: a 2011 Kauffman Foundation report found $1bn-plus funds had never done well. A PE firm made \~$1–1.2bn when Google bought DoubleClick for $3.1bn, but it sat in a $4bn fund. Gerstner: the top 5% of funds earn about 4.5x cash on cash (StepStone data).
- AI capex (Oct 2024): mega-cap capex runs at \~$250bn a year, and Google's quarter came in at \~$13bn vs $12.7bn expected. Gerstner says only companies of Google's scale can spend $50bn a year (GCP added \~$2.5bn of ARR in the quarter, at +35% growth). He frames it as Pascal's wager. Gurley: apart from Amazon's distribution centres, no breakout tech company of the last generation needed massive capex, and on Pascal's wager, "that's exactly how you would go over the top".
- When capex worries matter (Gerstner): capex has gone "fairly parabolic", but operating income and top-line growth "outpaced the increase in capex", so investors tolerated it. A recession with capex still "unhinged" would hurt the stocks.
- Masayoshi Son put $9 trillion of capex on 200 million GPUs at "very reasonable": replacing 5% of the global workforce saves about $9 trillion a year. Gurley's retort: "it was bigger than any number anyone else has said". Ball: services markets are usually "10x bigger than software", so AI spending targets headcount budgets.
- Gurley on AI productivity: CIOs report a 15–30% lift from coding copilots, not replacement. In tasks where a 5–10% error rate is costly (coding, self-driving), AI adoption will be slow; customer service already tolerates that error rate.
- Google (Oct 2024): search and YouTube revenue each +12%, AI answers monetise "at the same level", and the stock trades at the lowest multiple in the Mag 5. Gurley's caution: if an agent completes the booking, merchants that now pay 50–100% of a first transaction to acquire a customer might pay \~5%.
- AMD fell \~10% after hours despite strong growth, because guidance implied \~3% of the data-centre GPU market vs Nvidia's \~90%, not the 10% hoped for early in the year (Gerstner).
- Screen implication: treat rising capex as a risk only when it outgrows operating income and revenue; a capex surge funded by faster profit growth is not by itself a red flag.
