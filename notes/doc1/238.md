### 238. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Is pre-training hitting a wall? FSD 13, DOGE and the 2025 setup (BG2 Pod, Nov 2024)

*BG2 episode with Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter), recorded on the night of Nvidia's Q3 FY25 earnings call.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SewIKjE1Lec)

**Takeaway:** Gains from LLM pre-training are slowing, but both see AI demand holding up because inference, post-training and product features (memory, actions, voice) drive usage. For 2025 Gerstner expects a stock picker's market: rich index multiples and wide dispersion, with winners among tech firms that combine growth and AI-driven margin expansion.

- Gurley on scaling (Nov 2024): OpenAI and Anthropic pitched spending "a billion on this model, we're going to spend 10, and then we're going to spend 100", implying at least linear gains. If pre-training is topping out, that is "a surprise shift", even if AI keeps growing.
- Gurley's three questions if pre-training stalls: does it dent demand for the biggest Nvidia systems, where its edge "is greatest at the largest cluster size"? Does a shift to inference change which hardware wins? Does a ceiling let rivals "catch up faster" with OpenAI (e.g. DeepSeek)?
- Gerstner's answer: "the second derivative is slowing", but pre-training is one vector among several (post-training, data quality, inference-time reasoning). Nvidia called Hopper demand "exceptional" and Blackwell "staggering" and beat on revenue.
- Gurley: startups train on Nvidia but can run models elsewhere, and public tests show Groq, Cerebras and TPUs "outperforming on inference". He notes Huang now argues that very large context windows could need large systems for inference too.
- Gerstner: pre-training advantage has been commoditising fast, so if it decelerates, the edge shifts to post-training and inference-time reasoning. Those may be less commodity because of a better "data flywheel" or architectural breakthroughs. Gurley: if pre-training tops out, nobody trains on a $100bn system. Gerstner, an OpenAI investor, says that "may not be a bad thing", given doubts about where the returns would come from.
- Demand evidence (Nov 2024): Microsoft's \~$10bn of AI revenue is "almost all inference", and Nvidia says half its revenue is inference. Azure grows 34% on a $66bn run rate and expects to accelerate. Gurley notes 3,200 of Snowflake's \~10,000 enterprise customers use its AI products daily. Gerstner adds that software has re-accelerated after two years of digestion. OpenAI could "double, triple the number of users" of ChatGPT with no model gains, because consumers barely use current capabilities.
- Tesla FSD (Gerstner, Altimeter estimates): miles per critical disengagement rose 100x from v12.1 to v12.5, with another 10x expected from v13 (\~25,000 miles vs Waymo at maybe \~20,000). At that pace it reaches 500,000 miles by mid-2025, the human rate, and robotaxis launch in a couple of cities "probably in Q2".
- Gurley's caveats: Waymo has years of driverless experience and probably remote oversight that Tesla must build. A national standard needs "liability limitations" because jury awards could cripple the industry.
- Industry structure (Gerstner): Tesla sells "2 million of the world's 80 million cars". Either self-driving becomes a commodity box in every OEM's car, or it is an extinction event taking the industry "from 20 OEMs down to three or four". He compares it to 2009, when he told his mother to buy Apple and short Nokia.
- Audits: Gurley says two of the largest federal departments failed audits for several years, while a public company that fails an audit or changes auditors "you get taken to the god wood shed". Gerstner: Supermicro was "Down 90%".
- 2025 setup (Gerstner, Nov 2024): tax cuts, deregulation, falling rates and the AI super-cycle on one side; tariffs, lower government spending and multiples near highs on the other (S&P 500 at 24x, Nasdaq \~29x). "We're closer to trumpets in the air than blood in the streets", so he is "not all in".
- Gerstner on dispersion: Target fell 25% on a miss (tariff and consumer worries), while Snowflake rose 20% after hours on low expectations and 29% growth. At one point the S&P 500 was "actually down five" without its top 10 tech names.
- Gerstner on margins: since Satya Nadella became CEO, Microsoft's net margin has expanded 1,000 basis points: "revenue has doubled, but earnings have almost quadrupled". He wants the "5 10 20 tech companies" with both AI tailwinds and margin expansion.
- Screen implication: a failed audit or a change of auditor is a hard red flag; auditor changes are disclosed in 8-K filings and could be screened.
