### 239. Brad Gerstner & Bill Gurley — Tesla FSD 12, open vs closed AI models and Delaware vs Elon (Bg2 Pod, Mar 2024)

*Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Bill Gurley (Benchmark) on Tesla's rebuilt self-driving model, open- vs closed-source AI in the enterprise, the Delaware ruling on Elon Musk's pay package and a quick market check.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bD1rrBBcJSA)

**Takeaway:** The biggest mispricings come around "phase shifts", when consensus models built on steady deceleration break: Nvidia's data-center revenue came in at $96bn vs $22bn expected. Tesla's switch to an end-to-end imitation-learning model fed by 5 million cars may be such a shift, while open-source models squeeze the margins of closed model vendors in the enterprise.

- Gerstner: "every sell-side model on Wall Street has linear deceleration in growth rates". Nvidia: consensus at the start of 2023 had data-center revenue at $22bn for the year; it ended at $96bn. EPS was expected at $5.70 and "now it looks like it's going to be $25".
- Gerstner's hold rule: while "our numbers are higher than the street's number" the stock keeps rising. Once the street catches up, it "becomes more of a of a market performer". Investors asked whether to sell Nvidia at 200, at 300, at 400.
- FSD 12: Tesla threw out its rules-based C++ stack for a neural net trained on video of its best drivers, "video in and control out". Gurley: hand-coding could never cover every corner case; by Occam's razor the simpler approach "has a much better chance of of going all the way".
- Gerstner was told the new model improves "5 to 10x better per month" than prior systems, and that the engineering team is \~1/10 the size of the Cruise and Waymo teams.
- Data moat: 5 million cars, with \~99% of data filtered out on the car. Gurley: cars record disengagements and abrupt braking, steering or acceleration, which can be weighted extra in training. Gerstner: "Something magic happens around a million cars", because long-tail events occur only "tens or just hundreds of times".
- Rivals (Gurley): Waymo and Cruise "just don't have that many cars" and theirs cost $150,000. Mobileye doesn't control the car's design; BYD or another Chinese maker is likelier. He'd heard Waymo's headcount matches Cruise, whose financials "were horrific". A robotaxi service on a 10-year model would "have to go raise a hundred billion" of capex.
- FSD economics (Gerstner): Tesla makes \~$2,500 per car without FSD; 7% of buyers paid $12,000 for it. Halving the price to $500 a month at 20% penetration keeps contribution about the same; 50% would create "billions of dollars in incremental EBITDA". He expects Tesla to push penetration, because more users means more data.
- Model race (Gerstner, Mar 2024): Claude 3 Sonnet does GPT-4-level work at "a fraction of the price". Llama 3 and GPT-5, the first trained only on H100s, are rumored for May–July. Gurley: labs are "all racing up the same hill" on the same benchmarks; developers get more excited about low-end pricing than top-end performance.
- Enterprise goes open (Gurley): the best CIOs became open source first. Startups prototype on a premium model, then in production "become very cost focused" and play models off each other (one moved between four platforms). Small vertical models (e.g. legal) may beat general ones.
- Margin stacking (Gerstner): with closed models "Amazon's got to get paid, Anthropic's got to get paid, Nvidia's got to get paid". Open source removes one layer, and "Zuckerberg is going to drive the price". He'd refocus OpenAI on consumer, even "rename the company ChatGPT". Anthropic's sales force went from 2 to 25 people.
- Gurley on regulatory capture: proprietary labs lobby to restrict open source, which "could become a precedent where all companies would try and eliminate open source". A senior House member told Gerstner: "We need competition".
- Delaware voided Musk's 2018 pay package. Gurley: it paid no cash and its first tranche required the stock to double; the real problem is RSU packages where people get "paid when the stock doesn't move". Plaintiff's lawyers seek \~$5–6bn for a client with nine shares. If upheld, he argues every Delaware company must consider moving.
- Gerstner (a former corporate lawyer): Delaware's corporate code is almost word for word Texas's and California's. Its edge was precedent and shareholder-friendly judges, which this ruling upends.
- Market check (Gerstner, P/E Mar 2021 → Mar 2024): Meta \~20x → \~23x; Google \~25x → just below 20x on search-share fears; Apple a little lower; Amazon "quite a bit lower" though retail and cloud are improving. Nvidia's multiple hit a 5–10-year low as EPS went from \~$5 to \~$25. The test for all of them: "are companies and consumers getting massive benefits out of the models and inference".
