### 240. Brad Gerstner, Bill Gurley & Bob Mylod — Stock comp is a real expense; AI Cold War; valuing LLMs (Bg2 Pod, Mar 2024)

*Gerstner (Altimeter) and Gurley (Benchmark) with Bob Mylod, former Priceline CFO and chair of Booking Holdings, on stock-based compensation (SBC), then Apple–Gemini, TikTok and Microsoft's hiring of Inflection's staff.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPt6Cs1hd2k)

**Takeaway:** SBC is often a tech company's largest expense, yet it is adjusted out of EBITDA, so tech profits are overstated. Count it as cash and judge it as a share of free cash flow, not as a dilution percentage. LLM revenue is "experimental", with near-zero switching costs, so it does not deserve recurring-revenue multiples.

- History (Gurley): startups once ran \~10% annual option dilution, steering to \~3% near an IPO. After the dot-com bust, a large investor in a big internet company pushed zero-basis RSUs: one RSU is worth \~2.5–3 options under Black-Scholes, so dilution "might drop from, say, 4% to 1.2%". RSUs then spread everywhere.
- Mylod: FAS 123 (2002–04) forced companies to expense options. RSUs had always been expensed, so issuers switched to RSUs and began pro-forma-ing SBC out of earnings. Priceline did too, but "always viewed $1 of of an RSU as fully being worth $1".
- Alignment (Mylod): an RSU granted at $20 still holds $10 after a 50% fall, while an option is worthless. Management can lead "a 20% decline and pick up 80% of the value of their equity award".
- Mylod: SBC is often "the single largest expense component" of a tech company, yet some boardrooms don't treat it as an expense, so SBC has run away for 10–15 years. Gurley: "these tech companies are far less profitable than we actually think they are".
- Hidden cost (Gerstner): Google reportedly offers AI engineers $500,000 cash plus $1.5M of RSUs vesting monthly; excluding SBC pretends the engineer costs $500,000. Gurley: a CEO fretted over a $400,000 salary while the RSU grant was $10 million.
- Gerstner: $20 of earnings with 10% annual dilution means a shrinking claim per share. Mylod: buybacks to offset SBC are "a huge cash expense" kept below the line, so cash flow looks better than it is. Gurley: boards that think offsetting buybacks make SBC disappear are "financially ignorant", and employees sell as the company buys.
- Dilution % misleads (Mylod): it is just "a commentary on the valuation of the company". A $100bn company with $1bn of FCF and 1% dilution has "just wiped out all my free cash flow". Diverting 30–35% of FCF a year for 20 years gives away "literally, you know, a third of the company".
- Better metric: SBC as a share of operating or free cash flow; Booking's latest proxy cites it. Gerstner: of 40–50 tech names charted, \~10 are under 20% of FCF. Microsoft (Mar 2024): SBC $2.8bn a quarter, \~$11.2bn a year, "only be 10% of free cash flow and 35 basis points of dilution".
- Booking (Mylod): shares fell from \~50–52M to 35M over \~10 years and the stock went from $10 to $3,400; "if the enterprise value has doubled, the share price has tripled". It stopped excluding SBC "a long, long time ago". Mantra: "a dollar has to be worth a dollar, whether it's in cash or comp".
- Comp committees (Gurley): consultants benchmark peers; if enough boards pick the 75th percentile, pay ratchets higher, and nobody asks if the plan is good for shareholders. Mylod: every board thinks its team is "above the mean". Gerstner: bonuses tied to adjusted EBITDA explain the behavior (Munger: "Show me the incentives").
- Exemplars (Gurley): Netflix lets staff choose cash or options on a slider, has no RSUs and dilutes \~1.5% a year. Gary Steele took stock participation at Splunk "from 87% to 15". Test payouts with the stock up 20/40/80/100%: "if you're only doing zero-basis RSUs, you've got a horrible alignment problem". Companies with lower SBC earn higher multiples.
- AI Cold War (Gerstner, a ByteDance shareholder): reports put >90% of ByteDance revenue and >100% of profit outside the US; TikTok US does \~$8–10bn of revenue at a loss. He sees a real chance it is shut down and expects China to respond, e.g. on Apple.
- Inflection (Gurley): Microsoft hired the staff instead of buying the company (Facebook paid 10% of its market cap for WhatsApp). The Series A was \~$250–260m at a $1bn pre-money valuation; "a yellow flag" that you can't pay any price for AI deals.
- LLM revenue: Gerstner "banned the use of the term ARR within Altimeter" for AI; it is "experimental run rate" (ERR). Gurley: developers rotate across "four or five" models; on a 0–100 scale, switching costs are "two". Gerstner: a large US bank that started on OpenAI's API is moving all AI to AWS open models next to its data: "the AI is finding its way back to the data".
