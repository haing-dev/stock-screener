### 241. Brad Gerstner & Bill Gurley — AI demand vs supply: the $2T compute build-out, power and LLM valuations (Bg2 Pod, Apr 2024)

*Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) and Bill Gurley (Benchmark) debate whether AI demand is real, what limits supply (power, nuclear regulation), and what private AI valuations imply, plus a market check.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g1C_5cbKd5E)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner thinks AI compute demand is "deeper and wider" than expected, driven by price elasticity, with power the next bottleneck. Gurley is more skeptical of LLM use cases and warns that entry prices already bake in optimism. Both expect most LLM companies to fail; Gerstner says "experimental" revenue deserves 1–2x, monopoly-like recurring revenue 10–20x.

- Gerstner: "it's different this time" has usually been true in tech, yet booms and busts still happen. Henry Blodget's 1998 Amazon $400 call looked "blasphemous"; Amazon is now "3,000 bucks". Gurley: rising enthusiasm raises "the entry price" on the marginal investment.
- Gerstner's Nvidia rule: he didn't sell when it went from 125 to 250–300 because "My numbers have gone up by more than the stock has appreciated." Bottom-up, training demand across small, medium and frontier models "is a lot bigger than we thought".
- Demand evidence (Gerstner): coding and call-center copilots became near-universal in enterprises within \~2 years. A CTO put a \~1M-token codebase in one prompt and it found six non-trivial bugs. Jensen Huang's chart: the 5-year data-center replacement cycle "is not going to be 1 trillion, it's going to be 2 trillion".
- Gurley's doubts: LLMs excel at structured language (coding, customer support, legal), but "LLM's not going to do much for you on a manufacturing floor". Companies that promised LLMs in every workflow "come off of it a little bit". Much new work bolts databases onto an LLM that only interprets.
- Gurley calls Sam Altman's smartphone comparison and energy-shortage warning "Jobsian level promotional techniques". Gerstner's counter is price elasticity (the Jevons paradox): flying cost fell to 1/10 since 1971 "and guess what? We got 10x as much travel."
- Venture risk (Gurley): "valuation is discounted future expectations", and private structures handle downward performance worse than public ones. A company that raised at $1.2bn with little revenue, burning \~$50m a year, struggles to raise again. Inflection and Stability AI were "early falls from grace".
- Capital intensity (Gerstner): getting 40,000 H100s to build your own LLM is "like, you know, digging a gold mine", with very different risk-reward from a software startup.
- Value capture (Gerstner): "There going to be 20 LLMs that go to zero." Off the frontier, models are "really fast depreciating assets" commoditized by open source, so "if you're building an index of LLMs, it may not be a very successful investment approach".
- Power (Gerstner, Apr 2024): one forecast has data centers going from \~4% of US power to 18–19% by 2030. China has "over 300 plants" built or in development; the US has zero under construction and \~13 proposed. A nuclear plant takes 7–10 years and a gas plant 2–3, so natural gas must fill the gap.
- Nuclear (Gurley): US costs are set by regulation, not technology; he wants a "zero-based regulatory rewrite". Independent producers make 25–30% of US power, and data centers buying from them could draw in regulators. He bought Constellation (CEG), a nuclear carve-out, "solely based on the bet that perceptions would shift on nuclear", before the AI angle.
- Market check (Gerstner, Apr 2024): the Fed raised its 2024 GDP forecast from 1.4% to 2.1% and core PCE from 2.4% to 2.6%; the 10-year is at 4.4% (low 3.5%). Hedge-fund net exposure to tech excluding the Mag 7 fell from 31% (late 2021) to \~19%. Software trades at 6.1x vs a 6.9x 10-year average; the Mag 6 trades above its 22x average, which he puts down to higher growth forecasts.
- Private AI multiples (a tweet, Apr 2024): OpenAI 18x revenue, Anthropic 20x, Glean 56x, Hugging Face 150x. Gurley: asking "is OpenAI a bargain" is a "relative valuation game, which is how bubbles are built". Gerstner: "I don't think a $20 a month fee for my consumer AI is going to be defensible"; Gurley: real memory could change that.
- Pricing (Gurley): a 60x price gap between a lab's top model and the next, and some say models are already "priced under cost". At late-stage entry multiples, "I'd be nervous."
- Gerstner's case for patience: 98–99% of search profits went to Google, which was clear only by 2004–05. Priceline bought at $1bn "was 120X in the public markets". But "ERR experimental run rate revenues" deserve "a one or a two x multiple", monopoly-like recurring revenue "a 10 or a 20 x". Gurley: LLM switching costs are "about as low as you could possibly imagine".
