### 242. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — The IPO debate, Tesla robotaxi vs Uber, and an earnings check (Bg2 Pod, Apr 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on why fewer companies go public, whether robotaxis threaten Uber, and the setup into Q1 2024 tech earnings.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D4BQCaVMjg4)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner thinks the shrinking public market is structural: deep private capital lets the best companies stay private. Both agree that staying private does not hide a weak profile. On robotaxis, both argue that owned fleets face a peak-vs-average capacity problem, so Uber is not under near-term financial threat.

- Setup (Gerstner, Apr 2024): US public companies fell from \~6,500 to \~4,000 in 20 years, while Jamie Dimon's letter counts PE-backed companies up "from 1900 to over 11,000".
- IPO bar (a head of capital markets quoted by Gerstner): $200–300 million of revenue, growth above peers (median \~50%), "attractive unit economics", a market cap above $2 billion and an IPO of at least $200–250 million. In the banker's view the window "is wide open. It's just a matter of price."
- Gurley: OpenTable went public on a $10 million quarter. Private is no hiding place: at $100 million of revenue and 10% growth, "it's not like you're better off" private, since you lack a liquidity premium and the cap table is more rigid. He puts the cost of being public at "probably two to five million a year".
- Profiles that cannot list (Gerstner): \~$200 million of revenue, 20% growth and marginal profitability. Software companies need net dollar retention "that shows that your customers love the product". Over a thousand private companies marked above $1 billion "still have to get worked through the system".
- Instacart (Gerstner): \~$40 billion private peak, IPO at \~$6 billion, and it "performed really well since the IPO". Boards need "the courage" to ignore old private marks.
- Why companies stay private (Gerstner): sovereign funds and growth equity now write multi-billion-dollar checks, and employees get liquidity through tenders. He calls this "permanent and this is structural" and warns of possible "adverse selection" into public markets.
- Gurley's pushback: stale private marks in big LP portfolios "be corrected all at once". Unlisted companies "rot in place" and "dilute 5 7 8% a year on new equity". A large private stake trades "by appointment", with a banker taking 5% or 10%.
- Gerstner's buckets: venture (<$100 million revenue, <$1 billion EV, high mortality), "quasi public" (a couple hundred million of revenue, well over $1 billion EV) and public. Growth is "the single greatest input to valuation", and "number two, right, is margin."
- Robotaxi economics (Gurley): owned fleets must be built to average or peak demand, and "you lose whatever your answer is". Cruise was losing \~$3 billion; Aurora lost $2 billion two years ago and a billion last year on virtually no revenue. A robotaxi must cost near the $25–30K of a used car; a Waymo vehicle "probably 150,000".
- Altimeter's estimates (Gerstner): \~$140,000 of gross bookings per Uber car falls to \~$100,000 when self-driving, and Uber's margin rises from \~7% of gross bookings to "12 or 14%" after new costs. Tesla (Apr 2024) has passed a billion FSD miles and adds over a billion a month by their estimate; he expects it to partner with Uber to fill off-peak demand.
- Insurance (Gerstner): car insurance rose 22% in the CPI because insurers lost 5–10% on underwriting in 2020–21 and waited on state regulators; they normally target "3, 4, 5%" with the rest from float. Gurley warns robotaxi litigation could make insurance costlier than for a human driver.
- Markets (Gerstner, Apr 2024): S&P and Nasdaq up \~6% year to date, small caps down \~3%. The 10-year rose from 3.9% to 4.7%, which alone implies growth multiples "down 10 to 15%". In 2023 stocks rose by being "less bad"; now "if you miss numbers, it'll be brutal to the downside".
- Margins (Gerstner): a large-company CEO plans to "grow our top line 50%" over 3 years while cutting personnel cost "by 10 to 20%". Consensus sees the hyperscalers' revenue reaccelerating but not data-infrastructure names (Datadog, Confluent, Elastic).
- Screen implication: for growth companies, revenue growth and margin drive value, and misses are punished when rates rise; growth that holds up and widening margins are the signals to track.
