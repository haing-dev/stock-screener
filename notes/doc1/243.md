### 243. Bill Gurley, Brad Gerstner & Sunny Madra — Llama 3, open models and where AI value accrues (Bg2 Pod, Apr 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) with Sunny Madra of Groq (AI inference cloud) on Meta's Llama 3, enterprise AI adoption, small on-device models, GPU spending and the IPO debate.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTwZzUApGkA)

**Takeaway:** Cheap, strong open models like Llama 3 commoditize the model layer, so value sits in enterprise relationships, distribution and the scale to fund frontier training. Gerstner adds that AI gains stick only with market leaders; in competitive industries they get competed away.

- Price collapse (Madra, Apr 2024): GPT-4 costs "$10 per million tokens input and $30 per million token output"; Llama 3 70B costs 60 and 70 cents. Within 48 hours Llama 3 became the most popular model on Groq, and developers swap out OpenAI without "any performance impact".
- How Meta did it (Madra): it trained Llama 3 on 15 trillion tokens, well past the "Chinchilla point" usually seen as diminishing returns. More training spend lowers inference cost, which matters because, per Amazon on Alexa (Gurley), inference was "way more of the cost" than training.
- Consumer pricing (Gurley): with Meta AI free, "I think the $20 concept is gone", a risk for OpenAI, \~50% of whose revenue is rumored to be consumer. He sees no consumer chatbot differentiated enough to win "80% market share".
- Gerstner's business-model test for closed models: either a billion consumers at \~$20 a month or ads, or enterprises that pay. New closed-model entrants will struggle to get venture funding; OpenAI is the exception with "100 million people using the product".
- Four ingredients to compete at the frontier (Gerstner): capital (leaders spend \~$40 billion a year, more than most sovereigns can), data, compute (including supply-chain relationships) and distribution (Meta touches 3 billion people). Throwing down on capex is "clearing the market of potential competitors".
- Gurley: Meta is not in cloud hosting, so open-sourcing Llama is "burning... the strategic ground" for rivals, like Google with Android. Older models depreciate fast: the "steepness of the price curve on a slightly older model" is unbelievable (Madra pulled Llama 2 from Groq Cloud).
- "the value is not in the model" (Gerstner); it is in the enterprise relationship and the services sold on top. Apr 2024 data: 64% of the Fortune 500 use Azure OpenAI, GitHub Copilot grew 35% quarter over quarter, and Satya Nadella said Azure was GPU-constrained.
- Enterprise adoption (Madra, citing an Andreessen Horowitz survey): companies are tripling AI spend this year, and 82% are on or moving to open source. Top uses are retrieval over company data (RAG), customer support and content generation. Many customers test in the cloud and then want it on-prem; a Barclays CIO survey (Gerstner) found 83% plan to repatriate some workloads.
- Who wins among AI buyers (Gerstner, crediting Gurley): in airlines the gains "get competed away", so look for a leader with "70 or 80% of a given market". Example: Lineage (cold storage, IPO pending Apr 2024), with 50 data analysts cutting spoilage and energy use, where savings go "to their bottom line". Gurley's counterpoint: the AI that improves a company's strategic position is "almost always a more traditional AI model" solving an optimization problem, not an LLM. Gerstner: generative AI still forced enterprises to organize their data.
- Small and fast models (Madra, Gerstner): a year ago a model needed a "$30,000-plus unit of compute"; now small models (Apple's OpenELM, Microsoft's Phi) run on phones. Humane's device was panned because inference had to go to the cloud, adding latency. Cheap inference is what let Meta offer AI to 3 billion users.
- Scaling (Gerstner): "I see little evidence that... the scaling has run its course". Musk, OpenAI and Zuckerberg are all building bigger clusters, and Yann LeCun sees two or three more generations before scaling stops paying.
- GPU cycle (Gerstner): every company that had reported had raised capex guidance. He stipulates "every supply shortage does ultimately result in a glut", but not this year. Meta fell \~10–11% on a $3–4 billion higher capex midpoint, after net income rose from $22 billion to $55 billion in two years as headcount fell from 85,000 to 69,000.
- IPOs and markets: Gurley says secondaries remove "the number one pressure" that pushed founders to list, and the "institutionalization of a belief in network effects" pours money into AI. Gerstner (Apr 2024): Azure AI "contributed 7% of growth" (\~$4 billion run rate); the largest tech firms benefit more than smaller ones.
- Screen implication: AI-driven margin gains are durable mainly for dominant market leaders; in fragmented industries expect them to be competed away.
