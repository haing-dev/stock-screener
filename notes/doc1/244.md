### 244. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — GPT-4o, Astra, AI disruption risk and China tariffs (Bg2 Pod, May 2024)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on OpenAI's GPT-4o and Google's Astra assistants, which businesses AI disrupts, Washington's AI plans, the May 2024 China tariffs and the earnings season.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHYoSBixdPI)

**Takeaway:** AI disruption is broad and fast, so the market should apply lower multiples to businesses whose future cash flows look less certain, starting with language and code. Gerstner cut net exposure (May 2024) as the Nasdaq hit a high while earnings outside the Magnificent Seven shrank and software guidance disappointed.

- GPT-4o (Gerstner): an end-to-end audio model: "It can listen, it can think, it can speak in one model". It is faster and more natural, but "Still didn't have memory, still didn't have actions". On benchmarks it "barely improved" in quality but "dramatically improved in terms of pricing", which suggests some plateauing.
- Gurley: OpenAI and Google are both tilting to the consumer, which means "six companies all running at the same prize". Voice suits only some apps; you don't want "four different hotel choices" read to you. His own phone did not match the demo's speed.
- Assets matter (Gurley): Google has "a ton of assets", meaning users, apps and data, while OpenAI and Anthropic have few. Gerstner thinks OpenAI's people and speed still push Google, whose assistant "definitely lagged OpenAI".
- Platform risk (Gurley): letting an ambitious app embed is "a bit of a parasite" risk for a platform, like AOL letting Google in. Meta fell from 40 to 18 after its IPO on fears Apple controlled mobile: "I bought it 40 went to 18".
- Business models (Gerstner): enterprise model pricing is plummeting. A large data company said OpenAI set the "pricing umbrella", so model serving is "a much lower margin business than the traditional software business". He sees consumer as the only route to payback on "tens of billions" of spend, and ChatGPT as "the Google verb of the moment of AI".
- Price cliff (Gurley): stepping back half a version saves "90% or 95% of the money", a "20X differential". Entrepreneurs design on the frontier model but "all back off to the affordable models" at runtime.
- Who it kills (Gerstner): Inflection, which raised a lot and built one of the largest H100 clusters, sold to Microsoft, and he asks "where is character in all of this?" Sam Altman warned that GPT wrappers should "go find a new line of work".
- Duolingo test (Gurley): the model taught him beginner Spanish with no coding for it ("That was all emergent"). Gerstner says Duolingo fell \~4–5% that day: with disruption at this scale, "the multiple you apply future cash flows just needs to be lower. Your margin of safety needs to be higher."
- Gurley cites Michael Mauboussin's competitive advantage period (CAP): how many years of cash flow a stock price embeds. "It's almost a moat measurement." Gerstner: that is why low-growth Coca-Cola can trade above a fast-growing tech company, as the market applies "a much higher discount" to whether tech cash flows last.
- Disruption list (Gerstner): vertical search sites like TripAdvisor "got eviscerated" when Google moved in. Altimeter's list of possibly disrupted companies "is as long as it's ever been". Business process outsourcing (BPO) and call centers are now exposed, and "the market will discount it before it actually happens".
- Gurley's calibration: language and code are "10 out of 10, high alert" because LLMs are built for language and code is structured language. For BPO "maybe the warning signal's at six".
- Washington (May 2024, Gurley): the Senate AI roadmap asks for $32 billion a year, vs NIH at "a little over 40 billion" and the NSF at nine. Gerstner: "Washington never drips anything." Both criticize the new China tariffs (25% steel and aluminum, 50% semiconductors, 100% EVs, 50% solar); Gurley calls them "highly inflationary" and bad for competitiveness.
- Markets (Gerstner, May 2024): S&P earnings excluding the Magnificent Seven shrank quarter over quarter. 98% of software companies hit guidance, but 54% guided below consensus, the worst since 2022. He cut net exposure, preferring confident AI compounders (Microsoft, Nvidia) at "21 times" for the Mag 6, and is wary of retail and other names not exposed to AI. Sizing is "a question of units of risk": with the Nasdaq at a high and risks rising, keep "a portion of your stack working" so you can act if markets fall: "maybe a little bit less makes sense".
- Screen implication: companies whose core product is language (tutoring, translation, call centers) can de-rate before results show damage; treat AI exposure as a reason to demand a bigger margin of safety.
