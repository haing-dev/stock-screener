### 245. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Grok 3, the consumer AI race, AI capex and DOGE austerity (BG2 Pod, Mar 2025)

*BG2 episode in which Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter, an OpenAI investor) assess Grok 3 and the race for AI consumers, then turn to AI capex, China and the market impact of tariffs and DOGE cuts.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGmOYNWiV1A)

**Takeaway:** Benchmarks are converging, so the AI race will be won on consumer usage, product and the capital to keep spending. Gerstner expects one winner to take 70–80% of consumers, and he is holding half his normal risk because tariffs and federal spending cuts withdraw liquidity.

- Gurley read Grok 3 as proof of a ceiling, not of pre-training headroom: the biggest cluster yet "slammed up against this the ceiling that's holding everyone in". Bigger clusters and more parameters "won't buy you much".
- Gerstner: the benchmarks matter less than usage. In the search wars many engines scored well, but value went to Google alone. He expects "70 or 80% share go to the winner" in consumer AI, not a 99% monopoly. Gurley adds that "coming in second place with 20% share" may still be a good place to be.
- OpenAI's numbers (Feb 2025, per Gerstner): 400 million weekly users, \~$11–12bn of expected revenue this year and an estimated 700–800 million monthly users. He calls \~1 billion monthly users a "magic number" for funnelling users into weekly use and then into paying.
- Google (Gerstner): public companies report Google organic clicks "down 20 to 40%" so far this year, as AI answers fill the results page. He says "SEO is dead", and 80% of his own Google use has moved to ChatGPT. Gurley: ChatGPT took informational searches first, not the commerce searches that earn most of the money.
- Gerstner: "Google's moat was not a technological moat"; it was distribution and mind share. Only an "orthogonal attack" from something 10–100x better could hurt it, and ChatGPT was that attack.
- Lock-in (Gurley lists four routes: memory, voice, a feature no one has, network effects). Gerstner: "if you get memory the switching costs explode", and conversion from free to paid should rise too.
- Losses vs capex (Gerstner): a leaked OpenAI forecast showed losses of \~$20bn a year in 2025 and 2026, but that mixes capex for future products with the cost of serving users. Microsoft spends \~80% of its free cash flow on capex, yet we measure its profit as net income. Gurley: an o1 Pro or deep-research query can cost "20 40 50x" a normal one.
- Resilience is liquidity (Gerstner): every tech wave had a "zone of disillusionment" when spending ran ahead of revenue. Google and Meta have "a printing press in the back room"; OpenAI and xAI must keep raising capital.
- Gurley on unit economics: today's model costs 20x yesterday's, so a model is "a fast depreciating asset the second you're off the frontier". Gerstner: excess capital kills cost discipline, unlike Tesla, where scarce capital forced Musk to make money on every car.
- China (both): Gurley calls it "remarkably naive" to think the US can keep China from frontier AI. Gerstner says the Biden diffusion rule risks a "Huawei level belt and Road" of AI chips abroad. Gurley: "if I owned Nvidia my number one concern" would be excessive regulation from Washington.
- Austerity (Gerstner, Feb 2025): tariff revenue could go from $56bn last year to \~$500bn, and DOGE could cut $500bn–$1trn of federal spending over a couple of years. That withdraws liquidity, so he holds "half as much as I would normally own" and expects a possible "10 to 15% draw down".
- Gerstner: companies with federal revenue face outright declines, not slower growth; one airline's government ticket sales were down 50% year to date. Gurley notes that venture investors had only recently got comfortable backing companies that sell to government.
- Screen implication: for companies with large federal-government revenue or heavy reliance on Google search traffic, check by hand for a structural (not cyclical) revenue decline.
