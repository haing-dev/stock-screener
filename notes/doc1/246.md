### 246. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Liberation Day tariffs, open-source AI, OpenAI's valuation and the CoreWeave IPO (BG2 Pod, Apr 2025)

*BG2 episode recorded right after the 2 Apr 2025 "Liberation Day" tariff announcement, with Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter, an investor in OpenAI, CoreWeave and ByteDance).* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b0DO0tP06eQ)

**Takeaway:** The tariffs came in at the high end and froze corporate planning, but Gerstner expects a deal-driven retreat and says fear is the time to buy. On AI, he defends OpenAI at \~20x forward revenue as the cheapest leader, while Gurley warns that giant raises postpone unit economics.

- Tariff math (Gerstner, Apr 2025): tariffs go from $77bn last year to \~$750bn headline, \~$600bn after exemptions, which include pharmaceuticals and semiconductors. China faces 54% (34% on top of 20%). He expects the plane to land at "three or 400 billion in tariffs" after deals.
- Gurley on why it hurts: moving a plant from Thailand to the US "might take three years", at a higher cost. Unclear, changing policy makes it "very hard to allocate capex", so firms pause hiring, which can spread to jobs and spending.
- Gerstner, with the Nasdaq "down peak to trough post Trump at almost 18%" and many names down 40–50%: "the best opportunities to buy something are when people are a little fearful." Altimeter is getting "a little bit more positive" on its favourite companies.
- Gurley on open source: China has backed it for over a decade because it neutralises IP-theft accusations. Big US firms use it as a defensive tool when behind: Google with Kubernetes against AWS and Android against Apple, and Meta with Llama.
- Gurley sees a "short window" to lead Western open models if Washington limits DeepSeek, which he hears has been forked over 1,500 times on Hugging Face. An OpenAI open-weight model is a "clever move", like Kubernetes: it undercuts rivals' API business and runs on others' servers.
- OpenAI round (Gerstner): \~$40bn led by SoftBank in two tranches (\~$10bn, then \~$30bn), at \~$260bn pre and \~$300bn post. "market leaders never look cheap". On \~$13bn of 2025 revenue and a \~$15–18bn exit run rate, that is \~20x, vs \~50x for Anthropic and \~80x for the merged X/xAI.
- Gurley's counter: headline numbers bigger than the tranches "smells of being promotional", and "unit economics get postponed" in a power-law race. OpenAI guides to a $5–7bn loss this year, and his loose estimate is $15–20 a year to serve each free user.
- Gerstner: in 2020 Uber was called a business that would never be profitable, and it will make \~$6bn of free cash flow this year. ChatGPT has 20 million paying subscribers and 500 million weekly users (Gurley: a 4% conversion rate), and growth is compute-limited.
- Gurley: Google is exposed because it maximised revenue per visitor using "LTV math versus transactional math". Booking.com may pay Google $50 for a booking that earns it \~$20, but behind an agent "the most you can share is 10 of the 20". Gerstner: even $10 a booking is upside for OpenAI but replaces \~$50 for Google.
- CoreWeave IPO (Gerstner, a large IPO buyer): it broke its offer price, falling to \~$37, then rose on a Google deal. Customers now include Microsoft, Meta, Google, OpenAI and Nvidia, easing the worry that it was "too dependent upon Microsoft".
- Gerstner's model: 4–5 year contracts are priced to pay back capex, opex and GPUs in three years. The fourth year is the margin, and consensus (\~25% EBIT margin) credits nothing after the contracts end. A100s (2020) are still in use, and OpenAI ran V100s (2017) for \~7 years.
- Gurley: CoreWeave leads with huge growth, but "the capex is invested so far ahead" of the product that the income statement can't show the unit economics. GPU value isn't binary, because "the earning power of that product drops over time", so "six years straight probably isn't the best fit". Incumbents have moved from four-year to six-year lives. Gerstner says CoreWeave doesn't need more than four, but it is "a highly levered business" that must delever.
- Gurley's riddle: Salesforce, Netflix, Square, Amazon, Palo Alto Networks, Facebook, Snap, Proofpoint, NetSuite and CoreWeave "all broke issue". Judging an IPO by whether it trades below its offer price is "such a wrong way to look at things".
- Screen implication: falling depreciation relative to gross PP&E can mean asset lives were stretched, which flatters earnings. Check useful-life changes by hand for capital-heavy tech (GPU clouds, hyperscalers).
