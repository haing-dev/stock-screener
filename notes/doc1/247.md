### 247. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — 2025 outlook: rich valuations, the AI capex boom, rates and DOGE (BG2 Pod, Jan 2025)

*BG2 start-of-year episode: Bill Gurley (Benchmark) questions Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on how his fund sets its public-market stance for 2025, covering AI capex, Nvidia and memory, rates and federal spending.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yPD1qEbeyac)

**Takeaway:** Big tech is not in a bubble if it hits its growth, but the entry price is higher, so Altimeter is less net long than in 2023–24. The big risk is the 10-year rate: without spending cuts to offset tax-cut stimulus, rates rising toward 5.25–5.5% would anchor stocks.

- Process (Gerstner): each December he reviews errors and omissions from the past year. He then forecasts where the world will be in December of the following year. He journals it, and each team member does the same independently before they compare.
- Valuations (Jan 2025): S&P 500 at \~23x earnings, vs \~22x at the Q4 2021 peak and 16x at the 2022 trough. Meta trades at \~23x and Google at \~21x. Nvidia has fallen from a 66x peak to 36x consensus, or "28 or or 30 times" Altimeter's estimates. Not a bubble, but "not as good a deal" as at the start of 2023 or 2024.
- Earnings (Gerstner): the big five (Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia, Amazon, Google) grew earnings 44% in 2024 while the other 495 S&P companies grew 2%. Consensus for 2025 has the big five at 21% and the rest jumping from 2% to 11%, led by healthcare (+4% to +20%), industrials (-4% to +16%) and materials (-10% to +17%). Tax cuts explain only \~30% of that, so a miss is the "Boogeyman".
- Positioning: Altimeter started 2023 \~95% net long (longs minus shorts) and 2024 \~80%, and is lower now because "the price of Entry" is higher. Q1 headwinds for mega caps are the strong dollar and capex "higher than people think".
- Capex: big-tech capex took "the vast majority of the incremental free cash flow". Gurley notes Meta and Microsoft are above 25% of revenue on capex and near 100% of free cash flow. Microsoft guides to \~$80bn this year, against \~$10bn of inference revenue that Gerstner calls the ROI.
- Gerstner: the bet can be offensive or defensive ("the prisoners dilemma"). In venture "the goal is the least amount of money in for the most amount of money out". After a decade of high returns on incremental capital, those "returns are compressing" in the short run.
- Gurley, citing Mike Mauboussin \[?\]: what matters is not that free cash flow is used up "but what's the return on the incremental investment". Gerstner: that is hard to model early in a phase shift. In 2023 all 26 Wall Street analysts on Nvidia missed the consensus forecast by 80%.
- Nvidia (held since $120 a share): data-center revenue went from $61bn in 2023 to an estimated \~$200bn in 2025. The \~$60bn rise from 2024 to 2025 roughly equals the hyperscalers' extra capex, and Jensen has \~40 "AI factories" as customers, so Gerstner thinks consensus implies a share loss he doesn't expect. Nvidia rose 140–150% in 2024 while Intel fell over 20%.
- Memory (SK Hynix, Micron): Gurley notes SK Hynix trades at \~6x forward earnings, the commodity pattern where "you buy it with the highest multiple and sell it with the lowest multiple". Gerstner argues high-bandwidth memory is now "secular not cyclical", so he can win on earnings and on a re-rating.
- Power names (Gerstner): rather than buy energy stocks as AI plays, he asks analysts "why is that better than Nvidia". They are "a derivative Nvidia", and Altimeter's "essentialism" means bigger bets on its best ideas.
- Rates (Jan 2025): the 10-year rose almost 100bp to \~4.7% even as inflation fell toward a \~2.2% consensus. The market fears \~$400bn a year of tax-cut stimulus without offsetting cuts. If the 10-year goes to "525 or 55" it is "an anchor" on stocks.
- Spending math: federal outlays were $4.4trn in 2019 and \~$6.7trn in 2024. Growing 2019 at 2.5% a year gives a \~$5trn baseline, a $1.7trn gap. Gurley adds that per-person healthcare costs grow faster than 2.5%, so that sector needs its own fix.
- Google (Gerstner): "the largest innovators dilemma" in Silicon Valley history, because it is nearly impossible to replace "a 99% incremental margin business" with an AI market it won't monopolise. Gurley's bull case: Gmail, Docs, Android and Gemini's local reviews form a strong AI bundle.
- Screen implication: for memory and other commodity-like chipmakers, a low P/E near peak earnings is the classic cyclical trap. Value them on cycle-average earnings unless the business has demonstrably become secular.
