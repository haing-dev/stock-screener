### 248. Michael Dell, Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Negative cash cycles, the AI talent war and compute demand (Bg2 Pod, Jul 2025)

*Michael Dell, founder, CEO and \~half owner of Dell Technologies, joins Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on the Invest America Act, Meta's AI hiring spree, AI productivity, Dell's AI server business and markets, recorded over the Fourth of July weekend.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X52BNWZrXSk)

**Takeaway:** Dell's lasting edge is working capital: six days of inventory and a negative cash conversion cycle made its return on capital "essentially infinite". On AI, Dell is "98% confident" the productivity gain is far bigger than the PC's, and says spending is below what a 10–20% gain would justify, with multiples "nowhere near" 2000 levels (Jul 2025).

- Gurley's 1990s Dell call: Michael Mauboussin taught him to look at ROIC. Dell's "cash flow relative to the earnings was really high and the ROIC was 10x anyone else in the business", so he initiated at strong buy. Dell went public at \~$150 million; Dell says the stock rose 130,000% with seven splits.
- Build-to-order beat inventory (Gurley): with component prices always falling, "we calculated they got a 200 basis point gross margin advantage" from fresher inventory. Dell: "a structural competitive advantage. Uh by the way, it still is."
- Dell: rivals held \~90 days of inventory in distributor and dealer queues; Dell had "six days of inventory" for about seven years in a row. Paying suppliers later than customers pay you gives a negative cash conversion cycle, still "typically around negative 50 days", so "you're return on capital is essentially infinite".
- AI talent war: Meta's \~$15 billion Scale deal and reported "75 to$100 million annual pay packages" (Gerstner). Gurley traces it to late-stage private money: labs burning "two, three, four, five billion dollars a year" were already paying \~$10 million a year. Zuckerberg views risk "as spending against a percentage of his market cap", and Gurley thinks "it may be the right math".
- Founder control as an edge (Gerstner): he went "all in" on Meta in 2022 because "it's founder controlled". Risking "1% of his company" to reboot around AI is "a very very rational economic decision", funded by "the world's biggest printing press". Gurley's rejoinder: "Apple and Google have the same exact printing press and chose not to do this." Dell expects a culture and fairness problem from pay gaps.
- Gerstner on the frontier labs: "if you're not bringing in 10, 20, 30, $40 billion of annual revenue, I don't think you can stay in this game". He thinks OpenAI and Anthropic have enough "escape velocity", but "it's not a long list".
- AI productivity (Dell): maybe "only 10%" of large companies have figured it out. Gains of 10% are "pretty easy", 20% reasonably common, with "sightings of 30% or 40%". On a $114 trillion economy (2025), a 10–20% gain would justify AI investment of "two to four trillion dollars per year", far above today.
- Incumbents must reinvent (Dell): big companies with data, brands and IP "will be destroyed by new companies that come in with a totally clean slate" if they don't move, within 3–5 years. Two years ago he told Dell's leaders a faster rival would arrive in five years, and "the only way we're going to prevent that is we're going to become that company".
- Dell's AI numbers (Jul 2025): server and networking grew 58% last year; Q1 AI orders were $12.1 billion vs \~$10 billion of AI server shipments in all of last year; backlog "a little over 14 billion". Nvidia's reference design "doesn't really work", so the value Dell adds is engineering, supply chain and financing for 100,000+ GPU clusters. It shipped the first GB300s to CoreWeave.
- Not 2000 yet (Dell): look at "what the multiples were on earnings and cash flow" then: "We're nowhere near that for the most part". Demand rides tokens, which "explodes" as use moves to reasoning, agents and multi-agent systems.
- Enterprise and edge (Dell): \~3,000 "Dell AI factories" delivered in a year, to firms that "bring the AI to their data not the data to the AI", often with smaller open-source models. Custom ASICs are "gaining a lot of share" at hyperscalers and model makers, a small number of large customers.
- Markets (Jul 2025, Gerstner): Nasdaq up 32% off its bottom two months earlier; Dell stock went from \~$72 to \~$120. The 10-year sat between 3.7% and 4.7% for two years, and 85% of S&P 500 companies beat. Dell bet markets are higher in 3–5 years as companies "compound their earnings on a double-digit basis". When markets dipped, Dell "bought back 22 million shares".
- Policy: Invest America (Gerstner) seeds $1,000 in the S&P 500 for children born after Jan 1, 2025; employers can add $2,500 a year pre-tax. Gerstner would rather let "deprecated chips out of Nvidia go compete against Huawei in China"; Dell's MTOPS story (a $399 PlayStation exceeded the export limit) shows crude controls backfire.
- Screen implication: Gurley's original Dell case is computable from filings: ROIC far above peers, cash flow above earnings, and inventory and payables days that give a negative cash conversion cycle.
