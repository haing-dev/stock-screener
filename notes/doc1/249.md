### 249. Bill Gurley & Brad Gerstner — Nvidia after GTC, Google–Wiz and the "negative gross margin AI" theory (Bg2 Pod, Mar 2025)

*Bill Gurley (Benchmark) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) on recession fears before the Apr 2 tariffs, Google's $32 billion Wiz deal, Nvidia's GTC, export controls and the unit economics of AI companies.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ymEtsnDif5M)

**Takeaway:** Gerstner sees Nvidia at \~20x next year's earnings as "not a demanding multiple" that should grow with earnings, and Google–Wiz at 30x revenue as proof the M&A window is reopening. Gurley warns that many AI companies may be selling compute below cost, so headline revenue overstates real economics until a reset.

- Macro (Mar 2025, Gerstner): the Nasdaq fell \~10% after their early-February episode. The Fed cut its 2025 GDP forecast from 2.1% to 1.7%, but Bank of America's Brian Moynihan saw consumer spending "up 6% year-over-year", and managers sit "all in their bottom quartile of exposures". The economy "will easily absorb" tariff revenue rising from \~$65 billion to \~$150 billion, but a trillion "pushes us into a recession". Gurley backs comparative advantage: "I don't think there's anybody that wants to build a $40 microwave in America."
- Google–Wiz (Gerstner): $32 billion all cash for a cloud-security firm with "something like a billion dollars in in ARR" this year, "just over 30 times forward revenue", while Google trades at \~5x. Wiz was founded in 2020. He reads it as a litmus test: "the M&A market is back".
- Gurley's read on the deal: EU pressure likely rules out anything near search, AI or YouTube, so Google looked where it lacks dominant share: enterprise cloud, and security as a differentiator against AWS and Microsoft. The $3.2 billion breakup fee shows approval is not easy.
- Gerstner's loss: Altimeter backed rival Lacework with Sutter Hill, which "raised a lot of money, too much money I would argue" and lacked product-market fit relative to Wiz. Lesson: "the winners tend to to walk away with uh, you know, a disproportionate amount of the prize."
- Gurley: "repeat entrepreneurs in the enterprise space are a golden ticket", because go-to-market lessons repeat and each platform shift (mainframe to cloud) lets a new entrant in. Workday, Bret Taylor and Frank Slootman are his examples.
- Valuation gap (Gerstner): public "great businesses that are growing, you know, 10, 20, 30%, that are trading at six or seven times revenue" vs Wiz at over 30x leave "ample room for buyers and sellers to meet in the middle". FTC chair Andrew Ferguson's line, "go to court or get out of the way", suggests faster rulings.
- Nvidia (Mar 2025, Gerstner): stock \~$115 vs a \~$150 peak, at \~20x next year's and \~24x this year's consensus earnings, below the S&P; Costco trades at 50x. Jensen Huang called the DeepSeek scare "a profound and deep misunderstanding" and said needed compute is 100x what was thought a year ago. He now sees data-center spend reaching "a trillion dollars per year by 2028".
- Gerstner's framework: Nvidia is a \~$2.7 trillion company "that's going to grow at whatever the rate of its earnings growth is"; "You've seen all the multiple compression you're likely to see." Consensus has revenue topping out at \~$250 billion, which implies share loss he doubts. He holds a smaller position after cutting risk early in 2025.
- Gurley's doubts: the stock didn't move on GTC, so the news was priced in; pre-training scaling has "slowed materially"; and token counts are "an ultra promotional way to talk about things" (he discloses Benchmark backs Cerebras). Huang as "chief revenue destroyer" clashes with customers who moved "from a four-year depreciation to a six-year depreciation" on servers. Gerstner: software upgrades keep Hoppers improving, so longer lives hold.
- Custom chips: Huang said most custom ASICs "never scale into any commercial production". Gerstner reads AWS's Trainium price cuts as weakness: "You usually don't cut costs on things, right, when when when things are going great". Gurley calls government action "the number one risk on the stock"; Gerstner says the Biden diffusion rule will "lead to a Huawei belt and road", as US limits once did in telecom gear.
- Consumer AI (Gerstner): these are "win or take most markets"; a challenger must be "10x better than chat GPT". DeepSeek and Grok spiked to #2 in the App Store and faded, while ChatGPT stayed #1 with 400 million+ weekly users and is "massively supply constrained". Gurley agrees but notes OpenAI faces "four or five of the most powerful companies in the world".
- Contribution margin (Gerstner): Anthropic sells much of its API through AWS and pays a large fee, leaving "very little or no contribution margin", while OpenAI sells direct. "if you're not meaningfully positive at contribution margin then you have no chance of covering the cost uh on a fully burdened P&L". Ask of any revenue figure "who do they have to pay to get that revenue". Google raised under $50 million before its IPO, at near-zero marginal cost.
- Gurley's "negative gross margin AI theory": credits, VC money and a winner-take-all mindset push startups to price to market, not cost. If each layer (hyperscaler, model maker, app) sells below cost, the end user is subsidized and "you're triple counting revenues". Expect "substantial resets as people get in touch with unit economics". Falling model prices tempt founders to accept negative margins; the analogy is e-commerce "selling dollars for 85 cents", not Google.
- Screen implication: for AI-exposed names check gross margin and its trend, revenue sold through partners, and whether D&A has fallen relative to equipment as useful lives are extended.
