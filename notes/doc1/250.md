### 250. Jensen Huang, with Brad Gerstner & Clark Tang — The OpenAI deal, a $5 trillion capex case and why free ASICs lose (Bg2 Pod, Sep 2025)

*Nvidia's founder and CEO, interviewed by Brad Gerstner and Clark Tang of Altimeter days after Nvidia agreed to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI, on AI demand, circular-revenue fears, Nvidia's moat, China and immigration.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pE6sw_E9Gh0)

**Takeaway:** Huang argues demand is driven by two compounding exponentials (more users, and far more compute per use from reasoning), on top of a refresh of all general-purpose computing. On his math, world AI capex could reach \~$5 trillion a year against \~$400 billion today, and a glut is "extremely low" probability until that shift is done (Sep 2025).

- The OpenAI deal (Gerstner): Nvidia will invest \~$100 billion over time as OpenAI builds 10 gigawatts, which "could be upwards of 400 billion in revenue to Nvidia". Huang says OpenAI is "likely going to be the next multi- trillion dollar hypers scale company"; investing before it gets there is among "the smartest investments we can possibly imagine". His regret: "we were so poor, we didn't invest enough".
- The new deal adds to OpenAI's builds with Azure, Oracle (OCI; "five, six, seven gigawatts") and CoreWeave. It helps OpenAI self-build its own data centers, with the direct relationship that xAI, Meta, Google and Microsoft already have.
- Wall Street vs the builders (Gerstner): the 25 sell-side analysts' consensus has Nvidia's growth flatlining at "8% growth 2027 through 2030". Huang: "we have no trouble beating the numbers on a regular basis".
- Huang's demand case: (1) general-purpose computing is over and the installed base will be refreshed as accelerated computing; (2) hyperscale recommenders, search and shopping are moving from CPUs to GPUs, "hundreds of billions of dollars"; (3) AI augments human intelligence, \~55–65% of world GDP or "$50 trillion".
- The math: a $10,000 AI that makes a $100,000 employee two or three times more productive is an easy buy. If $10 trillion of GDP is augmented and tokens earn 50% gross margins, "$5 trillion" a year of capex "seems to make sense", vs \~$400 billion today, a "four to 5x increase". Alibaba plans 10x more data-center power by 2030, and "Nvidia's revenue is almost correlated to power."
- Glut risk: Gerstner's skeptic case needs AI revenue to go from \~$100 billion (2026, excluding Meta and recommenders) to a trillion by 2030. Huang: "we're already there", since hyperscalers' existing revenue is now AI-driven. The chance of a glut is "extremely low" until all general-purpose computing and recommenders convert, which "will take a few years".
- Nvidia builds to orders, not ahead: "we're at the end of the supply chain, right? And so, we respond to demand". The supply chain is geared so "If we need to double we'll double." Customers' forecasts have "always" been too low, so Nvidia is in "scramble mode", and "everyone has now concluded that they dramatically underbuilt".
- Circular-revenue charge (Cisco/Nortel): OpenAI's \~$400 billion build must be funded by its revenue, equity and debt, and "smart investors and smart lenders will consider all of these factors". The equity stake is "not tied to anything"; Gerstner adds OpenAI has no obligation to use Nvidia chips.
- Annual cadence: with Moore's law "dead" (transistors and power cost about the same each year), only "extreme code design" of chip, system, network and software keeps token costs falling. Kepler to Hopper was \~100,000x in 10 years; Blackwell over Hopper "30x in one year". Spectrum-X is "the fastest growing Ethernet business in the world".
- Moat: "there's just more competition than ever before, but it's harder than ever before", as rising wafer costs demand co-design across "six seven eight chips a year". Scale is the barrier: "what customer would place a $50 billion PO on an architecture" or start $50 billion of wafers on an unproven architecture? Suppliers pre-build "several hundred billion dollars at a time" only because Nvidia's is proven.
- ASICs: Google's TPU succeeded through "foresight"; "You're supposed to take 100% of a tiny company, a tiny industry", not a few percent of a huge one. ASIC vendors charge "50 60 points of margin", so large programs move to customer-owned tooling, as Apple did. Power-limited buyers want the best tokens per watt: against Nvidia's "75 points" gross margin vs "50 to 65 points" for others, a 15% discount can't offset Blackwell's 30x, so "even if they gave it to you for free" you'd choose Nvidia.
- Valuation (Gerstner): the moat is growing, yet Nvidia's "multiple is much lower than most of those other people", which he puts down to the "law of large numbers" at $4.5 trillion. Huang: "Nvidia will likely be the first 10 trillion dollar company."
- China: Gerstner says Nvidia once had \~95% share there, and being forced out handed Huawei "monopoly profits". Huang says China is nanoseconds, not years, behind; "our guidance includes no China", and anyone who thinks the market unimportant "has their head deep in the sand". On the $100,000 H-1B fee, "it's a great start" but "sets the bar a little too high". Gerstner heard Chinese AI graduates coming to the US fell from 90% to "10 or 15%".
- AI and jobs at Nvidia: "100% coverage" of engineers and chip designers with AI, and "we're hiring more people" because productivity created more ideas. "intelligence is not a zero sum game." Advice to CEOs: the exponential train is impossible to time, so "Just get on it while it's going kind of slowly".
