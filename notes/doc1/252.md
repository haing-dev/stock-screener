### 252. Brad Gerstner, Bill Gurley & Rene Haas — Stargate's real equity check, DeepSeek and AI margin expansion (Bg2 Pod, Jan 2025)

*BG2 episode recorded the day after the White House Stargate announcement: Gerstner (Altimeter) and Gurley (Benchmark), with a cameo from Arm CEO Rene Haas. They also cover executive orders, DOGE, TikTok and public valuations.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYzYOPkxLis)

**Takeaway:** The $500bn Stargate headline does not need $500bn on day one. Gerstner's bottoms-up model needs only \~$78bn of equity through 2028, with debt doing the rest. That leverage is the risk Gurley flags if capex outruns demand. AI is "a sport of Kings": chips and power stay scarce, and subscale players get folded in.

- The announcement (Jan 2025): a new company plans to invest $500bn over four years, starting with $100bn. SoftBank has financial responsibility and OpenAI operational responsibility. Gurley: that is \~$125bn a year, against Microsoft's \~$80bn, the highest now.
- Gerstner's model: \~250,000 GPUs in 2025 is \~$13bn of capex. At 25/75 or 30/70 equity to debt, that needs only \~$3bn of equity. 2 million GPUs in 2026 is \~$100bn of capital (70–80% GPUs), \~$25bn more equity and \~2.7 gigawatts of power.
- Through 2028 that adds up to \~$78bn of equity and \~7.5 GW in Abilene. "Elon is 100% right nobody has $500 billion", but nobody needs it on day one. Dylan Patel's (SemiAnalysis) estimate is \~$100bn spent by end-2027 on \~800,000 GPUs.
- Gurley's caution: that assumes \~3:1 debt to equity, like CoreWeave, while Equinix has run closer to 1:1. Capex is slow to bring on and "could overrun demand", and "if you have a three to one you know debt to equity ratio that could be a real painful you know downside".
- Gurley on motives: Sam Altman wants his own compute (xAI's own infrastructure is a competitive edge), Microsoft may not have wanted to build that big, Masa joins every big wave, and Oracle gets to stand next to the hyperscalers.
- Demand (hosts): the industry has been "chip constrained for two years". 2 million GPUs is about a third of Nvidia's expected \~6 million this year. Citing Dylan, buyers are "making three three-year bets", not 12-month ones.
- Competition (hosts): Google, Microsoft and Meta must spend more to secure GPUs. Amazon "has never been that aggressive with capex", and Anthropic has "less than a billion in Revenue". Gerstner: "if you're subscale on any level I think those players ultimately have to get folded".
- Rene Haas (Arm; Masa owns \~90%): every AI data center "needs a base CPU", and Arm supplies it (Grace in GB200). Bottlenecks: permitting and regulation, TSMC leading-edge fab capacity ("the only game on town"), HBM memory, multi-site training and cabling labor.
- Haas has no good precedent for Stargate's structure; the closest is GlobalFoundries and Mubadala. Gurley still doubts it: you can't put 3:1 debt on an entity and not expect it to stand alone and create equity value.
- DeepSeek (Gurley): distillation packs the same power into a smaller model. That means either "commoditization of the foundational models" or "so much more performance per token price" that the market grows. He calls US export controls "futile": "constraints Drive creativity".
- Policy (Gerstner): the Commerce AI diffusion rule was already published, so the regulation freeze does not cover it and Commerce must repeal it. State-level AI bills (\~25 states) may meet a federal preemption push. Gurley rates DOGE "high impact still low probability".
- Markets (Jan 2025, Gerstner): Druckenmiller sees the most pro-business administration of his lifetime after the most anti-business one, but says things are "priced reasonably high". Gerstner adds that the S&P ex-Mag 7 needs a big earnings acceleration on already high multiples. Altimeter would cut exposure if rates went to "five or five and a half percent".
- Gerstner's margin thesis: one internet CEO said "we've doubled our revenues and our costs keep coming down because of AI". The next 5 years are "a golden moment" of margin expansion for "Market leaders who can hold on to that revenue".
- Netflix was up \~$40bn in market cap on its quarter (Jan 2025). It raised guidance despite the FX headwind Gerstner expected (2–3% for most internet companies), so the FX-adjusted raise was bigger than expected.
