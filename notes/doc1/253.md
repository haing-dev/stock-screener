### 253. Brad Gerstner, Gavin Baker, Andrew Fox & Clark Tang — The SpaceX IPO, frontier vs open-source tokens and the AI capex math (Bg2 Pod, Jun 2026)

*Gerstner (Altimeter) hosts Gavin Baker and Andrew Fox of Atreides and his partner Clark Tang two days before the SpaceX IPO. They cover SpaceX's levers, new frontier models, GPUs vs ASICs, AI capex and a market check.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tx9jT2c6e3U)

**Takeaway:** They break the SpaceX IPO ($135/share, $1.77tn) into separate bets: Starlink, terrestrial AI compute, the model, and a call option on orbital compute. Each looks doable on its own. In AI, revenue is accruing to frontier models and compute is monetizing at rising rates per gigawatt, so they say the \~$1.5tn of 2027 capex "maths". Altimeter has still cut exposure to "medium small" after a steep run.

- SpaceX IPO setup (Gerstner, Jun 2026): banks forecast $160bn of revenue in 2028 vs \~$18bn last year. Bears note few companies "8x their revenue over 3 to 4 years". His pitch: "a must buy, a must own, a set it and forget it" for institutions.
- Baker's two levers: how fast SpaceX brings on terrestrial data centers (Elon did one in 122 days, and "Speed is literally cost") and its coding model. Citing Tang's analysis, xAI's Google compute deal earns more operating profit per gigawatt than Anthropic, Meta, Google or OpenAI.
- Tang's cross-check: the leaked $160bn implies \~$14bn per gigawatt per year for the AI business. SpaceX just signed Anthropic at 22–23 and Google at 50. The numbers work terrestrially "before you even have to take the leap of faith" on orbital data centers.
- Fox on the core business: launch is "the crown jewel", and rapid reusability is the key variable. SpaceX aims to fly both Starship stages 30–50 times before a refit. Cadence goes from \~160–165 launches last year to the high hundreds, then thousands.
- Starlink: under 1% of global households. Banks have connectivity revenue going from \~$10bn to $50bn by 2028, which Baker calls "0.3% penetration of the global telecom market". His formula: "better faster, cheaper has been a winning formula".
- Orbital math (Fox): rapid two-stage reuse cuts launch from \~$1,500/kg (Falcon) to \~$250/kg, and one Starship launch carries \~5 MW of compute. That is \~$5bn of capex per gigawatt in space vs \~$20–25bn per gigawatt for the non-chip build on the ground. Reliability is the open risk: "GPUs melt and lasers fail".
- Baker: data centers are not commodities, because Elon designed them from first principles. Tang: xAI secured "maybe up to 20%" of early Vera Rubin capacity and can resell spare compute "at actually you know best in class margins and payback periods".
- Gerstner's AWS analogy: in 2009–10 investors hated AWS "Because he was consuming all that free cash flow", while Bezos "was digging the biggest gold mine". Altimeter's projection had Cursor (\~700–800 people) exiting the year at up to $10bn of revenue, and the model is the least-discussed upside.
- IPO mechanics (Jun 2026): the average max post-IPO drawdown across \~20 big IPOs (Facebook, Alibaba, Shopify and others) is "over 50%". Altimeter sets a base position and sizes around it. Baker: Elon owns \~50% and is locked up 365 days, and price/sales fell from "100 times trailing" to 39x after the new deals.
- Frontier vs open source: Gerstner says the evidence six months in is that "frontier tokens are capturing the vast majority of all the revenues". Baker: "two things can be true", with open source likely carrying most tokens via routing (Harvey). Another panelist adds that strong open source "may be very bearish for the frontier models" but is "really bullish for compute and hardware".
- Nvidia (Baker): it could become "the world's dominant provider of open source AI" and a top cloud provider if customers keep competing with it. Tang: Nvidia has held share against ASICs. In a watt-constrained world, more "tokens per watt" is "literally revenue", so the panel expects Nvidia to beat its on-paper \~30% (10 of 27 GW) of OpenAI's commitments.
- Capex math (Gerstner): Mag 7 free cash flow is down \~80% from a few years ago. Morgan Stanley moved 2027 capex from $950bn to $1.1tn, and he thinks it is closer to $1.5tn against \~$300bn of AI-lab revenue. The guests are more bullish: gross margins are probably 60–70%, and inference revenue ends this year "well over 200 billion". Training is \~35% of spend ("increasingly less than half", per Fox) and earns no revenue yet.
- Pricing has inflated, not deflated: Tang says monetization per gigawatt rose from \~$20bn at the start of the year to "30 to even pushing 40". Gerstner: "you want to own asset-heavy businesses in inflationary environments". He calls the capex rational because millions of businesses and consumers are independently choosing to pay.
- Market check (Jun 2026): semis doubled and tripled while internet is down 16% and software down 8% on the year, with CPI back above 4%. Altimeter dialed back from large to "medium small". Baker: the hunt for "the next bottleneck" is over ("That game is over"). Gerstner: AI usage has been "seasonal for the last three summers".
