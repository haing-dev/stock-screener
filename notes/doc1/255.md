### 255. Albert Bourla — Pfizer: spending the COVID windfall, fixing R&D returns, and China at "half the cost and three times the speed" (NBIM, May 2026)

*Chairman and CEO of Pfizer (33 years at the company, CEO since 2019); interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ymR4wPmF7o)

**Takeaway:** Pfizer put its COVID windfall into \~$80bn of acquisitions rather than buybacks, and it is fixing R&D that had strong success rates but "mediocre" dollar returns. Bourla's biggest worry is China, which develops early-stage drugs at "half the cost and three times the speed"; he gives Western pharma five years to transform with AI.

- Focus: Pfizer sold consumer health because a company should be "very good at few things" and leave diversifying to investors. Science, consumer goods and off-patent generics each need a different culture.
- COVID: the vaccine came in 8 months because a crisis aligned government, staff and hospitals. Regulators and governments have since gone back to old habits: "you need common enemy to motivate people".
- The windfall: the options were dividends, buybacks ("our shares were at 50") or investing. He chose "almost 80 plus billion dollars in acquisitions", three of them 80% of the total cost, and "still the jury's out", especially on Seagen.
- The cliff: COVID revenue was $56 billion in 2022; "Now it is six" (May 2026). The stock went from best to worst performer, and he wondered "if I'm a failure just months after I was the hero of the world". He says 2024 and 2025 were "exceptional years" of revenue growth, beating analysts and cutting costs.
- Seagen (more than $40bn, per Tangen) was a bet on antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), and Seagen was the only player with four products on the market and 13 in the clinic. In ADCs the antibody is the GPS and the drug the warhead. If the technology delivers it will be "one of the most successful acquisitions"; if not, a "modest return", but "I don't think we will lose money".
- Obesity: liver toxicity killed two promising Pfizer assets (otherwise it would be first with an oral drug), so it bought Metsera, outbidding Novo Nordisk. It was "85, 90%" sure of the weekly drug; Phase 2 data after the deal confirmed a monthly product, while Lilly's and Novo's "will be weekly".
- R&D scorecard: almost two in 10 Pfizer candidates succeed versus about one in 10 for the industry. Phase 2 success rose from 15–20% to "50 plus" (industry \~25–30%), and regulatory success is "almost at 95%". But measured by dollars in versus dollars out, "we were mediocre", because it "miscalculated the commercial potential" of what it developed, with silos between R&D and commercial.
- The fix: a capability gap takes a "5 6 7 years journey", but wrong focus is a culture, governance and leadership problem you can change "within months". He appointed new R&D heads chosen for focus and merged early and late development end-to-end to fix handovers.
- AI: Paxlovid was "the first machine learning molecule" with such impact. Models "can do way more than what we are using it for"; the barrier is inertia and fear, so Pfizer pushes "AI literacy" (3,000–4,000 trained, certifications coming). The center supplies data, compute and cloud, while units own use cases and must deliver. He expects very different standards of care within 5 years, creating "new winners and new losers".
- China (May 2026): its five-year plan targets global pharma players by 2035–36. In 2025, eight of the top 10 institutions in the Nature Index were Chinese and "only one was US". A competent regulator and strong, fast IP enforcement back it, and "for every one thing that we have they have 10 companies working on it".
- He expects China to be better than the West at early-stage research "within two years". Licensing Chinese molecules (e.g. a deal with 3SBio) "is not a long-term strategy", because China will take drugs to market itself as it did with electric vehicles, solar panels and batteries. Western pharma has "five years to do it".
- Policy: the US should stop putting "80% of their effort" into slowing China and work on beating it. The drug-pricing debate "drove 2025 to be the lowest multiple in the history of the industry and the lowest available fund for biotech".
- Leadership: since 2019, every Pfizer manager has been scored "every 6 months" by reports and peers. His weak spot is being too passionate, which can shut people down, so he calls "a round" of views. He ranks structure below strategy, strategy below leadership, and leadership below culture: "culture eats strategy for lunch".
- Optimism: his mother survived a Holocaust firing squad and told him "Don't tell me you can't do it. I survived the firing squad." No one follows a pessimist: "pessimists are usually right, but there is nothing great that has happened in humanity without an optimist behind it".
