### 256. Thomas Schinecker — Highlights: the future of cancer treatment, Europe's slipping trials and the pricing bargain (NBIM, Nov 2024)

*Short cut of Nicolai Tangen's "In Good Company" interview with Roche CEO Thomas Schinecker.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dz7DcmOnX44)

**Takeaway:** Cheaper genome sequencing, antibodies and immunotherapy have transformed cancer care, but each new drug is a 13-year, 1-in-10 bet that needs about 10 years of pricing to pay back. Europe is losing trials to the US and China.

- Cancer milestones: sequencing a genome took more than 10 years and cost billions in 2003; now it takes days and costs "hundreds of Swiss francs or dollars". Antibodies and immunotherapy followed, and a "personalized cancer vaccine" is next.
- Launch math: a drug takes \~13 years and only "one out of 10" makes it, so Roche's record of "two new medicines every year" for 10–15 years is "quite a feat"; it aims for 20 this decade. A brain-shuttle protein attached to an antibody could bring an Alzheimer's drug "by 28, 29 timeframe".
- Europe: Tangen notes Europe's share of clinical trials "has halved over the last 10 years". The US offers startups better venture capital; Europe must cut the bureaucracy that turns trial approval into "a year of lost time".
- China: Roche has in-licensed Chinese molecules and "the science is great". The US still leads, "but China is catching up very quickly".
- Pricing: a drug has "around 10 years", shortening in some places, to earn back the nine failures behind it; 44 Roche medicines are on the "WHO list of essential medicines".
