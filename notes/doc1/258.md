### 258. Vasant Narasimhan — Novartis: value from focus, how the R&D board kills projects, and why pharma is leaving Europe (NBIM, Jun 2025)

*CEO of Novartis (in his eighth year; previously head of development), a physician who worked on malaria and TB in developing countries; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_z4Jow0c7A)

**Takeaway:** Shedding its conglomerate parts left a 235 billion pure play plus almost 100 billion of spun-off companies, which Narasimhan counts as value unlocked by focus; the pure play relies on innovation, not diversification, to get through patent cliffs. Narasimhan warns that European price controls are pushing launches and plants to the US and China, and that China is now a source of novel drugs and the world's fastest trial recruiter.

- Focus: Novartis was "a sprawling conglomerate" (consumer health, generics, Alcon eye care). It took five to six years to exit consumer health, spin off Alcon and Sandoz and sell its Roche stake. Market cap is 235 billion, and the spun-off companies add "almost 100 billion": "we really unlocked a lot of value by focusing".
- The diversification myth: stable side businesses were meant to offset patent cliffs, but they distracted management and misallocated capital. Novartis got through its major patent expiries "because our innovation engine actually delivered the next wave of breakthroughs". "Play to win and not play not to lose."
- R&D design: four therapeutic areas (oncology, cardiovascular, neuroscience, immunology) plus platform leadership: "world leader" in radioligand therapy and in cell and gene therapy, and one of the leaders in RNA therapeutics. A strategy and growth unit run by a former Bernstein analyst challenges even early researchers on whether a project will "move the needle".
- The Innovation Management Board, which he has chaired for 13 years, is "the lifeblood meeting of the company". About eight core members meet for four- to six-hour sessions, assume the pre-read is read, hear a three-minute summary and a slide of reasons for and against, and use a portfolio analytics group. Real debate replaces rubber stamping, and killing projects takes being "intellectually honest": "most of our projects ultimately fail".
- Science bets: earlier intervention (e.g. early prostate cancer), cell therapy that can "reset the immune system" and turn back autoimmune disease by "15 or 20 years", RNA drugs taken "every six months or maybe once a year" instead of daily, and radioligands that aim radiation at tumors.
- AI: its most lasting impact will be in R&D. Novartis is "one of their two partners" at Isomorphic Labs (Google DeepMind), with "over six different projects" asking whether AI can "drug the undruggable". Palantir (since 2017) built its "data 42" data lake, and Amazon automated its US patient services. Lesson: "We chased far too many AI and data science projects"; pick use cases and partners.
- Europe (Jun 2025): governments have "commoditized the innovation" through budget caps and generics propped up at "artificially elevated prices". With a population slightly larger than the US, Europe's market is "half or even less than half" of it and not growing, while China is the second-largest market and growing double digits.
- The drift: "30 to 40% of new drugs are not launched in Europe". Novartis had four plants in the UK, and "We have zero today". He expects a slow slide to a crisis, as happened to Japan's pharma sector.
- His three fixes: a Europe-wide list price adjusted by GDP per capita; no caps on sector growth (in Italy "our entire growth in Italy is paid back to Italy at the end of the year"); and the US model of high rewards for innovation with "generics prices that are the lowest in the world". The EU's new data-protection proposal is still "worse than where we were 3 years ago".
- US pressure: the US wants manufacturing to move, companies have made "$250 billion of announcements" of US investment, and the Trump administration is pushing international reference pricing. Novartis is building "manufacturing capacity in the US for the US", and likewise in China, and making its clinical trials more flexible.
- China: since 2014 it modernized its regulator, raised manufacturing standards, built a national reimbursement list (NRDL) and made end-of-life drugs very cheap. After a boom and a retrenchment, Novartis is "no longer seeing fast follower alone" from China but novel targets, with trial start and recruitment "the fastest in the world" and approvals nearly as fast as the US. China-origin drugs now win US approval, and big pharma scouts Shanghai alongside Boston and San Diego.
- Trial data risk: if the US and China stopped accepting each other's clinical data, it would be "hugely damaging". The US provides "about 30% of most clinical trial recruitment" and Europe has slowed, so Asia is needed to finish trials on time.
- Leadership: by year eight he sees the CEO as "chief energy officer"; leadership is "about the possibilities you create". He hit a wall as a "know-it-all" leader, and in R&D "one out of six, seven, eight projects succeed". His routine is mindset, movement, nutrition and recovery, because "so much of leadership comes down to managing your nervous system".
- Culture: inspired, curious and "unbossed" (purpose, growth, autonomy) means "high support, high challenge", not anarchy. He credits it with putting Novartis at the "top of the league tables on TSR and growth" for three to four years. Advice: build range, it's "not a race", and find sponsors.
