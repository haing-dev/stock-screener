### 262. Geoffrey Garrett — Leading as a human, delegating with guardrails, and the cost of shrinking trade (NBIM, Mar 2025)

*Dean of USC's Marshall School of Business and former dean of Wharton, with earlier roles at Oxford, Stanford and Yale; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's In Good Company in late March 2025.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nyMPFdOwTFE)

**Takeaway:** Leadership now runs "inside out": know yourself, listen, delegate with guardrails and win hearts and minds rather than command. As a "classic liberal" economist, Garrett sees shrinking global markets as a big net negative, and the rise of Chinese EVs as a transformative force for the world economy.

- Agility comes from two C's: curiosity beyond technical skills, and context (culture, history, language). "we used to think about leaders as heroes and now we need to think about leaders as humans."
- Crises crowd out strategy: COVID showed him he is better at strategy than "instantaneous operational challenges", and that in a crisis operations push strategy aside. Know your weak spots and compensate for them.
- New role: ignore books that give you "100 days to change everything"; be an active listener and meet people where they work. His "iron law": meetings default to the senior person's office, so leaders never get out and see the place.
- Power distorts the signal: staff hear a boss's question as a leading question and take any "thought bubble" seriously. Be "humbly confident" and don't show you're the smartest in the room, or "the collective intelligence doesn't increase".
- Delegate with guardrails: micromanaging piles work on your desk and stops people growing, so give room to make mistakes that won't badly damage the person or the organization. Tangen (NBIM: 700 people, 9,000 holdings) calls micromanaging "totally unbelievably destructive".
- Hiring: test scores are only the first filter; essays and interviews show the whole person, because employers want people who can do the job on day one and lead by year five or 10. He looks for "technical fluency plus human leadership" (data science now rivals finance at Wharton and Marshall), and sees the MBA as a tool for "the career pivot" rather than a career accelerator.
- Trade (Mar 2025): shrinking global markets are "a big net negative"; the US tariff debate shows "there's a real cost to reshoring stuff which is prices go higher", and "globalization reduced prices and reduced interest rates in America for decades". Economic ties also make war less likely (Kant).
- EVs as a window on the world economy: Chinese EVs, "BYD above all but maybe Xiaomi", are "a transformative force for the global economy". Trump's instinct is tariffs high enough to force US production; Germany, where VW is weighing plant closures for the first time in its history, can't shield its carmakers without risking access to China's market. EVs need far fewer assembly workers, China dominates the green-energy supply chain, and Indonesia bets on nickel. Tangen: Norway got to \~90% of new cars sold being electric through subsidies, a national charging network and perks.
