### 263. Kyle Bass — From bank leverage to sovereign debt, the Japan trade and why he owns nothing Chinese (Hoover Institution, Jun 2025)

*Founder of Hayman Capital Management, a Dallas hedge fund focused on global events, known for betting against subprime; interviewed by Jon Hartley on Hoover's Capitalism and Freedom in the 21st Century podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r_BDYDx5Pk4)

**Takeaway:** Bass treats macro like a special situation: map a system's balance sheet and its players' incentives, then bet on the outcome they are forced into. That logic took him from subprime to sovereign debt and Japan. It now leads him to expect a war over Taiwan, own nothing Chinese and favour the US (Jun 2025).

- Method: he began as a special-situations analyst in Bear Stearns' risk-arbitrage group, "whiteboarding" each merger, spin-off and bankruptcy. Work out the "proclivities of the players" and handicap the outcomes: "on Wall Street you only need to get like 55% of those right".
- Subprime (Hayman launched Jan 2006): a Las Vegas bartender with three homes bought on easy loans was the anecdote; leverage was the case. Lehman was 36x levered with $50–60bn of this paper in a warehouse facility and Bear Stearns 29x. Lever 30x and own assets that lose \~40%: "it's just a math problem".
- He stress-tested the thesis by asking people at the Fed and Kenneth Rogoff to prove him wrong: "explain to me how I'm wrong, please."
- Next step: bailouts move "bad private assets to public balance sheets", so ask which sovereigns can carry them. The US could: its banks had \~$1tn of equity on \~$17tn of assets (\~1x GDP), lost \~$800bn and were recapitalized with common and preferred equity. Iceland and Ireland had banking systems 10x GDP and fell in succession, then Greece.
- Japan: debt was \~200% of GDP then and 265% now (Jun 2025), so rates "can never" move and "the currency is the ... escape valve". He saw a fork in the road: Abenomics inflation or collapse under the debt. Calling it a simple bet against Japan is "kind of folklore".
- The Japan trade covered both branches. Two-thirds of the fund bought JGB "bond market optionality"; one-third bet with the Bank of Japan on a weaker yen. Abenomics took the yen from 85 to 120: the bond leg was lost, the yen leg made "many multiples" and the fund made \~250%.
- Japan today (Jun 2025): rates are pegged out to 10 years and the 30-year is left to "flap in the wind", but he thinks "Japan's got it under control". Sovereigns rarely default: "they print ... but they always pay".
- Inflation and fertility: asset prices have run far ahead of wages. FHFA's house price index rose 50% in 2020–24, and Chinese homes reached 26x median income. Young men can't buy homes, marry or have children, so birth rates fall, the social fabric tears and "we should expect more and more war".
- China work (2012–15): he moved the team from Japan to China and asked Wall Street firms for a primer on the Chinese banking system; "no Wall Street firm had a bank primer". Chinese banks are 320% of GDP, and two are bigger than JPMorgan, in an economy reported at \~$18tn vs \~$30tn for the US.
- Taiwan (Jun 2025): three paths are a soft takeover if the KMT wins in 2028, a blockade (the island holds two weeks of energy) or an all-out assault. An assault is hard: the strait is 110 miles wide, tides reach 22 feet and the window is \~three months a year. He expects Xi to move "in the next two or three years" and the US to "certainly hobble them financially".
- China as an investment: "investing in communism has never worked in the long run ever". Over 18 years the CSI 300 lost a third while China's GDP reportedly grew 505%, so "The westerner is going to get left holding the bag". Trading it is "picking up dimes in front of bulldozers".
- Supply chains: the rare-earth magnet talks were "a hostage negotiation", not a trade negotiation. CEOs such as Ford's "shouldn't rely on China for your supply chain".
- The US is "the best place for your capital": "4% of the world's population ... 26% of the world's GDP ... 60% of the world's capital markets". The caveat is \~$1.8tn deficits going forward. Europe has no fiscal union, central taxing authority or deposit guarantee scheme: "It's just an idea".
- Screen implication: cheap China-based listings and companies that depend on Chinese inputs or Taiwan need a manual geopolitical check; Bass expects financial sanctions, not just tariffs, if China moves on Taiwan.
