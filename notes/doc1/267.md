### 267. Rob Citrone — EM macro the Tiger way: top-down themes, bottom-up baskets and asymmetric shorts (Capital Allocators, Jul 2022)

*Founder of Discovery Capital Management, a \~$2bn emerging-markets-focused hedge fund trading equities, credit, currencies and rates. He trained at Fidelity and under Julian Robertson at Tiger and ran money for the Soros family for over 20 years. Interviewed by Ted Seides.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4hLv9hrEGYc)

**Takeaway:** Citrone builds positions top-down and expresses them through the slowest-moving instrument, often EM equity baskets. From Robertson and Soros he kept two habits: exit fully when conviction goes, and cross-check every idea outside the firm. In mid-2022 he was bearish on liquidity, short Turkey, the yuan, Japanese bonds and the DAX, and long Brazilian rates.

- Process: macro analysts generate "probably 80 of our ideas" (\~80%), and bottom-up analysts pick the most efficient securities. EM equities react more slowly than currencies or debt, so he often trades country baskets, e.g. the "five or six best banks to be short". A macro investor without tech analysts is "at a big disadvantage".
- Portfolio: start with views on risk, equity direction, the dollar and rates, then fill in \~10 themes of 10–15 positions each (120–150 in all; a basket counts as one). Equity net short tops out at \~20–25%. Credit, currency and rate shorts can be larger, since credit carries about half of equity volatility and currencies about a third.
- Robertson's and Soros's shared habits: when conviction went "they just got out of the position and they took whatever the market price was", and they cross-referenced every idea. In the Lehman crisis Citrone told Soros Eastern Europe was the next domino; Soros got Gordon Brown on the line and a rescue plan followed.
- Tiger's big 1990s macro trade was done through stocks: short Japan, long the US, as the Nikkei fell from over 40,000 to \~9,000. The Japan shorts lost at every quarter-end as stocks were ramped into the March year-end, but Robertson "stuck with that through thick and thin".
- Shorting: losses are unlimited, so keep shorts small and look for big skew. Tiger's pre-1997 Thai baht short was down $225m at one point. With rates unsustainable and "no reserves left" he kept "tremendous conviction", and it paid (\~40% within about a month, as he recalls). Robertson: "the best shorts go down ninety percent", so keep them on.
- The best shorts have "a fundamental flaw in the business model" or end in bankruptcy; the rest are 30–40% corrections. Chinese red chips (1997–98) fell 90% after buying assets from their parents with debt "nobody talks about". Two Mexican builders shorted in Jan 1995 were still shorted, near bankrupt, five years later.
- Liquidity (mid-2022): in 1987 the Fed flooded the market and stocks recovered; now "there's not much the fed can do because of the inflation issue". After \~$6tn of QE (\~$3tn in the pandemic), taking $3tn out "has to have a significant impact on asset prices". As the reserve currency the US can lean on a dollar that strengthens in crises, and it is "inflating our debt away right now".
- Shorts (mid-2022): Turkey is "maybe the best short in the world", with reserves of "minus fifty billion" and 70–80% inflation; its CDS went from \~400 to 800 and he expects "well over a thousand". China "is going to ultimately become uninvestable", so he has been short the offshore yuan (CNH) since Aug 2021, tempered by a \~15% chance Xi is not reappointed and some chance he surrounds himself with reformers.
- Japan caps the 10-year at 0–25bp and "there's no way that's going to hold", so the long end is "one of the best fixed income shorts" for 50–75bp. Europe: recession within months, gas at "six times" US prices and a possible "accident" within two years. He is short the DAX (no energy names, many industrials) and sees Italian debt as a future short.
- Longs (mid-2022): to profit from falling rates as inflation peaks, choose Brazil at 14% over the US at 3.5%: instead of 100bp "you can make 800 basis points", plus a "12 net yield" on the currency. Argentina's 2023 election could make it "the best performing market in the world". Long term he favours the US and India, "the new growth country of the world".
- Tech (mid-2022): multiples are still too high: "anytime a company's still being priced to sales or they're talking about the tam i want nothing to do with that company probably a short". He expected a mild recession by early 2023.
- Business: Discovery launched in 1999 with $5m ($4m his own) and made 22% net in its fifth month as Turkey's market doubled. It "grew too fast" to \~$15bn and diluted its ideas. After a bad 2019 he credited management fees back to investors who stayed and recouped the losses in under two years.
- Biggest mistake (2019): two 10–15% positions that depended on US regulatory decisions. The rules changed under the Trump administration, and the size relative to liquidity made them hard to cut. Good analysts change their minds: "the worst analyst is the one who just gets dug in".
