### 268. Scott Bessent — Imagination, asymmetry and the chip stack: a macro career from Soros to Key Square (Capital Allocators, Nov 2024)

*CEO and CIO of Key Square Group (launched with $4.5bn) after two stints at Soros Fund Management, a decade under Stan Druckenmiller and five years as CIO; later US Treasury Secretary. Interviewed by Ted Seides days before the 2024 election.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wchyQ-XoBlE)

**Takeaway:** Bessent's method is to imagine a different state of the world, then find the instrument with the most asymmetry and size it by how much the year has already earned. Policy mistakes and policy U-turns gave him his best trades, from the 1992 pound to the Abenomics yen. In Nov 2024 gold was his biggest position.

- Imagination first, quoting Bruce Kovner: "imagine a different state of the world and believe it could happen". The subprime trade needed imagining "a national recession in US housing"; a famous mortgage manager called mortgages a buy halfway through the crisis, and his fund "went down 100%".
- Training: Jim Rogers ("research research research", checking "do the numbers match the story"), then Jim Chanos from Sep 1988. Shorting financials in 1989–90 taught him "how bad policy creates good investment opportunities": the Comptroller of the Currency made banks write "see-through" buildings down to zero even when borrowers were current. He left in Jan 1991 when he "can't find anything to short".
- The Soros and Druckenmiller style: "the micro drives the macro". You can't forecast the PCE deflator better than anybody, but trucking companies, Home Depot struggling to keep products, or bank CEOs on souring credit cards tell you a lot. Then pick "the most bang for the buck" (for an oil view: beaten-down drillers, the futures curve or options), and check what a trade really is: one Australian idea had a 98% r-squared with the S&P.
- 1992 pound: his input was that UK mortgages float with the base rate, so higher rates to defend sterling "would be bankrupting the voting public". The ERM band made it asymmetric: "the most they could push us back was 2 and a half%", and a break could pay "whatever in a day".
- His own 2000 fund raised $1bn on a promise of no macro after LTCM, a mistake: "never do what the investors tell you to do do what you're good at". Equities cost him asymmetry: bullish on oil, he saw oil stocks lag because analysts priced off "the mid curve", when he should have owned crude.
- As Soros CIO (from Sep 2011) he took MF Global's forced sale of Italian bonds within days, "a three and a half billion dollar trade", repo included. Outside managers ran hub-and-spoke; a "great manager" brings one to three big ideas a year.
- Abenomics: after Fukushima a consultant flagged Abe, who could reshape the Bank of Japan board, and the yen was the "most expensive" currency on purchasing power parity. He skipped the JGB "Widowmaker": "the Yen was the ultimate expression and that equities would be a good derivative". The framework was current account, value vs history and positioning, since "lack of volatility leads to volatility".
- Execution: with the yen stuck at 78–82, a vol fund sold him yen puts at 6 vol vs \~4 on screen, thinking it was "picking us off". His targets were 95, then 105 and 115. He told Soros he had "no idea whether it's going to work": the policy did not have to work to move prices.
- Sizing and risk: "I tend to trade in thirds" and add when the year's P&L allows, like a poker chip stack (Annie Duke). He resets it every Jan 1, and cutting risk in December has almost always been right "because there's always a counter Trend". Risk is "probability and severity" (Russian stocks fell 95% in a day). Never be "concentrated illiquid and leveraged": he prefers liquid plus leverage, with part of the book exitable in 24–72 hours.
- Macro is "more Babe Ruth than Ted Williams", so investors chase and flee. Allocators do best by trimming a trusted manager after big years and adding in slumps. Key Square uses \~30 consultants instead of local offices, and SPVs with large funds for occasional big ideas.
- Best trades come from spotting when a government or management heading for "a brick wall" will brake: "policy is personnel". Years of watching policymakers show "when they are going to slam on the brakes", "also a very good risk control metric".
- Views (Nov 2024): a Republican sweep could bring a "growth shock" and make the Fed's "jumbo rate cut" a mistake; a Harris win means divided government facing a 7% deficit. "we are in a long-term bull market in Gold" on central-bank buying, "my biggest position". Xi is "a leninist", Japan could be a "secular bull market", and he predicted Marine Le Pen would win the French presidency within 18–24 months.
