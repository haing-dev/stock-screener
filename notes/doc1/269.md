### 269. Scott Bessent — The bond market is king: running Treasury through the Iran oil shock (The Master Investor Podcast, Mar 2026)

*US Treasury Secretary, formerly Soros Fund Management CIO and founder of Key Square, interviewed by Wilfred Frost at the Treasury two weeks into the Iran war; he was called to the Situation Room mid-interview.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq_L011kMLs)

**Takeaway:** Bessent's investing rule, that the crowd is right most of the time and the money is made at the turns, now shapes his policy view. The bond market matters most, and what counts is that it keeps functioning. On the Mar 2026 oil shock, he says the duration matters more than the price and markets already price the future.

- Contrarian at the turns: "the crowd is right 85 or 90% of the time and that's the momentum", but you make "a lot of money" when you "imagine a different outcome than the consensus". His biggest wins took "the other side of elite opinion": the ERM, US housing ("never experienced a down national housing market") and "Japan can never change".
- Method: imagine a different state of the world (Bruce Kovner's line), then work out "when, why, how" and how to implement it. Investing joins numbers and narrative: "think about scenarios and then can you prove it?"
- Timing: his team's strength is "the ability to put something back on the shelf" after immense work, beating endowment bias. After Fukushima (Mar 2011) Japan shut its reactors and imported fuel, turning a current-account surplus of \~3% of GDP into a deficit. Yet the yen sat at 78–83 and "nothing really happened".
- The catalyst came from a Japanese contact (Funabashi) flagging Abe's return on a reflation platform, with three Bank of Japan board seats, including the governor's, opening up. He now calls the three arrows "a fantastic success": shareholder rights, return on capital, "womenomics" and labour mobility.
- The bond market is king: "it's ultimately the bond market". Markets go up and down; what matters is that they are "continuous and functioning". People panic when there is no "price discovery", "when markets close, when there's the threat of gating". In 2025 Treasuries had their "best year since 2022", and the US was "the only G7 bond market with lower yields last year on the 10-year".
- From investor to policymaker: for 30–35 years he had his "ear up against the door" where policymakers met. Now he asks "what can be done, what should be done, what will be done" and how markets will react, and guides markets without giving away material non-public information.
- Oil (Mar 2026): WTI started the year under $60 and briefly hit $114–115, per Frost. For the economy "it's probably the duration", not the level (2008 peaked at \~$147). "The market lives in the future": an FT report of a 300–400m-barrel IEA release caused the biggest one-day reversal before the 400m-barrel release, the largest ever, was confirmed.
- War logistics (Mar 2026): Navy escorts through Hormuz "as soon as it is militarily possible", and $20bn of US (DFC) insurance for Gulf tankers because Lloyd's cover lapses on force majeure. The war had cost \~$11bn so far. After it he sees a chance of "a substantially lower oil price regime" over the medium term.
- Fed and QE: the Fed must weigh energy-driven inflation expectations against how long the slowdown lasts. The Bank of England's QE was better because "extraordinary measures should only happen in extraordinary times"; the Fed kept buying for years, which helped bring "the great inflation 22 and 23". Revaluing Treasury gold (booked at $42/oz) is "very very independent" of shrinking the Fed's balance sheet.
- Private credit: "that's why it's called the shadow banking system". He sees "some wobbles" but nothing systemic, and watches its links to regulated banks and insurers.
- Tariffs: after the Supreme Court ruled out IEEPA tariffs, a "global 10% tariff" (Section 122, for 150 days) plus Section 301 probes rebuild "the tariff wall"; he expects partners to return to their deals.
- America's chip stack is bigger, not smaller (Mar 2026): an energy exporter with military and tech leads, and "70 or 80% of all the compute in the world" vs 40–50% a few years ago. He sees 3% growth after the war vs the EU's 0.3%, while debt to GDP "is higher everywhere in the world".
- Investing advice: "you have to know what level of risk you're willing to take" and never get "out over your skis": "You never want to have to sell at the bottom. You never want to find yourself like chasing at the top."
