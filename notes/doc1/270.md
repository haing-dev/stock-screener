### 270. Scott Bessent — Inferential edge, asymmetric trades and the US fiscal endgame (Real Vision, Nov 2024)

*Founder and CIO of Key Square Group, ex-Soros CIO and later US Treasury Secretary. Interviewed by Ash Bennington \[?\] in spring 2024, just after Japan intervened at 160 yen; posted under this title in Nov 2024.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xENnM4WNWus)

**Takeaway:** Bessent hunts for an "inferential Edge": build a theory, prove it, and check it with Wall Street only at the end, shelving ideas that are already priced. His best trades were asymmetric bets against "never", or against fear already priced into options. In spring 2024 he saw the US fiscal path nearing an endgame, with gold as the hedge.

- The Soros–Duquesne model starts in equity research: "the micro drives the macro". They never tried to out-forecast payrolls or core PCE; they talked to companies and built a mental model. FX made him the most money (then equities, fixed income, commodities), by "thinking like a corporate": what makes a cash-rich company move money across borders or not "repatriate earnings".
- Process: "we study history we observe the present and we try to imagine the future", looking for systems "accelerating or could break down". First a theory, then proof with data and a check of the framing, and only then the Street, "as the final check". If positioning is high or option skew shows it is priced in, the work goes "up on the shelf"; often "we compile the research and then we don't trade".
- 1992 ERM: the 2.5% bands forced the Bank of England to buy unlimited sterling at the floor, and UK mortgages floated with the overnight rate, so hikes to defend the pound were "destroying the British housing market". Flawed structures "always take longer to break down than you think"; funds, banks and corporates only "brought forward the moment".
- Asymmetry: Paulson's subprime CDS cost \~5% a year of carry (as he recalls) in a five-year fund, so you could "lose 25%" or make "250 350%". Claims that something can never happen are "red red meat for us": "Never Say Never and never say always".
- Behaviour creates asymmetry too. After Brexit and Trump (S&P futures went limit down on election night 2016, when he built "a very large position"), managers feared a Le Pen win in 2017. With the euro at 1.05, skew favoured 0.90 puts (a "15 big figures" fall), while 1.125 and 1.15 calls had no buyers but him: "the market was paying me to take the risk". The euro rose only to \~1.08 on Macron's win, so he bought more.
- China (spring 2024): less worried than most about war over Taiwan, more about decoupling and a China "exporting deflation" up the value chain. He tracks trade with the developed world vs the Global South, re-exports, US tech sanctions, the PBoC and fund flows, and asks who would "build a factory in China now". The yuan may look cheap, but what is "the equilibrium for not getting your money back"? He asks whether there is a "5 10 20% chance" that such assets get frozen.
- US fiscal (spring 2024): a "7% budget deficit" with "no financial crisis no recession and no war". Yellen moved issuance to bills, "like an Emerging Market". Interest will cost \~$1.1tn this year vs \~$900bn for defense, or $1.6tn if rates are not cut. Bill-heavy issuance and Powell's late-2023 pivot eased conditions and sent stocks up 25%.
- Endgame: Key Square is the final chess move, and "the game is almost over". On the release valve: "my guess is that it's going to eventually be the dollar it could initially be rates". Gold should have fallen "5 10 could be 15%" as US real rates rose; "instead gold went up 15%", a sign of buying by central banks (the PBoC), the Global South and US consumers (Costco sells "a hundred to $200 million" a month).
- Japan (spring 2024): long Japanese equities as the "Pure Play" on a governance re-rating, but wrong in expecting yen strength; the yen is "40% undervalued but ... in currencies valuation never matters". His divergent view: "rate hikes are actually stimulative to Japan" given its savings, so it need not fall into the fiscal dominance the US is "flirting with". He keeps expressions simple ("keep the main thing the main thing"): Abenomics was "a gigantic short position" in the yen plus Japanese stocks, no JGBs.
- Skepticism: meeting Sam Bankman-Fried in early 2022, he heard him claim he could hold customer funds and be "my own risk engine", and told him he looked like "a US Bank in 1907 Without A lender of Last Resort". Lesson: "the more credential someone is the more you have to examine them".
- Advice: take risk, know how much, avoid "fixed obligations" and get in front of a big trend, because "the big Trend covers up a lot of the mistakes". He sees a bifurcated economy of asset owners and debtors, with card delinquencies at a high for the Philadelphia Fed series (since 2012).
