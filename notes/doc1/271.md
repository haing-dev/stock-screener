### 271. Jens Stoltenberg — What is the fiscal rule? Spend only the oil fund's return (NBIM, Oct 2024)

*Former Norwegian prime minister and finance minister, and later NATO secretary general, who helped design Norway's fiscal rule; a short clip with NBIM CEO Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wuv71H4b2t8)

**Takeaway:** All of Norway's oil and gas revenue goes into the fund, and the budget spends only the fund's expected return, estimated at 3% a year. That keeps the wealth for future generations and stops oil money from overheating the economy. Stoltenberg prefers an imperfect but credible rule to one that keeps changing.

- Why a rule: net oil revenues began in 1996 and promised to be far bigger than expected, and Norway feared "Dutch disease". It had two aims: share the resource with future generations, and "separate earnings from spending" to "prevent overheating" of the economy.
- The rule (set up when he was prime minister in 2000): all revenues go to the fund, and "the only thing we're going to spend was the financial return", estimated at "3% in yield terms per year" on average. Norway "has spent zero of the oil" revenues, so the rule "could last forever".
- It "worked even better" than planned: bigger production, higher prices and good management made the fund much larger. Its return now finances about 25% of the state budget, "roughly almost one one quarter", probably more than its designers would have set.
- A new risk: with so much of the wealth in the stock market, "big fluctuations" can hit the Norwegian economy in a way they did not before.
- Credibility beats perfection: "it's better to have an imperfect rule which is credible" than one constantly changed, since a "self-imposed restriction" works through "the magic of the Line in the Sand". In the 1990s experts, including a group led by a former central bank governor, said politicians would spend the money; "we have proven them wrong".
