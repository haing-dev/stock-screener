### 274. Adam Selipsky — AWS: an early cloud migration, scale economics and Amazon's long-term culture (NBIM, Dec 2023)

*CEO of Amazon Web Services (AWS), the cloud leader with more than a third of the market, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen of NBIM, which owns more than 1% of Amazon and runs all its own operations on AWS.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i6-HNbhAiuM)

**Takeaway:** Selipsky sees the cloud as still early, with only \~10–15% of world IT spend moved (2023), and generative AI as "the next huge thing" to run on it rather than a separate market. AWS's edge comes from scale that lowers both its costs and its prices, a 5–7-year head start, and a culture built for long-term bets.

- Runway (2023): the world spends "several trillion dollars" a year on IT, and Selipsky estimates only \~10–15% has moved to the cloud, so the migration is "very early".
- Scale economics: at over $90bn of annual revenue (2023) across 32 regions, each with at least three availability zones, AWS pools customers' peaks (retailers on Black Friday, US tax firms in April, news sites in the morning) into smooth demand. High utilization lowers its costs, and it has cut prices "over 120 times" in 17 years.
- Head start: rivals took 5–7 years to offer even "vaguely" competitive products, a lead Jeff Bezos has called "the largest gift in business history". Tangen noted that Microsoft and Google were growing slightly faster than AWS (2023).
- Why incumbents were slow: "we were so threatening to their business model they didn't want to believe it". For 30–40 years old-guard software and hardware firms "overcharged customers" and "intentionally locked them in"; elastic, pay-as-you-go cloud with falling prices threatened "those high prices and high margins", so dismissing AWS was easier.
- On competition: "if you look to your left and you look to your right and you don't see anybody next to you you may have dramatically overestimated the attractiveness of your Market segment". Rivals keep AWS "sharper", but it avoids a "competitively driven strategy".
- Adoption came in stages of doubt: a bookseller can't do this, no enterprise will use it, nobody will run mission-critical work on it. Startups came first, then Netflix and NASA's Mars rover; over 80% of the world's unicorns run on AWS.
- AI is "enabled by the cloud": training and running models needs so much capacity that the costs "can be really enormous", so the work goes where economics and capacity are best. Generative AI is "not a different thing" but the next big cloud workload, and companies win by bringing their own data to the models.
- Anthropic used AWS from its founding. Amazon invested (Tangen: "billions"), Anthropic made AWS its primary cloud and helps improve AWS's custom chips; Selipsky called Claude 2 "as good as anything in the world" (2023).
- Chips (2023): AWS's own Graviton chip uses 60% less power than equivalent chips, yet AWS still buys "enormous quantities" from Intel, AMD and Nvidia and foresees no change. Quantum computing could be "very disruptive" but is "years away".
- Long-term bets: AWS was a "crazy idea" that would take years to become big and profitable, and project Kuiper (several thousand low-earth-orbit satellites, started \~5 years ago, first two test satellites just launched) is "very Capital intensive". Amazon plans 3, 5 and 10 years out, not on a "90day cycle"; Selipsky ties short-termism to investor pressure over quarterly earnings and a culture of "short-term Optics".
- Asked whether AWS is most of Amazon's value, he left it to "the investment Community to try and sort out" and stressed that the parts sell together (carmakers buy cloud, put Amazon devices in cars and supply its electric delivery vans).
- Customer obsession has two parts: understand customers far more deeply than most firms bother to, then take every important decision from their side; many firms price "just about profit maximization". Before building anything of substance, teams write a full press release and FAQ ("working backwards").
- Speed mechanisms: 16 leadership principles, two-pizza teams that "control your destiny", and memos of up to six pages read silently for the first 15–30 minutes of a meeting instead of PowerPoint. "speed is a choice".
- Leadership: fight the "incumbent mentality" of preserving existing revenue streams and keep reminding staff "it is still day one". Advice to the young: build skills over a long career and be "vocally self-critical".
