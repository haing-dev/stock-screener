### 276. Andy Ransom — Rentokil: density deals, "cities of the future" and why pest control keeps growing (NBIM, Feb 2024)

*CEO for over 10 years of Rentokil Initial, the world's largest pest-control provider, where he has worked for 15 years; a former M&A lawyer; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eRY0NWE1L18)

**Takeaway:** Pest control is a route-density business whose demand is pushed up by climate, cities, regulation and rising hygiene standards. Rentokil buys local firms to add density (more visits per technician lifts gross margin), to get into tomorrow's big cities early, and to gain technology, and it walks away from bad deals.

- Demand: pests are "almost biblical", always with us. Growth comes from a warming climate "altering the breeding season of flying biting crawling insects", tighter regulation, rising standards, rising middle classes and urbanization, because rats "go where the food is".
- Why customers pay: health protection, but "they need their reputation protected as well"; a cockroach in a restaurant is no longer shrugged off in the social-media age.
- Regulation forces innovation: Europe imposed a "ban on permanent baiting" (poison only once there is a problem), and California is looking at limits, over chemical use and the secondary poisoning of wildlife. Rentokil answered with connected monitors, "a burglar alarm for pests", and is moving toward chemical-free solutions.
- Next wave: an Israeli camera-tech business bought \~18 months earlier uses micro cameras and facial recognition to tell "rat number 12" from rat number 17, tracking behaviour to fix problems more effectively.
- Threats: vector-borne diseases remain a big concern (dengue is rising sharply in Brazil; Zika worried the Brazil Olympics), and he says malaria has caused more deaths than any other cause. Bed bugs in Paris and the UK are real, with "a little bit of media um hype", and callouts have "significantly increased".
- Deal reason 1, density: in "a network business", 20 customers on one high street earn far more than 10 because less "windshield time" lets a technician do seven visits a day instead of six, for a "much greater gross margin". Many deals are "density deals".
- Deal reason 2, "cities of the future": half of the world's pest control is done in the US. In 50 years it won't be 50/50 as cities in China, India, Brazil and Colombia grow, so Rentokil buys the best local firm early and builds density. Reason 3 is capability and technology.
- Deal discipline: check strategic fit, then "detail detail detail", and "don't get seduced by the process" when diligence finds problems: "the best deal that you can do is to avoid a bad deal". Walk away even at a late stage.
- Negotiation: think "three steps ahead" like a chess player and learn what the seller wants (maximum price, certainty or speed). He was once "a table Banger" but learned that being "hard and tough and shouty" isn't the best approach; out-prepare the other side and "don't treat everyone the same".
- Integration: in business services "it's all about people", so confirm cultural alignment before buying; afterwards "execution counts way more than strategy".
- His decade as CEO: the first five years went on process, cost, structure, innovation, "getting out of bad businesses" and acquisitions; the next five on people. A colleague told him "you play not to lose", and he now treats colleague retention as the lever: engaged, safe staff mean happy customers who buy more and pay, funding reinvestment. Headcount is now 60,000.
- Safety is the first agenda item at every meeting, and anyone may walk off an unsafe job; he had fatalities on his watch in an earlier explosives business.
- Management: hire people better than you, give them entrepreneurial freedom and "hold them to account"; he reads in detail and asks questions rather than giving orders. Senior tenure is long (the head of IT 32 years; Ransom, the finance director and the UK head 15 years each).
