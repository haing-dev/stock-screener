### 277. Anne Marte Pensgaard — Why Norway wins at the Olympics: patience, broad youth sport and psychological safety (NBIM, Mar 2026)

*Professor of sports psychology who has shaped the mental training of Norway's Olympic athletes, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen after Norway's record-breaking Winter Olympics.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VHrsivw774)

**Takeaway:** Norway's medals come from decades of patient system-building: no rankings for children under 12, many sports before specializing, expertise shared across sports, and teams whose rivals help each other. Her mental training teaches athletes to act before they feel confident, and she sees psychological safety, meaning disagreement without punishment, as the base of any good team.

- The long game: after the Calgary Games, which Tangen calls Norway's worst, the country built Olympiatoppen, a shared elite-sport center pooling physiology, nutrition, psychology, training and talent development. The culture took 20–30 years: "this takes time. It's not a quick fix in any way", and today's results reflect what was done 10 years ago.
- The Norwegian model: no result lists and no single-winner prizes until age 12, so more children stay in sport longer, which matters in a tiny country. Many champions bloom late, at 15–16, so aggressive early selection would lose "the biggest talents".
- Generalists win: trying several sports builds physical range and a flexible mindset. The same holds at work, where a broad background helps you understand colleagues and analyze situations from different perspectives.
- Shared knowledge: science, coaches and athletes form a tight community with meeting places across sports, which US colleagues envy; the US Olympic squad is three times bigger, which makes this harder.
- Role models create clusters: when a teammate you beat in training wins a medal, "So if he can, I can."
- Focus training: athletes learn to separate thoughts from action ("I'm not my thoughts"), do the task without waiting for a confident feeling, and let mastery follow; "confidence kind of becomes a result of your focus".
- Psychological safety means you can disagree without being punished a week later, not job security for poor performers (Tangen's point, which she accepts). It needs constant work: "A good culture can uh die in a second if the trust is not there anymore."
- Teams of rivals: Norway's alpine skiers compete hard but train together, on the logic "if I don't win, I want you to win, not someone from Sweden or Denmark or somewhere else". A few simple rules (no phones at meals, cooking together) show respect.
