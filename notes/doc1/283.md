### 283. Michael Dell — Building a tech empire: highlights (NBIM, Dec 2024)

*Short highlights cut of Nicolai Tangen's "In Good Company" interview with the founder, chairman and CEO of Dell Technologies.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jaaDki7pQWE)

**Takeaway:** Dell spotted that IBM charged about six times its parts cost, built a direct-sales PC maker that compounded \~80% a year for eight years, and took it private in 2013 when the market wrote it off as a dying PC company.

- At 16 he found that none of the IBM PC's parts were made by IBM and that its price was "roughly six times the cost of the parts". IBM was then \~80% of the value of the technology sector.
- Incorporated in May 1984, Dell had $6 million of revenue in year one and \~$33 million in year two, then grew \~80% a year compounded for eight years.
- Going private (2013): as Dell moved into cloud, networking and storage, "the more we did that the less the market liked it", and the market's logic was "the PC's dead so therefore Dell's dead". With "billions of dollars of free cash flow every year", he chose to "buy back all the shares".
- Dell bought EMC (\~$67bn) while private and became fully public again at the end of 2018. Tangen calls it the most profitable private equity deal ever; Dell: "that's what I'm told".
- AI PCs: a new neural processing unit (NPU) speeds up AI on the PC, and maybe 85–90% of that AI will run locally.
