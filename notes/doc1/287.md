### 287. Christophe Fouquet — ASML: the EUV monopoly, Moore's law for AI and chip decoupling (NBIM, Feb 2025)

*CEO of ASML, the Dutch maker of the lithography machines that print circuits onto chips and the only maker of extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) systems, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen; NBIM owns 2.6% of ASML, \~$7bn.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hvQvrLjlJN8)

**Takeaway:** ASML's EUV monopoly came from a 20-year bet with no reasonable business plan, partly funded by its customers, on the only technology that could scale at the right cost. Fouquet expects AI demand to keep chip scaling going for at least 20 more years, but sees decoupling from China raising costs and doubts that subsidies alone can make new chip regions competitive.

- What EUV is: light at a 13.5 nm wavelength, and the shorter the wavelength, the smaller the features printed. The hard parts are the light source and the optics. ASML spent more than 20 years reaching 200 watts, "the first threshold to make euv economically viable", by hitting droplets with powerful CO2 lasers three times each, 50,000 times a second.
- Moat: ASML is the only company that can make EUV light "in a way that can be industrialized"; its latest machine is the size of a double-decker bus and ships in seven planes and 50 trucks. Fouquet thinks it is "very hard for anyone to start from scratch and do UV": ASML can do it because "we have done everything before".
- The bet (\~20 years ago): ASML chose EUV over e-beam and nanoimprint though it was less advanced, because only EUV could scale in resolution and productivity "at the right cost". It could not "make the business plan look even reasonable" and had only \~80% confidence, from understanding the physics. TSMC moving to 7 and 5 nm with EUV while Intel didn't proved the point.
- Customer co-investment: when ASML "was not a very rich company", customers co-invested in EUV and took ownership stakes. That proved their commitment and, Fouquet estimates, accelerated EUV by "maybe 3 to five years". Tangen put the cost at $6bn over more than 15 years; Fouquet said it was more.
- Zeiss, its optics partner, is "worse than a marriage because we can never get divorce". Each sticks to what it is good at; they don't merge because culture, history and business models differ, and a different partner "is always able to challenge" how you work.
- Moore's law (density doubling every two years) still holds in logic (Intel, TSMC, Samsung) and DRAM, and AI wants density to rise "by a factor of 16 every two years". Scaling "won't stop at least for another 20 years", limited by cost and energy. ASML has a firm 5-year plan and 10–15-year scenarios; a 2 nm transistor is \~200 times the size of a silicon atom.
- AI demand: model parameters will grow "most probably also exponentially", pulling demand for 3 nm, 2 nm and angstrom-class nodes. Inside ASML, AI's biggest value is faster R&D learning from its vast product data. Timing is the open question: "none of us know exactly at which speed".
- Winners: many firms can build model software; value sits in chip design (Nvidia, AMD, "a few more"), in manufacturing (TSMC, Intel, Samsung) and in the lithography they need. "you cannot pick one because if you were to take out any of those name everyone lose".
- Hyperscalers' own chips are "very good" and will improve. Today's GPUs and high-bandwidth memory were not designed for AI, so major redesigns in logic and memory are coming, and AI has replaced mobile as the product that sets the industry's roadmaps.
- Decoupling: the industry was built for an open market in which revenue from older products funds the next generation. Splitting markets raises costs (less revenue for R&D; making chips in the US and Europe is "a lot more expensive than Asia") and slows innovation.
- China has no EUV and is "at least I would say 10 years behind", likely 10–15 years in logic: EUV entered R&D around 2016–17 and production around 2018, when China was already a couple of generations behind. Without EUV, advanced logic can't be made "in volume" or "at a cost that is reasonable".
- Taiwan: supply-chain concentration is a risk, and ASML itself presents "a similar risk". Fouquet personally sees very bad Taiwan scenarios as "very unlikely", though tension could rise.
- Industrial policy: new chip regions need inherited R&D (Japan's 2 nm effort uses IBM technology). Chips Acts help "to restart the engine" but don't fix structural cost and flexibility gaps, and "subsidies are usually something that do not last forever". Of the 50 largest tech companies only four are European (Tangen); Fouquet cites cost, flexibility, scarcer capital (much of it from the US) and power prices.
- Culture and leadership: ASML (44,000 staff) is "a nonpolitical organization" where staff argue with the CEO. His rules: humility, authenticity, "stay on content" instead of relying on processes, and simple stories, because "you cannot convince people"; you give them what they need to convince themselves.
