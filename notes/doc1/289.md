### 289. Dario Amodei — Anthropic: $10bn training runs, chips as the leading indicator and AI safety (NBIM, Jun 2024)

*Co-founder and CEO of Anthropic, maker of the Claude models and backed by Amazon and Google; he led GPT-2 and GPT-3 at OpenAI before leaving at the end of 2020. Interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xm6jNMSFT7g)

**Takeaway:** Amodei expects scaling to continue, with $10–100bn training runs in 2025–27 that could yield models "better than most humans at most things". Chip companies earn first because training comes before deployment and revenue; he bets the revenue will follow, but concedes the build-out could still be "a bust" if models stop improving.

- Scaling costs (Jun 2024): a frontier model costs \~$100m to train, models then in training \~$1bn, and $10–100bn runs should come "in 2025 2026 maybe 2027"; a \~$10bn run "could start sometime in 2025". Anthropic had raised a little over $8bn. If algorithms and chips keep improving, he sees "a good chance" of models "better than most humans at most things" by then.
- Cost curve: models trade intelligence against speed and cost (Claude 3 Opus, Sonnet, Haiku), and each generation pushes that frontier outward, so cheaper mid- and low-tier models do what only the top model could. He rejects the reading that things are "leveling out": "we can do more with less but we can also do even more with more resources". Models smarter than today's best will run on phones.
- Cost base: compute is "almost all of our expenses", more than 80% (it "can be publicly backed out"), so pay is set by what is fair rather than by budget. Anthropic had \~600 staff at 3.5 years old; its culture motto is "do the stupid simple thing that works", and his sister runs the company day to day while he sets ideas and strategy.
- Who makes money: "the valuation of the chip companies is a leading indicator", AI companies' valuations a present one and downstream companies' a lagging one, because training comes before deployment, which comes before revenue. Nvidia's \~$3tn value (nearly twice NBIM's fund, Tangen noted) reflects anticipated demand; the revenue "is not present yet".
- Bust risk: it is "possible" that "all of this will be a bust" if the models stop getting better. His bet is the bull case: models earn a great deal of revenue, chip demand rises further and value reaches chips, AI firms and downstream users, since "the pie is going to be so large".
- Revenue and payoff: AI companies' revenues seem to be "growing roughly 10x a year", which could reach hundreds of billions in 2–3 years and eventually trillions a year. Biology excites him most: "a million copies" of an AI as knowledgeable as top scientists could multiply discoveries like gene editing. His rough path, which he called a guess: 2–3 years to capable systems, \~5 to discoveries and 5 more through regulators, "a little over a decade".
- Macro: very large real productivity gains "would tend to be deflationary rather than inflationary"; he declined to size the effect.
- Chips: Nvidia moving from launches every other year to every year reflects chips' importance and competition. Google and Amazon build their own chips, Anthropic works with both, and "I don't think it's only about Nvidia anymore". Hyperscalers supply chips and cloud, Anthropic the model, "a layered cake"; using both clouds avoids "too much" exclusivity.
- The biggest bottleneck is data, not chips, and synthetic data should lift it, as AlphaGo Zero learned purely from self-play.
- AGI is not one moment but "this smooth exponential". Models were like "early undergrads" or interns, heading to graduate level, including for drug development, investing and trading. In companies they must be wired into databases and internal tools, not act like a smart stranger "who knew nothing about your company".
- AI in investing: interpretability (understanding "maybe" 3% of how models work) could show whether a model trained on past financial data is reasoning or has "memorized" the outcome, so that it "knows the future".
- Safety: a responsible scaling policy (Sep 2023) tests each bigger model for misuse (bio, cyber, election operations) and autonomy (training models, setting up cloud accounts, financial transactions); none had reached a dangerous level. In his "race to the top", standards spread (Google and OpenAI adopted similar frameworks) and law then enforces the consensus on "those 20%" who don't comply.
- Geopolitics: chip and equipment export limits could keep China and Russia "maybe two or three years" behind, which "doesn't give us much margin"; "the idea of powerful AI plus autocracies terrifies me". National models are like nuclear weapons: some allies build their own (France), others rely on partners.
- Concentration: as in the 19th century, a powerful technology is concentrating wealth. He worries about a "closed loop" of Silicon Valley firms selling AI to one another and wants it to reach health, education and government services.
