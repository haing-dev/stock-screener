### 291. David Schwimmer — LSEG: a data business that gains from volatility, the Refinitiv turnaround and UK listings (NBIM, Feb 2024)

*CEO of London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) after 20 years at Goldman Sachs; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wYKa5E9bVRA)

**Takeaway:** LSEG is mostly a data company: \~70% of revenue is stable, growing data and analytics, and its trading venues add upside when markets are volatile. Changing how the acquired Refinitiv operated took it from 1–2% growth to \~7%.

- What LSEG is: "the world's leading financial markets infrastructure and data provider". Data and analytics (number one in real-time financial data), the FTSE Russell index business and risk intelligence (the data banks use for background checks, KYC and AML) are "roughly 70% of our Revenue".
- The rest is execution venues: the London Stock Exchange in equities, dealer-to-customer and dealer-to-dealer FX venues, and a controlling stake in Tradeweb in fixed income.
- Earnings shape: the data base is "very uh stable and growing", and inflation, rate moves and geopolitics add upside because "volatility tends to be fairly good for our business". Geopolitics also lifts demand for data.
- AI raises the value of clean data: model quality is "entirely uh dependent on the quality of the data that goes into them", so data integrity, lineage and auditability are becoming a growth driver.
- AI at work (Feb 2024): LLMs help service reps answer over a million customer queries a year and will later answer customers directly. LLMs now write due-diligence reports that staff fact-check. A "meeting prep" tool built with Microsoft drafts in about 15 seconds a memo that took someone a few hours.
- Refinitiv: the deal closed at the end of January 2021 and brought \~25,000 people to LSEG's \~5,000. Refinitiv grew 1–2% a year "on the back of a 2% annual price rise"; by the last quarter (Feb 2024) it grew about 7%.
- Integration: LSEG was "a collection of great assets" run in silos, and some units were "comfortable with mediocrity". After three months of listening he built one review and calibration process, a monthly meeting of the top hundred leaders and staff-written values. It is a multi-year job.
- Risk culture had to be imported: Refinitiv came from Thomson Reuters, a media company with "very little concept of risk management". LSEG now reviews minor incidents as well as serious ones, to fix issues before they grow.
- UK companies moving to the US: there is "a stronger narrative around that than the facts would actually suggest". Moves happen when most of a company's market is in the US, or for pay: a UK fintech or biotech listing in New York "might get paid three or four times more". He expects "the pendulum will swing".
- CEO pay: pay should align with "the shareholder experience" over the medium to long term, but must be globally competitive to hire US talent. UK media and proxy agencies apply a different standard, and in the US "success is more celebrated and failure is more tolerated".
- Digital assets: LSEG stayed out of the Bitcoin boom, expecting it to "flame out" and turn more regulated. It prices digital assets, is seeking approval to clear them, and is building "asset class agnostic" digital market infrastructure (tokenization through custody) with the industry.
- Judgment: gut feel is fine when you understand the drivers; without that it can "Verge on into the realm of of Reckless". Hire great people, align them on strategy and give them "a lot of running room". He thinks "it's more important to be respected than liked", and his advice is "don't set your own limits".
