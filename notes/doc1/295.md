### 295. Elon Musk — AI's moving bottlenecks, bet-the-company risk and speed as a weapon (NBIM, Apr 2024)

*CEO of Tesla and SpaceX, founder of xAI and owner of X, in a live "In Good Company" recording with Nicolai Tangen on the X platform.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rQBZ3vKRA0)

**Takeaway:** Musk sees AI compute growing \~10x a year, with the bottleneck moving from chips to voltage transformers to electricity, and training data running out. In business he takes bet-the-company risks only when unavoidable, treats speed in the right direction as the best offense and defense, and expects electric vehicles to win despite fierce Chinese competition.

- AI pace (Apr 2024): AI hardware coming online grows "at least by a factor of 10 every year", plus software breakthroughs. He expects AI smarter than any single human "around the end of next year", and AI compute exceeding all humans combined within 5 years.
- Moving bottlenecks: last year it was chips (Nvidia supply), this year voltage transformers ("Transformers for Transformers"), and in one to three years electricity availability. Talent is pouring in, including people who would once have done physics.
- The data wall: all books ever written fit on one hard drive and are "a small number of train training tokens". The two unlimited sources are synthetic data and real-world video, where "Tesla has by far the most real world video of anyone".
- xAI (Apr 2024): Grok 2 was training on roughly 20,000 H100s, due in May and expected to beat GPT-4. Grok 3 would need 100,000 Nvidia H100s training coherently.
- AI regulation: a safety regulator like those for aircraft, cars and medication makes sense, though AI moves faster than any agency can follow. Most important is to train AI "to be as truthful as possible"; programming it to be politically correct (his example is Google Gemini's images) is "where the danger lies".
- China: AI is concentrated in San Francisco and London, but China "won't be far behind". In cars, "the Chinese car companies are by far the most competitive" and pose Tesla's toughest challenge.
- EVs: all vehicles going electric is "only a matter of time", with ebbs and flows along the way.
- Bet-the-company only when unavoidable: each new Tesla volume model was an order of magnitude bigger than the last and would be 90% of production: Roadster \~600–700 a year, Model S 20,000, Model 3 \~half a million, Model Y over a million. The radical Cybertruck was possible because it was not a bet-the-company decision.
- Speed: "the best offense and defense is speed"; over 3,000 missiles were fired at the SR-71 Blackbird and none hit. But a company must be "a vector not a scaler": fast in the right direction, with course corrections like a guided missile.
- SpaceX: the first three flights failed, and had the fourth failed "we would have gone bankrupt". It took about 10 years to see that Starship needed a new architecture (methane and liquid oxygen, staged combustion) instead of Falcon's refined jet fuel and gas-generator engines. Falcon 9 is now the most reliable rocket, launching every two to three days.
- Innovation needs incentives that accept failure: "if we're not occasionally blowing up an engine on the test stand we're not trying hard enough".
- Managing talent: smart people "manage themselves"; he sets the goal, asks if they agree, and occasionally says "you just got to trust me on on this one". Crisis mode means true 100-hour weeks with about six hours of sleep, only for emergencies: "it sucks".
- Mars: self-sufficiency needs 1–10 million tons landed. At 100 tons a flight that is 10,000 flights, with five times the tonnage in Earth orbit for refilling. He expects uncrewed Starships on Mars within \~5 years and people within 7–9 years, using less than 1% of Earth's resources.
- Tesla (Apr 2024): "there's going to be bumpy quarters", but "the long-term future of Tesla is extremely strong", and the Swedish union dispute "storm has passed".
