### 297. Dev Kantesaria — FICO: the score monopoly that waited 25 years to raise prices (Business Breakdowns, May 2023)

*Managing partner of Valley Forge Capital, a FICO holder since 2018, breaks down Fair Isaac's scores and software businesses with host Zack Fuss \[?\] of Arena Capital.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33NBOf-cHNY)

**Takeaway:** FICO scores are a cheap, essential common language for US consumer credit, a natural monopoly protected by its network effect rather than by its maths. After 25 years without price rises, FICO began rotating price increases in 2018, turning a volume business into a mid-to-high-teens compounder with almost no capex and large buybacks.

- Two halves (May 2023): revenue of \~$1.5bn splits evenly between scores and software, but scores earn over 75% of operating income at margins "north of 85 percent". Software margins are \~30%, and the group's \~40% EBIT margin "could be a lot higher".
- Reach: over 15 billion scores a year, used in over 90% of credit lending decisions, over 99% of credit securitizations and by 90 of the top 100 US lenders. A score costs less than a dollar, some a fraction of a penny. In 1995 Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac required FICO scores for all the mortgages they handle.
- Three layers of customers: FICO sells only the model. The credit bureaus pay it (\~75% of scores revenue), add their own markup at near-100% margins and sell reports to lenders, who pass the cost on to borrowers in closing costs. So the bureaus don't resist price rises, and "you have to go many layers deep" to find who pays.
- The price is tiny against the value: under 5% of a \~$50 three-bureau report, and under a dollar on a $400,000 mortgage. Even a score at 10 times the price (\~$5) would still offer "tremendous value".
- The trigger: before 2016 FICO had not raised score prices for 25 years, so growth came only from volume. From 2018 it rotated "special price increases": mortgage scores +30%, autos by about the same the next year, then credit cards. They added \~$60m of annual revenue at 95%+ incremental margins. Spotting this change, after following FICO for years, is why Valley Forge bought in 2018.
- B2B scores (\~two-thirds of scores revenue; mortgage, auto and card/personal-lending roughly equal) are cyclical: scores revenue fell about a third in 2007–09. Price increases now let FICO hold or grow scores revenue when volumes fall, and B2C (\~a third) is far less cyclical.
- VantageScore, launched by the three bureaus in 2006, claimed to score 40 million more people than FICO 9. With the FICO 10 suite (Jan 2020) the gap falls to \~10 million, and among people who can actually get credit to 0–2 million. FICO dominates mission-critical originations and monitoring; Vantage has significant share in cheap marketing and lead-generation scores, where accuracy matters less.
- Switching doesn't pay. Synchrony moved to VantageScore in 2021 after a three-year transition, saving an estimated $2–3m a year on $18bn of revenue while giving up cheap securitization. As with Moody's and S&P ratings, a non-FICO deal looks lower quality and pays more, which he guesses at 0–50bp. "two kids in a dorm room at MIT" could build an equal model; the network effect is what matters.
- After reviewing FICO's mortgage monopoly, the FHFA required both FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 for Fannie and Freddie loans, but cut the bureau reports needed from three to two. That shrinks the bureaus' mortgage revenue by a third, so they "may now be regretting that fight". The overhang gave "many opportunities to buy the company at depressed levels".
- AI risk to scores looks limited: the Gini coefficient of FICO's scores has sat at \~0.70 for \~15 years, "close to Peak predictability", and in 2021 most scores still came from the 2009 FICO 8 model. Upstart layers its own algorithms on top of FICO, as banks have for decades, and AI risks discrimination that an objective score avoids.
- Consumers: myFICO (\~40–45% of B2C) and royalty partnerships such as Experian Boost (renewed for five years) are high-margin and growing fast. The Open Access program lets banks give FICO scores to customers free, keeping FICO the default against VantageScore-based Credit Karma.
- Software (usage-based, multi-year subscriptions) is more competitive and AI will reshape it. Falcon protects \~two-thirds of card transactions worldwide using data from 9,000+ institutions, and Triad manages \~two-thirds of the world's card accounts. Rebuilding on FICO Platform cut software margins from the mid-20s to the low double digits (FY2021); the platform now grows 40–60% a year (\~20% of software recurring revenue) and software margins rose from \~10% to \~30%.
- Capital: free cash flow compounded 15% a year for 10 years and should grow in the mid-teens "if not the High Teens". Capex was under $10m over the last two fiscal years for a company worth nearly $20bn. Almost all free cash flow went to buybacks, retiring \~20% of shares in five years and 25% in ten, with no defensive acquisitions needed.
- Verdict: "you really can't sit down on a piece of paper and design a Better Business than what FICO is". Of almost 1,000 IPOs and SPACs in recent years, none had as compelling a model; FICO is "an accident of History" that can't be engineered.
