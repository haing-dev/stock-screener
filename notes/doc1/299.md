### 299. Jens Stoltenberg — Highlights: a more dangerous world, Ukraine and leading through crises (NBIM, Oct 2024)

*A 10-minute highlights cut of Nicolai Tangen's In Good Company interview with Jens Stoltenberg, who had just stepped down as NATO Secretary General and was earlier Norway's prime minister.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q-WtuRzSYoM)

**Takeaway:** The world is more dangerous and autocracies are aligning, so peace in Ukraine depends on convincing Putin he can't win on the battlefield. Europe should strengthen NATO rather than duplicate it, and leaders do best by taking even difficult counterparts' concerns seriously (Oct 2024).

- Threat picture (Oct 2024): "the world has become more dangerous", with full-scale war in Europe, a Middle East conflict at risk of escalation, US–China rivalry and constant cyber, hybrid and terror threats. NATO is stronger and protects "close to 1 billion people".
- Ukraine: "the quickest way to end the war is to lose a war", which would bring occupation, not peace, so the West must convince Putin "he will not win on the battlefield". North Korea, Iran and China are the main suppliers and enablers of Russia's war, so NATO must also work with partners such as South Korea and Japan.
- Defense and technology: EU defense helps if it overcomes the fragmentation of Europe's defense industry and raises spending. Space is being weaponized, with Russia and China building offensive capabilities, and AI may change warfare "as fundamental as Industrial Revolution did"; NATO's drones and surveillance already use it.
- Leading in crises: "remain calm" and "make sure that you sleep"; he suppresses feelings while a crisis is acute and lets others help him. With strong leaders, take their concerns seriously: Trump's demand that Europe spend more on defense was "absolutely the same as President Obama had done before him or President Biden does now".
