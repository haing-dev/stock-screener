### 300. Peter Harrison — Highlights: Schroders' 200 years, the UK market's decline and building investment teams (In Good Company, Nov 2024)

*Short cut of Nicolai Tangen's interview with the CEO of Schroders, the \~200-year-old, family-backed asset manager.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TRumdQ66sF4)

**Takeaway:** A family owner with a 20-year view let Schroders keep changing as passive, private markets and collapsing fees reshaped the industry. The UK regulated its savers out of equities, and Harrison's main regret is not moving faster into private markets.

- Industry shifts: massive growth of passive, a move to private equity and private debt, collapsing fees and a governance revolution. US managers won on scale and partnership structures, and in private markets the bigger you are, the bigger the moat.
- UK: the country's large domestic savings pool was "regulated that out of existence" into government bonds, and a governance code rewarded voting against management. London's private-market boom runs on foreign capital, so "the returns are not going to UK Savers". Brexit, the Truss budget and the gilts crisis followed; "scars take a while to heal".
- Teams need brilliant leaders, great tools, the right size and closeness to local markets, plus room to be wrong "for three or four years at a time", as long as you understand the risks they take and the path dependency of returns. AI will change wealth advice first, as people turn to AI assistants, and PMs must have the new tools and data in front of them.
- His regret is not being "more bold": he should have moved into private markets earlier and bought more teams. Schroders bought small to protect its culture, but the disruption came "way quicker" than anyone thought.
- ESG has been politicized and has "passed Peak debate", but companies' climate work is "very real". US managers have stopped talking about China in public but haven't pulled back from what is the largest savings market after the US.
