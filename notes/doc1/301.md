### 301. Vinod Khosla — Highlights: improbable bets, fast learners and free expertise, labor and energy (NBIM, Oct 2024)

*Highlights cut of Nicolai Tangen's In Good Company interview with Vinod Khosla, co-founder of Sun Microsystems and founder of Khosla Ventures.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lAdBFY2me7g)

**Takeaway:** Experts extrapolate the past, so Khosla backs persistent, fast-learning founders on "low probability high impact bets". He expects AI and robots to make expertise and labor nearly free, and fusion to make energy cheap.

- Only "the improbable" matters, because expert opinion extrapolates the past. Khosla committed to OpenAI in 2018: the timing of a breakthrough was unknowable, but the trend was exponential and still on its flat early part.
- In 1996 he backed Juniper on TCP/IP when no telco believed in it: "we got a 2500x investment over about five years", $7bn on $3m. Google was "a couple of flaky PhD students". He looks for resolve, persistence and "low probability high impact bets".
- Judging founders: over a month or two of talks, watch how fast they learn and whether they reason from first principles; "I've done this for 30 years" is a bad sign.
- Predictions: expertise will be free, as AI nears the median professional and can be copied without limit. Labor will be free, with two-legged robots within about five years; a billion of them working 24-hour shifts could do more work than all human labor today. Natural-language programming would put 70 billion agents (10 for each of 7 billion people) to work.
- Medicine, food and energy: drugs designed for one person's cancer, robots replacing pesticide spraying, and fusion proven viable within five years, then scaled in the 2030s–40s by retrofitting coal and gas plants. His advice to the young: pursue your passions, without the old caveat "as long as you can make a living at it", because he expects enough abundance to support everyone.
