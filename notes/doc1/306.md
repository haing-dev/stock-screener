### 306. Stanley Druckenmiller — Highlights: bottom-up macro, invest then investigate, and the 1992 pound (NBIM, Nov 2024)

*Macro investor (Duquesne, formerly Soros's Quantum Fund) in a short cut of his In Good Company interview with Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=itG58Jwu12A)

**Takeaway:** Druckenmiller builds macro views from what companies say and from financial conditions. He buys first and investigates later, and bets big only when conviction is high. In Nov 2024 he saw no near-term slowdown, but called 7% deficits at full employment unsustainable.

- Bottom-up macro (Nov 2024): "we're listening primarily to companies", and they show no material weakness except maybe housing, so no economic problem in the next 3–6 months. As "more of a market animal than an economist" he watches financial conditions: still "very very loose", though tighter in the four or five weeks since the Fed cut as the dollar and yields rose.
- Deficits: "debt to GDP can't go up forever". Reserve-currency status has let the US do things more radical than the UK did without a market revolt, but deficits of \~7% of GDP at full employment are "a recipe that can't last forever". It hasn't bitten yet because 80% of homeowners refinanced (the average mortgage is under 4% vs 8% at the margin) and companies termed out debt that rolls over in 2025–26, so trouble is likelier "late 25 early 26".
- Nvidia: three or four years earlier his young analysts saw Stanford and MIT engineers moving from crypto to AI. They suggested Nvidia, which he thought was a gaming company; he bought "a pretty good chunk" after it fell from \~400 to \~150, and ChatGPT came out a month later: "it was just total luck".
- "invest and then investigate" (Soros): markets are fast, and two or three months of analysis can miss the move and leave you paralysed, since it is hard to buy at 160 what you watched at 100. Buy "a meaningful position but not Earth shaking", do the work, then sell if it was a mistake or add if not.
- Conviction: from Soros he learned "when you have conviction you should bet really big". In Sep 1992, after the Bundesbank president wrote that the mark and the pound should no longer be linked, he took the funds to 100% long marks and short sterling. Soros called it "a one-way bet" and said "we should put 200% of the fund in this trade".
