### 309. Jensen Huang — Nvidia: AI's "iPhone moment", a new kind of computer and the production of intelligence (NBIM, Nov 2023)

*Co-founder and CEO of Nvidia (founded in 1993, when he was 30), maker of GPUs and DGX AI supercomputers, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R9Mnn-HSS4o)

**Takeaway:** Huang argues AI is a new kind of computer that needed every layer redesigned, from chips to networking, and that ChatGPT marked a phase shift like Windows 95 or the web browser, because anyone can now program in plain language. He expects knowledge-work productivity to be "insanely boosted", starting with chip design, software engineering, drug discovery and climate simulation.

- Origin: AlexNet, a computer-vision network that "shattered records" and needed Nvidia GPUs, convinced Nvidia that deep learning would change computing "from chips to systems to operating systems to Data Centers to networking to the algorithms all the way to applications". It built the DGX AI supercomputer, and Huang hand-delivered the first one outside Nvidia to OpenAI in 2016.
- A new computer: GPUs built to simulate virtual worlds were "almost a perfect starting point", but the CPU, PC architecture, I/O and networking all had to change, so Nvidia bought Mellanox to link "millions of GPU cores". GPT-3's 175 billion parameters fit on no PC or phone; training takes "a giant data center" and weeks, so halving training time matters.
- The "iPhone moment": computing shifts like water turning to ice, "at first slowly and all of a sudden abruptly". PCs grew linearly for 4–5 years until Windows 95, a decade after the PC appeared; scientists used the internet for 5–8 years before Mosaic and Yahoo. Each shift made computers easier to program: there are few mainframe apps but over five million iPhone apps.
- Programming in human language lifts the number of programmers "from tens of millions to several billion" and closes the technology divide. Huang called ChatGPT "unquestionably the single best software product the world's ever made": over 100 million users and no manual (2023).
- Timing is the edge: large language models were "quite abundant" in the open, so what held others back was not access but "The Willpower" and the insight that GPT-2 to GPT-3 had turned a marginally useful product into an incredibly useful one. The same timing insight produced the PC, the internet, Google search and the iPhone.
- Production of intelligence: AI is "the next Industrial Revolution" because companies will mass-produce intelligence instead of physical goods; Nvidia's "AI factories" turn data into better software every day. Domain knowledge, the reason most companies exist, can now be put in every employee's hands.
- Nvidia's own productivity: its chips, "a couple of inches per side", are "impossible to build anymore without artificial intelligence" (like laying out a city 1,000 times bigger than New York). Each generation's R&D budget is "probably something like $5 billion", more than building a rocket.
- Engineers are Nvidia's "single greatest expense". With Copilot-style tools, which Microsoft estimates already write 40–50% of the code on GitHub, their output is amplified 2x, and Nvidia expects 10x. AI weather models run "10,000 50,000 times faster" than numerical simulation.
- The biggest problems for the next 5–10 years: digital biology (AI has "learned the language of proteins", so it can design proteins from a desired function and lift drug discovery's low success rate) and climate (regional forecasts to guide infrastructure, insurance, reinsurance and farming decisions, using less energy).
- Who gains: countries with few computer scientists (India, Southeast Asia, Africa) get "an extraordinary opportunity", and developed countries can cut costs. On jobs, in the near term "it's more likely that someone uses AI is going to displace our jobs" than AI itself.
- Regulation "is necessary", and only governments can impose it, as with food, drugs and transport; what AI generates can be regulated even where speech can't. The technology should be democratized rather than left in one company's hands.
- Corporate character: Nvidia faced existential crises "maybe five six seven times" in its first 15 years. Huang credits a vision that was "directionally right" plus resilience, agility, "the utter incredible ability to suffer" and an attitude of "how hard can it be" (it always turns out "incredibly hard").
- Habits and advice: he works every day, wakes at 5 and is asleep by 9:30. Tangen quoted his 2003 line, "my will to survive exceeds almost everybody else will to kill me". Advice: "think from first principles", discount most advice as opinion or dated assumptions, and learn to love the job you have.
