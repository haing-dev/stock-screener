### 318. Ken Griffin — Citadel as a research business, the US fiscal binge and AI hype (In Good Company, May 2024)

*Founder and CEO of Citadel and Citadel Securities, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen of Norges Bank Investment Management.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iQt9pgksZ3Y)

**Takeaway:** Griffin runs Citadel as a research business: "trading is simply how we monetize our research", and capital goes to the best risk-takers when the best opportunities appear. He warns that US fiscal stimulus at near full employment is feeding inflation and leaving less room for the next recession, and that most corporate "AI" stories are really digitalization.

- Origin: as a Harvard freshman he bought two Home Shopping Network put contracts on a Forbes thesis and made "$2,000 or $3,000". The market maker paid him less than intrinsic value, which led him to derivatives pricing and convertible bonds. Citadel started in Nov 1990 with one strategy, convertibles and warrants vs the common stock, priced with mathematics and software while most of the market used "paper pencils and rules of thumb".
- Learn from wins, not only losses: people "don't study their winners", though you make money in finance when you have winners.
- Edge: Citadel does best where it has "a clear competitive advantage in assimilating information processing that information and reacting to that information". Stock picking and weather forecasting for commodities are both research.
- Data fits the question: for airlines, prevailing airfares; for inflation, "breaking down every element of what the BLS looks for"; for commodities, possibly "a worldclass meteorology effort" on short-term weather. The aim is to find what drives a business's revenues or a commodity's demand, then process it quickly.
- Structure: verticals (global commodities, long/short equities, several credit businesses) whose heads act as entrepreneurs with a "clean sheet of paper", as if capital were unlimited. Griffin's job is talent and making sure "that we push Capital into the hands of our best Risk Takers when the best opportunities present themselves".
- PMs work in teams for healthier debate and need a repeatable process they can teach. Careers derail when, bluntly, "they're just not good Risk Takers": large, highly concentrated positions with no clear competitive advantage, and no fix even when shown the facts.
- Speed vs accuracy: the cost of changing your mind sets the pace. A 40-year real estate development needs thoroughness, but if you buy "$500 million of apple" and find you missed a salient fact, you can get out the next day. "sometimes it's more important to be nearly right and quick than it is to be absolutely right and slow". Gut feel counts only as subconscious analysis from many at-bats.
- Still unsolved at Citadel: multi-period optimization, i.e. building today's best portfolio from different views of a stock over the next day, week, month and year.
- Macro (May 2024): the US is near full employment with inflation "somewhere around 3 and change percent", yet the government is on "a massive spending binge". The stimulus is "creating hotter inflation than we would like" and leaves fewer degrees of freedom for the next recession. It also risks crowding out private capital and is "borrowing from our children our grandchildren and our great grandchildren".
- His fix is productivity: better K-12 education, people back in the office (Citadel works 5 days a week there; remote work cuts mentorship and leadership development), regulation that helps small and medium firms compete, and free trade between North America and Europe instead of protectionism.
- Bubbles are hard to see from inside because each brings its own justifying metrics (dot-com "eyeballs on pages"). The dot-com thesis proved right: "people just got carried away in valuations for a period of time". AI adoption should be quick, but when CEOs describe AI wins, in most stories "there's no AI involved at all"; it is digitalization, which AI has put back on the agenda.
- Citadel uses AI to draft emails, summarize research, tag data and make software engineers more productive. It has used machine learning for \~8–9 years; it "plays a very important role" in pricing assets and a small one in risk management.
- Passive investing works only if enough fundamental investors set prices, so traditional asset managers must stay viable. Individuals should hold a broad index fund or a broadly managed active fund; large pools like NBIM should index a large share, diversify across assets and split work between in-house teams and external managers by relative advantage.
- Citadel Securities trades almost 25% of all US equity volume, with \~$400bn of daily turnover across asset classes (May 2024). A market maker uses its capital so that a $50 million seller doesn't push the price unduly while it waits for the eventual buyer.
