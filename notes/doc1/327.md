### 327. Michael Miebach — Mastercard: the network in the middle, big tech as partner and threat, and fraud as a growth business (NBIM, Jan 2025)

*CEO of Mastercard, a former Citi banker (14 years) who spent \~10 years in the Middle East and Africa; his first podcast, with Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company".* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VDU4O3lFhgQ)

**Takeaway:** Mastercard sits between banks, merchants and every new payment player. It treats fintechs, big tech and buy-now-pay-later as growth drivers it can route through its network, as long as it moves fast enough that partners don't build around it.

- Scale (Jan 2025): Tangen cites more than 140bn transactions and over $9tn of volume last year; Miebach corrects him to "150 billion transactions". NBIM owns 1.3% of Mastercard, worth more than $6bn.
- Revenue mix: "core payments is about 60% of our Revenue"; the other 40% is payment-related services that make payments safer (fraud scoring, digital identity) or smarter (loyalty, faster payment for small businesses).
- Runway: cash is still common in developing economies and cheques in the US, and commercial payments are "a lot more clunky". Continental Europe lagged the US in digitization before the pandemic but has "dramatically caught up".
- Big tech: Apple "in theory could decide" to do payments itself, and he calls that absolutely possible. The lesson from Apple and Amazon is to move fast. The network must work for 27,000 banks in 220 countries and territories, and "if we hold them back then the likelihood increases that they're looking for Alternatives".
- Partnering pays: tokenization was born with Apple Pay and now runs at 1 billion transactions a month. Mastercard aims to end passwords and one-time passwords by 2030 in favour of biometrics.
- Fintechs and neobanks are mostly "a growth driver" because they digitize new transactions ("1 plus one is three"). Its Start Path incubator shows "where's smart money going". With ISVs, payment providers and processors in the chain, who is a competitor or partner is "completely blurred", and Mastercard is "hyper paranoid" about new rivals.
- Buy now, pay later (Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay): banks like lending tied to a purchase because the "cost of acquisition is much lower". Mastercard Installments lets any bank offer it at any merchant on the network. Investors had asked "isn't this the end of MasterCard".
- Digital currencies: blockchain's value is programmable payments that release only when conditions are met. The ECB's digital euro (pilot targeted for 2026) is welcome competition.
- Regions: Europe offers "the most choice" (MobilePay, Blik, Bizum, single-country card schemes), so Mastercard must be clearly differentiated there. China's QR-code payments grew in a "protected environment". Africa is leapfrogging to mobile financial services, and Mastercard invested $200 million in MTN's fintech spin-off.
- M&A rule: start from strategy and buy what Mastercard is "not fast enough or good enough" at building. Targets are cybersecurity, including the planned, "not yet approved" purchase of Recorded Future, "the world's number one threat intelligence company", and commercial payments.
- Fraud: Tangen puts global losses at nearly $500bn a year. Mastercard spent $8bn on cybersecurity over five years, has used AI for a decade and generative AI for \~3 years, and now predicts "the next likely fraudulent transaction". Partners, including governments, say "as a network you have more data than anybody".
- COVID test: the CEO handover was announced on 25 February 2020, two weeks before the US lockdown, and "a month later our Revenue fell off a cliff". With "no playbook for a pandemic", he built psychological safety. The behaviours Mastercard now rewards: "move fast grow together create value".
- Mastercard Foundation: at the 2006 IPO, 11% of the stock went into an independent foundation (\~$50bn of assets, per Tangen). A separate half-billion-dollar impact fund and a goal of bringing 1 billion people into the digital economy are framed as "smart business".
