### 328. Brad Smith — Regulating AI in layers, the US–China AI race and why trust pays (NBIM, Aug 2023)

*President of Microsoft, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen with Ulf Sverdrup, director of NUPI (the Norwegian Institute of International Affairs), on AI regulation and geopolitics.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KXGklwtwDa0)

**Takeaway:** Smith argues a listed company should back AI guardrails because losing public trust puts shareholders' investment at risk. He wants regulation built in layers that match AI's architecture and coordinated among like-minded democracies, and he puts the US "months not years ahead of China" in frontier models while China moves faster in deployment.

- Microsoft (Aug 2023) is NBIM's second-largest holding, worth roughly 40,000 kroner per Norwegian (Tangen).
- Why back regulation: "if you lose the Public's confidence you fundamentally I think put your investment and the interest of your investors at risk". Microsoft must "manage for the decade and the quarter simultaneously".
- Regulate in layers: applications (a bank using AI must still meet banking law), frontier models (safety standards, certification before release, perhaps licensing) and the data centers that run them (physical and cyber security).
- Lesson from social media: euphoria after the Arab Spring, then "within four or five years it had been weaponized". Agree first on a short list of problems, then coordinate fixes internationally.
- Coordination among like-minded democracies: the G7 plus India and Indonesia, or "the G20 minus China and Russia so it's sort of the G18". Narrow coalitions of governments, companies and civil society move faster (the 2018 Paris Call, the Christchurch Call).
- Europe: Sverdrup notes the EU plans to apply its risk-based AI Act by 2026. Smith's first reaction to the draft was "this is going to regulate Europe out of AI use", but he expects a balanced law. Europe's leverage: "it's difficult for any company in my view to Aspire to Global importance without being present in Europe".
- AI race (Aug 2023): three US groups (OpenAI with Microsoft, Google with DeepMind, Anthropic) lead frontier models, "months not years ahead of China". China leads narrow uses such as vision, built on huge face data, and "one should never underestimate for a moment how quickly China moves" in deployment. Biometric and genomic data favour China and the UAE.
- Narrow vs general models: instead of one model that does a thousand things, someone can build "a thousand different models that each does one thing well", which is cheaper and less compute-intensive.
- Revenue by politics: \~95% of Microsoft's revenue comes from democracies (by the Economist Intelligence Unit's ranking) and 5% from the rest. China is only \~1.5% of revenue, controls stop the Chinese military from using its technology, and Microsoft is basically absent from Russia.
- What shareholders should expect: the aim is to "Remain the most trusted company for what has become the most important technology in the world", because "a tech company is not entirely different from a bank": it must be trusted for decades.
- Copilot is meant to help people think "better faster more efficiently", not to replace them. Developers who use it to write code are more productive and happier because it removes drudgery.
- Security: bad actors will use AI (Russia runs disinformation in 23 languages), but AI helps defenders more, as in Ukraine, "where defense has proven to be stronger than offense". Endpoint protection pushes a fix to every device "in somewhere between minutes to a couple of hours". His deadline for election defenses was 1 Jan 2024.
- Standards first, then law: build on NIST's AI risk management framework, and have the US government buy AI only from companies that self-certify to it. The model is the bus emergency brake: one common standard that national or local laws build on, here "safety brakes" for AI that runs the electrical grid.
