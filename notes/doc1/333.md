### 333. Alexander Stubb — Who decides the new world order: the Global South, AI power and a more European NATO (NBIM, Sep 2026)

*President of Finland (commander-in-chief and in charge of foreign and security policy), a former prime minister, who wrote a book this year on how the old order fell apart; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's In Good Company.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZbzN8Ir6cIo)

**Takeaway:** The world order is resetting as it did in 1918, 1945 and 1989, and the Global South will choose between rules-based cooperation and "multipolar transactional chaos and conflict". Stubb's answer is "values-based realism": keep your values, compromise for results, rearm Europe and engage pragmatically with Trump (Sep 2026).

- A reset like 1918, 1945 and 1989: Western dominance is over, but the East won't dominate either. The Global South has the population, economy and technology to decide between multilateral norms and "multipolar transactional chaos and conflict"; he wants a reformed UN in which the South has agency.
- The West's error was an "innate arrogance that history ended". China "played the long strategic game much better than we Europeans" in Africa, where a Western leader lectures against China from a Chinese-built airport, highway and hotel. Europe needs a "more dignified" foreign policy: less finger-wagging, more concrete cooperation.
- Tangen raises a fourth, technology bloc built on compute, data and AI, from a piece Stubb and Mark Carney wrote this summer. Stubb says it's "not really a fourth block as such" but would now call his framework a "rectangle of power", with a "global north" the US no longer wants to lead. AI will change work, politics, war and science; dominating it takes compute and electricity, and he wants access treated as win-win, not a transaction.
- Big tech vs states: democracies can't yet cope with social media and modern technology, "they're losing the struggle", and he asks whether Anthropic or Facebook is more powerful than Norway and Finland. Regulation runs "one, two, three years behind", so he wants control bodies that start in the US, then Europe and China, and end at the UN; US–China cooperation will come "out of necessity", given a balance in critical minerals, compute and chips.
- "Values-based realism": hold to democracy, rule of law and human rights, but global problems need compromise. In diplomacy "you look for win-win situations", in business it is often zero-sum, and "the world will not work on transactions".
- Three paths: continued transactional chaos with more wars and ad hoc "unholy alliances"; a loose multipolar world or regional blocs (EU, ASEAN, Mercosur, African Union); or his ideal, regional bodies joined in a reformed UN, with Security Council seats for at least one more country from Latin America, two from Africa and one more from Asia.
- Finland never disarmed: outside NATO after the Cold War, it bought 64 F-18 fighters, kept conscription and bought artillery second-hand, because of the wars of 1939–44. That made it so NATO-ready that after Russia attacked Ukraine in 2022 it had "the quickest accession negotiations in the history of the alliance".
- NATO is "in a transition", not a crisis: spending is rising "from 2% to 5%" and "defense industries are booming all around Europe and the United States". Europe takes over conventional forces (land, air, navy, cyber, space, stockpiles) and must replace some capabilities the US won't provide "during peace times nor in a crisis situation".
- Hybrid war: "the line between war and peace has been blurred". Cut cables, airspace violations, GPS jamming and sabotage are Russian intimidation, but not yet an Article 5 matter.
- Ukraine (Sep 2026): he expects "a defense victory" for Ukraine, now doing better militarily, politically and financially than at any point in the war. It won't end through Russia's "economic problems" or "bank runs" or battlefield losses, but "if the current regime feels a pressure from the population"; over half of Russians now want it over, as refinery strikes bring fuel rationing. Prepare "for a cold winter in Ukraine".
- Europe: "I've never seen Europe more united". Brexit, COVID vaccines, Ukraine, energy shortages and a tough US administration ("pressure from the east and pressure from the west") made it geopolitical. Under tariff pressure the EU closed long-delayed trade deals with India, Mercosur, Japan and South Korea; he'd rather see Canada "as the 28th member state of the EU rather than the 51st state".
- Trump is "one of the most consequential presidents in American history", and what Europeans won't admit is that he "is very effective in what he does". Engage where you can move things (NATO, Ukraine): the US will buy 11 icebreakers, four built in Finland and seven in the US.
- Small-country power: "influence comes from information and information comes from networks and networks come from personal relationships". His biggest failure was taking attacks personally as prime minister; now he separates the office from the person. Working 20 hours a day "That's working stupid".
