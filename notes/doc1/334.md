### 334. Marc Rowan — Highlights: private credit, indexed markets and holding back on risk (NBIM, Nov 2024)

*Highlights cut of Nicolai Tangen's In Good Company interview with Marc Rowan, co-founder and CEO of Apollo Global Management.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p67KL7Yhj50)

**Takeaway:** Most private credit is investment grade, public markets are driven by index flows rather than price discovery, and Apollo's rule is "purchase price matters". In Nov 2024 that meant staying senior in the capital structure rather than reaching for risk.

- Apollo had $40bn of AUM in 2008 and over $700bn "13 14 years later". Rowan credits being positioned in front of "fundamental change", not skill alone.
- Private credit covers "almost every asset on a bank balance sheet" and is mostly investment grade; leveraged direct lending is "a very small part of our business".
- Public markets are "indexed and correlated": 60%+ passive, 10 stocks are 39% of the S&P 500, and active managers have failed to beat the index 90%+ of the time for 20 years. Investors "may not own private Equity but you will own Equity that is private"; the next five years are about "fixed income replacement".
- "purchase price matters": in private equity Apollo trades hard work for a better price; in credit it seeks excess return per unit of risk. Now (Nov 2024) reaching down the capital structure "feels like a bad bad idea today", while senior positions paid for origination and structure are "a tremendously good use of time".
- The US setup looks good: unspent fiscal stimulus (a nearly $2 trillion infrastructure bill, $52bn for chip plants, the IRA), three years as the top recipient of foreign direct investment, and 500bp of hikes after which "home prices went up stocks went up". The worry is "a $2 trillion deficit in peace time with a 4% unemployment", a long-term rather than short-term risk.
