### 340. Daniel Pink — Highlights: regrets, timing and the key to good breaks (NBIM, Dec 2024)

*Short cut of Nicolai Tangen's In Good Company interview with Daniel Pink, author of The Power of Regret, Drive and When.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A-WMaLVwOW8)

**Takeaway:** Learn from regret instead of denying it, and let evidence set your daily routine: analytic work in the morning, nothing critical in the afternoon trough, and breaks that are social, moving, outside and phone-free.

- The no-regrets creed is "profoundly bad advice". His survey of \~26,000 people in 134 countries found the same four core regrets everywhere. One is the foundation regret: small early choices, none of them cataclysmic, that add up, as in "I spent too much and saved too little".
- Talking or writing about a regret turns a "Blobby" feeling into concrete words you can analyse. Admitting mistakes rarely costs respect: in many cases people "actually think more of us".
- Silver medallists look less happy than bronze medallists. Upward counterfactuals ("if only") feel worse but can help you do better; downward ones ("at least") console, like the bronze medallist who beat fourth place.
- CEOs probably don't have stronger drive; they may just be better at politics, tall, male or more conformist. Founders and CEOs who rose through the ranks are "different species".
- For the 80% who are not night owls, do analytic work early, when vigilance peaks; early to mid-afternoon is "generally a terrible time of day" (more errors in healthcare, so no discretionary afternoon procedures in his family). The best break is a 15–20 minute afternoon walk with someone you like, without your phone, talking about anything but work.
