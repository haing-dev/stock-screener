### 343. Sam Altman — OpenAI after ChatGPT: a handful of base models, copyable leads and fast-depreciating capex (NBIM, Sep 2023)

*Co-founder and CEO of OpenAI (\~500 staff in 2023), maker of ChatGPT and GPT-4, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O77UyYK51s4)

**Takeaway:** Altman expects "extremely powerful systems" by the end of the decade and only a handful of massive pre-trained models, with most players fine-tuning on top. A model's capex depreciates fast and a lead like GPT-4 can be copied once people know it is possible, so the lasting edge is researchers who can find "the next Hill to climb".

- Method: what AI becomes has to be answered "empirically", by deploying it and co-evolving it with society; he cites Alan Kay, "the best way to predict the future is to invent it". OpenAI could see "the next few years" clearly, with a roadmap.
- The surprise was the breadth of use despite flaws: models "hallucinate a lot" and are "not very smart", yet developers spend about half their time in ChatGPT and feel 2–3x as productive. A one-person business owner told him "I got a virtual employee in every category".
- AGI: he expects "by the end of this decade" "extremely powerful systems that change the way we currently think about the world". His personal AGI test, a system that finds new science humans couldn't, "may take a little bit longer". The risk to the view: "we could be drinking our own Kool-Aid we could hit a brick wall soon", though he sees a smooth exponential "that has much much further to go".
- Constraints: chips matter, but people are "even more of a constraint". Once people know something is possible "a lot of people replicate it", so what counts is reaching GPT-6 and 7 and finding "the next Hill to climb". Better algorithms, better chips and more chips "multiply together".
- Capex: Tangen asked how to justify pre-training spend that lasts a short time. Altman doesn't expect "as many massive pre-trained models in the world as people think", just "a handful" with many fine-tuning on top. GPT-4 "does depreciate fast", but "you're not just depreciating the capex one time for the model": the research becomes "new IP" for the next one. He would not give training costs beyond "quite a lot".
- Who profits: chipmakers "will end up with profits", but not all of them; "many people" will share "this massive economic boon". He expected many good model makers, with Google "racing to catch up" (2023).
- Access: GPT-4, "the most capable model in the world" as far as he knew, is open to anyone at "very cheap API rates" except a handful of embargoed countries, and integrations (carmakers adding voice control) are "staggering". ChatGPT usage "took like a little bit of a flatline during the summer" but was up (2023).
- Microsoft: "they build us computers we train models and then we both use them". The firms are aligned at the top, and "no contract in the world is what makes a partnership good"; working problems out with Satya Nadella and Kevin Scott is.
- Regulation: self-regulation can't do it all, and governments can do "anything they want". He expects Europe to regulate AI "but reasonably"; disclosing that you're talking to a bot is "super reasonable". Global rules should cover only systems that could cause "Grievous harm to the entire world", as with nuclear weapons: not GPT-4, but a future "gp10".
- Productivity targets: Tangen had asked NBIM staff for 10% higher productivity in 12 months; Altman called it "under ambitious". With 15–20% or more of NBIM's staff writing code, he judged 20% "appropriately ambitious": "it's better to set a goal that is like slightly too ambitious than significantly under ambitious".
- Risk: a failed startup means going back to a job, while staying 20 years in a job without progressing "seems catastrophically risky". Europe lags on fear of failure, which Silicon Valley tolerates. His own startup (age 19–26) failed, and "no one but me spent much time thinking about it".
- Why Silicon Valley stopped doing research: "it got so easy to make a super valuable company" and people grew "so impatient on timelines and return Horizons" that capital went to applying the internet and mobile to every industry. OpenAI began as a research lab and "bolted on a product company"; researchers get a high-level direction and "a huge amount of Freedom".
- Leadership: new senior leaders fail at hiring and developing teams, communicating vision and thinking strategically, so he writes down how each will "screw up" and revisits it at 3 and 6 months. Delegation means "high quality people" and "setting the training wheels at the right height"; for big decisions he talks to \~50 people.
- Beyond AI: abundance needs the costs of intelligence and energy brought "way down"; he expects fusion to work "very soon", at under a penny per kWh. Crypto has "so far failed to deliver on that promise".
