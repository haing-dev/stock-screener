### 344. Satya Nadella — Microsoft: "no such thing as a franchise value", the full AI stack and a learn-it-all culture (NBIM, Mar 2024)

*CEO of Microsoft, then the world's most valuable company, in his 32nd year there, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNuLPWu38IU)

**Takeaway:** Nadella treats AI as his fourth platform shift, after PC/client-server, the web and mobile/cloud, in which "there's no such thing as a franchise value" and every past win is up for grabs. Microsoft's answer is to compete at every layer of the AI stack, a bet placed early through OpenAI, and a "learn it all" culture that keeps a giant from turning complacent.

- Franchise value: in tech "there's no such thing as a franchise value", so "even the battles we won and the battles we lost are all up for grab again". Early 2024 was "year two" of AI, his fourth platform shift; each shift grew out of the last (he first saw Mosaic as a browser on Windows) and then went beyond it.
- Size of the prize: tech spend is \~4–5% of GDP. If AI lifts it to \~10% over 5–10 years, the real question is what happens to "the other 90%": breakthroughs in health care, materials and the energy transition. He likens AI to Britain spending 10% of GDP on railroads in the Industrial Revolution, whose payoff was the whole economy.
- OpenAI: Microsoft backed it "long before it was conventional wisdom", looking as always for "high ambition" partners (Intel with Windows, SAP with SQL Server); in tech "you have to be all in and hope it works". Google is formidable as the "vertically integrated player", from data and silicon to models and distribution, and the partnership adds competition where Google would have been "the default winner".
- The full stack: the best AI infrastructure on Azure for training and inference (Nvidia, AMD and Microsoft's own silicon, serving OpenAI, Mistral and its own small Phi models); a rebuilt data tier (retrieval, embeddings, vector search, longer context); and Copilots for code, Microsoft 365 and service, sales and finance. His confidence began with GPT-3 to 3.5 inside GitHub Copilot. A platform company must accept rivals at every layer and "keep each layer competitive on its own".
- Scaling: "we definitely are not going to bet against scaling laws", but nor will Microsoft assume today's architecture is the last. Its Phi small models show that curated data ("textbooks is all you need") can deliver strong capability with far less compute.
- Sovereign models: citing Dartmouth economist Diego Comin's study of technology diffusion, countries that get ahead "don't reinvent the wheel": they import the best general-purpose technology and add value on top. For Norway, fine-tune existing foundation models before building compute and pre-training: "start from the value ad and then back into whatever is needed".
- Geopolitics: nation-states hold the power. A US multinational must "earn permission and license to operate one country at a time" by improving public-sector efficiency, local firms' competitiveness, education, health and small-business productivity.
- Science: quantum is "the ultimate breakthrough" for simulation, and AI acts as an "emulator of that simulator". With the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, Microsoft's materials model found, simulated and produced a battery material with 70% less lithium.
- Gaming: with Activision, Microsoft is a full-stack game publisher and platform, and gaming "on a secular basis is probably going to be if not already the biggest entertainment category". Microsoft's DirectX graphics stack helped make GPUs accelerators. His rule for any business: "it has to be an end otherwise it's not a business".
- Culture: when Microsoft first became the most valuable company (around the early 2000s, he recalls), staff thought "we must be God's gifts" instead of regrounding to innovate. Drawing on Carol Dweck's growth mindset, he made the goal to be "a learn it all versus a know it all", which worked because it spoke to people as humans, not as "a topdown slogan".
- Empathy drives innovation: it means meeting customers' "unmet unarticulated needs", and "design thinking is applied empathy"; he learned it as the father of a son born with cerebral palsy. Following Colin Mayer, a business must create a "profitable solution" to the challenges of people and planet, so empathy and execution are no trade-off.
- Leadership and advice: he starts each day from what he is not good at, and wants confidence and humility, "not hubris". His father's diary logged people met, ideas generated and tasks completed. "never wait for your next job to do your best work", and define your job broadly.
