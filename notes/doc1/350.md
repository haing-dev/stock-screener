### 350. Evan Spiegel — Ecosystems over features, Specs glasses and AI-written code at Snap (NBIM, Jun 2026)

*Co-founder and CEO of Snap (Snapchat), interviewed by Nicolai Tangen.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i4LjivMgK9w)

**Takeaway:** Snap's features keep getting copied, so Spiegel builds moats out of ecosystems and long hardware cycles, and he credits founder tenure with letting Snap invest consistently for 12+ years in AR glasses. He sees close-friends messaging as slower-growing but more durable than broadcast social media, and says Snap's code is now mostly written by AI agents (Jun 2026).

- Origin: social media saved everything publicly, so people posted only "pretty and perfect moments". Snapchat built visual messaging between close friends and family, with privacy as "the foundation of self-expression". An earlier project, Future Freshman, failed: "Only my mom signed up".
- At 22 he turned down a $3bn offer from Mark Zuckerberg (Tangen), because the founders saw "a huge long-term opportunity" in a vision unlike traditional social media. Snapchat now reaches nearly a billion people a day, including more than 3 million Norwegians.
- Innovation engine: a design team of about nine people, totally flat, reviews hundreds of ideas a week, and the culture rewards creativity and risk-taking rather than promotion. Stories grew out of users asking for a send-to-all button: posts that vanish after 24 hours, with no public likes and in chronological order. Early uptake was minimal.
- Being copied (Instagram took Stories, WhatsApp disappearing messages) is "a heck of a lot better than coming up with ideas that nobody wants to take", but it forces a moat. His answer is ecosystems: Snap has what he calls the largest AR platform, with developer tools, because "as you build an ecosystem, that's much harder to copy than an app feature".
- Specs AR glasses: people spend "seven or eight hours a day hunched over a screen", so "We need to fundamentally change what a computer is". Consumer-grade Specs launch later in 2026 after more than 12 years of steady investment (camera glasses, a second camera, a display, its own operating system and developer ecosystem). Developers built hundreds of lenses for the 2024 developer edition.
- Why Snap can win where bigger players failed: rivals "turned their initiatives on and off uh abruptly" and pivoted between consumer and enterprise, which damages long-term R&D.
- Hardware horizon: Snap plans hardware through 2030. When a product ships, it is already years into the next generation, so competitors are "only seeing what we worked on several years ago". "hardware is always harder than software", which makes it one of the most compelling places to invest.
- Three-to-five-year plan: more than a billion users; revenue diversified into subscriptions and direct revenue alongside advertising; Specs moving from early adopters to a mass-market glasses product by the end of the decade.
- Growth vs durability: "communication services just grow slower than social media because social media is broadcast-based", but communication is more durable, and that durability has built long-term value. Friend groups shrink with age, which suits a close-friends product as users grow up.
- AI in software (Jun 2026): the way software is written has changed profoundly. "It's mostly written by agents. I would be stunned if humans are writing any code at all by next year." The shift from AI as a tool to engineers overseeing agents happened "in basically 6 months". A tool for advertisers to find creators, which Snap could not fund before, was built in weeks.
- AI and management: AI will automate feedback and career planning, so spans of control could double from a traditional 7–10 direct reports, with less hierarchy. The test of any hierarchy is "how fast is bad news travel". The 2022 restructuring cut staff by \~20% (Tangen), and the culture took time to recover.
- Long tenure as an edge: CEO since 21 and now 35, he notes "the tenure of public company CEOs is incredibly short today", while real R&D takes far longer than 4 or 7 years.
- Values are kind, smart and creative, and kindness is what makes creativity possible. Advice: try many things while young, when "the switching costs are very low".
