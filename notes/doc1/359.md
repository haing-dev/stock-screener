### 359. Andrew Forrest — Fortescue: breaking the iron-ore oligopoly, "crazy brave plan A" and going fossil-free by 2030 (NBIM, Jul 2026)

*Founder and chairman of Fortescue, the Australian iron-ore miner he started in 2003 (Tangen introduces him as chairman and CEO); interviewed by Nicolai Tangen in Oslo.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5pJIbhziKjU)

**Takeaway:** Forrest built Fortescue from "a mortgage" by taking on BHP, Rio Tinto and Vale, sizing the project around the biggest ship loader and staying focused on iron ore. He now bets that cutting out diesel and running on renewables by 2030 will make Fortescue, already the lowest-cost producer per ton by his account, cheaper still, and argues that energy subsidies, diesel tax credits above all, keep rivals from following.

- Anaconda Nickel, his first company: a $1.4bn financing for a new nickel-cobalt process (up to 45,000 t of nickel and 3,500 t of cobalt). The builder, Fluor, guaranteed it at a lump-sum fixed price but "took some shortcuts", and basic parts (acid plant, power station, pressure let-down system) failed. Glencore took it over; it is now Glencore's purest nickel and cobalt supply. Lesson: "Never rely on contracts. rely on people".
- Fortescue (2003) began as a "concept stock" when Forrest had little more than a mortgage. BHP, Rio Tinto and Vale owned their own ports and railways, underused but "used as a barrier to entry"; he offered a shared port and rail system, and when the incumbents refused to use it he became a producer himself, "several times harder". BHP and Rio had controlled Australian iron ore for 60 years.
- Sizing: the binding constraint was a ship loader, not the rail line or port. Loaders then did 25 million tons a year and a new design arguably 40 million, enough to justify the port, railway and mines behind it: "it had to be big and that's what sized it". Financing terms kept changing, and a cyclone killed three people, two of them on site.
- "crazy brave plan A": anyone may try something no one has done, but only with "a bulletproof plan B", so that when plan A fails, as it usually will, the company is not at risk.
- Focus: rivals moved into copper and other metals; Fortescue stayed in iron ore, "the most important mineral in the world", and "focus focus focus saved us many times". Staff with nickel, zinc or copper ideas were told to pursue them elsewhere.
- China: the incumbents told steel mills they would lose supply if they dealt with Fortescue; some big mills and many smaller ones broke ranks, enough to get started. China is "a contact sport", driven by parents who farmed to survive; he expects the next generation to be less determined.
- Fossil-free by 2030: after a PhD in marine ecology begun in 2016, at 54, he set out to change a miner in the "hard to abate" sector. In 2022 Fortescue committed to using no fossil fuel by 2030, with no offsets, and he says it is "on track and probably ahead of track". By then his third of the company was worth \~$20bn (a $60bn company).
- The economics: rivals will follow once Fortescue saves a billion dollars a year by eliminating a billion litres of diesel (or equivalent). It already has "the lowest cost already per ton", and fully green its costs will be "out of reach of any competition".
- Oil and sovereignty: a former Saudi oil minister warned "never let the oil price get over $80" a barrel or oil would be replaced. Forrest says a reconstruction of the energy market "is upon us", with orders and interest in Fortescue's electrification up "10 20 30x", and "until you make your own energy, you can't really claim you're a free sovereign country".
- Subsidies: take them off all energy and let it compete. Diesel fuel tax credits hand a dozen big miners $2.5bn a year to compete with motorists for scarce diesel, and give his rivals an excuse not to go green.
- AI: with operating costs of several billion a year, AI should save "hundreds and hundreds of millions of dollars a year". It already runs Fortescue's autonomous haul trucks and a green grid 600 km long and nearly 500 km wide with gigawatts of storage, where AI and distributed batteries replace the rotary inertia of big turbines.
- Culture: 10 written values, led by humility, including "Crocodile closest to your canoe" (fix the nearest problem and stay on course). Let go of strong performers who are poor colleagues: "they make sure no one else does".
- Philanthropy: his foundation (set up 25 years ago) works on oceans, rainforests, AI safety between the US and China, and modern slavery, measured in its Global Slavery Index. He and his wife are giving their capital to philanthropy rather than leaving it to their children.
