### 363. Peter Carlsson — Northvolt: batteries as a scale business, Chinese competition and the cost of a Western supply chain (NBIM, Feb 2024)

*CEO of Northvolt, the Swedish battery maker he started in 2016, and a former Tesla supply-chain and operations executive; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen in Stockholm.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40GHmhSUPOc)

**Takeaway:** Carlsson says batteries are a scale business where "it is big or go home": China has a 5–10 year head start and handles 80–85% of lithium processing, so Northvolt competes on customization for carmakers, recycling and new chemistries such as sodium-ion. The price of entry is steep: $70–90m per GWh of capacity, 200–300 GWh to matter ($20–25bn) and 300–400 strong process engineers per factory.

- Mission: battery supply chains are energy-intensive with a big carbon footprint, so Northvolt builds on renewable power with recycling built in. In 10 years he wants it fully circular (at least 50% of output from recycled material), the Western leader in Europe and North America, and growing in India, the Middle East and Africa with sodium-ion.
- China: "it is big or go home", and China has "a 5 to 10 year head start" in scale, strategic positions from mining to vehicles and formidable operations. Head-on competition is hard, so Northvolt stretches the technology, recycles and customizes.
- Not a commodity: five or six years ago carmakers saw a battery as a "black box"; now it is a chemistry system tuned to the brand (cold-weather performance for Volvo, fast charging for Porsche). Commodity 280 Ah LFP cells are very cheap from China, but cells for cars that compete with Tesla are not commodities, and Northvolt bids against CATL, LG and Samsung. Its carmaker contracts were essential to proceed and to convince investors "we will not go the way solar cells went".
- Talent: system architects who understand both the chemistry and the manufacturing process are worth their weight in gold; fewer than 100 people in the world have that full view, and Northvolt hired a handful, mainly from Asia. Each factory needs 300–400 very good process engineers, the industry's big bottleneck; Northvolt grew from 25 to 6,000 people in 6 years.
- Capital: "70 to 90 million US dollar per gigawatt hour", and at least 200–300 GWh to matter, so \~$20–25bn over time: "this is not for the poor people". It also takes a scalable, repeatable blueprint for lines and factories.
- Tesla lessons: a "super super clear Mission" breeds passion and loyalty. When early cars each lost money, Musk had the whole bill of materials (\~3,500 part numbers) analysed on the "cost of atoms" against the price paid. Carlsson still assumes any budget or timeline can be cut 30–40%.
- Nordic rules: too many regulations for a scale-up, and in Sweden one can hardly operate without a collective bargaining agreement (Tesla tried). A Chinese rival he visited runs two shifts, "12 hours a day six days a week"; Northvolt needs five shifts to fill a factory, so he wants more flexible working hours. Sites abroad are about proximity to customers and talent, not rules.
- Why batteries: meeting Europe's Paris goals (\~80% CO2 cut over three decades) means electrifying transport and power, and wind and solar make grids less stable, so storage is crucial. Stockholm's grid is maxed out from 9:00 to 14:00 and may need \~50% more peak capacity as commuters charge EVs; storage in the city beats digging in more copper.
- Lithium: plentiful (hard rock in Canada, Australia, Serbia and Portugal; South American brines, whose desert evaporation ponds worry him; new direct-extraction methods emerging), but 80–85% of processing is in China. Northvolt formed a joint venture with Galp in Portugal for one of Europe's first lithium refineries.
- Regional market: batteries are heavy, hazardous to ship and needed just in time, so the business will regionalize. Carmakers will set battery-supply strategies in the next couple of years and "take 10 years to execute them"; if Europe misses the window, the infrastructure and capital will be locked in elsewhere, as with solar panels and microchips.
- Chemistry: LFP (roughly half the energy density of high-performance nickel cells) grew on fast Chinese execution and suits storage and cheaper cars, but would leave Northvolt "almost 100% dependent on Chinese active material", with a large carbon footprint and little recycling value. Its sodium-ion cell with a Prussian blue cathode gives LFP-type performance without nickel or iron-based cathodes, survives heating towards 300° without thermal runaway, and should scale in 2026–27.
- Recycling: dismantle and fully discharge packs, crush and sort them into black mass, then separate it hydrometallurgically (furnaces lose the lithium), recovering "over 90 towards 95%" of nickel, cobalt and lithium. Northvolt is commissioning Europe's largest recycling facility, a 4 GWh plant.
- Leadership: the core words are bold, passionate and excellent; "you cannot run at maximum speeds all the time", but sprint at bottlenecks. With 125 nationalities, bridging Asian hierarchy and the flat Nordic model takes time. He gives people responsibility until they show they can't handle it, judges managers on results and on how they attract talent, goes to the source of problems and decides with 80% of the data.
- Screen implication: a pre-scale battery maker needs tens of billions of outside capital and years of ramp-up before it earns a return, the opposite of the self-funded leaders the tech screen looks for; scale, contracts and engineering talent are manual checks.
- Context (added Oct 2026, not from the talk): Northvolt filed for Chapter 11 protection in the US in Nov 2024 and for bankruptcy in Sweden in Mar 2025.
