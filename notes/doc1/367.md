### 367. Bjørn Gulden — Adidas: performance product, street culture and a fast, decentralized turnaround (NBIM, Sep 2023)

*CEO of Adidas, back after 10 years there in the 1990s and 10 years as CEO of Puma; a former professional footballer, interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdWtvbkSJlY)

**Takeaway:** Gulden says Adidas lost focus when China, COVID, the Russian war, an aggressive direct-to-consumer plan and the loss of the Yeezy line hit at once. His fix is the old Adidas DNA: the best performance product, translated into lifestyle and street culture, which is the bigger market, run with speed and local decisions.

- Diagnosis: Adidas "was slow in in reacting" to China slowing, COVID and the Russian war. Its direct-to-consumer (DTC) push came with "a very aggressive sales plan and also I would say aggressive profit plan" that "didn't really fit the landscape". Losing Kanye West's Yeezy line came on top: "many things that happened at once", and the company "lost Focus".
- The fix: "to be the best sports brand", "mirror performance with Street culture" and be "very very focused on product marketing and the consumer". "on PowerPoint everything is is easy", but people take time; he is "extremely optimistic about our midterm" (Sep 2023). Per Tangen, the share price "went up by 30" when Gulden got the job.
- Brand recipe: first the right performance product and technology (Spain had just won the Women's World Cup in Adidas), then athletes, teams and federations for exposure. Then "to make money you need to translate that into a lifestyle": the lifestyle and street market is "much bigger than the sports markets".
- Follow the consumer: "you cannot decide what is relevant for the consumer you need to pick up Trends and then commercialize it". Influencers can be very local, from a pop act in Korea to a designer in Sweden.
- Product heat: the Samba, an old football shoe, became "probably the hottest" shoe in the world within six months, so "demand is much bigger than Supply" (Sep 2023).
- Partner risk: Yeezy was "one of the most successful collabs in the in the in the history", lost when Adidas broke the contract after West's statements. "when you work with third parties that could happen".
- Speed: in the pandemic and the freight crisis, supply chains "got 10 times more expensive" and some producing markets shut. "when the reality surpasses your plan your plan is not relevant anymore". He would rather staff decide and be wrong than wait, and hates "ending a meeting with another meeting".
- Decentralize: consumer tastes in China and the West, and in the US and Europe, have drifted further apart, so Adidas runs a matrix "more locally focused" than a central hierarchy. Headquarters is the sum of its offices in Germany, LA, Portland and Shanghai: "a global company that happened to have roots in Germany".
- AI already does work "where we used to have a lot of people" (product text, page and marketing optimization) and is starting to shape decisions on launch volumes. It can "take subjectivity out of many decisions", but needs human judgment alongside.
- Innovation that matters most is in materials and product: lighter materials, carbon plates in running-shoe midsoles and materials not made from oil. E-commerce, a channel that did not exist 15 years ago, "has been huge".
- People first: "most businesses are people business", so his biggest role "is actually HR". He gave his phone number to all 60,000 employees, gets "a couple of hundred direct messages every week" and holds a monthly town hall for all staff.
- Risk: "I would not gamble with a business that I don't own". As a public-company CEO he acts "on behalf of the shareholders", so he takes less risk at work than in private.
- Advice: learn accounting (balance sheet, expense vs capex, cash flow vs P&L) "because it's the base for everything"; otherwise marketing and sales people get "manipulated by Finance people". Kotler's four Ps are still "as relevant as they were".
