### 369. Christopher Nassetta — Hilton: consistency plus service, an asset-light model and Blackstone's record buyout (NBIM, Nov 2023)

*CEO of Hilton since he came in with Blackstone's 2007 buyout; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JIl-2HBArV0)

**Takeaway:** Hilton doesn't own its hotels, so it wins by delivering premium market share and returns to the owners: consistent product, friendly service, loyalty and technology earn a price premium that home-sharing can't match. Nassetta sees a "golden age of travel" (Nov 2023) and credits Blackstone's patient, doubled-down buyout for turning an undermanaged laggard into the fastest grower among the majors.

- Scale (per Tangen, Nov 2023): the world's second-largest hotel group, in 123 countries, with more than 7,000 properties and 1.3 million rooms; NBIM owns 1% (\~$300 million). Nassetta cites 7,300 hotels.
- What makes a hotel: location first, then product quality and amenities, then service, "the real uh differentiator". Customers want "a consistent high quality experience" and service that doesn't treat them "like an honors number or a room number". Get both right and "they will be very very loyal" to a brand.
- Five-minute test: he says he "can tell within five minutes of walking into a hotel" where it stands on customer satisfaction, and is "very very rarely wrong". He checks whether the product is modern, fresh and clean, and whether the team is happy and attentive to guests.
- Basics beat flourishes: Hilton's best hospitality "is not that exciting it's getting the basics right". AI and other tools mine customer feedback worldwide; the number one complaint, especially in the US (still the largest market), is the quality and quantity of towels, so a new terry program is rolling out across the system (Nov 2023). He recently sat through 12 hours of focus groups.
- The model: "we don't own the real estate", so Hilton must "drive the premium market share and the premium returns to owners". He calls it "one of the best business models on Earth".
- Airbnb is "a good business" but serves "a different trip occasion". It is "a value play" for leisure groups who want more space, kitchens and longer stays, without consistent quality, amenities, service, connecting technology or loyalty. Those are what earn Hilton "a premium".
- Demand: "we are entering what I would say is a golden age of travel" (Nov 2023). COVID sped up the shift to spending on experiences rather than things, and leisure, business and group travel are all strong, the opposite of forecasts that travel would be permanently altered.
- Airfares haven't hurt. The pre-COVID mix of two-thirds flying and one-third driving has flipped and stuck ("still two thirds of people are driving"). Inflation also lifted incomes: "everything's moved up including profitability and people's pay".
- Blackstone's 2007 buyout: Hilton, then 85+ years old, was a very middle-of-the-road performer, fifth to seventh among the majors on growth, and to Nassetta "the most undermanaged company we had ever seen". Blackstone put in $5.6 billion, then "another billion" plus in the Great Recession, when headlines said it had lost $5.5–6 billion. He believes it is still "the largest private Equity profit in the history of private Equity".
- The fix: culture, brands (nine then, 22 today), technology and innovation, which made Hilton "the number one" grower among the majors. Blackstone's mantra is "buy it fix it and sell it", but it gave him free rein plus help on strategy, HR, branding and global relationships. Blackstone's Jon Gray is still chairman, though Blackstone "sold out many years ago".
- Leadership is "culture and strategy" with "a steady hand on the wheel": "it's really easy to jerk the wheel" when generative AI and other "shiny objects" flash by. Chase them all and you do "a lot of things and none of them very well". His advice to his younger self: pull the big levers, have a "spectacular strategy" and a resilient balance sheet, and grind.
- Delegate "almost everything"; a CEO who can't has a style problem or the wrong team. "what got you from A to B is is probably not what's going to get you from B to C". The pitfall is comfort, the belief that "if it worked for the last five years it'll work for the next five years". He is retooling teams in technology, innovation and lifestyle brands (Nov 2023).
- Paris Hilton: ignoring her large following was "silly", so since about four or five years ago she appears in Hilton's campaigns, TV ads and social media, DJs for it and advises on lifestyle brands.
