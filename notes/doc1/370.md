### 370. Debra Crew — Diageo: drink better not more, a cyclical squeeze and Scotch's maturation moat (NBIM, Mar 2025)

*CEO of Diageo (Johnnie Walker, Guinness, Smirnoff, Baileys, Tanqueray, Don Julio) and former CEO of Reynolds American; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LHjUSZnjQFk)

**Takeaway:** Crew calls the spirits slump (Mar 2025) cyclical, a squeeze on discretionary wallets from prolonged inflation, on top of slow moderation: volume per head has drifted down since 2012 while value grows because people "drink better not more". Diageo leans into premium brands (\~62% premium-plus vs \~35% for the industry), faster innovation and small formats.

- Runway: total beverage alcohol has grown "consistently for 20 plus years", and alcohol takes "less than 2%" of discretionary spending, a steady share. Now (Mar 2025) the industry is "quite challenged": cyclical pressure and "prolonged inflation" have squeezed discretionary wallets and volumes. "it's cyclical it's going to come back".
- Moderation: 70% of households drink, but "volume per caps actually peaked in 2012" and has fallen slightly since, by about "a bottle of wine". Value keeps growing because "people want to drink better not more", and spirits per head held up by taking share from beer and wine, including at meals.
- Gen Z moderates less than people think. "zebra striping" means a Guinness, then a Guinness 0.0, staying with the brand. They also come "into Spirits faster" through ready-to-drink (RTD) cans, tequila and spirits with food.
- Occasions are spreading: more socializing at home (a trend that predates COVID) and in "third spaces" such as stadiums and festivals, which favors new formats and sizes like RTDs. 86% of the time people drink in the company of others.
- No trend break from weight-loss drugs ("supposedly 15 million Americans" use them at any time) or from US cannabis legalization. Moderation is mostly "a developed Market phenomenon".
- Portfolio: \~62% is premium-plus vs an industry average around 35%. Diageo sold value brands (value and popular price points in India) that "represented a lot of volume" but weren't set to grow. It buys where it lacks a brand or price tier: ultra-premium tequila, and non-alcoholic Ritual in the US after Seedlip in the UK. She expects almost a quarter of the category to be super-premium-plus by 2028.
- Casamigos, bought from George Clooney (per Tangen for a billion dollars), "it's been a great brand for us win-win". Diageo has 13 billion-dollar brands and is number one or two in many categories.
- Scotch economics: Johnnie Walker Black is a 12-year-old whisky, so stock for sale years ahead must already be in barrels. Skill in distilling, blending, maturing and global distribution makes it a competitive moat, and there are "great margins across scotch" because prices track time in the barrel, from Red up to The Vault, a personal blend for £50,000. Tangen's point: brown spirits need "pretty Deep Pockets", with no sales while stock ages.
- Tequila: blue agave takes seven years to grow before any aging. Don Julio Reposado (aged at least six months) is among the fastest growers, a surprise that has "taken over kind of the world". Tequila spans white and dark spirits and day and night occasions.
- Small formats: 50 ml and 375 ml bottles "are driving the growth in the US right now", and Diageo now puts premium brands (Don Julio 1942) in 50 ml at \~$15–20. They suit "a pressured wallet" without cheapening the brand if merchandised well; the pack-size idea comes from Pepsi and Coke.
- Data: a consumer choice framework tracks \~220 million occasions in 37 markets for "Precision marketing". A market growth framework weighs tax, regulation and profitability. Some bets run 10 years (a Chinese whisky distillery); near term she sees more opportunity in developed markets that are recovering faster.
- Tariffs (Mar 2025): Diageo sells in 180 countries and loves free trade, but has long lived with duties (Scotch into India). They are "short-term disruptive", and "the uncertainty that's the hardest thing to plan for"; in the long run they can be mitigated.
- Consumers "drink in repertoire" and like to explore, so bars matter: people often order what bartenders recommend or the menu shows.
- Culture: staff swapped the value "be the best" for "be better", because it bred "a culture of perfection" in which bad news traveled slowly. Lower-risk launches now go through two stages instead of five, cutting "almost a year" from some processes, and killed launches (an RTD margarita with a straw) are celebrated for their lessons.
