### 371. Jane Sun — Trip.com: the 55443 formula, service as the moat and customers first through COVID (NBIM, Aug 2024)

*CEO of Trip.com Group, which she joined as CFO in 2005; her first podcast, with Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company".* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P79EQHCQbAs)

**Takeaway:** Trip.com scores itself 5 on service and brand (hardest to copy), 4 on technology and product (copyable) and 3 on price (parity, never the cheapest). Sun manages by numbers, watching gross bookings daily, and says putting customers and partners first in COVID, funded by borrowing 20 billion, is why both came back.

- Scale: NBIM owns more than 2% (\~$700 million, Aug 2024). In 2023 Trip.com had \~$60 billion of "total proceeds" (gross bookings for airlines, hotels and other partners), \~$6 billion of commission revenue and a \~$2 billion "bottom line". It earns a commission as a sales channel.
- The "55443" formula: 5 means "way above the market", where "the second player without 5 to 10 years cannot catch up". Service and branding get 5. Technology and product get 4: above the market, but once released "people can copy it". Price gets 3: "we never brand ourself as the lowest price leader".
- Role models are Singapore Airlines and Four Seasons, which charge a premium and earn trust through small details of service. Trip.com's version: a live person answers within 30 seconds, 24/7, in the customer's language, with a solution within 2 minutes. When disaster strikes, staff reach customers on the ground within 2 minutes.
- Brand trust: before COVID its priciest tour, 80 days around the world at \~$200,000 per person, sold out in 17 seconds.
- Operating leverage: over the past 10 years growth ran 20–30% a year, but "our head count in the call center is very flat" thanks to back-end investment. She expects people to be needed only for unexpected events. The growth strategy is one-stop shopping: flight, matching hotel, airport car, restaurant and weekend tour.
- AI (Aug 2024), with \~10,000 engineers: a conversational trip planner (TripGenie), Copilot cutting coding time by 15–30%, customer service, and generated video and picture content.
- Planning ladder: GDP grows maybe \~5%, travel "a couple of percentage above" that (8–10%), and Trip.com targets 15–20% compound growth going forward. The number she watches daily is "the gross proceeds we bring to our business partners". A project whose return "doesn't make any sense" gets no resources, "no matter how good the story".
- COVID: "customer first", partners second, Trip.com third. When China closed its borders on Chinese New Year's Eve 2020, customers' prepaid money already sat with airlines and hotels. Trip.com "cumulatively" borrowed 20 billion to advance refunds and set up a 2 billion fund for small partners' cash flow: "now business is back customer really trust us".
- With revenue at zero and 35,000 employees, the chairman and Sun took zero salary until the industry recovered and VPs took a 50% cut; it kept the team and ran lean instead of laying people off.
- Culture: big-company discipline with startup passion. "baby tiger" units (car rental, for example) get their own CEO, CFO and CTO plus central support for marketing and legal, which lets Trip.com run 70 product launches at once. Hiring tests integrity, hard work, curiosity and optimism.
- Trends (Aug 2024), her three E's: event-driven travel (concerts, festivals, sports; Taylor Swift), experiential travel (wellness, skiing, diving, hiking) and eco-friendly travel (electric rental cars, solar panels at partner hotels).
- Chinese outbound travel: "the demand is there the buying power is there"; Dubai airport told her Trip.com customers spend two to three times its average buyer. Visas are the bottleneck: the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand do well, while Germany has "a long waiting list for six months".
- Overtourism: travel provides "about 10% plus" of global GDP and over 10% of jobs, so the answer is content that steers visitors from the top sights to lesser-known places. Over 50% of staff, over 40% of middle managers and over a third of executives are women, helped by policies such as "2 hours 2 days and two years" and paid egg freezing.
