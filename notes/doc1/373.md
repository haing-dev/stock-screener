### 373. Chris Kempczinski — McDonald's: a global core menu, QSC&V execution and kitchen data in 15-minute slots (NBIM, Jul 2023)

*CEO of McDonald's, who started at Procter & Gamble and worked at PepsiCo and Kraft; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=On17TvxL3ws)

**Takeaway:** McDonald's believes its structural advantages protect it as long as it executes on quality, service, convenience and value, so it benchmarks against its own standards rather than rivals. Franchisees and suppliers own the restaurants and the supply chain, and data, soon AI, drives everything from kitchen staging to personalized drive-thru menus.

- Scale (Jul 2023): per Tangen, the world's biggest employer, making 6.5 million burgers a day. About 2 million people work in its restaurants, about 65 million people visit each day, and one in eight Americans has worked at McDonald's.
- Menu: a "Global core menu" of about 15 items that every restaurant must carry (Big Mac, fries); beyond that each market adds local items. That balance "has been I think the formula for our success".
- Reach: in its markets "roughly 80 percent" of consumers visit at least once a year, and the average user comes "three maybe four times a month". Changing how people eat is "beyond what even a brand like McDonald's is capable of doing"; its role is to disclose all nutrition information.
- Edge: Ray Kroc's QSC&V (quality, service, convenience, value, in his words). "we don't actually spend a ton of time uh benchmarking ourselves to our competitors"; it measures itself against internal standards with data by restaurant, hour and customer. If it executes, "the structural advantages for us are going to be what protect us".
- Franchising: franchisees license the trademark, core equities and support services, and set pay themselves; McDonald's wants "local entrepreneurs" with "pride of ownership". With 5,000 franchisees and no owned farms or factories, it sets standards (deforestation-free palm oil and beef), audits suppliers and picks "the best of the best", so collaboration is a core norm.
- People: staff want values they feel good about, fair pay and benefits, and flexibility; an app now lets crew see and swap shifts. Restaurant turnover is about 10% a year in Italy but "over 100" in the US, with a wide spread between career crew and people who leave within weeks. A US restaurant general manager runs "a three million dollar business or so", and many crew go on to become franchisees.
- eProduction: instead of a manager's intuition, each restaurant's history says what to stage in every 15-minute slot (at 11:30, eight hamburgers, a basket of nuggets, fresh fries). It is a sales and operations planning process that makes service faster and food hotter.
- AI (Jul 2023): "over the next call it five years you're going to see a pretty significant impact from AI". Uses: custom training in local languages for 2 million people (each limited-time offer needs training) and drive-thru menu boards tailored to the roughly third of daily visitors who identify themselves. The hard part is choosing where to lead, where to be a fast follower, and what to insource.
- Off-premise: post-COVID, "on the go" eating (delivery, drive-thru, digital) is enduring, and a Texas test restaurant has no dining room. But he does not see restaurants without people "in my career Horizon"; AI should be a co-pilot that takes stress out of the job.
- Cultural norms: "ruthlessly prioritizing", "discuss and not present" (not a PowerPoint culture), collaboration and inclusion, since McDonald's serves roughly 80% of the population. He pushes "horizontal" over "vertical" thinking and to "steal shamelessly": "ninety percent of the time" someone at McDonald's has already faced your problem.
- Succession, borrowed from P&G and PepsiCo: define the critical experiences each role needs, agree an honest view of each person's strengths, and keep slates for every senior job 1–3 and 4–6 years out. Otherwise succession becomes "an availability decision", not a strategic one.
- Leadership: set the "tone at the top", speak with clarity and listen, since everyone "is more of an expert in their area than I am in mine". He became CEO 25 years after business school, crediting hard work: "I'm not the smartest person in the world".
