### 375. Andrea Guerra — Prada: luxury's return to the old normal, patient creative tension and buying Versace (NBIM, Apr 2026)

*CEO of the Prada Group for about three years, after 25 years as a public-company CEO (including Luxottica); interviewed by Nicolai Tangen in Milan for NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yl7o8kMmbVM)

**Takeaway:** Guerra says luxury grew 3–5x in 10–15 years, which "any textbook" says can't last, so the industry must return to the "old normal" of exclusivity, selectivity and hospitality. Prada's answer is patience: long-term creative directors, few new stores, emotion over price, and scale across distinctive brands (Prada, Miu Miu and now Versace) toward a \~€10 billion ambition.

- Setting (Apr 2026, per Tangen): the group has grown quarter by quarter since Guerra took over three years ago, Miu Miu is "arguably now the hottest brand in luxury", and Prada has just bought Versace.
- "this industry is upside down": after "a couple of decades of constant growth", luxury grew "three times, four times, five times in the last only 10 15 years". "Let's go to any textbook. That's not possible." Now comes "a period of normalization", because "maybe we've been a little bit spoiled".
- "It's not the new normal, it's the old normal": luxury is "exclusivity, selectivity, is value, is dreams, it's experience, it's hospitality". Example: a refurbished Hong Kong store gives a whole floor to an apartment, dressed by the creative directors, for dinners and private occasions.
- Patience: winning brands keep "a constant positive tension between brand management and creative direction". "we don't have a creative director coming in and going out. We have long-term commitments. And that's the only thing that the consumer will pay in the long term." Isolated hits are "short term".
- Miu Miu (started 1993) is an inclusive, women-only brand you can wear at 25 or 50; women's brands four to five times bigger show "there is space to grow". But its hot streak is "a five year cycle, which has lasted it longer than what we expected", so "we need to be ready for normalization".
- Discipline: "my usual answer is no". Miu Miu had 170 stores three years ago and has 176 now; he prefers to enlarge stores rather than open new ones, and sees maybe 180–185 in five years, "but that's it". No new categories and no menswear.
- Selling: "people and emotions are the vehicle to everything", and "when you're able to manage the mix, your profit and loss is solid". On price, "you should never talk about pricing": once the talk turns to price, the brand has made a mistake. The industry "has lost one consumer out of five" in recent years, a sign it "has gone a little bit too far out".
- AI (Apr 2026) is used mainly in CRM: choosing which message goes to which client and when, and matching new drops to look-alike past buyers. The result: "conversion rates which I've never seen in my life".
- Young customers are "the biggest concern, the biggest opportunity". Re-Nylon, a sustainable version of Prada's signature nylon, comes with messaging on ocean plastics, influencers and "certain price points", and "it's working really well". Sustainability is not slipping down young people's priorities.
- Versace (owned by Americans for 7 years): "We were not out for acquisitions". Prada's "two engines" could take it "somewhere close to 10 billion euros", and Versace adds a different, pop-and-glamour DNA. It was "mismanaged uh as a brand not as a business"; the plan is to "plant seeds" patiently under new creative chief Pieter Mulier, with Donatella Versace still involved.
- Scale, not a conglomerate: "we're not in a niche business", so distinctive brands plus "a certain kind of scale" protect the group. Recreating an LVMH-style conglomerate? "Absolutely not."
- Regions (Apr 2026): North America and then China went through "digestion"; China now shows "a kind of plateau and slightly positive dynamic". Europe, "by definition a touristic market", is normalizing: Asian visitors are at about 50% of 2019 and Americans down 20–25% from 2022–23, because of the wars and hard east-west flights. Europe has the slowest economy, "but we have the heaviest currency today".
- Succession and style: the founding family has set the route, "It's very clear Lorenzo will be the leader in the long term", with Guerra helping the transition to Lorenzo Bertelli. Debates can run 10 days and aim for "an upper compromise"; budgets are capped at 10 pages. "energy beats experience", and his advice to the young: "Be patient."
