### 379. Andrea Orcel — UniCredit: capital allocation, the Commerzbank stake and the 40% cost/income bank (NBIM, Feb 2026)

*CEO of UniCredit since 2021 and a former investment banker (Goldman Sachs, Merrill Lynch, UBS); interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oUCIBWBo1b4)

**Takeaway:** European banks need scale to afford technology and must match fintech service and efficiency within five to seven years. Orcel runs UniCredit on capital allocation, ranking businesses by cost/income and by return on capital against a 9–12% cost of equity; a good bank now needs a \~40% cost/income ratio and a high-teens ROE.

- Europe's biggest banking challenge is "Scale and transformation": without scale a bank cannot invest enough in technology. Legacy banks and fintechs will converge within five to seven years, so incumbents must match fintech journeys and efficiency and win on what fintechs lack: "primary clients" and the ability to handle many products and complexity.
- The new benchmark: ten years ago a solid commercial bank ran a 50% cost/income ratio and earned 12–13% ROE. Today it needs \~40% and "an ambition to be in the high teens" (\~18%). The industry is "becoming more appealing", but only for banks that rethink process, technology and AI.
- Capital allocation: banks long ignored that equity has a cost: "my cost of capital is 9 10 11 or 12% or when I started 17". UniCredit ranks its businesses by cost/income and by whether their "capital being rewarded above what it cost me", then fixes the low-return ones before giving them more. The aim is "the optimal intersection of growth and distribution" through "quality" growth that defends margins where competitors don't go.
- M&A discipline: "M&A is a tool to your strategy", not an end in itself. The Banco BPM bid came after waiting three and a half years for the right terms and was priced to return 15%, "about two points above" what buying back UniCredit's own shares earned (captions: "my share back"), to allow for risk. Running 13 markets as 13 legal entities with 13 boards let the Italian team close (9 months) and integrate (11) it while the rest carried on; he admits "our evaluation did not come true".
- Commerzbank: in Sep 2024 UniCredit bought a 5% stake from the German government, thinking it had implicit support from Berlin and the bank's leadership; "that changed in the morning", which he puts down to coalition politics. Rather than retreat (an admission of error) or sit still, it built the largest position it could short of a bid through end-2025, converting derivatives toward a 30% stake with no bid "in the foreseeable future". He expects close to €800 million a year of post-tax earnings from it if Commerzbank meets its plan.
- Competition limits: authorities block deals close to 25% market share in any area, and Orcel agrees that above 20% "you need to start asking yourself if there is enough competition". Germany's market shares are far lower. After UBS took over Credit Suisse, Swiss retail competition is fine, but competition for corporate clients has declined.
- Europe's case for bigger banks: industry is the engine and "banks and capital markets are the gasoline". In Spain and Italy dealers of European car brands are empty while Chinese brands' are full, and Europe has lagged US and Chinese growth for 15 years. "government budgets are not enough", and current regulation stops banks from financing the transformation.
- Client experience is the test: if switching from Netflix to your banking app feels worse, "you will not survive in five years". Consumer-loan approval for a client who wasn't pre-approved fell from seven days to 13 minutes: 23 process steps became two and credit models run online all the time. The rebuild took three years; mortgages are next (an answer within a week). "First you need to redesign the process. Then you need to automate".
- AI (Feb 2026): at end-2024 most European banks treated it as "a curiosity"; by end-2025 all talked about it, but giving Copilot to every employee does not change how work is done. A large-company credit file takes an experienced officer six weeks; a pilot AI engine built in one week did it in 14 minutes with 98% accuracy, and 80–90% of large-cap credit staff prepare files. Banks that don't cut costs: "in five or six years you're not here".
- People: UniCredit's reskilling unit has moved \~650–700 people since end-2022, and he thinks banks cut the human touch too far: "if I'm taking a mortgage of 250k I want to see a person in front of me".
- Stablecoins: tokenising bonds, equities and mortgage contracts is "just a change in technology eliminating intermediaries", now a priority. Crypto is "too speculative for a commercial bank like us". Stablecoins are all in dollars, so Europe risks being disintermediated by the US dollar; UniCredit joined a European bank consortium for a euro stablecoin, targeting Q3 2026.
- Planning when nothing is stable: before COVID, low rates, low defaults and no inflation kept three-year projections "within one standard deviation". UniCredit Unlocked (Dec 2021) took nine months to build; Russia invaded Ukraine soon after and the plan "went in the bin". Now he sets a "direction of travel" and lets country and business teams re-plan, judging whether their adjustments head the right way.
- Results and culture: Tangen notes the share price went from 8 to 70 in Orcel's five years, plus 7 in dividends, "10 times". Orcel credits listening: "Most if not all of the good ideas that transform uni credit come from listening to people in the branches". Nine months of interviews with 20,000–30,000 of 80,000 staff found integrity, ownership and care; changing a culture dramatically takes "more than a decade".
- Decisions: "the outstanding traders are right 55% of their time" and cut losses on the rest with discipline. Decide on "thorough but incomplete information at speed", then watch and cut a mistake fast instead of defending it because the boss made it.
