### 388. Ariel Emanuel — From agent to owner: UFC, WWE and the scarcity of live sports (NBIM, Oct 2025)

*CEO of TKO Group Holdings (UFC, WWE, PBR) and long-time head of Endeavor, who turned a Hollywood talent agency into an owner of sports and live events; interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5APzGySzJSQ)

**Takeaway:** Emanuel moved from representing sports and talent to owning them, because the owner who runs the broadcast, sponsorship and production business keeps "90% of the value" rather than an agent's 10%. Live sports are scarce and streamers need them, so he sells rights through research-heavy, multi-buyer processes. When the market never valued Endeavor as a conglomerate, he split the sports assets into the pure-play TKO.

- From fees to ownership: "half my life was representation, half my life was ownership". Because Endeavor and IMG negotiated sports rights worldwide, a property that was "localized but had a global possibility" could be bought and made to yield "a great deal of value".
- UFC (bought 2016): Endeavor already represented it and had negotiated many of its broadcast deals. Owning and running it meant "we could get 90% of the value um as opposed to 10% of the value". The bull-riding league PBR, bought earlier, had shown it could buy and "resurrect" a sports company.
- The thesis: distribution was moving to streaming, which needs sports, and "there's not a lot of sports to own". He calculated that Fox "had to have the product". Centralised international distribution, sponsorship, site fees and production (a global facility in London and five different locations) meant "we could take a lot of costs out and add a lot of value".
- What makes UFC a good asset: a one-day, six-hour event; "four quadrants" of demographics (young men, older men, now women); global, with fighters in every country in the world; 15- or 25-minute fights that are "great for social"; and the dominant player in its sport.
- Selling rights: a research team builds 100-page decks for each buyer (Netflix, Amazon, Apple) showing "Here's who you're not touching" and what those new subscribers are worth. Each buyer has its own motive: ESPN wanted a big launch for its new service, and "Netflix wanted to get to an advertising base".
- Negotiation: "fair is where we end up", a Warner Bros lawyer's lesson. With one buyer you close; with several "you drag it out as long as you can to get the highest price". Keep your word, because "It's not your last deal if you're talented."
- Conglomerate discount: after Endeavor listed, "The market never really understood" a group spanning sports, social and books, and "That conglomerate conversation never happened". Meanwhile representation businesses were getting $7bn valuations, so "the pure play was the right thing". TKO now holds UFC, WWE, PBR, On Location (premium ticketing) and IMG; Endeavor's representation business is private.
- Why media giants struggle to own a sport: WWE talent is on signed contracts and UFC fighters are independent contractors, a "very kind of fluid situation". So "it's very difficult for a media company like a Disney, a Comcast, a Warner Brothers, a Netflix to own a sport".
- Keeping a sport young needs three things: a social-media strategy with the athletes, pressure on distributors about their marketing, and sponsor integrations around events. He says "we have some of the youngest demos in the business".
- Priorities (Oct 2025): "We've just finished the majority of the broadcast deals" at home and abroad. Still to do: WWE and UFC integration savings, a slow boxing roll-out and jiu-jitsu. A boxing event on Netflix drew 70,000 people to Allegiant Stadium, and TKO seeks an addendum to the Ali Act. Tangen cites \~350 events a year.
- A missed deal: just three months after buying UFC, Endeavor worked on buying Formula 1 from its private-equity owner. Brexit created "a banking issue", and he and Silver Lake's Egon Durban said "we're probably going to regret this".
- Live-events bet: he expects AI to bring four-day or even three-day work weeks, and more free time spent on live experiences. Endeavor has raised \~$2bn of equity for assets such as Frieze (art fairs), Barrett-Jackson (collector cars), tennis and food festivals, to build "a mini version" of Live Nation.
- Lessons: one of his biggest failures was not going public the first time. "it's not how many punches you can throw it's how many punches you can take." TKO is run as a "democratic dictatorship" with "multiple leaders" and weekly calls on each property, so that when mistakes come they can be mitigated.
