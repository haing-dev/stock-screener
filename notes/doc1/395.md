### 395. Stefano Domenicali — Formula 1 as an entertainment platform: scarce races, new fans and long-term contracts (NBIM, May 2025)

*President and CEO of Formula 1, after more than 30 years in cars and motorsport at Ferrari (including as sporting director), Audi and Lamborghini (as CEO); interviewed by Nicolai Tangen on NBIM's "In Good Company" podcast.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1iQFttdkQGU)

**Takeaway:** Domenicali runs F1 as an entertainment platform that competes with movies and music, not other sports. With only 24 races and 20 drivers its content is scarce, and long-term contracts with partners leave few calendar slots free. Growth comes from new fans (Drive to Survive, the US, a younger and more female audience), more sponsors and, next, fan data, on top of media rights, sponsorship and promoter fees.

- An entertainment platform: F1 is "definitely a sport", with the race and the drivers ("the jewel of our sport") at the centre, but "we are not here to compete against other sport platform we are here to compete with movies with with music with events".
- New fans: the audience used to be "male 90%" and older; in new markets it is now "40% women 60% men 35 years of age".
- Scarce content: with 24 races and 20 drivers, F1 has "not a lot of content" next to sports with 100 games or hundreds of athletes. 24 is the maximum, and historic venues keep their place only if they keep investing. Long-term agreements with partners keep F1 "very healthy" but leave very few free slots, so new venues may come by rotation. The US went from one race to three in three years; the Far East and Africa, the only continent without a race, are next to watch.
- Revenue: the main streams are "media rights, sponsorship, promoter fees", and the next step is fan data, used to personalise content and offers for millions of fans. A couple of years ago F1 had four global partners; it now has many more, tiered as global, regional, technical and official. When people on F1's first earnings calls (it is a public company) asked how it could keep growing, his answer was "there is always place to grow".
- Storytelling: Netflix's Drive to Survive, which grew out of Chase Carey's American approach and was helped by COVID free time, opened new markets "to people that didn't have a clue who we were". It showed drivers usually hidden by "gloves overall helmets", and the rivalries it shows are real. The Apple film F1 with Brad Pitt (producer Jerry Bruckheimer, director Joseph Kosinski) opens in mid-June 2025, shot during race weekends; a kids' TV feed uses child commentators.
- Governance: the FIA is the regulator and F1 is the "commercial right holder", unlike sports where one federation runs everything. Cadillac joins as a new team next year after meeting criteria of credibility and "added value". Red Bull owns two teams, which he accepts because it "has invested a lot to develop the sport".
- Rules: next year's regulations are about technological relevance (sustainable fuel; power split 50% electric, 50% combustion), not reshuffling the order, since the gaps between cars have "never been so so close". If one team gets ahead, a mechanism helps the others catch up faster. F1 targets carbon neutrality by 2030; most of its emissions come from logistics and from events of 400,000 people, not from the fuel burned on track.
- Car vs driver: the best driver in the worst car would not lead the championship: "if you don't have a good car as a base, you cannot be a champion." The great drivers can "slow down everything" in their heads to find milliseconds.
- What the best teams have: "the best organization, the best people" and the best process. People must handle pressure (a pit crew changes tyres in two seconds), work as a team and know the business.
- Speed as an organisation: "Speed means to be the fastest with the elements that you need to make a decision", without gathering "More information to delay the process of a decision". Each evening he checks whether he took decisions that day, and every event ends with a debrief against a checklist. When his people make a mistake, "is my fault", and the job is to fix it.
- For investors: "to think big", and to add an emotional side to purely rational parameters.
