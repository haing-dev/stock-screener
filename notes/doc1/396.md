### 396. Bob Iger — The magic of storytelling: highlights (NBIM, Nov 2024)

*Short highlights cut of Nicolai Tangen's "In Good Company" interview with the CEO of The Walt Disney Company.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NVLX-pG9GJc)

**Takeaway:** Disney's stories last because people share them across generations and the brand keeps its core values. Iger manages creators with empathy, and on his return as CEO he put creativity back at the centre of the company and had to learn patience.

- Why Disney lasts: people enjoy its stories together, which "compounds the impact"; its values stay consistent, so "you know what you're going to get when you see the name Disney on a product"; and it makes things "enjoyed by multiple Generations all at once".
- Managing creatives: "empathy is the biggest key". A story comes from within its creator, so even when Disney must push for better results, it should do so "by putting yourself in their shoes".
- Judging hits after 50 years: he is surprised on the upside, by something that resonates more than he expected, more often than by an unexpected flop. Staying in tune with the public is "one of the things that we get paid to do".
- Return as CEO (two years after stepping down in 2020): he calmed the company, put creativity back at the centre, and streamlined the organisation so it was clear "who was responsible for for what". The hardest part was patience, "starting from a very low point": "it's almost impossible for a perfectionist to be patient".
- Routine: up just after 4am, coffee, then a workout of about an hour, "for sanity and vanity purposes".
