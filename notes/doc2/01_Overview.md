### The 12 biggest cross-cutting lessons

1. **Barriers to entry beat growth.** Airlines grew for a century with almost no cumulative profit (Hohn). Own businesses whose dominance you'd bet on for 10+ years (Kantesaria, Dorsey, Li Lu).
2. **Survive first.** Losses wreck judgment, and intact capital is what lets you exploit crises (Singer, Watsa). Funds die from redemptions and drawdowns at the wrong time, not from volatility (Quantedge, Orr, Jensen).
3. **Omissions cost more than mistakes.** The biggest regrets are the 50–100x stocks never bought or sold too early (Li Lu, Kantesaria, Gaonkar, Davis, Sundheim). In venture, missing a great company costs more than backing a poor one (Hoffman).
4. **Be contrarian *and* right.** If everyone agrees, there's no alpha (Matei, Rubenstein); the edge is an "important difference of opinion", found "maybe just a few times a year" (Einhorn). Great companies and discoveries looked wrong to smart people at first (Hoffman, Armstrong, Perlmutter).
5. **Distrust your own conviction.** "Conviction is often bias" (Xin), and 100% certainty is a red flag (Gaonkar). Most errors are overconfidence (Carver). Use blind analysis and written independent views (Perlmutter).
6. **Cut losers and add to winners.** A market moving against you is "one of the strongest tells" (Orr). Positions can become *less* risky at higher prices (Pilecki). Great investors exit quickly when wrong (Rubenstein) and let winners become their biggest positions (Stahl).
7. **Edge is relative and decays.** Edge is what the marginal participant lacks (Lebron), and it returns when rivals leave: passive investing emptied the value industry (Einhorn, Apr 2024). Shared data creates crowded trades (Jha). NLP signals get arbitraged away (Kantos), and each automation wave collapses pattern-matchable alpha (Ying).
8. **AI accelerates research and encodes judgment; it doesn't find alpha alone.** Data is too scarce and markets too unstable (Chan, Gebhardt). Judgment and disciplined execution stay human (Ying, Yokokawa), but analysts using agents can cover 3x the names (Akash).
9. **Culture and speed are management choices.** Culture is what leaders do under pressure, repeated relentlessly (Walmsley, Ricks, Vélez). Speed comes from parallel work, pre-set criteria and small "jet ski" teams (Ricks, Bloisi).
10. **Curiosity plus humility is the common trait.** It shows up in billionaire investors (Matei, Rubenstein), founders (Gentz, Forrest Li) and Nobel scientists (Perlmutter) alike.
11. **Quality compounds; cheapness doesn't.** Over long holds your return converges on the business's return on equity, "no matter what your starting price is" (Davis, Terry Smith via Munger). A cheap, low-return business is a value trap without a catalyst (Davis, Greenblatt, Bravo).
12. **Sizing decides outcomes as much as selection.** "sizing is probably 70 to 80% of the equation" (Druckenmiller, via Soros). Set size from the worst case before the trade, not after it (Gerber, Sundheim, Sandler).

### Where the experts disagree

| Question | One side | Other side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is AI a bubble? | Not a collapse-style bubble: demand is real and power-constrained (Ullal, Hoffman, Nadella); AI is *under*-hyped (Bloisi) | "The bubble is ahead of us" (Jensen); AI is "over its skis" (Singer); big tech may commoditize (Kantesaria); "there will come a glut for sure" (Altman, Oct 2025) |
| How concentrated? | 8–15 stocks at \~10% each (Kantesaria, Hohn, Dorsey); bet big when there's no downside (Li Lu); 40–50% in one idea (Alfred) | \~40 names because idea ranking is unreliable (Pilecki); \~350 positions (Orr); \~300 markets (Quantedge) |
| Moat vs management | Management now \~70% of the weight (Dorsey); "the single biggest determinant for us is management" (Watsa) | Management matters only "somewhat"; the business always wins (Hohn) |
| Shorting | Never made cumulative money; you can't fund the losses (Hohn, Li Lu); a "negative carry business" (Alfred) | Short structurally doomed or empire-building firms, not frauds (Orr); short on catalysts (Gaonkar); short overvaluation only with "some kind of deterioration" (Einhorn), pre-sized for squeezes (Sandler) |
| Living much longer | \~50% chance of longevity escape velocity in our lifetime (Armstrong) | Radical extension is "highly unrealistic today" (Ramakrishnan) |
| Will AI make markets efficient? | More efficient overall (Ying) | Noisier, with more entropy (Cedar) |
| Sell a compounder when its multiple expands? | No: valuation sales of Costco and Amazon were Davis's biggest mistakes; "fair value is a very broad range" (Davis); "selling the Costcos too early" is the biggest mistake (Sundheim); trimming swaps a possible drawdown for a certain tax bill (Stahl) | Yes, if the price rises and the facts don't, the IRR falls, so reallocate (Haley, 2022); low turnover is an output, not a goal (Dorsey) |
| Are banks quality businesses? | Yes: durable franchises valued as if fragile; banks at 10–12x vs utilities at \~20x with about twice pre-crisis capital (Davis, late 2024) | No: avoid (Hohn); "black boxes", so size smaller (Greenblatt); few moats "we can understand" (Haley) |
| Is ROIC the test of quality? | Return on capital is the single most important measure (Terry Smith); pre-tax return on tangible capital above 20% (Greenblatt) | "No longer the canonical test"; meaningless where investment is expensed (Dorsey); GAAP can be badly distorted (Davis) |
| Can a simple screen do the job? | Two factors (EBIT/EV, return on capital) roughly doubled the market's return over 17 years (Greenblatt) | "You can't screen for switching costs", and visible moats are efficiently priced (Dorsey); Greenblatt keeps only part of Gotham's money in formula-like strategies |
| Own serial acquirers? | Avoid: diworsification (Kantesaria); acquisition-led revenue targets are "empire building" (Haley, 2019) | Over half of 104 multibaggers grew partly by acquisition despite an \~80% failure rate (Haley, 2020); small, programmatic deals can work (Dorsey) |
| Underwrite multiple expansion? | Never: "it's nowhere in our models" (Davis); a thesis resting on it may not be a great thesis (Haley) | Most of the money comes from re-rating when doubts prove wrong (Sundheim); two-thirds of the median multibagger's return came from rerating (Haley's study) |
| Cut when a position moves against you? | Yes: price is the tell (Orr, Pilecki); exit with no emotion and ignore cost basis (Druckenmiller); reassess, then reduce or exit (Einhorn) | No: size up front so you never have to cover; cutting after the fact destroys capital (Sundheim); buy more if your written reasons still hold (Watsa) |
| Should generalists own biotech? | No: unpredictable (Kantesaria); outside what Alta Fox can understand (Haley) | Yes, through a trusted specialist team and sector timing (Druckenmiller, 2025–26); "Science is just a language" (Flynn); generalists will hold 5–10% within a decade (Casdin, 2022) |
| Is Bitcoin a monetary hedge? | Yes: own it in an inflationary bull market (Druckenmiller, 2022); "the most conservative investment there is", with supply capped at 21m (Stahl, 2024–26); $1m a coin by 2033–35 (Alfred, Nov 2025) | No value "above what a cost to mine them" (Tepper, May 2018); gold "is already recognized as a globally acceptable central bank reserve asset" (Einhorn, May 2022) |
| Do index funds distort prices? | No: "99.9% of active management is random noise minus fees" (Kantesaria); even Stahl called indexing's record "rather compelling" | Yes: float weighting underweights owner-operators and shrinks the true float toward "a hyperbola" (Stahl); passive emptied the value industry (Einhorn, Apr 2024) |
| Is AI hardware a growth or a cyclical trade? | Growth: "estimates are like 50 to 70% too low" (Shaulov); "It's all been earnings" (Sacerdote) | Cyclical: AI plays are "either hardware or capital goods," so "valuations look really full" (Marques, Pertento, May 2026) |
| Is application software an AI casualty? | Yes: Whale Rock was net short app software entering 2026 (Sacerdote); horizontal applications are in trouble (Shaulov) | No: domain leaders deploy "most of the agents being deployed right now" and retention is holding (Bravo, May 2026); "core systems don't tend to get swapped out" (Kacher) |
| Will US companies keep beating the world? | Prices assume so, but that "doesn't usually repeat"; take risk mostly outside the US (Karniol-Tambour, Jun 2022, May 2025) | "US exceptionalism in the private sector is still here," so money leaves slowly (Citrone, May 2025), though he runs \~70% of risk abroad |
| Does slowing growth compress the multiple? | Yes: slowing growth with high multiples is "a mix that doesn't give you much upside" (Cohen, Point72, May 2025) | Not if cash flows are durable: Costco trades above Amazon's multiple at a fraction of its growth (Sundheim, May 2021) |
