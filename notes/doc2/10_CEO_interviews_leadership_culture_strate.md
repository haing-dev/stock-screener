## CEO interviews: leadership, culture, strategy & capital allocation

The sixteen CEOs and founders share a pattern. They find a large market with real customer pain, attack it with a structural cost or technology advantage, and concentrate resources. They treat culture and speed as things leaders deliberately build, and in 2025–26 they are rebuilding their companies around AI.

| Leader | Company | Signature lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emma Walmsley | GSK | Culture is the CEO's job; single-point accountability; free capital by spinning off what nobody values |
| David Ricks | Eli Lilly | Speed is a management choice: parallel work, pre-set go/no-go, "beat the last team's record" |
| Jack Dorsey | Block | Build on open protocols; don't rush into advertising; let users co-create the product |
| Nikesh Arora | Palo Alto Networks | Obsess over product; platformize a fragmented market; in crises, create certainty |
| David Vélez | Nubank | Attack oligopolies with a 20x cost edge and fanatical customer love |
| Forrest Li | Sea | Ride the tech wave; build local solutions from first principles; stay humble |
| Jayshree Ullal | Arista | Win white spaces the giant ignores; "do the right thing" even when it's costly |
| Dylan Field | Figma | When software is abundant, design and taste differentiate; hire for slope |
| Reid Hoffman | LinkedIn / Greylock | Deploy, iterate, learn; don't drive every risk to zero first |
| Robert Gentz | Zalando | A clear flywheel aligns leadership; "dare to fail" |
| Brian Armstrong | Coinbase | Compete on trust; be right before consensus; "action produces information" |
| Alain Lam | Xiaomi | Honest prices; 10x resources on one product; own the core tech |
| Fabricio Bloisi | Prosus | Ambidextrous organizations with "jet skis"; AI is under-hyped |
| Yu Kai | Horizon Robotics | "A terrible business can be a great business"; be the platform |
| Satya Nadella | Microsoft | "one company with one platform logic"; keep the fleet fungible; "agents are the new seats" |
| Sam Altman | OpenAI | "Platform plus killer app"; bet compute ahead of demand; work with people for a decade or more |

### 1. Start from customer pain and product obsession

- Find a big market protected by an oligopoly and full of pain. Vélez took 5 months to open a Brazilian bank account and got a card at 1,200% interest; Brazil's top five most-hated companies were banks. Xiaomi saw low-quality, high-price phones. Shopee's founder couldn't accept that next-day e-commerce didn't exist in Southeast Asia.
- Customer love pays in the long run: Nubank's NPS reached 90–94, and it refunded customers after a bug they never noticed (Vélez). Sea and Zalando track satisfaction daily.
- "Tech companies die when they stop focusing on product" (Larry Page, via Arora). Products take 4–7 years to build.
- Trust is the moat in regulated businesses (Armstrong). Arista resolves support issues in 25 minutes on average, ICU-style (Ullal).

### 2. Speed is a management choice

- Lilly roughly halved development times by running steps in parallel (accepting financial risk), pre-specifying the results needed to advance, and setting each team's record as the next target (Ricks). "Companies seen as slow should look first at their leadership."
- Xiaomi launched a car in under 3 years from a standing start. Chinese automakers' model cycles are \~2 years versus \~5 in Europe (Lam, Yu Kai).
- "Jet skis": 5–10-person teams test 20–30 versions before any big budget. Big companies fund a $1B idea and lose a year (Bloisi).
- A clear flywheel lets leadership align and scale fast: Zalando went from €5M to €1.2B in four years (Gentz).

### 3. Focus and concentration of resources

- Xiaomi put \~10x the usual R&D into one first car instead of many models, and focuses on three things a quarter (Lam).
- "Put all your wood behind one arrowhead": the world values only a few things you do (Ricks).
- GSK spun off a consumer division that no analyst asked about, freeing the balance sheet for R&D (Walmsley).
- Horizon has done one thing (computing for robots) for 11 years, and Arista entered only white spaces that Cisco ignored (Yu Kai, Ullal).
- Microsoft is "not a conglomerate" but "one company with one platform logic." "One capital outlay" serves Azure, M365 and Xbox cloud gaming, and the blended return must match its margins (Nadella, Oct 2025).

### 4. Moats seen from the operator's side

- "Heavy investment, slow return" can be the moat: auto-grade chips take 5–8 years to reach production, which keeps VC-funded entrants out (Yu Kai).
- Complexity is a moat: Zalando's pan-European logistics became a B2B product, and Shopee built its own address system (Gentz, Forrest Li).
- Platformization of a fragmented market (Arora); custody depth that makes products sticky (Armstrong); software architecture that self-heals (Ullal).
- Field's warning: far less software has moats now, so design, craft and point of view decide winners.

### 5. Culture is what leaders do

- Culture is "not an HR issue" but the CEO's job. Say it relentlessly, since a third of staff may be new; hire, pay, promote and fire for it; measure it with anonymous team feedback (Walmsley).
- Vélez has personally run culture onboarding for every new hire every month since 2013. Bloisi holds brutally transparent "strategy share" sessions covering what's going badly.
- Values that recur:
  - customer obsession;
  - ownership ("owners, not renters");
  - frugality (Zalando's €60 book debate);
  - humility (Sea's foundational value);
  - "do the right thing" (Arista replaced customers' gear at its own cost).
- Being too nice is a failure mode. Lilly added structured conflict, with a contrarian team arguing against every Phase 3 advance (Ricks). "Kindness means being extremely direct" (Field).
- "Dare to fail": without calculated risk you are playing "poker without blinds" (Gentz).

### 6. People: hiring, firing and scaling yourself

- "Companies grow exponentially; humans grow linearly." Vélez's biggest mistakes were changing the team too slowly. Hire missionaries, not mercenaries.
- Hire for slope, the rate of improvement, and ignore degrees (Field). Hire entrepreneurs, empower them and fire when it doesn't work (Bloisi). "Hire people better than you" (Walmsley).
- Grow your own talent with graduate rotations: Sea and Quantedge do this where no experienced pool exists.
- Leaders must scale themselves:
  - Move from solving every problem to clarifying goals and staffing (Ricks's Canada lesson).
  - "Show your work" so people can reason without you (Field).
  - Step in only on ambiguous or long-term calls and on culture (Gentz).
  - When people escalate, ask what they've already tried with their peers (Ricks).
- In a crisis, "your job isn't to talk about winning but to create certainty" (Arora). Leaders take the hardest, unpopular decisions personally (Forrest Li).
- Build founding teams from people you've known "for like a decade plus," mixed with "some new energy and volatility" (Altman, May 2023).

### 7. Adopting AI inside the company

- Force initial adoption: Armstrong required every engineer to try AI coding tools within a week, and >50% of Coinbase's code is now AI-written. Bloisi taught every executive to code; 20,000 Prosus staff build agents.
- AI handles \~80% of Sea's customer inquiries at human-level satisfaction. One AI fix added 8% to Lilly's GLP-1 output. Xiaomi simulated 100+ material formulas.
- Use AI to grow and learn, not just to cut costs; cutting too early slows adoption (Field). If you aren't finding it useful in substantive work, "you're not trying hard enough" (Hoffman).
- Agentic commerce is coming: agents will transact with stablecoins and order on users' behalf (Armstrong, Bloisi). Seat-based software and phone apps face disintermediation (Arora).
- Every Microsoft employee gets M365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot, and "any planning, any execution starts with AI." A network lead who couldn't hire built agents instead (Nadella, Oct 2025).

### 8. Strategy, markets and geopolitics

- Contrarian timing: "if it's consensus, you're too late" (Armstrong). Every successful idea looked dumb to smart people at first (Hoffman).
- Expand into new markets as a "call option," not betting the house (Vélez). Build ecosystems on high-frequency use cases (Bloisi).
- Europe is a recurring frustration:
  - forced divestments (Bloisi);
  - a \~2-year drug-access lag (Ricks);
  - fragmentation and too many stock exchanges (Gentz).
  - Their prescription: "get on the pitch" (Hoffman) and build a positive "European dream" (Gentz).
- Going public brings discipline and trust (Field, Armstrong, Gentz). Share prices reflect external perception, so stay true to fundamentals and be wary of euphoria at peaks (Forrest Li).
- Prefer partnership to zero-sum framing with China and Europe (Yu Kai). Anything innovative eventually intersects with government, so build a policy muscle (Armstrong).
