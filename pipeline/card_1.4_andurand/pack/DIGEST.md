## 1. Verdict

The notes agree, mostly by assertion, on how to build trend: size by volatility, mix speeds, count price drivers
not markets, never time it. They do not establish why trends exist (four mechanisms conflict). Tests with numbers
come from one paper (U01), one blog (U02) and studies only described on air. Pods: Trend, Economic trend, Carry,

(new commodity markets, a no-trade buffer, a faster book scaler) and four lines in pod specs, and adopts nothing;
the other 26 proposals are folded in or dropped. Largest unverified dependency: the one new pod rests on a seller's
unshown claims (U04). Rule 5 is open: no note dates a thesis before 2011; Economic trend's sources are 2017 and 2023.

# C. Thesis note 1.3c testing method

## 1. Verdict

1. QP13 firmly establishes one thing: a test of no skill that assumes only a zero expected specific return (the return left after the market's part is taken out) and keeps false alarms at 1 in 20, even against a made-up fund with steady gains and a hidden total loss (SHOWN: stated inequality, constructed example). Its price is not quantified: the tested stream must multiply by 20.
2. The rest is asserted, or shown on dice and four hand-picked assets.
3. Supported in our sheet: whole-month resampling instead of a t table; counting candidates; alpha against what is already held; registered checkpoints; sizing below the growth optimum (consistent, in words only); the holdout read as a screen.
4. Contradicted: citing these slides for the tail test (their own fund passes it until the loss comes) and the name Foster-Stine on a regression t-test. A one-year criterion is cut against.
5. Added: take market exposure out before judging the book; alpha earns a pod some amount, not by itself an equal share; the error budget covers only losses that show on the window.
6. Four protocol items (P1-P4, no new decisive test), one diagnostic (M1). The martingale test is dropped: as held a typical path (about even odds) clears the bar only at a Sharpe gap of about 0.84 on the design window.
7. Weakest link in the source: the scale (leverage) of the tested stream is never discussed, yet it sets the verdict; power is not shown.
8. Weakest link in our sheet as the slides see it: every guard against "many small gains, rare big loss" reads only losses that already happened.

## 4. Rule layer

Common to all rules. Panel markets as excess returns: S&P 500 (daily closes; SPY live), US 2-year note, US 10-year bond. Rules at equal risk; opposite positions net, also against cards 1 to 3; nothing sized by conviction; every version run and averaged. R1 to R4 are date rules on daily closes: the dates are the signal, so no weekly tranches (fixed here; step 3.2 checks it against shop rule 2). A date that is not a trading day moves to the trading day before. Gates read the close of the day before entry. Expiry day X is the third Friday of the month: the reader's reading of his Opex, defined in no quote. R5 is checked at month-end in four weekly tranches, as in cards 1 to 3.

**R1. Year-end window.** Long S&P 500 from entry to exit. Entry: the last trading day of October ("late October", MT227-C05-X1) or the last trading day on or before 15 December ("the middle of December to the middle of January", MT206-C04-2). Exit: the last trading day on or before 15 January ("till about the middle of January", MT104-C17-2). Form A: always long. Form B: long only if the S&P is above the previous year's last close (reader's bar), since the flows come from the year's gain: "end of year flows and a year that market is up 25% or very positive" (MT183-C13-2); the one number he ties to losing them is a fall of 20%: "If we were to lose 20% of the market, we would lose all those positive flows at the end of the year" (MT206-C05-2). Form C: long if above, short if below: "a bit of a magnifier to whatever is happening going into the end of the year" (MT163-C20-2); reading "whatever is happening" as the sign of the year to date is the reader's. Versions: 6. Also MT206-C03-2, MT104-C16-2. Hindsight: first stated 2024; flagged. Ledger: three direction calls right, one level call wrong. In the first season after he stated it the S&P rose from 5708.75 on 2024-10-01 to 5842.91 on 2025-01-14 (MT206-V001-1, right); from 6075.11 on 2024-12-05 it fell (MT132-V004-1, wrong); levels on R1's exact entry days are not in the pack. Not in the pods: none reads the calendar. Trend is often long the S&P on the same days, so the placebo with shuffled dates is the test that counts.

**R2. Option-cycle window.** Long S&P 500 from the close N trading days before X to the close of X; flat otherwise. N is 12 or 15 in a four-week cycle: 20 trading days less a gap of one and a half weeks or of one (MT076-C08-4). In a five-week cycle N is 13 in both versions: 25 trading days less his "two and a half" weeks (a fixed 15 would leave two). Second choice: also hold through the next trading day, since "Opex Fridays and the Mondays are usually the strongest day" (MT227-C05-3). Versions: 4. That the gap sits after expiry rests on "for the next two weeks" into May expiry (MT076-C07-4) and on claim text of MT076-C06-X1. No short in the gap: he speaks of risk, not of a fall. Also MT031-C03-1, MT163-C09-1. Hindsight: 2024; flagged. Not in the pods: as R1. The rule is long 55 to 75% of days (reader), so most of its return is plain exposure.

**R3. After the ramp.** Gate (reader's bar for "after a big run at the end of the year", MT132-C08-2): the S&P is above its close on the last trading day of October. Window 1: short S&P 500 from R1's exit day for 10 trading days ("January 13th to 15th window", MT132-C11-1; "one and a half to two week window", MT132-C08-3). Window 2: short from the close of February's X to the close of the first trading day after March's X (MT150-C17-3; "if we can hang on through Feb, then kind of creates potential energy for March", MT150-C17-X2). Versions: the two windows (2). Also MT132-C08-2, MT227-C06-3. Hindsight: window 2 is one dated case, 2020; window 1 is one live call, wrong at 12 months in the ledger (MT132-V005-1); the 2026 pullback call is scored wrong from its 2025-10-13 start (MT104-V001-2), yet the ledger's own levels have the S&P 2.7% lower on 2026-03-10 (6781.48) than on 2026-01-09 (6966.28), so it is not a miss of a January-February pullback; by the ledger's levels window 1 lost in 2025 (5842.91 on 2025-01-14, 5994.57 on 2025-02-03); flagged. Not in the pods: Trend is long after a ramp; this is the opposite.

**R4. Month-end re-leveraging.** T is the month's last trading day. Take the sign of the S&P's move from the previous month's last close to the close of day T minus d minus 1. Hold that sign (long after a rise, short after a fall) from the close of day T minus d to the close of trading day d of the next month. The count d is 3 or 5 (2 versions); he gives no days: "some major effects do fall at the end and beginning of months" (MT206-C02-5). After gains, "reinvestment happens fund etc. at the end of month" (MT206-C03-X1); after a falling month levered funds "take their risk to constant to where it was last month" (MT076-C07-1). Hindsight: 2024; flagged. Not in the pods: Trend's fastest version looks back one month, so R4 may repeat it for a few days (step 3.2).

**R5. Disinflation steepener.** On while the 12-month change of CPILFESL (first published where vintages exist, lag 1) is below its value 6 months earlier: when "inflation cyclically is declining", "we start to see a counterintuitive steepener" (MT206-C08-2). Version 2 also needs FEDFUNDS at least 0.25 below its level 6 months earlier; that every cut steepens the curve is claim text of MT206-C10-5, so it is a version. The 6 months and the 0.25 follow card 1 (reader). Action: long US 2-year note, short US 10-year bond, equal sensitivity to a 0.01 change in yield, built as card 3's R2. Off otherwise. Versions: 2. Also MT031-C13-4. Hindsight: 2024; flagged. Ledger: steepener stances 1 of 2; his 10-year level calls 0 of 3; in 2024-10 he said the steepener he had argued for months was "not playing out" (MT206-C11-1). Against the cards: card 3's R2 holds the same pair on another condition (a flat slope); card 1's R3 and card 2's R1 hold the same 10-year short; card 1's R1 and R2 buy the 10-year.
