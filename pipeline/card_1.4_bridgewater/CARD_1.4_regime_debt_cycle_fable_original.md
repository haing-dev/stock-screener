# Playbook card 1.4 (2 of 5): regime and debt cycle (Bridgewater)

Unit of 2026-11-22. Pack: 183 claims, six recordings, 2022 to 2025. Speakers: Ray Dalio (RD), Bob Prince (BP), Greg Jensen (GJ), Karen Karniol-Tambour (KT), and Noah Yechiely (NY), a former employee, second-hand for today. Labels: SHOWN (numbers or a worked case in the quote), SHOWN (words), ASSERTED, reader (my inference). Double quotes mark verbatim quote text; claim text means the machine summary.

## 1. Verdict

1. The playbook, as far as the pack shows it: follow how debt, deficits and policy feed spending, profits and the demand for bonds; work out what prices already assume; spread risk so that no single outcome sinks the book. The framework the brief names is not in the quotes: none of the 183 states growth and inflation regimes, risk parity, or a debt-cycle template.
2. Little of it can be coded: 2 rules, 8 versions. Debt, deficits, profits, savings, tariffs and positions have no series on the data sheet. The codable form of the growth and inflation idea is the Economic trend pod we already hold.
3. Strongest link: KT's profit accounting and equity arithmetic (SHOWN in words, one worked case in the quote).
4. Weakest link: the step from debt arithmetic to a dated market move. Nobody gives a threshold or a horizon. Ledger (machine-coded): 5 of 13 resolved rows right, one row disputed; the two headline calls, US stocks down in 2022 and yields up in 2023, missed at 12 months.
5. Dating: both rules are hindsight-flagged. Two pre-2011 actions are recalled (BP's levered bond hedge, RD's use of 1930s history); neither is dated in its quote and neither carries a rule.
6. Counts: 2 rules, 10 analyst questions, 9 open forecasts, 2 diagnostics.
7. Against card 1: agrees that easing into a boom is inflationary and that the years of zero rates and bond buying starve such a playbook; conflicts on Treasuries and on sizing (spread here, concentrate there).

## 2. Causal model

**Chain 1. Debt squeeze (RD, 2023).** Debt grows, so "Debt Service becomes a higher and higher percentage of your expenses" (MT058-C06-2). Holders need a real return: "if you don't have a return in relation to inflation that is High Enough by holding that debt you won't hold that debt" (MT058-C06-3). The central bank then "is forced to choose between is interest rates Rising a lot" (MT058-C07-2) or printing (claim text). Other side: investors who "all lost a lot of money" on bonds (MT058-C14-X1), reserve holders who fear they "can get sanctioned" (MT058-C14-3), a central bank now "essentially a seller of those bonds" (MT058-C14-2). ASSERTED. Limits: no threshold or horizon; if the bank prints, the loss comes through money, not bond prices; ledger: his yields-up view wrong at 12 months.

**Chain 2. Fiscal limit (GJ, 2025).** Past a limit, "spending money becomes counterproductive rather than productive" (MT068-C09-1), and "the correlation between bonds and equities and currency will shift when you hit limits" (MT068-C10-3): "we're all Brazil now" (MT068-C08-3). Other side: investors who hold bonds as a hedge. ASSERTED. Limits: each asset's direction and the place of the limit are claim text; no trigger.

**Chain 3. Competition for capital (GJ, 2025).** Deficits plus AI spending give "this big competition for capital between fiscal and AI" (MT068-C11-1), so real rates rise (claim text); if politics forces rates down, "lowering real rates into a boom like this is particularly inflationary" (MT068-C08-2). Other side: bond holders. ASSERTED. No series for either demand.

**Chain 4. Profits need spending that is not wages (KT, 2022).** A business grows its margin only if people pay "without paying labor" (MT147-C01-1). The money comes from household "leverage borrowing spending down your savings" (MT147-C01-2), company borrowing (MT147-C01-3) or the deficit (MT147-C01-4). When these fade, with spending "totally funded by households basically dis saving" (MT147-C03-2), mortgage rates up (MT147-C03-3) and "the tightest labor market in decades" (MT147-C03-4), profits fall. Other side: shareholders, since "the market is not expecting a very significant slowdown" (MT147-C05-2). SHOWN (words): an accounting identity, which she calls "a mechanical answer" (MT147-C01-X2). Limits: no timing. Ledger: growth slowed, yet the S&P rose 16% in 12 months. In 2025 she expects the deficit back: "26 is where you can actually get the fiscal stimulation" (MT138-C03-4).

**Chain 5. Reading what is priced (KT, 2022).** A stock is discounted cash flows (MT147-C04-X1). Worked case: "if interest rates rose one and a half percent on a 10-year bond and maybe you have a 10-year stream of earnings that's a 15 equity decline" (MT147-C04-4). So "equities have basically fallen in line with the fact that rates have risen that's it" (MT147-C05-1): no profit fall is priced. SHOWN (one worked case; arithmetic, not evidence of returns). Also, ASSERTED: "back then about 30 of the stock market was commodity producers today it's more like 3" (MT147-C04-2), so the index gains less from inflation than history suggests.

**Chain 6. Policy asymmetry (KT, 2025).** Tariffs raise US prices, perhaps as "a one-off" (MT138-C04-X1); abroad "it's much more deflationary" (MT138-C04-1). The Fed is stuck, others can ease: "I like fixed income a lot more in other countries" (MT138-C03-5). On the dollar, "the big currency drivers" will "take a while to work through governance" (MT138-C04-4). Other side: investors "extremely vulnerable" if the US stops winning (MT138-C03-1). ASSERTED, a dated view. Ledger: euro and inflation rows right, slowdown and yen rows wrong (the yen is in no quote).

**Chain 7. Bonds as the hedge, and its floor (BP, KT; firm-level).** Institutions hold bonds because "if there's a recession at least I'll get some easing and that'll be kind of balancing out of all my risky assets" (MT138-C06-X1). BP recalls the fear that in "a deflationary depression it everything's going to go down together" (MT049-C07-1) and the answer built: "25 year duration implemented with futures two two times two and a half times leverage" (MT049-C07-2; an action, no year). The hedge had no room "where rates were zero" (MT138-C06-2). This is the nearest the pack comes to the balance idea. SHOWN for the mandate's terms; ASSERTED for the hedge: no result is given.

**Chain 8. Regime change (BP, GJ, KT, 2025).** The old world was "globalization and free markets" (MT049-C11-2); the new one is "modern mercantalism" (MT138-C01-2). It is inflationary: "You get tariffs" (MT068-C07-2), and "there's nothing more inflationary really than military spending" (MT068-C07-3). Capital "is more concentrated in the US than ever" (MT068-C01-2); its holders are the other side. ASSERTED. GJ concedes: "I thought it would have bigger impact than it has" (MT068-C18-2).

NY, second-hand: macro investing reads "how much the economy is growing inflation", forces "shifting the entire asset class together" (MT053-C03-3). Nothing follows from it that Economic trend does not hold.

## 4. Rule layer

Common to both rules, taken from card 1 so the two layers net cleanly (reader): checked at each month-end with data as known then (lags as in section 3); CPILFESL and UNRATE as first published where vintages exist, else latest, flagged; markets from the book's panel as excess returns; rules at equal risk, opposite positions net, nothing sized by conviction; four weekly tranches; every version run and averaged. Card 1's two entry versions are not in the pack and are not used.

**R1. Not paid to hold.** Real yield = GS10 minus the 12-month percentage change of CPILFESL. On while the real yield is below the bar; off otherwise, no tail. Action: short the US 10-year bond. Versions (4): bar at 0.0, or the lowest third of the real yield's own expanding history (at least 120 months, reader); with or without a hedge gate, which requires the 12-month correlation of monthly S&P 500 and US 10-year returns to be above zero (the sign change, reader). RD gives only a direction ("High Enough"): at zero the holder loses to inflation; the lowest third is low against what holders have known (reader). The gate adds the second reason to hold bonds, the hedge (MT138-C06-X1, MT068-C10-3); joining the two is the reader's step. Claims: MT058-C06-3, MT058-C07-2, MT058-C14-X1, MT138-C04-2. US only: the sheet names no inflation series for other countries. Hindsight: stated 2023, no action in the quote; flagged. Not in the pods: Trend shorts bonds only after they fall; Value may hold this position if its bond anchor is a real yield (check at 3.2); card 1's R4 needs core inflation above 5.0 or 4.5, R1 does not.

**R2. Rates-only repricing.** On when (i) GS10 is at least X points above its level 12 months earlier, and (ii) the S&P 500's 12-month price change in percent is no lower than minus 10 times that rise: stocks have fallen by no more than the rate arithmetic. Action: short the S&P 500 from the first on-month until 12 months after the last. Versions (4): X 1.5 (her figure) or 1.0 (earlier entry, reader); with or without a labour gate, UNRATE at or below its lowest of the prior 240 months ("in decades" read as two decades, reader). The 10 is hers. The 12-month window is the Compass one for the yield; the hold is card 1's convention, since she gives only "the lag" (MT147-C03-X2). Claims: MT147-C04-4, MT147-C05-1, MT147-C05-2, MT147-C03-3, MT147-C03-4. Hindsight: a live call in June 2022, after 2010; flagged; the ledger scores it wrong at 12 months. Not in the pods: condition (ii) excludes deep falls, so R2 is mostly on while Trend is flat or long stocks (reader); Economic trend reads growth and inflation releases, not this arithmetic; card 1's R4-B shorts stocks only above its inflation level.

**Not rules, because Economic trend or Trend would hold the same position** (reader, from the pod descriptions; step 3.2 confirms): growth falling, so long bonds (chain 7); inflation rising, so short stocks and bonds and long commodities (MT147-C05-3; MT138-C06-4, commodity part claim text); bonds of the country that can ease against bonds of the one that cannot (chain 6); following a currency move that slow money has not finished (MT138-C04-4; Trend).

## 8. What would prove it wrong, when it stops, disagreements

Wrong if:
- R1: in design months the bond's return in on-months is no worse than in off-months.
- R2: the S&P's return in the 12 months after on-months is no lower than in other months.
- Chain 4: profits stay at a record share of GDP with no rise in household, company or government borrowing (F9, unresolved after four years).

Stops working when:
- The central bank buys the bonds: RD names this branch himself. R1's stop line for the spec: WALCL rising over 12 months.
- Short rates sit at zero (chain 7): much of the holdout window (reader). Card 1 reports a like remark from Druckenmiller on the bond-buying years.
- The framework is public. NY: with "many investors trying to chase the same" thing, "the returns are falling" (MT053-C03-1).

Disagreements inside the pack:
- Bonds. RD 2023: holders will leave. GJ 2025: "there is a place for bonds in portfolios" (MT068-C10-X1). KT 2025: likes bonds, outside the US. BP's founding story is a levered long-bond hedge.
- BP, March 2025: policy makers "bring things into a reasonable balance" (MT049-C11-1). RD and GJ describe a squeeze.
- Stocks after a devaluation. RD recalls "the stock market went up a lot" (MT058-C02-1) and also "they devalue the dollar and stocks go down we have this inflationary" (MT058-C02-2). Two cases, opposite signs, sorted by inflation only afterwards.
- KT against herself: 2022 rests on the deficit fading; 2025 expects it back.
- The warning on US concentration is repeated (KT 2022, KT 2025, GJ 2025) while GJ notes diversification "hasn't worked for 15 years" (MT068-C09-3). Ledger: neither scored row a hit.

Against card 1:
- Agree: easing into a boom is inflationary (MT068-C08-2; card 1's R3 bond leg). Printing lifts financial assets (KT: "lots of money is printed", MT147-C02-4); as in card 1 it stays out of the rule layer: the series miss most design years.
- Conflict: card 1's R1 and R2 buy Treasuries after rates rise; R1 here sells them when the real yield is low. Card 1's R5 buys a country's stocks, and in one version its bonds, after its currency falls; chain 2 says that at a fiscal limit all three fall together, and RD's second case says stocks fall if inflation follows.
- Conflict in process: card 1 sizes big and cuts fast; this card spreads risk and names no stop. The shop's equal-risk rule sides with this one.

## 10. Diagnostics (zero slots, design months to 2010-12)

**D1. Room to ease.** Do bonds stop balancing stocks when the short rate is near zero (chain 7)? Countries in the panel with a 3-month rate on the sheet: TB3MS, IR3TIB01JPM156N, IR3TIB01CHM156N, IR3TIB01DEM156N then IR3TIB01EZM156N, IR3TIB01GBM156N, IR3TIB01CAM156N, IR3TIB01SEM156N. Gate, fixed now: if the other mean is above zero and the low-rate mean is less than half of it, on at least 30 low-rate months, a floor version (short rate at least 1.0) is added at step 3.1 to every rule of cards 1 and 2 that buys bonds as a hedge, and a short rate below 1.0 becomes a stop-working line in this pod's spec. ## 13. Self-check: the strongest argument against each rule

- R1. His own model has two branches, and if the bank prints the bond short loses: real yields were low for most of the 2010s while bonds rallied (reader's recollection, not the pack). His one dated view scored wrong. On my recollection the real yield was above zero when he spoke, so the first version was off that day. Value may already hold the trade.
- R2. Its one live use saw the S&P rise 16% although the growth forecast behind it came true. Without a profit series the rule keeps only the rate half of her argument. The 1.5 is an example she gave, not a threshold she uses. The 10-year stream is a round number; a longer one makes condition (ii) easier to meet.
- Both. Six recordings by one firm's leaders, made partly to present its approach; the only failures told are GJ's on COVID and mercantilism. The firm's real system is not in the pack: these rules are my reading of public remarks, not Bridgewater's process.
