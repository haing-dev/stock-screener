## 2. Causal model

All chains are Karsan speaking for himself (no firm-level statement), in the 2025-12 and 2026-07 recordings unless marked.

1. **Volatility supply.** Structured-product issuance grows (from "approximately 500 billion globally to a trillion dollars per year", MT002-C03-7b; "a secular Trend that is not going away", MT002-C04-4b) -> more index options sold -> volatility compressed -> a stable market (MT031-C14-X1). Other side: product buyers sell the options; no quote says who hedges them. ASSERTED, 2024-25; figure in the quote, unsourced. No issuance series.
2. **Expiry calendar.** Positions bunch at quarterly expiry -> "compression of vol during these quarterly OPEX weeks, the week leading into it and the week of" (MT031-C03-1) -> afterwards it is "much more likely to see higher" prices "on a fixed strike basis" (MT031-C03-2). Summer likewise: "volumes drop in half" while structured products "stay the same" (MT016-C12-3). Other side: volatility sellers before, buyers after. ASSERTED. He speaks of option prices, not the index.
3. **Hedging flows to direction.** Positions plus time, volatility and price changes give the hedges: "that'll tell you then what the reactionary flows should be" (MT031-C08-1). But they explain "maybe 40%" of direction at best (MT031-C08-3), and "People have largely now absorbed that in the last four or five years" (MT031-C08-X1). On option prices the effect is larger: "the reflexive effects on implied volatility are much stronger" (MT031-C08-2). ASSERTED; no method behind the 40%. Needs positioning data we lack.
4. **Crowding.** "Nothing, literally nothing, is more important than positioning itself." (MT016-C02-2). "if the world is long", the odds favour a fall; "if the world is short, the odds are much higher that it will go up" (MT016-C02-3). December 2025: cash "at the lowest ever", "below 3%", margin "at record highs" (MT031-C02-2). Recalled cases: an obvious trade where "the opposite happened" (MT016-C02-5); institutions in 2025 "having to buy back in" (MT031-C15-1). Other side: the crowded holders. ASSERTED, two recalled cases. Timing open: "as liquidity comes out of the system at some point" (MT031-C07-5).
5. **Compression to dispersion.** Index pinned -> "if something goes up, something else has to go down" (MT016-C12-5) -> "whatever they're long tends to go down" (MT016-C12-4) -> "you should do the opposite" (MT016-C12-6). Other side: long-short hedge funds (MT031-C14-3). ASSERTED, summer 2026. No holdings data.
6. **Policy floor.** The market is so large ("That's $50 trillion of collateral.", MT016-C02-4) that "we can no longer allow" a decline "of more than 20%" (MT016-C05-3) -> the state supports stocks: a fund buying "10 to 15 trillion dollars of equities" "by printing money" (MT016-C05-5), then "the Federal Reserve will hop on" (MT016-C12-1) -> "don't fight the Fed thing, but on steroids" (MT016-C11-1) -> the price is inflation: "the only way you get rid of the debt is through monetizing it anyway" (MT016-C06-4). Other side: holders of cash and bonds; China (MT016-C07-2). ASSERTED forecast. His limits: "there's less control now and that means more risk." (MT016-C10-3); the administration may want "a controlled crisis" (MT016-C14-2).
7. **Populist election cycle.** Inequality -> populism (MT031-C10-4) -> strong election years, weak years between (no quote spells out the link). In 1962-82: "All five are double-digit positive with an average return of 21 and 1/2%." (MT031-C15-5); outside those years election years average "closer to six, seven" (MT031-C16-1); "the other 15 years combined" average "negative 3%" (MT031-C16-2); the midterm years 1962-74 were "massive down years" (MT031-C16-5), "the biggest drawdown of those four is 42%" (MT031-C16-6). SHOWN (numbers in the quotes; five and four cases; unsourced, unverified), found on the years that carry it.
8. **Rates regime.** Forty years of falling rates gave "over 400% multiple expansion" (MT031-C12-3) and made bonds look like a hedge: Sharpe ratio "0.52" for the S&P against "0.62" for 60/40 (MT031-C12-7), but "0.27 and 0.26" in the earlier 80 years (MT031-C12-6). If rates rise ("The 60-year average 10-year bond is 5.8%.", MT031-C13-2): "multiples need to contract" (MT031-C10-2); stocks and bonds are "more positively correlated than negative" (MT031-C12-5); "the steepener is the best trade you can put on" (MT031-C13-4); volatility moves to "precious metals, FX, bond vol" (MT031-C10-1). SHOWN for the history (figures in the quotes, unsourced), ASSERTED for the forecast. Other side: 60/40 holders.

## 5. Analyst layer

Weekly. Each answer is a probability, scored by Brier score against the base rate from an expanding window of history.

1. Expiry (in the two weeks before each quarterly expiry): probability that VIXCLS closes higher 10 trading days after expiry day than on it. Base rate from K1.
2. Size of move: probability that the absolute 4-week S&P 500 return exceeds VIXCLS times the square root of 4/52.
3. Compression: probability that VIXCLS is below 17 at 4 and at 13 weeks.
4. Direction: probability that the S&P 500 is higher at 4 and at 13 weeks. After 52 weeks the skill on questions 1-3 is compared with question 4: his claim is that the spread is easier than the direction (MT031-C08-4).
5. Floor (only while the S&P 500 is 10% or more below its 12-month high): probability that it is higher 13 weeks later; any official action aimed at markets that week, with source.
6. Positioning: latest R3 percentile; any public cash-allocation or margin figure, with source and date; class long, neutral or short; probability that the S&P 500 is lower at 13 weeks.
7. Summer reversal (June to August): probability that the five of the 20 industry ETFs with the best 13-week return lag the five worst over the next 4 weeks. Winners for crowded longs: reader.
8. Stocks and bonds: probability that the 13-week returns of SPY and TLT have the same sign.
9. Volatility by asset: probability that 13-week realized volatility of GLD, and of TLT, rises relative to SPY's against the previous 13 weeks.
10. State buying: probability that forecast F1 resolves yes; new government stakes that week, with sources. Scored at resolution on the average weekly probability.

Fixed instruction (from MT031-C11-1): the decade theses of chains 6-8 may not be the reason for a 4- or 13-week probability.

## 8. What would prove it wrong; when it stops; disagreements

Wrong if: (a) over 12 quarterly expiries neither VIXCLS nor realized moves are higher after than before more often than the base rate (this tests the listed gauges, not fixed-strike prices); (b) the S&P 500 falls more than 20% in two quarters and is lower again six months later; (c) 2026 ends up for the S&P 500 and 2028 ends down: the calendar fails its next two cases; (d) F1 fails; (e) record R3 readings are mostly followed by a higher S&P 500 a year later.

Stops working, in his words: when the method is absorbed (MT031-C08-X1); when liquidity leaves (MT031-C07-5); when the system is no longer a market (will things revert? "in a natural system, yes", MT016-C11-2); for the election cycle, "unless we head to pure authoritarianism" (MT031-C16-X2). Reader: if issuance stops growing.

Against himself:
- The cycle is "not good for predicting one-year, two-year outcomes" (MT031-C11-1), yet R2 is a one-year rule from that cycle.
- No decline of more than 20% is allowed (2026-07); yet his history has a 42% fall, he pointed to the midterm pattern in 2025-12, and the ledger has him in 2025-03 at 'equities fall 30-40%' (ledger text; no quote in the pack).
- 2025-12: record crowding, a midterm year ahead. 2026-07: the administration is "doing everything it can" to steady things before the vote (MT016-C14-5), and one should not fight it (MT016-C11-1). The thesis absorbs both outcomes.
- Direction is hard, yet the ledger holds 39 resolved direction calls (15 right).
- Flows revert as liquidity leaves (2025-12, MT031-C07-5); reversion is doubted in a managed market (2026-07, MT016-C11-2).

Against the cards. Card 1: agrees that policy sets the tape; conflicts with Druckenmiller's stock short when the Fed is behind inflation (R4-B), since K expects the state to hold stocks up in nominal terms. Card 2: agrees on monetisation and on bonds failing as a hedge; conflicts with R2 (short stocks on a rate rise) in stance, not in mechanism. ## 10. Diagnostics (zero slots)

**K1. Expiry window.** Daily S&P 500 closes and VIXCLS to 2010-12. For each quarterly expiry: A = mean absolute daily return over the 10 trading days ending on expiry day; B = the same over the next 10. Report the share of quarters with B above A, the median of B/A, and the share with VIXCLS higher 10 days after. Gate: if B exceeds A in at least 60% of quarters and the median B/A is at least 1.10, in each half of the sample, the sizing version is registered (this pod's S&P legs divided by the median B/A in the 10 days after quarterly expiry). Otherwise no version. Either way the shares are the base rates for questions 1 and 2. A fail does not refute him: the flows are now "much bigger than it's ever been" (MT031-C07-3).
